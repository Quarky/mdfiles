
# EXTENDED RESIDENTIAL EXPERIENCES
## The Ventral City — Jorra, Cato Neimoidia

# PART ONE: THE RESIDENTIAL MID-LEVELS
## Extended Living World — District III

---

## THE APARTMENT BLOCKS IN DEPTH

The mid-levels are not just a transit zone between the commerce district and the Ossivane.
They are the densest concentration of interrupted lives in the ventral city. Every apartment
is a room someone left in a hurry or left carefully or left believing they would be back
within the week. Fifteen years have not touched the intention behind the objects.

The party can enter any unit they can open. Most doors are unlocked — the residents
took their keys with them out of habit and there was no reason to lock an abandoned city.
Some are jammed by structural settling. A few are sealed from the inside, which means
something either locked itself in or was locked in by someone else.

---

### THE HIGH ROUTE — WALKING THE EXTERIOR BRIDGES

The exterior walkways connecting the apartment blocks are designed for convenience:
covered, wide enough for two Neimoidians to pass comfortably, with decorative
ironwork railings. Fifteen years have not been entirely kind to the ironwork.
Some sections are solid. Some sections are not.

**Moving through the high route:**

Every third crossing, the GM should check the state of the bridge:

| Roll or describe | Bridge condition | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| Sound | Solid, ironwork intact | No check required |
| Settled | One side has dropped 15cm | Average Coordination or lose footing, grab rail |
| Separated | One anchor point pulled from the wall | Hard Athletics or Coordination to cross |
| Gone | Bridge is simply missing | Route blocked, find another way |

**What the party sees from the high route:**

The high route runs along the exterior of the building stack. On one side: the apartment
windows, most dark, some with curtains still hanging. On the other side: nothing.
The canyon. The mist. The distant suggestion of the forest floor far below.

At certain points the high route passes directly alongside a window rather than between
buildings. The party can see directly into apartments they pass. The GM should describe
one or two of these as they move:

> *A dining room. The table is set for a meal that was never served. Three place settings,
> the dishes still stacked neatly in the center of the table as if someone was about to
> serve. The chairs are pushed back slightly — not knocked over, just pushed back, the
> way you'd push back a chair if you were getting up to answer the door and expected
> to return in a moment. The food is long gone. The dishes are not.*

> *A child's bedroom. A mobile hanging from the ceiling, turning slowly in the
> draft from a broken window — not much, just enough to suggest motion.
> The bed is made. The toys are on the shelves. A stuffed creature of some kind,
> clearly well-loved, sits propped against the pillow as if waiting.*

> *A study. Papers on the desk. A chair pulled out from the desk but not knocked
> over. On the wall, a map of Neimoidian trade routes, the kind you would have
> in a home rather than an office — a person's working map, not a decorative one.
> Three routes are marked in red ink. Someone was tracking something.*

### APARTMENT BLOCK A — THE THREE PLACE SETTINGS

**Ground floor:** Flooded. Knee-deep standing water, dark and cold. The Mist Eel
population has moved in. Their electrical activity makes the water glow faintly in
the darker sections — beautiful and alarming simultaneously. Electronics do not work here.

**Upper floors:** Accessible via internal staircase (partially submerged ground level
requires wading through cold water to reach the stair door, or the high route from
an adjacent building).

**Unit 3 — The Frozen Meal:**

The most complete apartment in the block. The family that lived here left in such
a hurry — or with such certainty they were coming back — that the apartment is nearly
undisturbed. Fifteen years of absence have created a specific kind of stillness.
Dust on everything. The food gone but the dishes remaining. A coat hung by the door.
Three pairs of shoes arranged neatly below the coat hook — adult, adult, child.

> *Three pairs of shoes below the hook. The smallest pair are child-sized,
> fastened neatly with their closures done up, as if the child had taken them
> off properly before coming inside. They are well-made shoes, the kind
> that cost something. They are clean.*

**What can be found in Unit 3:**

The family clearly had time to pack some things — the closets have gaps where
clothing was taken — but did not have time or did not choose to take everything.
A search (Easy Perception) finds:

- A child's drawing in a protective case — a careful rendering of the view
  from the window, labeled in Pak Pak: *"What I see from my room."*
  The canyon is rendered in exquisite if slightly off-perspective detail.
  There is a tiny figure in the foreground with an arrow: *"Me."*

- A half-finished letter on the desk, ink long dried:
  *"I am going to tell you about my room because you have never been here.
  From my window I can see the clouds below us, which sounds wrong but is
  true. Father says we are safe. The Jedi are here and they are going to—"*
  *The letter stops there. Not at the end of a page. Mid-sentence.*

- A small collection of smooth stones arranged on the windowsill, clearly
  gathered by a child from the canyon below on some prior excursion.
  They are ordinary stones. Someone chose each one carefully.

**For the Pathfinder:**

A Bridge Gecko family has colonized the upper corner of Unit 3. Mother, father,
and three juveniles. They are not bothered by the party's presence. The Pathfinder
will know they are there before opening the door — a soft, warm collective presence,
uncomplicated by any of the grief in this apartment.

She can stand in this apartment full of someone else's interrupted life and feel
the geckos being entirely fine. This is perhaps helpful. It is also perhaps unbearable.

### APARTMENT BLOCK B — THE CALENDAR

**Unit 7:**

The most notable feature of this unit is immediately visible on entry: the far wall
of the main room is covered in a hand-drawn calendar. It begins two months before
the Republic assault on Jorra. It is drawn in careful, small handwriting — someone
who was tracking time with intention.

The calendar continues for six months after the evacuation date.

The days after the evacuation are not blank. They have small notations. The same
handwriting, but changed — more cramped, the letters smaller, less space between them.

> *"Day 1 — We left. Senn says we will be back within the week.*
> *Day 4 — Still in the upper city. Senn says soon.*
> *Day 12 — No word on when. I am keeping the calendar going.*
> *Day 31 — The building is still there. I can see it from the upper bridge.*
> *Day 58 — They are saying the underside is too damaged to return to.*
> *Day 89 — Senn does not talk about it anymore.*
> *Day 134 — I went back to the window today. I could still see it.*
> *Day 171 — I am going to stop now. The calendar is full."*

The apartment is otherwise stripped — this family took almost everything.
The calendar was not small enough to take or not the right kind of thing to take.

**What the calendar tells you:**

The person who drew this was watching from the upper city for six months.
They could see their building from a bridge above. They tracked the days
until they couldn't anymore. The calendar is not a document of loss.
It is a document of hope that became a document of loss becoming resignation.

If Sigg sees this, he reads the last entries without comment. Then he says:

> *"I wonder if they went back. After the prohibition was lifted. It wasn't —
> it still isn't — but some people went back anyway, in the first few years.
> Before the Empire really locked it down. I wonder if they came up here and
> read this and took it with them."*

He pauses.

> *"Probably not. People don't always go back."*

He is not talking about this apartment.

### APARTMENT BLOCK C — THE KEY DOCUMENT

This block is in worse structural condition than the others. The ground floor ceiling
has partially collapsed, making the internal staircase impassable above the second
floor. The upper floors are reachable only via the high route.

**Unit 11 — Upper Floor, Difficult Access:**

The apartment belonged to someone who kept a journal. Most of the journal is
water-damaged and illegible. One section survived intact because the book fell
behind a sealed cabinet and the microclimate behind the cabinet protected it.

Finding it requires a Hard Perception check or the Pathfinder's bonded Treveel
scratching at the cabinet from the tunnel system below (the Treveel can smell
the paper — it is curious about the dry smell in a wet environment).

**The Surviving Pages:**

Most of what survives is mundane — shopping, appointments, family observations.
Then, in the final weeks before the assault:

> *"Day 14 of the occupation. The Jedi came to the block today.
> The tall one, the one who always looks like he is listening to something
> only he can hear. He is the one who arrested Preem's father three weeks ago.
> Preem is eight years old. She watched from the window while it happened.
> She did not cry. I think she was too frightened to cry. I think we all are.*
>
> *He is not cruel exactly. He is not kind either. He is something else.
> Certain. He is completely certain about everything he does and that is
> worse somehow than cruelty. Cruelty implies doubt somewhere underneath.
> He has no doubt. He sees the future and acts on it. Some of what he sees
> must be true. He has stopped attacks before they happened. He has saved lives.*
>
> *But Preem's father was a tailor. He made clothes.*
>
> *Day 22. The Jedi with the certain eyes arrested two more from our building.
> He is following something he can see that we cannot. Maybe he is right.
> Maybe they were spies. Maybe Preem's father was something other than a tailor.*
>
> *I do not know. I only know that Preem is eight years old and she has not
> spoken in three weeks and when I pass her in the corridor she looks at me
> like she is trying to figure out if I am going to disappear too."*

**GM Note — This Document's Weight:**

This is the single most important found object in the ventral city for the story's
purposes. It is the story of Warde's crimes at human scale. Not the political
consequence, not the military history — a child who stopped speaking.

If the party has been proceeding through the ventral city gathering context,
this should land. If the Pathfinder finds it specifically — if the Treveel
led them to it — it is worth a moment of silence at the table before moving on.

Sigg, if present when this is read aloud or shared with him, says nothing
for a long time. Then:

> *"His name is in the gatekeeper as a teacher. As someone who wanted
> to pass on something good. And it is also in this journal.*
>
> *Both of those things are true at the same time.*
> *I find that harder to deal with than if he were simply a monster."*

### THE SEALED DOOR — OPTIONAL DISCOVERY

On the third floor of Apartment Block C there is a door sealed from the inside.
Not just jammed — deliberately sealed with a heavy internal bar, the kind
Neimoidian apartments have for additional security.

Whatever is on the other side has been there for fifteen years.

**Getting in:** The door can be forced (Hard Athletics), or the Treveel can find
a connecting maintenance access from the tunnel system (Average Mechanics to open).

**What's inside:**

An old Neimoidian male, deceased. He died approximately twelve to fourteen years ago
based on the physical evidence. He chose to stay when everyone else left. There is
no note — at least none that can be found — but the apartment around his remains
tells a story.

He continued living here for approximately one to three years after the evacuation,
based on the accumulated details. He had a supply of preserved food. He kept a
fire (a small, carefully contained one — there are ash deposits in a stone basin
he improvised). He maintained a daily routine — the evidence of habit is everywhere.

He kept a second calendar. Unlike the one in Block B, this one has no text.
Just marks. Every day, a small mark. Approximately four hundred marks total
before they stop.

On the windowsill, facing the canyon, there is a small shrine — ancestor
sculptures, three of them, arranged carefully. They are the only decorative
objects that were not taken when the evacuation happened.

He stayed for his ancestors.

**What the party does with this is their choice:**

There is nothing to take, nothing to recover, no quest element here.
This is just a person who decided not to leave and then died in his apartment
looking at the canyon. It does not require a response. It is just true.

The Pathfinder, specifically: if bonded with any animals, she will have felt
nothing from this room when approaching it. No animal presence. One of the
quietest places in the ventral city. Even the geckos do not come here.

### THE FLOODED GROUND LEVEL — DETAILED

**Moving through the flood zone:**

The water is approximately knee to hip deep throughout the flooded sections,
varying by location. It is cold — not dangerously cold for a short transit,
but uncomfortable enough that Neimoidians, who prefer warmth, would have
found it intolerable. The party may not.

The water is not clear. It has fifteen years of sediment, organic matter from
the vine growth above, and whatever settled out of the air over a decade and a half.
Visibility in the water is approximately zero. The floor beneath the water is the
original corridor floor, which is mostly flat and intact, but furniture has fallen
and objects have slipped off surfaces and are now invisible beneath the water.

Every twenty meters of travel through the flood zone:
- Average Resilience against strain from cold and physical difficulty
- On a failure result, the character's foot catches on something — furniture,
  debris, an object — which may be investigated (diving into the water,
  which requires a Resilience check against additional strain) or ignored

**What can be found in the flood zone (if investigated):**

The objects that fell into the water and were not recovered are a specific category —
heavy things, or things that fell at the last moment, or things people decided
were not worth carrying. On a successful Perception check while searching the water:

- A sealed document case, waterproof, containing three years of personal
  correspondence between two Neimoidian siblings. Undamaged. The letters
  are mundane — family news, business updates, minor complaints.
  The last letter, dated two weeks before the assault, ends:
  *"Come visit soon. We are fine here. The city is nervous but it will pass.
  These things always pass. I will have the guest room ready."*

- A child's toy — a small mechanical creature, once wind-up, now corroded.
  It cannot be fixed. It is the size of a fist. It was someone's favorite.

- A Trade Federation-era datapad, corroded and dead. The casing has the
  initials of its owner scratched into it: K.T.J. (This may or may not
  be Kuto Jaral. The GM can decide. If it is, returning it to him
  regardless of its function earns a quiet, extended moment of recognition.)

**The Mist Eels:**

The Mist Eels are not aggressive toward large creatures. They hunt small fish
and invertebrates that have colonized the flood water over fifteen years.
What they do is generate low-level electrical fields for communication and hunting.

In the water, these fields interact with anything electronic. Comlinks give static.
Scanners produce random data. Personal devices behave erratically. A character
who is wearing a significant amount of metal armor may occasionally feel a faint,
uncomfortable buzz — not harmful, just unsettling.

**For the Pathfinder:**

The Mist Eels are the most numerous animals in the flood zone and they are
surprisingly easy for a Force-sensitive to sense — their collective electrical
activity registers as a kind of crackling warmth in the Force, like static
that is also alive. The Pathfinder can navigate by this. She knows where
the densest concentrations are, which tells her where the main corridors are
versus dead ends, because the eels follow the current and the current follows
the open passages.

If she chooses to attempt a bond with a Mist Eel, she must be in the water.
The bond attempt requires Average Animal Empathy. A bonded Mist Eel can disrupt
specific electronic systems on command — useful for the Mist Eel's section
of the tactical toolkit later — but will not leave the flood zone.

### THE TREVEEL TUNNELS — DETAILED

The maintenance tunnels beneath the residential district run parallel to the
main corridors but approximately two meters above flood level. They were
used by maintenance workers to access the building foundations without
going through resident spaces.

**Entering the tunnels:**

Access points are in the sub-basement of each building — below even the
flooded ground floor. Getting to the access points requires either:

- Descending a maintenance shaft from the upper floors (Average Athletics)
- Finding an access hatch in the flood zone and opening it while standing
  in water (Average Mechanics, at minimum strain from cold)

**Inside the tunnels:**

The tunnels are narrow. Silhouette 1 characters fit comfortably. Silhouette 2
characters must remove external gear to pass through the narrower sections.
The ceiling is approximately 1.5 meters, which is fine for most Neimoidians
but means humans and other tall species travel bent.

The tunnels branch extensively. Without a guide, navigating them is Hard
Survival or Hard Perception. With a bonded Treveel, no check is required —
it knows every branch and has strong opinions about which routes are
structurally sound.

The Treveels have been using these tunnels as a primary habitat for years.
The current colony consists of one bonded-able adult (if the party has not
already bonded it), one mated pair, and three juveniles. They are not aggressive
but they will observe the party extensively and the juveniles will follow
at a distance.

**What the tunnels reveal:**

Moving through the tunnels gives the party a different perspective on the
district. They are below the floor level of every apartment they pass under.
Through the maintenance access points in the tunnel ceiling, sounds would
have traveled — conversations, footsteps, the sounds of daily life.

The tunnel walls, in the drier sections, have been used as a message system
by the ventral city's post-evacuation visitors. Mostly Syndicate runner marks —
route indicators, cache symbols, timing codes. But interspersed with the
Syndicate marks are older things. Scratched into the stone:

> *"We were here. — The Ossivane children, year before the war"*
> *(Seven names scratched below it, child-sized heights marked on the wall beside them)*

> *"This tunnel goes left to the school and right to the market. I am leaving
> this in case anyone needs to know."*
> *(No name. The handwriting is adult, careful.)*

> *"If you find this, the Willan family made it out. We are in the upper city.
> Unit 44, Bridge Level 3. Come find us if you can."*
> *(Whether the Willan family is still there, or still alive, is not known.)*

## THE DEAD GARDEN — EXTENDED

The Dead Garden in the palace district (commerce level edge) is a location
that develops over the course of the party's time in the ventral city.
It is not just a place they find Sanna Olpan — it is a place that accumulates meaning.

### First Visit

When the party follows the Neimoidian Hound here, the garden is quiet.
The hound drinks from the stone basin. The stone hoppers regard the party
from a distance. The juvenile arch serpent in the south wall moss does not move.

The garden contains:

- The worn spot where Sanna Olpan sits — a depression in the low stone
  wall where someone has sat many times, the stone smoothed by years
  of contact
- A small wrapped bundle of food for the animals — fresh enough to have
  been left today
- A cracked mosaic floor that would once have been beautiful — abstract
  Neimoidian pattern, the kind you find everywhere in the commercial district
- A single vine growing from a crack in the north wall that has produced
  three small flowers. They are not native to Cato Neimoidia. They are
  from the Neimoidian homeworld. Someone planted this vine deliberately.

The party can examine the garden and learn something from each element
without a skill check. The food bundle means someone comes here regularly.
The smoothed stone means it is the same person each time, sitting in the
same place. The homeworld vine means that person cared enough to bring
something from somewhere else and plant it here.

The Pathfinder can feel the accumulated care in this place. It is different
from the rest of the ventral city. Most of the ventral city feels like
suspension — lives interrupted and held still. This feels like continuation.
Someone has been continuing something here.

### Sanna Olpan's Arrival

Sanna comes at dusk. She comes every day. She has been coming here for
approximately six years, since she found the garden by accident while
trying to find a route to the ventral city to look for information
about her son's whereabouts.

She never found what she was looking for. She kept coming back to feed the animals.

**Her appearance:**

She is in her late fifties, Neimoidian, her skin the mottled gray-green
of significant age and stress. She carries a worn bag that contains
the food bundle. She has clearly been doing this long enough that it
is entirely routine — she does not scan the garden for threats before
entering, she does not hesitate at the entrance, she just comes in
and begins her task.

And then she sees the party.

**The encounter:**

She stops. She does not flee. She is not young and she is tired and
the hound, crucially, is still here — it has not indicated threat.
She looks at the animals in the garden and then at the party and
then she makes a decision.

> *She sets down her bag. Takes out the food bundle. Begins unwrapping it.*
> *"You didn't frighten the hound," she says. Not looking at them.*
> *"He frightens easily. He doesn't frighten easily at the same time.*
> *He frightens at wrong things. People who mean harm, he knows.*
> *You can sit if you want. There's room."*

**What Sanna knows:**

She is not an information source in the usual sense. She has not been
following the city's politics closely. She knows what she has seen
from her position — someone who moves carefully through the upper city
and the ventral approaches and who watches things.

She knows:
- That the ventral city has had Syndicate activity — she has seen the runners
- That Imperial patrols come near the entrance points occasionally but
  do not go deep
- That someone has been leaving food in the school building basement —
  she does not know who, she found it once when she was exploring
  (it is the nightcrawler colony that is drawn to it, not a person,
  but she does not know this)
- That her son Gren was arrested by Warde's forces during the occupation.
  She does not know where he is or if he is alive. She has been trying
  to find out for fifteen years.

**What she needs:**

She does not ask for help. She is too old and too tired to ask for things.
But if the party spends time with her and the animals and the garden,
she eventually says:

> *"I know someone who might know about my son. A stormtrooper captain.
> Old enough to have been there. He serves under Irbian now. They say
> he was loyal to Warde once, which means he saw what happened.*
>
> *I have never been able to get near him. A Neimoidian woman from
> the ventral city asking questions about a boy Warde arrested —
> they would notice that. They would make something of it.*
>
> *But you are not from here. You are asking about Warde anyway.*
> *Maybe you could ask for me too."*

This is a direct bridge to Tacker (TH-313). Sanna gives the party
Tacker's name, his posting, and the one detail she has heard:
that he keeps a personal memorial — a list of names, people he
remembers from his service on Jorra. She does not know if Gren's
name is on it. She has been trying to find out for fifteen years.

### Returning to the Garden

If the party returns to the Dead Garden after finding Tacker and asking
about Gren, the scene that follows depends on what Tacker told them.

**If Gren is alive (in prison, off-planet, or in the Syndicate):**
Sanna does not react with the relief the party might expect.
She sits quietly for a long time. Then:

> *"Fifteen years is a long time to be somewhere without your family knowing.*
> *He might not want to be found. People change. I should be ready for that."*

She is ready for that. She has been preparing for that possibility
for fifteen years while simultaneously refusing to accept it.
Both things are true.

**If Gren is dead:**

She already suspected. She has suspected for at least ten years.
She kept coming to the garden anyway.

> *"The hound found me two years after I started coming here.*
> *I think he knew I needed something to feed.*
> *He is a very practical animal."*

She keeps coming to the garden after this conversation. The party,
if they return to the ventral city at any point, will find her here.

# PART TWO: AFTER RETURNING TO JARAL
## The Module Quest Conclusion and Its Aftermath

---

## THE RETURN

Getting back to the upper city with Jaral's items is not guaranteed to be simple.
The party has been in the ventral city and has presumably stirred some things up —
the Syndicate cache may have been disturbed, the BX droids may have been engaged,
the structural condition of the high route may have worsened.

The GM should consider what has changed since the party went down and make the return
feel like a different journey than the descent. Not necessarily harder. Just different.
The party knows this place now.

**If the Rusted Stalker bonded:**

The stalker will follow the party to the crossing point and stop. It will not
go above. Its world is the ventral city. The Pathfinder knows this. The stalker
knows she knows. It sits and watches them until they are out of sight.

This is fine. The bond persists. She can feel it from above.

**If the Canyon Manta pair was observed:**

The external descent route, if used for the return, passes the manta nesting platform.
One of the pair is present. It does not react to the party. It is much larger up close
than it seemed from the Ossivane windows. The Pathfinder can feel it clearly — old,
calm, entirely unconcerned with anything happening on the bridge above. It has been
here for years. It will be here after they are gone.

## THE RETURN TO JARAL — SCENE BY SCENE

Jaral is in his apartment. He is almost certainly not asleep — he has not slept
well since the party went down, for reasons he would not articulate if asked.
His daughter Mira is there. She has been waiting with him without quite
admitting she was waiting.

When the party enters with the bag of items, read or paraphrase:

> *Mira sees the bag first. She stands up from where she was sitting and
> looks at her father. Jaral looks at the bag. He does not immediately
> reach for it. He puts both hands flat on the table, like someone bracing
> themselves, and says:*
>
> *"You found it."*
>
> *Not a question.*

### Item by Item

Jaral does not open the bag and dump everything out.
He takes items one at a time as the party presents them.
The GM should pace this — it should not be a rapid inventory check.
Each item is a scene, however brief.

**Datapad — family records:**
He takes it. Checks the screen. Dead, of course — the battery died years ago.
He sets it aside without trying to activate it.
> *"I know what is on it. I did not need to see it again.
> I needed it to exist somewhere other than my memory."*

**Datapad — business correspondence:**
He sets it aside without opening it or commenting on it.
He does not look at it again during the rest of the conversation.
The business is gone. The routes do not exist. He knows this.

**Holographic portrait:**
He holds the projector unit for a long moment. Does not activate it.
Mira, from across the room:
> *"Father."*
He sets it down carefully in the center of the table.
> *"Later."*
He means it.

**War bond certificates:**
He unties the ribbon — the same ribbon his wife tied fifteen years ago —
and fans the bonds out on the table. He looks at them for a long time.
> *"They are in better condition than I expected. The desiccant.*
> *She would have been pleased about the desiccant. She thought
> about things like that. I did not. I thought about routes and margins
> and whether the cargo would arrive on time.*
>
> *She bought these the week after the Separatists took Naboo.*
> *I told her it was foolish. That the Confederacy would not win.
> That the bonds were worthless.*
>
> *I was right. She knew I was right. She bought them anyway.*
>
> *She said sometimes you buy the thing that represents what you want
> to be true, and that is not the same as believing it is true,
> and anyone who cannot tell the difference is not paying attention."*

He looks at the party.
> *"I was not paying attention. Not then. Not about this."*
He refolds the bonds carefully, reties the ribbon.
> *"I am going to keep these."*

**Wife's journal:**
He does not open it. He holds it for a long time.
Mira starts to say something and stops herself.
Jaral sets the journal down on top of the war bonds.
> *"Not tonight."*
His voice is fine. He is fine. He is simply not doing that tonight
in front of anyone, including his daughter.

**The locket:**
He opens it. Looks at the portraits inside — his children,
young enough that they would have been grubs in the portrait.
Mira's breath catches slightly across the room.
He closes the locket. Does not set it down.
He keeps holding it. The rest of the conversation he holds the locket.

**Navigation charts:**
> *"These routes do not exist anymore. Three of them were
> Separatist-controlled by the time the war ended. Two more
> were redirected when the Empire reorganized the sector lanes.*
>
> *I drew these charts. Before computers did it. I made them
> by hand and I was proud of them because they were mine.
> Nobody makes charts by hand anymore."*
He examines them for a moment with the eye of someone who still
understands everything they show.
> *"They are correct. For what they describe. They are correct."*

**Ceremonial sash:**
He picks it up, sees the damage — minor, one corner frayed —
and waves a hand.
> *"It does not matter. Thank you."*
He means both sentences equally.

**Chest of knickknacks:**
He opens this one. Looks at everything inside, piece by piece —
each object is small, each is something a person would consider
not worth keeping but clearly could not throw away. He does not
narrate what each is. He looks at each, replaces it carefully.
Closes the chest.

> *"My wife kept these. I do not know what half of them are.
> I never asked. I should have asked."*

**The wine bottle:**
He sets it on the table. Looks at it for a moment.
Looks at the party. Looks at his daughter.
Then:
> *"I am not going to open this alone.*
> *Will you stay?"*
He is asking the party. He is also asking Mira, who has been
trying not to be visible in this room for twenty minutes.
Mira goes to get glasses.

**The crystal sculpture:**
The last item. He picks it up and the light from his apartment
catches it and throws small rainbows across the table and the
war bonds and the locket he is still holding.
He looks at it for the longest time of anything.
Then:
> *"My wife chose this. We had been married eleven years
> when she bought it and I had never once told her I loved it.
> I loved it from the day she brought it home.*
>
> *I am not a man who says things. I thought she knew.*
> *She probably knew. She was much more observant than me.*
>
> *But I should have said it."*

He sets the sculpture in the center of the table, where the light
will continue to throw rainbows. He does not move it.

## THE WINE — EXTENDED SCENE

Mira brings four glasses. She has clearly decided the party has earned
this and is not going to pretend otherwise. She pours.

The wine is a pre-war Neimoidian vintage — a specific cultivar grown
only on the purse worlds, no longer produced because the vineyard was
destroyed in the fighting. The bottle is the last one Jaral knows of.

It tastes like something that cannot be replaced. The party may or may not
be able to identify this. Jaral can see whether they appreciate it.

The conversation over the wine is unhurried. Jaral does not usually talk.
But he is old and tired and his things are on the table in front of him
and there are people in his apartment who went somewhere he could not go
and brought back what he could not carry himself. He talks.

**What Jaral talks about over the wine:**

About the Trade Federation — not defensively, not apologetically.
He was part of it. It was his world. He knows what it became. He has
been thinking about what it became for fifteen years.
> *"We told ourselves we were improving trade. Reducing conflict through commerce.
> There is something to that. There is also the rest of it. Both things are true.*
> *I was good at the routes. I was less good at the rest of it."*

About the ventral city — what it was before.
> *"You saw it as a ruin. I saw it as a home for twenty years.*
> *I want you to know it was not always dark.*
> *The canyon in the morning, when the mist was coming up —
> we would open the floor windows and the mist would come in,
> just a little, just the edge of it.*
> *My daughter used to catch it with her hands.*
> *She was three years old and she thought she could catch mist.*
> *I did not tell her she could not. I let her try."*

He looks at Mira. She is looking at the table.
> *"She does not remember this. She was too young.*
> *I remember it for both of us."*

About what comes next:
Jaral does not know what he will do with any of these objects now.
He brought them home to have them. He has them. The rest is unclear.
> *"I thought once they were back I would feel finished.*
> *I do not feel finished. I feel like something is resolved.*
> *That is not the same thing.*
>
> *Perhaps that is enough."*

## MIRA — A SEPARATE SCENE

At some point during the wine conversation, Mira finds a moment
when her father has stepped away — to refill glasses, to take
a quiet moment with the journal he has not opened yet.

She speaks to the party quietly and directly.

> *"He has been trying to get those things back for fifteen years.*
> *He posted that notice on the board every three months.*
> *He never changed the wording. I used to think he was being stubborn.*
>
> *I understand now that he was being faithful to something.*
> *He was going to keep asking until someone said yes.*
>
> *Thank you. Not for the objects. For saying yes."*

She is not sentimental. She is Neimoidian and she is her father's daughter
and she has grown up watching him grieve with the specific Neimoidian style
of grief that does not announce itself. She is thanking the party because
she is not capable of not thanking them and she does not want her father
to hear her doing it.

## PAYMENT AND AFTERMATH

Jaral pays. Five hundred credits per item recovered, as promised.
Eleven items: fifty-five hundred credits total.

He does not make the payment feel like a transaction. He counts it out
carefully and sets it on the table beside the wine. He does not hand it
to anyone specifically.

> *"I said I would pay. I pay what I say I will.*
> *That much I learned from the Trade Federation and it was worth learning."*

A small, dry humor. The first one. Mira's expression suggests this is normal
and she has been waiting for it.
