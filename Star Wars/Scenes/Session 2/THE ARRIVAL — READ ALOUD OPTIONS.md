## THE ARRIVAL — READ ALOUD OPTIONS

### Arriving via Shaft 14-C (Internal)

> *The wall gives way and cold air hits you — colder than the upper city, damp with
> something that smells like old stone and standing water and vegetation that has been
> growing without sunlight for a decade. You are standing in a wide concourse. The
> ceiling is high — this was designed to feel grand. Much of it is still intact.*
>
> *The lights are off. They have been off for fifteen years. Your glow rods throw
> shadows that make the space look enormous and then small by turns. Ahead of you
> the concourse opens onto what was once a public square. The floor is cracked.
> A vine has come through a collapsed section of the south wall and spread across
> half the ceiling. In the distance, through the far archway, you can see the edge
> of the bridge — and beyond it, nothing. Just the mist and the canyon far below.*
>
> *It is completely silent except for the drip of water somewhere to your left
> and the distant, barely audible sound of wind moving through structural gaps.*

### Arriving via External Descent (Exterior)

> *You come off the last section of cable onto a platform that juts from the
> side of the bridge like a balcony over an abyss. The mist is below you,
> above you, on all sides. The upper city is a distant glow somewhere overhead.*
>
> *The ventral city is right in front of you.*
>
> *It looks like the upper city reflected in broken water. The same towers,
> the same architectural language — but dark, and tilted in places, and grown
> over with things that have no business growing on a bridge in the middle of
> the air. A massive vine network has colonized the outer facade of the nearest
> building, climbing forty meters from some root source below. Several windows
> have been blown out. One entire corner of a building has collapsed, leaving
> a diagonal scar of exposed durasteel and old stone.*
>
> *Through the nearest intact window you can see the outline of furniture.
> A chair. A table. Someone's life, stopped fifteen years ago, sitting
> in the dark exactly where they left it.*
