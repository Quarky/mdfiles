# 00 — DELRISS LIVING-WORLD DASHBOARD

## What this guide is

This is the complete, splitter-ready GM guide for the Delriss interlude in *Chronicles of the Gatekeeper*. It is written to run at the table rather than summarize the published adventure. Every playable scene appears later as its own H1 section so an H1 splitter can turn the master into individual notes without breaking scene usability.

The guide preserves the published route and mechanics while adding the practical material needed for a living world: changing environmental states, NPC behavior, dialogue, off-screen motion, map zones, clue logic, failure-forward results, optional encounters, and consequences that remain true after the party leaves.


## Provenance legend

| Mark | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **[MODULE]** | Published *Chronicles of the Gatekeeper* fact, procedure, or rule, paraphrased for table use. |
| **[CAMPAIGN]** | Established campaign continuity that modifies how this group reaches or leaves Delriss. |
| **[ADDITION]** | New living-world support that does not replace the published outcome. |
| **[DEVIATION]** | A deliberate change to published order, assumption, or transition. |

When a paragraph has no tag, it is connective GM guidance built around the tagged facts. Additions should enrich play without being mistaken for published canon.

## Campaign boundaries

- **Start:** after Cato Neimoidia, when the group has the last recovered crystal but still lacks usable astrographic data for Moraband.
- **Core objective:** reach the submerged Jedi sanctuary, learn that its library cannot provide the needed route, recover the Moraband coordinates from the Delta-7 in the hangar, and escape the collapsing facility.
- **Active ship:** **[CAMPAIGN]** use the party's newer upgraded vessel, with space for its current companions and animals. Do not substitute the former small winged craft.
- **End:** after the party escapes Delriss and confirms the Moraband vector.
- **Next module:** **[DEVIATION/CAMPAIGN]** the campaign runs the separate Shard Moon story before the separate Moraband story. This guide provides only the handoff; it does not embed either package's scenes.
- **PC protection:** this guide does not redefine, rebuild, or overwrite any player character. Sigg remains an existing PC and is not treated as an NPC resource.

## Expected play length

| Mode | Likely table time | What to emphasize |
|---|---:|---|
| Focused published route | 3–4 hours | Lead, descent, one room per level, Delta-7, escape. |
| Full living-world session | 5–7 hours | Transit roleplay, approach decisions, layered exploration, archive handouts, Werron, escape consequences. |
| Two-session version | 2 × 3–4 hours | End Session A after the archive points toward the hangar; begin Session B with power, pressure, and predator clocks already active. |

Do not make exploration longer merely by adding locked doors. Length comes from meaningful choices: what to power, what to preserve, how much risk to take, which records matter, whether to repair Werron, and whether to attempt recovery of the Delta-7.

## The five truths the GM must protect

1. **The archive does not contain the Moraband route.** It contains meaningful history and the clue that makes the hangar solution legible.
2. **The Delta-7 navicomputer contains the route.** The party may reach that conclusion through skills, the technical fragment, Werron, or simple player reasoning. Never gate the only solution behind a single roll.
3. **The sanctuary survived the Purge but not the ocean.** The visible damage is primarily age, pressure, neglect, and predation—not proof of an Imperial assault.
4. **The leviathan is an environmental catastrophe, not a fair boss fight.** Weapons may redirect it or buy time; defeating it is not the expected win condition.
5. **Failure changes cost and condition, not destination.** Bad rolls add strain, lost time, damaged gear, split routes, or hard choices. They do not prevent arrival, erase the coordinates, or end the campaign.

## Information boundary

### Safe to show or say early

- Delriss is a known but neglected system.
- One moon has a globe-spanning ocean and a breathable atmosphere.
- The hidden Jedi sanctuary was an archive and retreat.
- Warde visited it before the gatekeeper was created.
- The sanctuary may have escaped the Imperial purge.
- The approach is physically dangerous even with an aquatic-capable vessel.

### Earned during exploration

- The facility evacuated in an orderly way before later structural destruction.
- The worst rents resemble animal damage, not weapons fire.
- The library's surviving works are historically valuable but contain no Moraband route.
- Technical records indicate a small craft may retain route data with only cockpit power.
- Werron is badly damaged and has little reliable memory.
- Something very large still moves around the ridge.

### GM-only until the reveal

- The enormous predator is still local and becomes increasingly aware of ship power, active sensors, vibration, and restored sanctuary systems.
- The Delta-7's navicomputer is intact enough to yield the Moraband coordinates.
- Retrieving the data or significantly powering the hangar triggers the final escalation if the leviathan has not already reached full awareness.
- The sanctuary is expected to be torn free and lost during the escape.
- **[CAMPAIGN]** the direct Moraband arrival is interrupted by the separate Shard Moon sequence.

## Run principles

### The world moves even when the players talk

If discussion becomes circular while the characters remain in a dangerous place, advance the fiction. Do not punish healthy planning, but let sound, water, pressure, power draw, and animal behavior continue. Use one visible sign before advancing a clock: a new leak, a stronger groan, a sonar return, a scuttler fleeing, or a light failing.

### Give the party enough information to make real choices

Announce risk before a decision. If powering a junction will help doors but increase the station's signature, say so through a warning, sensor readout, or obvious electrical surge. A consequence is meaningful when the players could understand the tradeoff.

### Preserve discoveries even on failure

Failed checks still reveal the critical fact. Success improves clarity, speed, or safety. For example, a failed archive search finds one damaged work and the hangar clue but costs time; a successful search finds several works without advancing instability.

### Let preparation return later

Record concrete advantages rather than vague goodwill. A mapped return route removes one escape obstacle. A repaired collar prevents a violent undocking. A fed scuttler colony points toward a safe duct. Redundant coordinate copies survive later ship damage.

## Session endpoints

- **Preferred one-session ending:** after Scene 8, once the Moraband vector is secured and the crew has had one quiet aftermath beat.
- **Two-session break:** after Scene 5, when the archive proves the route is not there and the Delta-7 clue points toward the hangar. End on a slow wave crossing the flooded archive from no visible source.
- **Emergency early stop:** after Scene 3, with the airlock open and the dark sanctuary ahead. Record every clock and entry route before ending.

## GM safety valves

If the session is running long, compress repeated doors and flooded corridors, never dialogue or consequential choices. Move the technical fragment earlier, let Werron point directly to the fighter after repair, and begin the leviathan attack as soon as the coordinates are copied.

If the session is running short, use one additional archive work, let Werron's partial memory play out, and give the party a real chance to attempt the Delta-7 repair before the attack interrupts them.

If the players miss the plot clue, an existing rank in Piloting (Space) or Astrogation is enough for a character to realize that the fighter may retain route data. This is **[MODULE]** and should be given automatically.

# 01 — DELRISS GM QUICK REFERENCE

## One-minute recap

The gatekeeper cannot provide Moraband coordinates. It remembers a concealed Jedi archive beneath the ocean moon of Delriss and believes Warde may have left recoverable navigation information there. The sanctuary is more than a kilometer underwater, fifty-plus meters across, three levels high, and canted roughly twenty degrees after years of pressure damage and attacks by a colossal aquatic predator. The library is ruined but contains a few salvageable works. The needed route is in the hangar's damaged Delta-7. A destroyed R-series droid can be rebuilt enough to function, but its memory is mostly gone and it calls every organic “Werron.” Once the party secures the route—or spends too long powering and repairing—the leviathan returns and tears the sanctuary from the ridge.

## Cast at a glance

| Figure | Wants | Fears/limits | How to play it |
|---|---|---|---|
| Gatekeeper | Help the PCs complete Warde's path; preserve the difference between memory and fact. | It cannot reconcile its remembered Order with the Purge and cannot know what became of Delriss. | Calm, precise, teacherly, willing to say “I do not know.” |
| Werron | Resume useful maintenance; categorize organics; protect systems it can still recognize. | Recognition core damage, lost memory, failing chassis, contradictory instructions. | Brisk diagnostic beeps, misplaced certainty, repeated use of “Werron.” Never a lore-dump machine. |
| Ridge-scuttlers **[ADDITION]** | Food, warmth, reflective salvage, protection of their eggs and dry pockets. | Harsh light, sudden vibration, being trapped. | Curious first, opportunistic second, violent only when cornered. |
| Abyssal leviathan | Investigate vibration, electrical activity, and prey movement; crush the intrusion in its territory. | It has no human motive and cannot be socially persuaded. | Show it through displaced water, shadow, sound, and damage before revealing its body. |
| Sanctuary | Equalize pressure, shed weakened material, conduct power through damaged paths, and continue collapsing. | It has no intact central control and cannot be made whole during this visit. | Treat it as an active environment with consistent responses. |

## Essential mechanics

| Situation | Published baseline |
|---|---|
| Plot route to Delriss | Average Astrogation. Success: about 19 hours. Failure: +6 hours. On the published severe negative result, exit near the gravity well and suffer a Critical Hit. |
| Descend in an aquatic craft | No check required by the published text. **[CAMPAIGN]** If a check improves drama, use Average Piloting (Space), but do not convert safe capability into arbitrary punishment. |
| Descend in a normal starship | Daunting Piloting (Space). Failure: 3 system strain +1 per Threat; Despair: Critical Hit. Arrival still occurs. |
| Dock a non-aquatic ship | Hard Piloting (Space). |
| Move underwater in ordinary spacesuits | Add 2 Setback to physical movement checks. |
| Open the powerless airlock | Average Mechanics or Skulduggery, or Daunting Athletics. |
| Read exterior damage | Average Gunnery or Hard Knowledge (Education): not blaster/ordnance damage. Hard Knowledge (Xenology): likely claws/teeth of a huge animal. |
| Search the library | Hard Perception or Knowledge (Education). Each success yields a work; the route is not in the library. |
| Repair damaged R-series droid | Daunting Mechanics; most memory banks must be replaced. |
| Restore Delta-7 cockpit/navicomputer power | Average Mechanics. |
| Make Delta-7 flightworthy | A few hours + Hard Mechanics. |
| Initial attack footing | Average Coordination; failure knocks prone and causes 1 strain +1 per Threat. |
| Station flips | Hard Coordination; failure knocks prone/creates delay as fiction demands. |
| Escape chase | Daunting Piloting (Space), reduced by two for an aquatic vessel. Failure: 3 system strain +1 per Threat. Published severe negative result: automatic Critical Hit. Repeat after failure, reducing difficulty by one each attempt to a minimum of Easy. |

## Critical clue ladder

| Clue | Minimum delivery | Better result |
|---|---|---|
| The damage is not an Imperial attack | The rents bend and tear unlike weapons damage. | Identify repeated claw/bite patterns and fresh scoring. |
| The archive is not the solution | After a fair search, state that no Moraband route survives here. | Recover multiple useful works and a clearer evacuation story. |
| The hangar is the next destination | A technical record or directory points to small-craft data. | Reveal the safest/quickest route and Werron's identifier. |
| The Delta-7 may hold the route | Give automatically to a PC with Piloting (Space) or Astrogation, or from the technical work. | Let Computers/Mechanics predict how to power only the storage bus. |
| The leviathan is near | Show a wave, shadow, impact, or fleeing scuttlers. | Estimate its direction and buy time by controlling signature. |

## What not to reveal early

- Do not name the leviathan before the party has enough evidence to understand the scale.
- Do not say the sanctuary is doomed when the characters first arrive. Show a damaged but explorable location.
- Do not say Werron is a companion reward. Let rescue arise from the fiction.
- Do not announce that copying the coordinates triggers the finale. Telegraph increased vibration and power signature instead.
- Do not begin the Shard Moon story inside this document. End with the clean campaign handoff.

# 02 — DELRISS LIVING-WORLD ENGINES

## Starting state

| Track | Start | Start at a worse value when… |
|---|---:|---|
| Sanctuary Instability | 0/6 | Begin at 1 after major travel delay; begin at 2 only after severe approach consequences or reckless exterior damage. |
| Power Draw | 0/3 | Never start above 0 unless the party remotely energizes the station. |
| Leviathan Awareness | 0/5 | Begin at 1 after repeated active scans or loud external weapons; begin at 2 only if the ship deliberately chases the huge sonar return. |
| Route Knowledge | 0/4 | Starts at 0 and rises through clues. At 3 the party should know the Delta-7 is the likely solution. At 4 the coordinates are copied. |
| Return Route | Unmapped | Mark Safe after a structural scan, deliberate markers, or a successful navigation effort during entry. |

## Sanctuary Instability clock

| Tick | What changes | Table signal |
|---:|---|---|
| 0 | Damaged but temporarily stable. | Slow creaks, silt, old drips. |
| 1 | Minor pressure equalization and stuck mechanisms. | A seam beads water; a door moves reluctantly. |
| 2 | A support or internal brace fails. | The whole facility shifts several centimeters. |
| 3 | New leaks and route changes. | One previously dry path becomes ankle- or knee-deep. |
| 4 | Serious collapse begins; checks during movement may carry Setback. | Metal shrieks; loose objects slide down the twenty-degree deck. |
| 5 | The station is coming off the ridge. | Viewports show anchor struts flexing; bulkheads buckle. |
| 6 | Full escape sequence; remaining exploration is impossible. | The facility rolls or drops and the leviathan commits. |

Advance for: long unresolved delays in hazardous areas, failed attempts that physically stress the station, opening major pressure seals without control, heavy explosives, restoring large power buses, or a leviathan impact.

Do not advance for: careful roleplay, a single reasonable search, reading a handout, or healthy planning in a temporarily secure space.

## Power Draw track

| Level | Benefit | Cost |
|---:|---|---|
| 0 — Dark | No energized hazards; lowest signature. | Manual doors, no lifts, poor terminal access. |
| 1 — Local emergency | Emergency lamps and one chosen local system function. | Leviathan Awareness +1 the first time; damaged circuits begin warming. |
| 2 — Section power | Hangar or archive systems can operate more easily. | Water becomes electrically dangerous in one area; Awareness +1. |
| 3 — Broad restoration | Most doors, pumps, and diagnostics briefly respond. | Immediate Awareness +1, Instability +1, and the system begins cascading toward failure. |

Power is not a morality test. It is a tactical resource. Let the players choose which section receives limited power rather than treating “restore power” as a single binary action.

## Leviathan Awareness clock

| Tick | Behavior |
|---:|---|
| 0 | Distant; no interest in the sanctuary. |
| 1 | A large return crosses the edge of sensors. |
| 2 | It circles the ridge at long distance; smaller animals alter behavior. |
| 3 | It investigates vibration and power; a shadow or pressure wave crosses the facility. |
| 4 | It makes a testing impact or scrapes the hull; the party knows something enormous is present. |
| 5 | It attacks in earnest. Begin Scene 7 at the next decisive trigger. |

Typical increases: repeated active sonar, major power restoration, ship weapons, explosions, repairing/starting the Delta-7, powering the main hangar, or copying the coordinates after the creature is already circling.

Ways to reduce or delay it once: shut down all nonessential power, launch a moving decoy, vent debris/heat away from the ship, exploit knowledge of its direction, or redirect scuttler movement without harming them. A clever plan buys one interval; it does not permanently remove the climax.

## Route Knowledge track

| Level | What the party knows |
|---:|---|
| 0 | Delriss may contain records relevant to Moraband. |
| 1 | The archive survived in part, but the facility's organization separates general lore from small-craft navigation. |
| 2 | The library has no route; a Delta-7 technical fragment or directory points toward hangar data. |
| 3 | The damaged fighter's navicomputer is the likely source and can be read without fully repairing the craft. |
| 4 | The Moraband coordinates have been copied and verified. |

The party cannot lose the campaign because a die roll fails. If necessary, advance Route Knowledge while also advancing Instability or consuming time.

## Off-screen motion

At every scene transition, silently update three things:

1. **Water:** which compartment is taking on water, draining, or equalizing?
2. **Animals:** where did the scuttlers retreat, and what did they disturb or carry?
3. **Predator:** what signal has the leviathan noticed, and from what direction is it approaching?

This produces continuity. A leak opened on Level One should exist on the return trip. A scuttler that stole a tool may leave it near its nest. A leviathan last detected beneath the ridge should strike from below, not teleport to the opposite side without explanation.

## Delay procedure

When the party debates in a hazardous place:

1. Let each player state a proposal.
2. Summarize the actual decision in one sentence.
3. Give an environmental warning.
4. If no decision follows, advance the most relevant clock once.
5. Change the scene physically and return control to the players.

Never advance multiple clocks merely because table discussion took real-world time. The fiction, not the stopwatch, determines pressure.

# 03 — SCENE 1: A SLIGHT DETOUR

## Scene card

| Field | At the table |
|---|---|
| Purpose | Convert the absence of Moraband data into a credible lead and let the party choose Delriss with informed expectations. |
| Start | The group has left Cato Neimoidia and has time to consult the gatekeeper. |
| End | Delriss is entered into the navicomputer and transit begins. |
| Required cast | Gatekeeper, PCs, any companions/animals already aboard. |
| Primary pressure | Uncertainty, not immediate danger. |
| Assets | Ship interior if available; otherwise theater of the mind. |

## What is happening without the PCs

The ship settles into ordinary post-departure routines. Systems cool, crew recover, animals resettle, and the navigation computer waits for a destination it cannot derive from the recovered crystal. The gatekeeper does not seize control of the conversation. It waits to be consulted, or manifests when the crew's discussion reaches the question of where Warde went.

If nobody activates it, a reasonable companion can ask whether the holocron knows another archive. The lead should emerge from the characters' need, not from a disembodied quest marker.

## Opening states

### If the crew is exhausted

> The ship is finally quiet enough for small sounds to matter again: cooling metal, a loose utensil ticking against a galley surface, an animal shifting in its enclosure. On the navigation display, the route field remains blank. The recovered crystal has given you history—but not a way to Moraband.

Pause. Let one player decide what their character does before the gatekeeper appears.

### If the crew is energized

> Cato Neimoidia falls behind you, but the next course cannot be plotted. Every useful route calculation ends at the same missing point. The holocron rests nearby, apparently silent while the crew argues over what can still be known.

Let the gatekeeper listen before speaking. It should respond to a question, not interrupt with a prepared speech.

### If the crew distrusts the gatekeeper

> Delriss appears in the navicomputer as an ordinary, neglected system: old coordinates, no current traffic advisories, no reason for anyone to visit. The gatekeeper offers only one verifiable claim at first: a hidden Jedi sanctuary once existed beneath the ocean of its habitable moon.

Independent confirmation of the system and its age is automatic. The sanctuary's current state remains unknown.

## Full step-by-step walkthrough

1. **Re-establish the ship.** Ask one player what their character checks first after departure. Include companions and animals as part of the lived-in vessel, not as inventory.
2. **Name the missing piece.** The group possesses history and purpose but not astrographic data for Moraband.
3. **Invite the holocron.** The gatekeeper manifests when consulted or when someone asks how Warde would have preserved a route.
4. **Establish its knowledge state.** If the gatekeeper has not yet been told about the destruction of the Jedi Order, it initially suggests the Jedi Temple on Coruscant. Let the painful correction happen in character. Once it understands the Temple is unavailable, it offers Delriss. **[MODULE]**
5. **Make Delriss a choice.** State the known facts: remote, underwater, probably unvisited, condition unknown. Let the party ask questions and prepare.
6. **Allow one downtime action per PC.** Each action may create a specific later advantage.
7. **Plot the route.** Resolve the Average Astrogation check and record the travel time/consequence.
8. **Close on commitment.** The ship enters hyperspace; the gatekeeper watches the stars vanish and does not pretend certainty about what remains.

## Gatekeeper performance guide

The gatekeeper is a preserved teacher, not an omniscient narrator. It distinguishes memory, inference, and hope:

- **Memory:** “Warde studied there before this holocron was made.”
- **Inference:** “He valued redundant records and forgotten places.”
- **Hope:** “If the sanctuary escaped notice, something may remain.”

It does not dramatize the Purge. When confronted with information that contradicts its remembered world, it pauses and revises. Its restraint makes the loss feel larger.

Useful tone lines:

- “I remember a place intended to be forgotten. That was its protection.”
- “A sanctuary is not the same thing as a tomb.”
- “I can tell you why Warde once went there. I cannot tell you what twenty years did to it.”
- “Hope is not evidence. Delriss is evidence enough to investigate.”

## Full conversation tree

| PC question | Gatekeeper answer | GM boundary |
|---|---|---|
| Why Delriss? | It was a remote archive and contemplative retreat. Warde spent time there. | Confirm location and purpose. |
| Why was it hidden? | Isolation protected study and kept the site outside normal traffic. | Do not imply conspiracy. |
| Who lived there? | A small rotating Jedi staff with service droids, not a permanent colony. | The gatekeeper cannot name survivors. |
| Was it secret from the Republic? | Obscure rather than illegal; known only to those with reason to visit. | Avoid inventing a cover-up. |
| Why underwater? | Natural concealment, solitude, and an environment with almost no settlement pressure. | Admit maintenance would have been difficult. |
| Did Warde leave the coordinates there? | Unknown. The sanctuary is the best surviving lead, not a guarantee. | Preserve uncertainty. |
| Why not another archive? | Other sites are exposed, destroyed, or impossible to reach discreetly. Delriss is one it can actually identify. | Do not invent a list of intact Jedi vaults. |
| Could the Empire have found it? | Possible, but the gatekeeper has no post-Purge records. | Do not reveal the true damage cause. |
| Are there survivors? | It cannot responsibly claim there are. | Let silence carry weight. |
| What is the moon like? | A breathable atmosphere above a global ocean; sanctuary well below the surface. | It can recommend aquatic preparation. |
| Can our ship descend? | **[CAMPAIGN]** The upgraded vessel is rated for controlled submersion, but pressure and docking remain dangerous. | Do not make capability absolute safety. |
| What should we bring? | Pressure protection, lights, repair tools, portable data storage, redundant air, and a way to mark routes. | Reward players who act on this. |
| What is Moraband? | A world the old Order approached with great caution. The gatekeeper does not understand why Warde chose it. | Do not start the Moraband exposition. |
| What if Delriss is empty? | “Then we will know one hope was false and choose again.” | The route will still be recoverable in play. |
| Why do you care? | It was made from Warde's earlier self and wants the truth of what he became, not merely completion of a directive. | Let this remain personal but controlled. |

## Preparation menu

Give each PC one meaningful action. Do not require all of these.

| Preparation | Check, if uncertain | Later benefit |
|---|---|---|
| Test aquatic controls and seals | Average Mechanics or Piloting (Space) | Boost on first underwater handling/seal check. |
| Prepare pressure suits | Easy Mechanics or Survival | Ignore the first suit-related Threat or notice a failing seal early. |
| Research Jedi sanctuary layouts | Average Knowledge (Education/Lore) | Recognize archive, hangar, and maintenance routing without a roll. |

## What is happening without the PCs

Water continues dissolving, corroding, and moving the archive. A data terminal intermittently retains charge in a damaged capacitor. Scuttlers nest in the warmest remaining dry cavity and carry reflective fragments there. At Awareness 3+, the leviathan passes close enough to displace the entire water mass. The archive is not waiting to be solved; it is disappearing.

## Player-facing openings by state

### Power 2+

> Light returns unevenly—and a blue-white arc snaps through the water along the low side of the room. Somewhere under the surface, a damaged circuit has energized the flood.

### Instability 3+

Add: “Each slow flex of the structure sends a new rain of dust and corrosion from the ceiling.”

## Archive map key

| Zone | What is visible | GM use |
|---|---|---|
| A — Entry gallery | Curved shelves, water rising down the slope. | Orientation and first salvage choice. |
| B — Central study ring | Tables, broken readers, open sight lines. | Safe staging at low Instability; becomes wave path later. |

## Full step-by-step walkthrough

1. **Show the 360 view and pause.** Let players choose where to look before calling for a check.
2. **State the stakes.** Most records are destroyed; careful work can still save individual pieces.
3. **Divide tasks.** Search, stabilize, power/isolate circuits, watch structure, and protect recovered works.
4. **Resolve discovery in rounds or zones, not one all-or-nothing roll.** Failure always yields the critical hangar clue after a cost.
5. **Reveal works individually.** Give the corresponding player handout when physically recovered.
6. **Make the absence explicit.** Once reasonable searching occurs, say plainly that no Moraband route survives in the library.
7. **Point to the hangar through at least two routes.** Technical fragment, terminal annotation, directory, existing skill rank, or Werron identifier.
8. **Let the party choose whether to continue salvage.** Explain rising clocks before they decide.
9. **End with an external pressure wave.** Water moves through the room without a local cause; smaller life flees.

## Search procedure

**[MODULE]** Hard Perception or Knowledge (Education) recovers meaningful material. Expand that into a table procedure:

1. Each participating character names a method and zone.
2. One successful Hard check recovers one work, plus another for strong Advantage/Triumph at GM discretion.
3. Failure recovers a damaged work or critical clue but costs time, gear condition, strain, or clock pressure.
4. Do not require five successful rolls to see five handouts; distribute works according to pacing and interest.
5. Once the party has two works or the Delta-7 technical record, the core conclusion becomes available.

### Skill differentiation

| Skill | What it is good at |
|---|---|
| Perception | Physically locating sealed cases, readable surfaces, and unusual preservation. |
| Knowledge (Education) | Identifying significance and reconstructing incomplete passages. |
| Computers | Reading damaged indices, terminal caches, and service metadata. |
| Mechanics | Safely powering/islanding readers and opening warped storage. |
| Coordination/Athletics | Reaching submerged or unstable shelving without destroying it. |
| Discipline/Force use | Holding focus amid loss or sensing where a meaningful object remains; not downloading facts from the Force. |

## The five recoverable works

| Work | What players receive | Plot/thematic use |
|---|---|---|

Do not summarize the full player handouts aloud if the players want to read them. Give a one-sentence find description and let the document exist as a discovered object.

## Individual discovery presentations

### Student's History

The sealed reader casing contains a student edition rather than a grand masterwork. A handwritten correction appears in the margin. This ordinariness is the value: the Temple was people learning, disagreeing, cleaning rooms, repairing tools, and receiving bad news.

### Record of Loss

The memoir survives in discontinuous passages. Salt has removed several names, making it feel less like a clue about a famous Jedi and more like a voice almost lost.

### Delta-7 specifications

The record is utilitarian and therefore better preserved. Emphasize the field-recovery instruction: isolate propulsion and weapons, apply diagnostic power, copy navigation data first. A student's note reads, “Data first. Heroics later.”

### Kelin Dass

Short fragments fit on separate display panes. Let a player choose which fragment their character keeps visible. No roll is needed to be moved by it.

### Aurilan on Form III

The surviving thesis asks what the defender is protecting and what actions would betray that protection even if they produced victory. It should resonate with later choices without predicting them.

## Terminal and evacuation loop

At Power 1, the damaged terminal can play fragments:

- “Archive closure sequence—”
- “Remove assigned personal effects—”
- “Small-craft records retained with hangar maintenance—”
- “Utility unit designation—Werron—diagnostic incomplete—”

The exact word “Werron” can appear as a service identifier. This prepares the party for the droid's repeated use of the word without explaining it.

At Power 2+, the terminal is easier to read but current leaks into the water. An Average Mechanics/Computers action isolates it. Failure still gets the clue but inflicts strain, damages a tool, or advances Instability.

## The negative clue

Say this plainly after a fair search:

> Whatever the archive once knew about Moraband is gone. You find references to navigation storage and small-craft maintenance, but no intact route solution here. The surviving Delta-7 specifications make one possibility obvious: a fighter's navicomputer can retain assigned route data even when the craft cannot fly.

If a PC has at least one rank in Piloting (Space) or Astrogation, **[MODULE]** they may realize the fighter possibility automatically even without the technical work.

## The impossible wave

When the scene needs to turn:

From the walls comes a low vibration too deep to be heard cleanly; you feel it through boots, bones, and the cases in your hands.

The hangar is barely large enough for a small freighter or several starfighters. A Delta-7 lies askew against the far hangar doors. Fuel, saltwater, and corrosion have spread through the low side. ### Dark hangar

A single Delta-7 interceptor lies askew against the far hangar doors, one wing canted upward. Beside the debris field, a torn astromech dome catches your light. 3. **Identify immediate hazards.** Fuel sheen, standing water, hanging cable, moving debris, and stressed doors.
4. **Handle Werron in stages.** Physical stabilization, power, recognition core, optional memory replacement.
5. **Deliver the Delta-7 clue automatically if already earned.** Do not demand another roll to think of the navicomputer.

8. **Copy and verify the coordinates.** Route Knowledge becomes 4. Reveal the navigation-solution handout.
9. **Ask whether they attempt full fighter repair or further salvage.** State the time and current clock warning.
10. **Trigger the finale.** Coordinate access, major hangar power, fighter startup, or Awareness 5 produces the first committed attack.

## Werron: what is published and what is table adaptation

**[MODULE]** The remains are an R-series astromech with the dome forcibly torn from its body and deep gouges in the chassis. Repair requires a Daunting Mechanics check and replacement of most memory banks. Little information survives. Its persistent quirk is calling every organic “Werron” in astromech speech, without knowing why.

**[ADDITION]** The actor and handout use **Werron** as a convenient table name for the damaged droid. That does not establish that “Werron” was certainly its original name. The recognition failure is more interesting if the party can interpret it several ways.

## Repairing Werron in stages

### Stage 1 — Stabilize chassis

Average Mechanics or careful physical work. This prevents further damage and lets the dome be reconnected temporarily. Failure succeeds with a cost: use a spare part, lose time, or leave mobility impaired.

### Stage 2 — Restore limited function

Hard Mechanics using salvaged parts. Werron can perceive, communicate in astromech, and operate one tool. Its recognition loop remains damaged. A character with no translator understands tone and repeated “Werron,” but not technical detail.

### Stage 3 — Published full repair threshold

Daunting Mechanics and replacement of most memory banks. Success creates a functional utility astromech with little autobiographical memory. Triumph may preserve one authentic memory fragment without turning Werron into an exposition source. Failure still allows limited operation; select one lasting impairment.

### Possible impairments

| Impairment | Table effect |
|---|---|
| Damaged locomotion | Must be carried or moves slowly; can still slice/repair. |
| Broken vocabulator link | Communicates only in tones plus “Werron” transcription. |
| Short power endurance | Can act for one scene or one major task before recharge. |
| Recognition loop | Calls every organic Werron and mislabels some objects by old duty categories. |
| Missing manipulator | Cannot self-repair but can interface with ports. |

## Playing Werron

Werron is competent inside narrow routines and confused outside them. Avoid babyish comedy. Its mistakes should feel like a damaged worker trying to return to duty.

- It checks seals before introductions.
- It becomes agitated when organics stand in marked hazard zones.
- It treats the Delta-7 as an unfinished maintenance task.
- It recognizes procedure more reliably than people.
- It cannot supply a lost history simply because the party repaired it.

### First activation

> The dome light steadies. The droid rotates toward the nearest organic and releases a burst of urgent binary. Your translator produces one confident word: “Werron.” The sensor turns to the next person. “Werron.” Then it points a tool toward the flooded power junction and scolds all of you.

The party should already understand the possibility through the archive, skill ranks, Werron, or player reasoning. No roll is required to have the idea.

# 09 — SCENE 7: DESPERATE ESCAPE

## Scene card

| Field | At the table |
|---|---|
| Purpose | Convert every prior choice into a payoff while the sanctuary is torn free and the crew escapes the leviathan. |
| Start | The leviathan makes its first committed impact or the sanctuary begins catastrophic separation. |
| End | The party's ship breaks the ocean surface and the creature falls behind. |
| Primary pressures | Collapsing routes, split positions, falling/flooding, ship access, underwater pursuit. |
| Adversary model | Environmental/cinematic threat, not balanced combat. |

## What is happening without the PCs

The leviathan continues attacking whether or not the party confronts it. Each strike transfers force into supports already weakened by decades of pressure and corrosion. Water seeks every new opening; unsecured objects slide toward the current low side; damaged power buses fail in sequence. The creature does not politely follow the party from room to room. Its body surrounds different parts of the structure, so its presence is felt through impacts, displacement, and glimpses until the exterior reveal.

## Full step-by-step walkthrough

1. **Establish the first impact at every occupied location.** Characters aboard ship, in the hangar, archive, and upper level perceive different consequences of the same event.
2. **Resolve immediate footing and danger.** Use the published Average Coordination check where appropriate; do not make securely braced characters roll without cause.
3. **Ask for goals.** Data, Werron, allies, archive works, fighter, ship, and route are competing priorities. Make players choose what each character is doing.
4. **Run one action interval.** Resolve exposed actions, apply prepared advantages, then advance collapse once.
5. **Change the route physically.** Flood, tilt, falling debris, jammed door, or revealed breach should follow established station state.
6. **Reunite or cross-cut split groups.** Nobody disappears because another group took longer at the table.
7. **Reach the ship or an alternate exit.** Pay off collar, return-route, power, scuttler, Werron, and fighter decisions.
8. **Reveal the leviathan fully.** The sanctuary tears free as the ship pulls away.
9. **Run the underwater chase.** Use the exact published difficulty and aquatic reduction, with crew support.
10. **Stop after the surface breach.** Give the table a breath and one interpersonal beat before Scene 8.

## GM posture

Run fast, but do not make choices meaningless. Announce the immediate hazard, ask each character for a goal, resolve in short intervals, update the collapse, and cut to the next exposed character. Avoid a single group Athletics roll that turns the whole finale into one number.

The published text explicitly says this is not a standard combat and that delays should not produce an arbitrary untimely demise. The party should have a good chance to escape with plans they invent. Costs can be serious: strain, Critical Hits, lost salvage, separated gear, Werron damage, or a harder chase.

## Opening by party position

### All in the hangar

> The first impact throws the hangar sideways. Water erupts from a seam above the control booth. The Delta-7 shifts against the doors, and Werron's dome rotates toward the passage out. A second blow begins somewhere beyond the hull with a low crunch and travels toward you plate by plate.

### Party split across levels

Describe the same impact from each location: archive water rises; Level One bulkheads jump in their frames; the ship's collar strains; sensors outside fill with one impossible return. Then ask each group for its immediate goal.

### Someone aboard the ship

That character sees the leviathan before the others: hundreds of meters of armored body, pale eyes, and a maw testing the sanctuary. They may warn, undock, reposition, fire, or create a decoy.

## Collapse clock

Start at the higher of current Instability or 3. Advance after each full action interval, major failure, or leviathan impact.

| Tick | Event | Mechanical/narrative effect |
|---:|---|---|
| 3 | First committed strike | Average Coordination: failure prone +1 strain +1 per Threat. |
| 4 | New breaches | One route floods or closes; previously prepared route remains available if protected. |
| 5 | Sanctuary rolls onto its side | Hard Coordination; unsecured objects and carried salvage move. |
| 6 | Anchor struts tear free | No further exploration; all actions must serve escape or rescue. |
| 7 | Facility slides down ridge | Return route changes; characters need a last fail-forward crossing. |
| 8 | Hull crush begins | Anyone still inside exits through ship, service lock, or emergency breach with severe cost but a genuine chance. |

If the clock began below 3, the first impact sets it to 3. Do not add empty ticks just to prolong suspense.

## Action-interval procedure

At each interval:

1. State the current physical change.
2. Ask each PC for one goal, not merely a skill.
3. Resolve the most exposed action first.
4. Apply earlier preparations automatically where relevant.
5. Let success create position or rescue; let failure create cost and another path.
6. Advance collapse once and show the leviathan's new behavior.

## Route cards

### Hangar to main airlock

Fastest if the party mapped the central route. Obstacles in order: shifting hangar debris, Level Two wave/flood, Level One bulkhead, collar. Remove one obstacle for Safe Return Route.

### Hangar to service conduit

Best for small characters/Werron but difficult for bulky salvage. One character may need to go ahead and clear snagged gear. If scuttlers are tolerant, this route remains open longest.

### Hangar to exterior service lock

Exposed suit route. Requires pressure protection or emergency improvisation. Leads to open water and a tether/recovery challenge, useful if main collar is crushed.

### Archive to ship

The archive group faces the impossible wave first. A successful route action reaches Level One; failure washes them toward the low-side shortcut or costs gear, not instant drowning.

### Level One to ship

Bulkhead and airlock states from Scene 4 matter. The party may deliberately flood a chamber to equalize pressure faster.

## Coordination checks and consequences

**[MODULE]** First violent shift: Average Coordination. Failure means prone and 1 strain +1 per Threat.

**[MODULE]** When the station flips: Hard Coordination to remain standing.

Use symbols to create motion:

| Result | Escape use |
|---|---|
| Advantage | Catch a handhold; keep an item; help an ally; land near the correct exit. |
| Triumph | Save another character from a check; keep Werron and the records; discover a direct breach route. |
| Threat | Strain; lost grip; loose gear; route noise; minor separation. |
| Despair | Major route collapse or suit damage; present an alternate route with serious cost. |

## The leviathan as environment

Describe one part at a time until the exterior reveal:

- scales scrape across a viewport;
- a pale eye wider than a person passes beyond the glass;
- water pressure reverses as its body displaces the ocean;
- jaws close on an outer deck, crushing rooms the party has left;
- its body strike tears supports rather than making attack rolls against PCs.

The native nemesis actor is a contingency for unusual structured scenes, not permission to turn the expected escape into a hit-point grind.

## If the party attacks

**[MODULE]** Personal blasters cannot penetrate the scales; lightsabers cut but wounds are too shallow to do more than irritate it. Underwater Gunnery difficulty increases by two, and some weapons may not function.

Still reward intent:

- starship fire can redirect its head or buy an interval;
- a precise shot at debris can create a decoy cloud;
- cutting a trapped strut may drop part of the station away from the ship;
- attacks on eyes/sensory structures may grant a Boost to escape but bring immediate retaliation.

State clearly through results that killing it here is not a realistic objective.

## Unusual plans

### Hold the sanctuary to the ridge

The party can delay separation for one interval with ship thrust, cables, Force use, or engineering. This creates time for rescue but exposes the ship or character to a later strain/position cost. They cannot permanently defeat collapsing supports under active attack.

### Abandon the docked ship

If the Delta-7 is sufficiently repaired or another craft exists, a transfer may be possible. Otherwise, pressure-suit escape can reach a repositioned ship. Never declare the campaign over because the original collar became inaccessible.

### Feed/redirect the predator

It is not interested in ordinary rations. A strong moving power/sonar/heat source can redirect attention briefly. Treat ethical/ecological consequences seriously if the plan endangers other animals.

### Stay for more salvage

Give one explicit warning and name what another interval risks. If they stay, let them gain the chosen salvage while collapse advances. Player agency includes choosing a dangerous cost.

## Reaching the ship

When the last character crosses, ask one final question: **what do they release or leave behind to close the hatch in time?** Accept answers established by play—tools, fighter, archive case, tether, cargo—not a newly invented beloved item.

If Werron is rescued, it may be the one to close the final internal seal. If left by choice, record that choice. Do not secretly teleport it aboard.

## Exterior reveal

> As the ship pulls away, the sanctuary finally gives you the scale of what has been attacking it. Hundreds of meters of armored body coils around the ridge. A mouth crowded with oversized teeth closes over part of the outer hull. The remaining anchor struts tear free, and the three-level structure rolls down the slope in a cloud of stone and metal. Then the creature turns its pale eyes toward your engines.

Reveal the leviathan handout now.

## Underwater chase

**[MODULE]** The pilot makes a Daunting Piloting (Space) check. An underwater-capable vessel reduces the difficulty by two, making the campaign ship's baseline Average.

| Result | Outcome |
|---|---|
| Success | Ship breaks the surface as the leviathan leaps/snaps behind it. |
| Advantage | Reduce strain, protect a damaged system, or gain cleaner atmospheric exit. |
| Triumph | Escape with no additional damage and recover a useful sensor record/scale trace. |
| Failure | 3 system strain +1 per Threat; repeat, reducing difficulty by one for each failed attempt to minimum Easy. |
| Threat | Seal alarm, frightened passengers, loose cargo, damaged sensor, or strain. |
| Published severe negative result | Creature bites the vessel, inflicting an automatic Critical Hit. The ship still receives another attempt. |

### Crew support during chase

| Role | Support action |
|---|---|
| Engineering | Vent heat, reinforce seal, manage strain. |
| Sensors | Call the creature's angle and the clearest current. |
| Gunnery | Redirect or create debris, with underwater difficulty increase. |
| Navigation | Find the fastest rise around ridge/thermal columns. |

| Werron | Seal a failing conduit or carry a coordinate backup. |

## Scene outcomes and records

Before the surface breach, record:

- who reached the ship by which route;
- strain, wounds, ship system strain, and any Critical Hit;
- Werron's final escape status;
- Delta-7 and archive-work status;
- gear or salvage deliberately left behind;
- whether a redundant coordinate copy remains safe;

## Ending image

> The ship tears through the surface into rain and open air. Water sheets from the viewports. For a fraction of a second the leviathan follows, jaws closing on empty wake, before its weight drags it back beneath the ocean. The surface swallows it. Behind you, no part of the sanctuary rises again.

Do not immediately cut to the next plot. Ask who speaks first and who checks on whom.

# 10 — SCENE 8: AFTERMATH AND THE MORABAND VECTOR

## Scene card

| Field | At the table |
|---|---|
| Purpose | Let survival and loss register, account for all consequences, confirm the route, and hand off to the separate next chapter. |
| Start | The ship has escaped the ocean and reached stable flight/orbit. |
| End | The crew commits to the Moraband vector, with the separate Shard Moon sequence queued next. |
| Primary pressures | Damage control, exhausted characters, rescued beings, meaning of recovered knowledge. |
| Assets | Ship interior/space view; navigation-solution and recovered-work handouts. |

## What is happening without the PCs

The ocean closes over the sanctuary's debris. Scuttlers and other small life scatter across the ridge; the leviathan eventually leaves once movement and power fade. There is no convenient second intact archive waiting below. In orbit, the old Moraband solution remains valid but more than twenty years out of date. The ship's damage continues to matter until characters stabilize it, and wet documents continue deteriorating until preserved.

## Do not skip the breath

The finale only matters if the table sees what survived. Begin with practical reality before exposition:

> The ship is airborne, but not quiet. Pumps work against water in the lower compartments. Warning lights mark strained systems. Wet gear lies wherever it was dropped. The ocean moon turns below, smooth from this height, giving no sign of the sanctuary that just disappeared into its depths.

Ask each player one question:

- Who or what does your character check first?
- What did they carry out that they only now realize they still have?
- What did they leave behind?
- What part of the escape are they refusing to discuss yet?

## Full step-by-step walkthrough

1. **Stabilize the ship.** Address Critical Hits, system strain, flooding, injuries, and frightened passengers.
2. **Account for people and objects.** Werron, archive works, gear, Delta-7, and coordinate copies.
3. **Verify the route.** The coordinates point to the Horuset system and Moraband. Old data remains old; this is a valid destination, not guaranteed safe transit.
4. **Let the gatekeeper react.** It recognizes the destination and admits Warde's choice does not fit the person it remembers.
5. **Resolve Werron's immediate future.** Repair, recharge, assigned duty, or acknowledged loss.
6. **Make knowledge matter.** Let players mention which recovered work their characters keep or read.
7. **Award 5 XP for recovering the coordinates.** **[MODULE]**
8. **Update the permanent consequence ledger.** Do not leave unresolved states in prose.
9. **Hand off to the separate next story.** **[DEVIATION/CAMPAIGN]** Use the Shard Moon module before Moraband.

## Source hierarchy

When details conflict, use this order:

1. Explicit current campaign continuity in this guide.
2. Published *Chronicles of the Gatekeeper* Delriss section for core facts and mechanics.
3. Existing campaign handoff facts that do not conflict with the current request.
4. **[ADDITION]** material in this guide.

Additions are not retroactively treated as official module canon. The provenance labels exist so a GM can remove optional material without breaking the published route.

## Published mechanical anchors retained

- Average Astrogation to Delriss; about nineteen hours on success and six additional hours on failure.
- Severe navigation mishap can produce a gravity-well Critical Hit.
- Aquatic craft can reach the sanctuary safely; ordinary ship descent is Daunting and can cause system strain/Critical Hit.
- Sanctuary is more than one kilometer underwater, over fifty meters across, three levels high, and canted approximately twenty degrees.
- Hard docking for a craft not designed for aquatic use.
- Ordinary spacesuits add two Setback to underwater physical movement.
- Powerless airlock: Average Mechanics/Skulduggery or Daunting Athletics.
- Exterior analysis establishes non-ordnance damage and possible huge-animal claw/tooth damage.
- Library search uses Hard Perception or Knowledge (Education) and yields the five named example works.
- Library does not contain the route.
- Skilled pilots/navigators may automatically infer the Delta-7 navicomputer solution.
- R-series droid repair is Daunting and replaces most memory.
- Delta-7 cockpit/nav power repair is Average Mechanics.
- Full Delta-7 repair takes a few hours and Hard Mechanics.
- First attack footing is Average Coordination; facility rollover is Hard Coordination.
- Leviathan encounter is narrative, not standard combat.
- Underwater Gunnery difficulty increases twice and some weapons may not function.
- Escape chase is Daunting Piloting (Space), reduced by two for aquatic craft; failures cause strain and make later attempts easier; a severe result can cause a Critical Hit.
- Recovering the coordinates awards 5 XP.

## Deliberate campaign changes retained

- The newer upgraded vessel is the active ship and is aquatic-capable.
- Existing animals and companions travel aboard as already established.
- The guide never creates or redefines Sigg or another PC.
- The actor/handout may call the damaged droid Werron for table convenience while preserving uncertainty about the source of that word.
- After Delriss, run the separate Shard Moon sequence before the separate Moraband sequence.

## Final GM promise

The purpose of the expanded material is not to force every table through every paragraph. It is to ensure the GM never has to invent the entire location when the players ask a reasonable question, choose an unexpected route, rescue a damaged droid, investigate the ecology, split up, linger over the archive, or attempt a plan other than the assumed one.

Run what the players engage with. Let the rest of the world continue moving.
