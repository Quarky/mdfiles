
# THE FACTORY DISTRICT
## A Living World Guide for Act 2 — Jorra, Cato Neimoidia

---

# PART ONE: THE FACTORY DISTRICT AS A PLACE

---

## WHAT THE FACTORY DISTRICT IS

The factory district of Jorra occupies the northeastern third of the bridge-city's
upper surface. It is not hidden or separated from the residential and commercial
districts by walls or formal boundaries — Neimoidian city planning did not think
in those terms. Instead the factory district announces itself through accumulation:
the smell of processed metal and chemical coolant that thickens as you move northeast,
the sound of automated machinery that underlies everything like a second heartbeat,
the gradual replacement of organic architectural curves with the more angular pragmatism
of industrial construction, the cargo haulers on fixed overhead tracks that run day
and night regardless of weather, carrying components and finished goods between
facilities on routes that have not changed in thirty years.

The factories themselves are vertical. Cato Neimoidia's bridge-cities have no horizontal
room to spare — every square meter of bridge surface is contested — so industrial
facilities grow upward and, where they can, downward, their lower levels penetrating
the bridge structure itself to make use of the thermal and mechanical advantages
of depth. The tallest factory towers rise forty or fifty meters above the bridge
surface, their upper levels shrouded in their own exhaust, their lower levels
descending into the bridge's structural layers where temperature and pressure
can be more precisely controlled.

Factory DE-13, where Tor Haido works, is one of the mid-sized facilities —
not one of the enormous foundries that dominate the northeastern skyline,
but substantial enough to have its own landing pads, its own security,
its own internal transit system connecting its seven floors. It produces
droid components and computer processing units. It has been producing
droid components and computer processing units since before the Clone Wars,
through the Clone Wars, through the change of government, and now for the Empire.
The components go into different droids now. The production line is largely the same.

---

## THE HISTORY THE FACTORIES CARRY

### Before the Wars

The factory district was built during the height of Trade Federation power,
when Cato Neimoidia was the economic heart of a merchant empire that spanned
hundreds of systems. The factories here did not produce weapons — the Trade
Federation outsourced weapons production to its droid contractors — but rather
the infrastructure of commerce: navigation computers, communication equipment,
cargo processing systems, the ten thousand small components that kept a
galaxy-spanning trade network functional.

The workers were Neimoidian, organized into production houses that combined
the functions of a labor guild and an extended family. A production house
owned its equipment collectively, negotiated contracts as a unit, and distributed
profits according to a complex seniority system that Neimoidian economists had
spent centuries refining. The production houses were not democratic exactly —
they were hierarchical in the specific Neimoidian way, where the hierarchy was
acknowledged but not considered shameful — but they had genuine collective power
and used it.

The factory district before the war was loud and busy and smelled of industry
and was considered by residents of the residential and commercial districts
to be the least pleasant part of the city. It was also considered the most
important part of the city. The ventral estates and the commercial towers existed
because the factories produced the wealth that paid for them.

### During the Clone Wars

When the Republic assault came to Jorra, the factory district was a primary target.
Not for destruction — the Republic wanted the factories, not their absence —
but for control. The production lines that had been making navigation equipment
and cargo processors were converted almost overnight to produce B1 battle droids,
vulture droid components, and weapons systems for the Separatist forces.

This conversion was rapid and total and left marks that are still visible
fifteen years later. Walk the factory district now and you can see where
the original production equipment was torn out — the anchor points in the
floors, the disconnected power conduits, the structural reinforcements added
to support heavier machinery than the original building design anticipated.
The original equipment went somewhere. No one in Jorra will tell you where.

The fighting in the factory district was close-quarters and industrial.
Republic clone troopers and Separatist battle droids fought through
production floors and loading bays and maintenance corridors. The factories
that were most heavily contested still show it — blaster scoring on interior
walls, replaced sections of flooring where the original was destroyed,
the occasional structural repair that does not quite match the surrounding
material. Factory DE-13 was one of the contested facilities. The southwest
corner of its third floor still has blaster scoring that no one has bothered
to resurface.

Warde fought here. Not in DE-13 specifically but throughout this district.
The party is walking ground he walked. In the factory district more than anywhere
else in the upper city, Warde's presence is not abstract — it is physical,
burned into walls.

### After the Wars — Imperial Production

When the Empire came to power and consolidated control of Cato Neimoidia,
the factory district was the first thing it cared about. The factories
were producing things and the Empire wanted those things. The transition
from Separatist production to Imperial production was managed by a series
of Imperial procurement officers who arrived, assessed, requisitioned,
and departed within six months of the war's end.

The production houses survived this transition, mostly. The Empire needed
workers and the production houses had workers. What the Empire did not preserve
was the collective ownership model. The facilities were formally transferred
to Imperial economic jurisdiction under the Factory Nationalization Decree
of Year 2 post-war. The production houses became labor units under Imperial
management. The workers kept their jobs and lost their ownership.

The older workers remember the difference. The younger ones — those who
came of age under Imperial management — do not have a comparison point.
Duke Irbian does not care about the workers' opinions on economic structure
as long as production quotas are met. The quotas are always met. There is
very little choice about meeting the quotas.

### What the District Produces Now

The current production mix in Jorra's factory district:

- **DE-13 (Haido's factory):** Droid components and computer processors.
  The droids the components go into are Imperial security and administrative
  droids. The computers go into Imperial naval vessels.
- **The Northern Foundries:** Hull plating and structural materials.
  Marked with Imperial Navy procurement codes.
- **The Precision Works:** Navigation and targeting systems.
  These facilities have Imperial security personnel on site.
  The party should avoid them.
- **The old chemical processing plant:** Closed for three years.
  The Whitescar Syndicate uses its lower levels for storage.
  There is no visible sign of this from the exterior.

## THE DISTRICT AT STREET LEVEL

### What It Looks Like

The factory district's streets are wide by Jorra standards — they need to be,
to accommodate cargo hauler traffic and the movement of equipment between
facilities. The overhead tracks carrying automated cargo pods are a constant
presence, the pods moving at irregular intervals, their passage creating
brief shadows and a mechanical hum that varies in pitch with the load.

The buildings press close on either side. The lowest levels of each factory
are blank-walled — no windows, the exterior surface unbroken except for
loading bay doors and ventilation grilles. Above the first few floors,
windows appear, but they are industrial windows: small, reinforced, often
covered in the accumulated grime of exhaust that the automated cleaning
systems cannot quite keep up with.

The air quality in the factory district is the worst in Jorra.
Not dangerous — the factories have filtration requirements that the
Empire enforces because it wants its workers functional — but there is
a persistent undertaste of processed materials and chemical compounds
that accumulates over time. People who work in the district long enough
stop noticing. Visitors from other parts of the city notice every time.

The light is different in the factory district. The tower heights and
their proximity to each other mean that natural light reaches the
street level only when the sun is directly overhead. For most of the
day the streets are in the shadow of the buildings above, lit by the
constant artificial lighting that is built into the lower levels of
every facility. This lighting is functional rather than aesthetic —
white-blue industrial luminescence that makes everything look slightly
clinical and slightly unfriendly simultaneously.

### The Workers

The factory district has approximately fifteen thousand workers employed
across its various facilities, working in overlapping shifts that keep
the production lines running around the clock. Shift change — which happens
at four-hour intervals across different facilities on slightly different schedules —
creates surges of foot traffic that make the district's streets briefly crowded
and then quiet again.

The workers are almost entirely Neimoidian. A few specialist positions
are held by members of other species — certain precision calibration roles
seem to attract Sullustan technicians in particular, and there is a small
but established community of Duros engineers in the navigation systems
facilities. But the bulk of the workforce is Neimoidian, which means
the factory district is one of the parts of Jorra most accessible
to a party trying to ask questions — most of the workers speak Basic
well enough for basic communication, even if they prefer not to.

The workers' relationship to the work is complex.
The older workers — those who remember the production houses —
have a relationship to the factory floor that is something between
professional pride and grief. They are good at what they do.
They are good at what they do in a place that used to belong to them
and now does not. They do not discuss this with strangers.
They barely discuss it with each other.

The younger workers are different. They did not experience the
production house system and they have not lost something they
once had. What they have is a job under Imperial management,
which is stable in the specific way that Imperial employment is stable:
the pay is consistent, the expectations are clear, the consequences
of failing to meet those expectations are also clear. They are not
unhappy exactly. They are not not unhappy either.

Both groups are suspicious of outsiders asking questions.
The older workers because they have learned to be.
The younger workers because they have been taught to be.

## MOVING THROUGH THE FACTORY DISTRICT

### Navigation

The factory district is organized around three main transit corridors
running northeast to southwest parallel to the bridge's main axis,
connected by cross-streets at irregular intervals dictated by
where buildings were built rather than any planning principle.

The corridors are:

**The North Corridor (Hauler Route):**
The widest street in the district, running directly below the main
overhead cargo track network. The haulers pass frequently here and
the noise level is significant. The North Corridor is the fastest
route through the district but the least discreet — there is
nowhere to stand without being visible to anyone on the street
or in the lower-level loading bays that face onto it.

**The Central Corridor (Worker Route):**
The most traveled street at shift change, running through the
center of the district past the largest concentration of worker
facilities — the canteens, the equipment storage buildings,
the administrative offices where Imperial management operates.
Factory DE-13 is accessible from the Central Corridor.

**The South Corridor (Service Route):**
The narrowest of the three, used primarily by maintenance vehicles
and service crews. Less trafficked between shift changes, more
trafficked during them. The South Corridor runs closer to the
bridge edge, where several older facilities back directly onto
the drop to the ventral city.

### Surveillance

The factory district has a higher density of Imperial surveillance
than any other part of the upper city. Duke Irbian is not interested
in the residents of the commercial and residential districts —
they are Neimoidians being Neimoidians, which is not his concern
as long as they pay their taxes and produce no Rebel activity.

The factories are different. The factories produce Imperial military
equipment and the Empire protects what it values. The surveillance
includes:

- Fixed holocam emplacements at every major intersection
- Mobile patrol units (two stormtroopers) at irregular intervals
  on the main corridors
- Imperial security checkpoints at the entrance to the Precision Works
  and the Northern Foundries (not at DE-13 — it is mid-tier enough
  to have standard Neimoidian security rather than Imperial military)
- A network of informants among the worker population that Duke Irbian
  has spent three years cultivating and that he considers his most
  valuable intelligence asset

The party should assume they are being observed at a low level
from the moment they enter the factory district.
Not targeted — just observed. The system is broad rather than precise.
What makes someone move from observed to targeted is asking the wrong
questions, meeting the wrong people, or being identified by Irbian's
informant network as persons of interest.

By the time the party reaches the factory district, they have probably
already triggered Hasar's interest. Hasar's interest and Irbian's
surveillance network are separate systems that occasionally overlap.
The factory district is one place where they overlap most — the
Syndicate has deep penetration of the worker population and Irbian
knows this and considers it manageable as long as it doesn't
threaten his position.

### The Cargo Hauler Track — Environmental Detail

The overhead cargo track is a constant presence in the factory district
and worth establishing clearly as an environmental element because it
affects multiple scenes.

The track runs at approximately twelve meters above street level —
high enough to clear the largest vehicle traffic, low enough that
the passage of a loaded cargo pod creates a genuine wind effect
at street level and a mechanical hum that varies in frequency.

The pods themselves vary in size from small courier containers
to large industrial carriers. The small ones pass every few minutes.
The large ones, which carry completed components between facilities
or to the loading bays at the district's edge, pass approximately
every fifteen to twenty minutes and create enough noise during
passage that conversation at street level is briefly impossible.

**Tactical note:**
The cargo track passes directly over the south loading entrance
of Factory DE-13. During the gang encounter, the timing of large
pod passages can be used tactically — a pod passing during
combat creates a fifteen-second window of significant noise
reduction in visibility and a brief wind effect that can affect
ranged attacks. The GM should track cargo pod timing and
incorporate it into the encounter.

# PART TWO: THE APPROACH TO DE-13

---

## LEAVING THE COMMERCIAL DISTRICT

The party moves from the commercial district to the factory district
through a transition zone — three blocks where the two districts
interpenetrate, the organic architecture of the commercial area
giving way incrementally to the more angular factory construction.

This transition zone is worth a few descriptive beats:

> *The smell changes before anything else. Something chemical —
> not unpleasant exactly, just present. The kind of smell that
> becomes background noise for the people who live with it
> and stays foreground for everyone else.*

> *The overhead cargo tracks appear first as a single line,
> then two, then five, running parallel northeast. The pods
> are moving. They have been moving all night and will be
> moving all day and will be moving all night again.*

> *The buildings get taller and less curved. The Trade Federation
> built its commerce district to impress. It built its factories
> to function. The difference is architectural and total.*

### The Shift Change — If the Party Times It Right

If the party moves through the district during a shift change —
which happens every four hours, the GM can decide whether this
is the case based on session timing — the streets are suddenly
crowded with Neimoidian workers moving in both directions,
the incoming shift and the outgoing shift briefly occupying
the same streets.

This has several effects:

**Cover:** The crowd provides natural concealment. The party
becomes much harder to track in a surge of several hundred
Neimoidians. Syndicate watchers cannot easily maintain visual
contact. A Hard Stealth check during shift change is Average.

**Observation:** The party can observe the worker population
at close range. A Perception check (Average) during shift change
notices the specific texture of this crowd — the fatigue of
the outgoing shift, the resigned readiness of the incoming shift,
the near-total absence of conversation between workers who
clearly know each other but have learned not to talk in public.

**The Drone-Beetle Bond Opportunity:**
This is the natural moment for the Pathfinder to bond the
Scavenger Drone-Beetle, as established in the living world
scenes. During shift change, when everyone is moving and
no one is paying attention to what anyone else is doing,
she can spend two minutes crouching next to a beetle on a
wall without attracting significant attention. Workers pass.
A few glance at her. None stop.

**The Syndicate Watcher:**
Sigg clocks a Syndicate watcher in the crowd at shift change
if the GM decides the Syndicate is actively following the party
at this point. The watcher is using the crowd the same way
the party is. Sigg does not react. He moves the party through
a different exit from the main flow and explains afterward.

## UNWANTED ATTENTION — THE KREEHAWK TAIL

Before the party reaches DE-13, they pick up a tail.

This is the module's Unwanted Attention beat, but given the full
living world treatment it needs texture and buildup rather than
appearing suddenly.

### The Signs — Distributed Across the Approach

The GM should drop these signals across the approach to the factory,
spacing them so that observant players can piece it together
before it becomes explicit:

**Signal 1 — Crossing the North Corridor:**
A swoop bike is parked on a side street. Not unusual —
swoops are common in the factory district, used by
delivery riders and younger workers. This one has garish
paint. A Perception check (Easy) notices the paint scheme
is Kreehawk colors. It is unattended. Or appears to be.

**Signal 2 — The Central Corridor:**
At a junction approximately two blocks from DE-13, a young
Neimoidian on a swoop is stopped, ostensibly checking a
datapad mounted to the handlebars. A Vigilance check (Average)
notes that the datapad screen is dark. The swoop's engine
is warm — it has been running recently. The rider is
watching the street behind the party without looking
like they are watching the street behind the party.

**Signal 3 — Outside DE-13:**
There should be a moment where the party has a choice to
enter through the front or the back. Before they make that
choice, if they pause at all, a Hard Perception check or
Average Vigilance reveals: two swoops at either end of the
block, positioned at angles that cover both the front
and the back entrances. The riders are talking to each other.
They are not looking at the party. They are very carefully
not looking at the party.

**If the Pathfinder is bonded with the Drone-Beetle:**
The beetle, if deployed, can access the building's exterior
surface before the party enters. In doing so it passes
near one of the waiting swoops and the Pathfinder feels
what the beetle feels: the heat of a warm engine, the
chemical smell of swoop fuel that is recent rather than
residual, the vibration of an idling motor being held quiet.

The Syndicate is already here. They arrived before the party.
Which means either they anticipated this destination or
they have been tracking the party since they entered
the factory district. Both possibilities are concerning
and both are true to different degrees.

### Sigg's Read

Sigg does not make a speech about this. He has been watching.
At the moment the party pauses to decide their entrance:

> *"Don't stop. Keep moving like we know where we're going.*
>
> *Two swoops, both ends of the block. The one on the left
> has been here longer — the exhaust residue is older.*
> *The one on the right arrived maybe ten minutes ago.*
> *That one is the one that followed us from the corridor.*
>
> *They are not going to do anything while we're on the street.*
> *Hasar wants to know what we're doing before she decides
> what to do about it. We have time.*
>
> *Front entrance. We are TaggeCo. contractors here about
> a component calibration issue. We have an appointment
> with the supervisor."*

He says all of this in a normal conversational tone
as they continue moving. He has done this before.

## FACTORY DE-13 — THE BUILDING

### Exterior

Factory DE-13 is a seven-story structure occupying approximately
a quarter of a city block. Its exterior is functional — vertical
durasteel cladding in the gray-green that was standard Trade
Federation industrial construction, now weathered to a more
uniform gray and patched in several places where the original
cladding was damaged and replaced with whatever was available.

The building's lower two floors have no windows. The upper floors
have small reinforced windows, most covered with internal blinds
or work screens. The building's primary identifying features from
the exterior are:

- The TaggeCo. corporate logo on the upper facade —
  the Trade Federation-era branding has been replaced
  with the current Imperial contractor's mark, but the
  mounting points of the old sign are still visible
  as slight irregularities in the cladding surface
- Three loading bay doors at street level on the north face,
  large enough to admit cargo vehicles
- Two personnel entrances — a formal front entrance on
  the Central Corridor face with a reception desk visible
  through the transparent door panels, and a service
  entrance on the south face leading to the loading area
- A landing pad on the roof, currently unoccupied
- Exhaust venting at multiple levels producing
  a continuous low-volume discharge that accounts for
  a significant portion of the district's ambient smell

### The Two Entrances — Tactical Assessment

**Front Entrance (Central Corridor face):**
A formal reception area staffed by an administrative droid —
a protocol-adjacent model, not a combat unit — that manages
visitor logging and appointment verification. The reception
area is visible from the street. Entering through the front
requires signing in (the droid will request identification
and the nature of the visit) and waiting to be escorted
to the relevant personnel.

Advantages: Legitimate, low-aggression entry. The droid
will page Haido if the party uses his name.
Disadvantages: Identity documentation required. The visit
is logged. Irbian's network will potentially receive
a flag about offworld visitors at DE-13.

**Service Entrance (South face):**
A loading area accessible from the South Corridor via an
alley. The entrance leads directly into the loading bay —
a large, noisy, industrially active space where workers
moving between tasks are unlikely to question people
who appear to know where they are going.

Advantages: No sign-in. Informal. Workers in the loading
area are too busy to interrogate visitors who look purposeful.
Disadvantages: Requires navigation through the building
to reach Haido's office on the upper floor.
Cameras in the loading area. Getting lost here
while the Kreehawks are outside is suboptimal.

**The Pathfinder's Option:**
If the Drone-Beetle has been deployed, it can enter through
a ventilation grille at ground level and navigate to
the upper floors before the party enters, providing
a rough layout of interior obstacles and personnel positions.
The beetle cannot transmit information with precision —
the bond gives the Pathfinder a sense of spaces and movement
rather than a floor plan — but it is more than they would otherwise have.

### Interior Layout — Floor by Floor

**Ground Floor — Loading and Receiving:**
The full footprint of the building, divided between three
loading bays (north face) and the receiving area where
incoming components are logged and sorted. The ceiling is
six meters — high enough for cargo lifters. Constant movement.
Automated sorting equipment runs continuously.
Five to eight workers present at any time.

The interior cargo lift (for goods) and the personnel
staircase both access from the loading floor.
The cargo lift is too slow and too visible for the party's
purposes. The staircase is against the east wall.

**Second Floor — Component Processing:**
The first production floor. Raw materials and subcomponents
are processed here into standardized parts. Heavy automated
equipment — pressing machines, etching systems, quality
control scanners. Very loud. The workers here communicate
via hand signals for most of the shift.

The noise on this floor provides cover for movement but
makes verbal communication between party members impossible
without shouting.

**Third Floor — Assembly:**
Droid components are assembled here. Partially automated
but with significant manual assembly stations where workers
hand-fit components that require precision the automated
systems cannot provide. Quieter than the second floor.
Workers are focused on their individual stations.

The southwest corner of this floor has the blaster scoring
from the Clone Wars battle — dark marks on the wall that
have been around it rather than removed. A Perception check
(Easy) identifies them as blaster fire. A Knowledge (Warfare)
check (Average) or the gatekeeper's memories suggest the
pattern is consistent with the kind of close-quarters combat
that characterized the Jorra fighting.

**Fourth Floor — Quality Control and Testing:**
Finished components are tested here before shipping.
The testing equipment generates irregular loud bursts
of sound when components are cycled. Otherwise relatively quiet.
Administrative offices along the south wall.

**Fifth Floor — Computer Processing Production:**
The higher-value production line — navigation computers
and tactical processors for Imperial vessels. This floor
has a secondary security system (not Imperial military grade
but above the standard building security) that monitors
for unauthorized access to the equipment. Getting through
this floor without triggering the secondary system requires
either a Stealth check (Hard, due to the sensor coverage)
or simply not going near the equipment.

**Sixth Floor — Administration and Management:**
Standard office configuration. TaggeCo. management,
production planning, logistics coordination. This is
where Haido's immediate supervisors operate. They are
not relevant to the party's purpose and are best avoided.

**Seventh Floor — Supervisor Level:**
Haido's domain. A floor of smaller offices for floor
supervisors, overlooking the production floors below
through large observation windows. The observation windows
are a key feature of this floor — from them, Haido can
see the production floors of floors two through five,
and from the party's position in his office, they can
see most of the building's functional area.

This also means that when the gang attack comes,
the party can see the swoops entering from the loading
bay on the ground floor almost as soon as they arrive.

### Haido's Office — The Room

Haido's office is reached via a gantry walkway from the
main seventh-floor corridor. The walkway spans a gap
where an internal wall was removed at some point to
create the observation window configuration below.
Walking the gantry, the party can look down seven floors
to the loading bay floor far below.

The office itself:

> *The room is small and sparsely furnished in the way
> that suggests the person who occupies it is not trying
> to make any statements about themselves through their
> environment. A desk. A terminal. A chair on each side
> of the desk. On the desk: a single datapad, positioned
> at a precise angle that suggests habit. A mug that has
> been here long enough to have left a ring on the surface.*
>
> *The floor-to-ceiling observation window fills the
> south wall entirely. Below it, the production floors
> descend — a cross-section of the building laid out
> in levels, the machinery on each floor visible from above.
> The lighting varies by floor, the cooler blue of the
> upper floors giving way to the harsher yellow of the
> assembly level below. It looks like something geological.
> It looks like the building has been cut in half and
> you are looking at the strata.*
>
> *A middle-aged Neimoidian turns from the window
> as you enter. His face falls visibly.*
>
> *"Yes," he says, recovering. "May I help you?"*

# PART THREE: TOR HAIDO — THE MAN AND THE MEETING

---

## WHO HAIDO IS

Tor Haido is not a brave man. He knows this about himself
and has made his peace with it in the specific way that
people make peace with things by deciding those things
are reasonable rather than shameful.

He worked for Warde because it seemed advantageous.
He worked carefully and gave information that was accurate
and useful. He was good at it in the colorless way that
people are good at things they do without passion.
When Warde's situation deteriorated and the information
Haido provided became information Warde used for arrests
rather than intelligence — when the thing he was doing
began to look different from what he had thought he was doing —
he found reasons not to examine that too closely.

After Warde left, Haido found himself in a specific kind
of danger: he had been an informant for a Jedi who was
now officially a criminal and a murderer. His relationship
to Warde made him suspicious to everyone simultaneously —
to the Empire (Jedi sympathizer), to the Neimoidian
community (Republic collaborator), to the Syndicate
(unknown quantity). He survived this by becoming
as invisible as possible and has been invisible ever since.

Fifteen years of deliberate invisibility have changed him.
He is careful about everything — the angle of the datapad
on his desk, the phrasing of every sentence, the precise
calibration of how much information to give and to whom.
He trusts no one and has structured his life so that trust
is not required. He has a job that needs him to be competent
rather than likable. He is competent.

He does not want to talk about Warde. He especially does
not want to talk about Warde to offworlders who have been
asking about Warde in a city where asking about Warde
attracts exactly the kind of attention that Haido has
spent fifteen years avoiding.

He is going to talk about Warde anyway. He can feel
it happening before they even ask.

---

## THE MEETING — DETAILED SCENE

### Before the Party Speaks

Haido knows why they are here before they say it.
He has been tracking rumors and whispers for fifteen years —
it is a survival skill he developed — and he has known
there were offworlders asking about Warde since the day
after they arrived. He has been hoping they would not
find their way to him. He can see from their presence
in his office that that hope was unreasonable.

He does not offer them a seat immediately. He stands
at the window with his back to them for a moment —
not dramatic, just a man gathering himself — and then
turns and sits behind his desk with the precision of
someone who does everything with precision as a way
of controlling what they can control.

> *"Please sit. You have come a long way to find me.*
> *I would offer refreshment but I suspect we both
> know this is not that kind of meeting."*

### What Haido Does Not Say First

The key to playing Haido is that he does not lie.
He evades. He redirects. He answers adjacent questions.
He tells partial truths that are technically accurate.
He does not lie because he is not practiced at lying
and because being caught in a lie in his current
situation would be more dangerous than the truth.

If the party asks directly about Warde:
> *"I am afraid I do not know what you mean.*
> *The name is familiar — the Jedi from the occupation.
> Most people here have heard of him. I did not know
> him personally."*

This is false. The party can detect this with an
opposed Discipline check against his Deception (Average difficulty).

If they push:

> *"I worked in a factory during the occupation.*
> *Many people passed through many factories.*
> *I cannot be expected to remember every individual."*

Still false. Still detectable.

If they produce Tieka Dalken's name as their source:
He stops. A long pause. Then:
> *"Tieka Dalken has a generous view of the past.*
> *She remembers things with more... charity than they deserve."*

This is a tell. He is not denying knowing her.
He is commenting on her characterization of events.
Which means he knows there are characterizations to comment on.

### Breaking Through

There are several approaches that work to varying degrees:

**Charm (Hard — add two Setback from resentment):**
Haido resents Warde. Charming him by appealing to
their shared interest in Warde's legacy reads as manipulative.
Charm works only if the party establishes genuine common ground
that is not about Warde — their own experience with Force users,
perhaps, or the specific texture of living carefully in an
Imperial-controlled city.

**Negotiation (Average):**
What Haido wants is not complicated: he wants to
not be in danger because of this conversation.
If the party can credibly offer that — not protection exactly,
but the absence of additional risk — he is willing to consider talking.

**Deception (opposed against his Discipline — Average):**
Lying to Haido about their purpose or identity. He will accept
a convincing cover story partly because he wants to accept it.
A successful deception lets the party extract information
without Haido feeling complicit. The downside: if the deception
is later revealed (and Hasar or Irbian will eventually reveal it),
Haido becomes unavailable as a future resource.

**Coercion (Easy but earns significant Conflict and closes future doors):**
Threatening Haido works. He is not brave. He will tell them what
they need to know if he believes the alternative is worse.
What coercion does not get: the additional information he would
volunteer to people he trusts. He will give the party the
minimum necessary to make them leave.

**The Honest Approach (Daunting, becomes Average with the right framing):**
Telling Haido the truth — that they are following Warde's trail,
that they have his holocron, that they are trying to understand
what happened. This is the hardest sell because it asks Haido
to trust strangers with information that could destroy his
carefully constructed invisibility. But it is also the only
approach that gets everything he knows rather than
everything he's willing to say.

The difficulty reduces if the party demonstrates they already
know significant facts about what happened — it signals
to Haido that his information is less of a secret than he thought
and therefore less dangerous to confirm.

**For the Pathfinder specifically:**
Haido is extremely attuned to deception and self-preservation.
He is less attuned to the specific quality of attention
that a Force-sensitive gives to a room. The Pathfinder,
if she reads him through the Force, finds something unexpected:
underneath the careful self-management, he is exhausted.
Not tired — exhausted. He has been vigilant for fifteen years
and he is tired of being vigilant and he does not know
how to stop. This is the emotional truth of Haido.
Approaching him from this direction — not pushing,
just acknowledging that she can feel that he is tired —
may be more effective than any skill check.

### What Haido Tells Them — Detailed

Once he decides to talk, Haido talks with the precision
of someone who has organized information in his head
for fifteen years and has never had anyone to tell it to.

**On meeting Warde:**
> *"He found me. I did not go to him. I think he foresaw
> that I would be useful before I had any idea who he was.
> That is the strange thing about the power he had —
> you would be recruited before you understood you were
> being recruited. He simply appeared one day and treated me
> as though we had an existing arrangement.*
>
> *I did not correct this impression. He was a Jedi.*
> *Correcting Jedi impressions seemed inadvisable."*

**On his work as an informant:**
> *"I observed. I reported what I observed. The Republic
> needed to know which workers in the factories were
> providing information to the Separatists, which production
> supervisors were sabotaging output, which residents
> were more loyal to the Confederacy than to the occupation.*
>
> *I was good at observing these things. I had been
> doing it my entire career, though for different purposes.*
>
> *The information I gave him was accurate.*
> *What he did with it became — complicated."*

**On Warde's deterioration:**
> *"He changed. I want to be precise about this because
> imprecision is how people become villains in stories
> when the truth is more interesting.*
>
> *He did not become cruel. He became certain.*
> *The difference matters. Cruel people know they might
> be wrong and do not care. Warde became incapable of
> conceiving that his information was incomplete.*
>
> *When he began acting on what he foresaw rather than
> on what he could prove, I began providing him with
> less information. Selectively. I told myself this
> was self-preservation. It was also — I think it was
> also because I did not want to be part of what
> he was becoming.*
>
> *I do not claim this was brave. It was the smallest
> possible form of refusal."*

**On the arrests:**
> *"Yes. People were arrested based on what I reported.
> Some of them were what I said they were.
> I do not know about all of them.*
> *I stopped checking, after a while.*
> *That is the honest answer."*

He pauses.
> *"I have had fifteen years to think about what that means.
> I do not have a satisfying conclusion."*

**On the cease-fire talks:**
> *"I knew about them. The location — the Sankar Palace
> in the ventral city — was given to me as part of my
> information network. Warde did not tell me his plans.*
>
> *I found out what happened the same way everyone else did.
> The noise. The fighting. He was gone before I could
> have reached him even if I had tried.*
>
> *I did not try."*

He is quiet for a moment.

> *"I want to tell you that I would have tried to stop him
> if I had known. I am not certain that is true.*
> *I have constructed a version of myself over fifteen years
> that would have tried. I do not know if that version
> existed at the time."*

**On N'got:**
> *"Zel N'got. Yes. He was Separatist intelligence.*
> *I discovered his assignment — he was specifically tasked
> to monitor Warde, not the general Republic presence.*
> *He wanted to understand Warde's ability. The Separatists
> feared it more than they feared his lightsaber.*
>
> *I did not have the opportunity to give this to Warde
> before everything ended. N'got survived. He is in the
> residential district, upper levels. He has been hiding
> since the war ended.*
>
> *He knows things I do not know. He was present at the
> palace. I was not."*

**On Tacker:**
> *"TH-313. He served directly under Warde. Clone trooper.*
> *He is still here — they reassigned him to Irbian's
> command some years ago. He is a stormtrooper captain now.*
>
> *He was loyal to Warde in a way that made the other
> troopers uncomfortable. He defended Warde's methods
> even when defending them was professionally risky.*
>
> *He kept a list. After everything — after Warde left —
> he kept a list of names. People Warde arrested.
> Whether as memorial or record I cannot tell you.*
>
> *I know about the list because he asked me once,
> years later, if I could confirm the name of a
> woman whose son had been arrested. I could not.
> But the fact that he asked tells you something
> about him."*

## HAIDO'S ANIMALS

Factory DE-13 has its own resident animal population
that the Pathfinder will be aware of throughout the meeting.

**Bridge Geckos:**
The standard ventral surface colonizers have extended
their territory into the upper floors of the factory
where the temperature differential between interior
and exterior creates ideal conditions. Three or four
are visible in Haido's office at any time — on the
window frame, on the upper wall, on the ceiling near
the ventilation output.

Haido does not notice them. He has worked in this office
for eight years and the geckos have been there the
entire time and he has never once looked at them deliberately.

The Pathfinder will notice them immediately. There is something
telling about this: the geckos are not avoiding Haido.
They are simply not registering him as relevant.
He is furniture to them. Whatever they sense about him —
his emotional state, his tension — does not read as threat
or opportunity. He has made himself that invisible.

**A Pale Spider:**
One pale spider, young and small, has established a
web in the upper corner of the observation window.
It is technically not supposed to be here — the factory
has a pest management agreement — but no one has noticed it.

The web is perfectly positioned to catch the small insects
that are attracted to the heat differential at the window.
The spider is thriving. It is entirely content.

The Pathfinder can feel its contentment from the doorway.
It is a small, steady warmth in the room that has nothing
to do with the complexity of the human conversation happening below it.

# PART FOUR: THE GANG ATTACK — FULL TACTICAL TREATMENT

---

## SETUP

The Kreehawk gangers have been watching from outside.
They have been patient because Lor Keln is not patient
but Hasar's instruction was to observe first. The moment
the party settles into conversation with Haido, Keln
decides that observation has provided enough and that
the fastest way to get what Hasar wants is to take it.

She is wrong about this but she is not going to be
told she is wrong by anyone available to tell her.

The attack begins approximately at the point when Haido
has given the party the most significant information —
the N'got lead, the Tacker connection. The timing is
not coincidental. The gangers have been listening to
what they could access from outside and waited for a
natural pause that suggested the information transfer
was either complete or nearly complete.

### The Warning

The Pathfinder gets the warning first. The Drone-Beetle,
if deployed inside the building, feels the vibration of
swoop engines entering the loading bay — different from
the regular hum of the factory, lower and more erratic,
multiple sources. The geckos in Haido's office freeze
simultaneously. Three seconds later the sound of the
swoops reaches the seventh floor.

Through the observation window, the party can see it:
two swoops entering the loading bay on the ground floor,
tilting to fit through the bay doors, their riders already
drawing weapons. Workers scatter. The sorting equipment
continues running because it does not know to stop.

Haido looks down through the window and his expression
goes through several things rapidly before settling on
a controlled fear he has clearly had practice with.

> *"These people. I thought you might bring them.*
> *I hoped you might not."*

He stands.
> *"There is a door at the back of this floor that
> connects to the maintenance staircase. It is not
> on the building schematic that visitors receive.
> I suggest you use it.*
>
> *Take what I told you. Make it worth something."*

He is not going with them. This is his workplace.
He is going to his desk and he is going to sit at it
and he is going to wait, because he has survived
fifteen years by being the person who stays in his chair.

## THE ENCOUNTER — ENVIRONMENT AND TACTICS

### The Factory as a Combat Environment

Factory DE-13's interior is not a neutral space.
It is a working industrial facility full of machinery,
equipment, production lines, structural elements,
and hazards that exist independently of any combat.
The encounter should feel like fighting in a factory,
not fighting in a room that happens to have factory props.

**Key environmental elements:**

**The Observation Windows (Seventh Floor):**
Haido's floor has floor-to-ceiling observation windows
looking down into the production levels below.
The windows are reinforced but not indestructible.
A result of Threat or Despair on a ranged attack near
a window can break it, creating a gap to the six-meter
drop to the floor below — and potentially to the
production equipment on the floors below that.

**The Gantry Walkways:**
The seventh floor is connected by gantry walkways —
elevated catwalks with partial railing — that span
the internal void of the building. Fighting on a gantry:
the standard environmental rules for elevated surfaces apply,
plus: the gantries are designed for foot traffic, not combat.
A result of Threat or Despair on an action taken while
on a gantry can dislodge a section of railing,
creating a fall hazard for anyone engaged with that character.

**The Production Floors (Floors 2-5):**
The swoops can operate on floors two and three — the ceiling
height is adequate on the loading floor and the lower
production floor. Above that the ceiling drops to four meters
and the machinery clusters make swoop navigation increasingly
difficult. The gangers know this and will use the lower floors
as their primary operating space, trying to draw the party down.

**The Cargo Lifter (Ground Floor):**
The automated cargo lifter runs on a fixed vertical track
in the northeast corner of the building. It operates on
a programmed schedule. A successful Computers check (Average)
at the loading floor control panel can be used to call the
lifter, hold it at a specific floor, or send it somewhere
useful (or somewhere a swoop bike is not expecting a platform
to arrive).

**The Sorting Equipment (Ground Floor):**
The automated sorting equipment on the loading floor
continues operating during the encounter. It does not
care about the fight. Conveyor belts, mechanical arms,
and sorting gates create moving cover and moving obstacles
simultaneously. A character can use the sorting equipment
as cover (upgrade a defense die while adjacent to active
sorting machinery) but must succeed on an Easy Coordination
check each turn or the machinery interferes with their movement.

**The Second Floor — The Noise Problem:**
If the fight moves to the second floor (the heaviest
production floor), verbal communication between characters
becomes impossible due to machinery noise. Characters on
this floor must use visual signals or the Force to coordinate.
This is significant and should be established before
anyone goes down there.

**The Industrial Windows (Exterior):**
The upper floors have small exterior windows.
Too small to fit through, but breakable.
A broken window on the upper floor — accessible to
the swoops from outside if they exit and re-enter
through the exterior — creates a potential new attack vector
that the party might not expect.

### The Attackers

**Lor Keln** on her Mobquet Flare-V — the gang's leader,
cruel, one-eyed, tactically aggressive. She uses the
Push the Limit ability freely, adding Setback dice
to her Piloting checks in exchange for mobility advantages.
She targets the party's most capable combatant first,
attempting to separate them from the group using the
swoops' speed advantage.

**One minion group of two swoop-mounted Kreehawk gangers per PC.**
They start at the loading bay on the ground floor and
ascend via the building's open internal structure
as the encounter develops.

### Tactical Development — How the Fight Moves

**Round 1 — The Entry:**
The swoops are in the loading bay, long range from the party
on the seventh floor. Workers have scattered. Keln announces
something about the party teaching a lesson. The gangers
are spreading out to control the exits.

The party's options at this point:
- Descend via the public staircase (brings them toward the swoops)
- Take Haido's maintenance staircase (exits at a different point,
  potentially behind the ganger positions)
- Stay on the seventh floor and use the observation window
  to engage from above (long range, but no swoop access up here yet)
- The Pathfinder option: the Drone-Beetle is already inside
  the building. She can feel the exact positions of all the riders
  through the beetle's contact with the floor surfaces.

**Round 2 — The Escalation:**
The swoops begin ascending through the building's internal
structure. They cannot reach the seventh floor — ceiling height
prevents it above the fourth floor — but they can get to
the fourth floor, forcing the party to either come down
or be pursued into a space where the swoops cannot follow.

At this point the first cargo pod passes on the overhead
track outside. The window vibrates. The noise from the
track briefly overwhelms the sound of the fight.
In that window, a stealthy character on the exterior
side of the building has a brief period of significantly
reduced detectability.

**Round 3 onward — The Factory Fight:**
The encounter settles into the specific texture of
fighting in a working factory:

*Cover that moves:* The sorting equipment and conveyor
belts provide cover but the cover itself moves on a
programmed schedule. A character behind a sorting gate
is protected until the gate opens. They need to know
when the gate opens.

*Hazards that don't care:* The machinery is indifferent
to the fight. It will continue processing components
regardless of what happens in its immediate vicinity.
A swoop bike that gets too close to active machinery
risks damage (Hard Piloting check to avoid contact;
failure inflicts 3 system strain and potentially dislodges
the rider).

*Elevation as a weapon:* The building's vertical structure
means characters can potentially drop things on swoops below,
force swoops into ceiling-height-limited areas to
negate their mobility advantage, or use the cargo lifter
to rapidly change elevation in ways the swoops cannot match.

### The Haido Factor

During the fight, Haido is at his desk on the seventh floor.
He does not participate. He does not flee. He is sitting
at his desk with his hands flat on the surface, which is
what he does when he is very frightened and has decided
that the safest thing is to not move.

If the fight comes to the seventh floor — if any ganger
or the party creates a situation where the seventh floor
becomes part of the combat space — Haido ducks behind
his desk. He stays there. He is not going to help.

But: if the party is losing badly, or if a ganger
moves to execute a downed party member, Haido does
something unexpected. He activates the floor's emergency
alarm — a building safety system he knows the location
of because he has worked here for eight years —
which triggers the loading bay doors to seal, disabling
the swoops' primary exit route.

He does not frame this as help. He frames it afterward as:
> *"The emergency system is tested quarterly.*
> *I simply happened to activate it."*

He is lying. They both know he is lying.
He helped. He is not going to admit he helped.

## AFTER THE FIGHT — IMMEDIATE AFTERMATH

### The Factory Floor

The workers who scattered during the attack have not returned.
The production equipment is still running. The combination
of abandoned workstations and continuing machinery creates
a specific kind of uncanniness — the factory doing what
it does without the people who make it meaningful.

If the party lingers, workers begin returning cautiously
within ten to fifteen minutes. A few stop and look at
the damage. No one says anything directly to the party.
One older worker pauses near the southwest corner of
the third floor — the corner with the blaster scoring —
and looks from the old marks to the new damage and back again
with an expression that the party can read without a skill check.

*It happened again. Of course it happened again.*

### Haido

Haido is still at his desk. He comes out from behind it
when it is clearly over. He assesses the damage to his office
with the precision of a man calculating what the repair
request will look like.

> *"Thank you," he says, which is clearly difficult for him.
> "I did not expect — I had hoped this would not come here."*

He pauses.
> *"I will need to explain this to building management.*
> *A Neimoidian saying there were offworld Force users in his
> office who were attacked by a swoop gang — this is not
> a comfortable explanation to give."*

Another pause.
> *"But it is the accurate one. I find I have fewer objections
> to accurate explanations than I used to."*

He looks at them for a moment. Then:
> *"N'got is in the residential district. Third building
> east of the intersection with Service Corridor 7.*
> *The upper floors — he takes a different apartment
> each night. He will be afraid of you.*
> *He is afraid of everything.*
>
> *That does not mean he is wrong to be."*

He sits back down at his desk. The datapad is still
at its precise angle. He straightens it slightly.
The meeting is over. He will not ask them to leave.
He will simply be done.

## THE PATHFINDER'S EXPERIENCE — THROUGHOUT

### In the Factory District

The factory district is the loudest, most human-dominated
environment the Pathfinder has been in since arriving on Cato.
The animal presence is thinner here — geckos, a few bat
colonies in the structural levels, the occasional pale spider.

What is present, densely and constantly, is the
emotional texture of fifteen thousand workers
doing work they cannot refuse in a place that used to be theirs.
A Force-sensitive Pathfinder who is not skilled at blocking
out human emotional presence may find the factory district
genuinely uncomfortable. Not painfully so — but there is
a low-level hum of collective exhaustion and suppressed
resentment that the Zeltron empathy amplifies.

The animals in the factory district are interesting specifically
because they are not affected by this. The geckos in Haido's
office are exactly as content as the geckos anywhere else.
The pale spider in its web is exactly as focused on its
web as any pale spider is. The animals have no relationship
to the political and economic history of this place.
They are just here because the conditions are suitable.

The Pathfinder may find this grounding. She may also find
it a strange kind of grief — that the place feels most
itself to her through the perspective of creatures
that do not know what it is.

### During the Fight

The Drone-Beetle (if bonded) is the Pathfinder's primary
tactical asset in this encounter. Through it she can feel
the vibration of swoop engines — she knows where the bikes
are without seeing them. She knows when a swoop is about
to change direction by the change in the floor vibration
pattern. This is not precise coordinates. It is something
more like a sixth sense about movement in the building,
and it is more useful in a cluttered industrial environment
than precise coordinates would be.

She can also feel Haido through the fight. Not his thoughts —
his emotional state, which is a specific flavor of controlled
terror that she has not felt before. He is not frozen.
He is consciously choosing to stay still because he has
calculated that staying still is the best option and he
is executing that calculation perfectly while being
absolutely terrified. There is something almost admirable
in it and something very sad.

### After the Fight

When the workers start returning to the production floor,
the Pathfinder is probably the first party member to feel
the shift — the careful, incremental return of people
who have learned to test the safety of spaces before
committing to them. It is a specific kind of animal
caution that is also human. She will recognize it.

## SIGG IN THE FACTORY SESSION

### On the Approach

Sigg is in his element moving through the factory district
in a way he is not in the ventral city. He understands
industrial environments — they have specific social rules
that are different from residential or commercial spaces
and he has learned those rules.

He moves through the district with the purposeful pace
of someone who belongs here, which is the most effective
camouflage available. He does not look at the surveillance
cameras. He does not avoid them. He looks like someone
who knows where the cameras are and is not worried about
them, which reads completely differently than someone
trying to avoid detection.

When he clocks the Kreehawk tail:
> *"Don't look. Keep walking.*
> *Two behind us, one at the junction ahead.*
> *They're not going to move yet.*
> *Hasar wants information before she decides.*
> *So do we. Let's get it first."*

### During the Meeting

Sigg does not sit during the Haido meeting. He stands
slightly to the side of the main conversation — not
participating directly, but processing everything.
He is the party's second set of ears, tracking what
Haido says and what he does not say simultaneously.

Occasionally he will redirect the conversation by
asking a question that seems tangential and is not:
> *"The northern production facilities — were they
> operational during the occupation, or were they
> converted?"*

The answer to this is not what he actually wants to know.
What he wants to know is whether Haido will answer a
simple factual question accurately, which tells him
how much of the Warde information to trust.
(Haido answers accurately. Sigg notes this.)

### After the Fight

When Haido activates the emergency alarm, Sigg is the
first party member to understand what he did and why.
He does not say anything in the moment. After they leave,
in the corridor outside:

> *"He activated the alarm. That sealed the bay doors.

> exterior windows on the upper floors — the only other
> way out. That gave us the angle on them.*
>
> *He did not have to do that."*

A pause.

> *"I like him slightly more than I expected to."*
