
# THE DEEP DESCENT
## From the Ossivane to Sankar Palace
## A Step-By-Step Immersive Guide

---

# BEFORE THE FIRST STEP

---

## THE MOMENT OF COMMITMENT

The party does not simply descend.

There is a moment before the descent where the shape
of what they are doing becomes clear. They have the
second kyber crystal. They know where Warde went.
They know what happened in the palace below.
They know enough that going down is not an act
of investigation anymore — it is an act of witness.

They are going to the place where it happened.
To stand in the room where it happened.
To find what Durael held onto after he could
no longer hold on to anything else.

Sigg is quiet before they go. Not anxious —
quiet in the way of someone organizing themselves
for something that requires organizing.

He checks his equipment with the thoroughness
of habit rather than necessity. Then he looks
at the Pathfinder.

> *"Ready?"*

Not asking if she is prepared. Asking if she is
willing. There is a difference and he knows it.

---

## THE STALKER'S WARNING

The Rusted Stalker is at the entrance to the
deep ventral route. It has been here since
the party established the bond — it has been
patrolling the boundary between the levels
it knows and the levels below with increasing
frequency over the past days.

It does not block their path.
It sits and looks at the Pathfinder with the
specific quality of attention that means:
*I am going to tell you something and I need you to hear it.*

What it communicates through the bond:

Not words. The stalker does not have words.
What it has is:

The memory of going to the edge of Level 14
and stopping. The feeling that stopped it —
not fear of a predator, not instinct about structural
danger, but something the stalker has no category for.
Something that made its fur stand and made it turn
around and not go further.

The stalker has been the top predator in the ventral
city for years. It does not avoid things.
It avoids this.

It is telling her this as clearly as it can.
It is also telling her: I will be here when you come back.
That part is the clearest of all.

She can acknowledge this or not.
The stalker will understand either way.

> *Sigg watches the stalker and then watches her.*
> *"It's not going to come with us, is it."*
> *A statement.*
> *He picks up his pack.*
> *"All right. Let's go."*

# THE DESCENT — STEP BY STEP

---

## THE OSSIVANE SUBLEVEL — DEPARTURE POINT

They leave from the Ossivane basement.

The vault is behind them — Jaral's items retrieved,
the combination memorized unnecessarily since they
will not need it again. The Rusted Stalker's
swept mosaic is underfoot in the lobby.
The crystal sculpture that catches light is gone,
carried back to Jaral, but the surface of the shelf
where it sat still has the faint mark of fifteen years
of one object in one position.

The southern basement passage is a door they have
not opened before. It was behind a stack of old
maintenance equipment — heavy frames for some kind
of mechanical system long since removed —
that the stalker used as a bedding area.
The stalker moved the frames over several weeks
without apparent purpose until the passage was accessible.
Whether it did this deliberately is a question
the Pathfinder cannot answer.

The door is unlocked. It has always been unlocked.
No one has needed to lock a door that goes
from an abandoned building to deeper abandonment.

> *The passage beyond the door is different
> from the Ossivane's finished spaces.*
> *This is infrastructure — stone and durasteel
> in the utilitarian language of things built
> to function rather than to be seen.*
> *The ceiling is lower here. The passage is narrow
> enough that you move single file.*
> *The chemical lighting from the Ossivane
> does not reach this far.*
> *Your glow rods take over.*

The passage runs south for approximately forty meters
before it joins a larger corridor — a service connection
built when the diplomatic complex below was
constructed, designed to allow maintenance access
between the residential levels and the palace
infrastructure without routing through public spaces.

No one has walked this corridor in fifteen years.
The evidence of this is in the dust — not thick,
the ventilation still moves air through this space,
but present. The party's footprints appear behind
them as they walk. Clear and specific.

> *You are the first people to walk here
> in a very long time.*
> *The passage records this.*
> *When you leave, it will record that too.*

---

## THE FIRST TRANSITION — CROSSING INTO DEEP VENTRAL

Approximately thirty meters into the service corridor,
something changes in the architecture.

The party crosses a threshold that is not marked
and not formal — there is no door, no designation,
no visible indication that they have crossed from
one zone into another. But the material changes.
The stone changes. The quality of the construction changes.

The Ossivane and everything above it was built in
the Trade Federation's residential expansion period —
good construction, maintained, the stone quarried
from standard Cato Neimoidian sources.

Below the threshold, the stone is different.
Older. Darker. Quarried from deeper in the canyon
walls, brought up at significant expense because
the Trade Federation wanted its diplomatic facilities
built from the best available material.

> *The walls are darker here.*
> *Not dramatically — a shade, maybe two shades.*
> *But the color is different and the texture
> is different and the weight of the material
> is different in a way that the eye registers
> before the mind processes it.*
>
> *The construction is more deliberate.*
> *The joins between stones are precise in a way
> that the maintenance corridor above was not.*
> *Someone built this to last.*
> *Someone built this to impress.*
>
> *They succeeded at both.*

The Pathfinder feels the change as well —
not in terms of the Force, not yet, but in terms
of the animals. The Bridge Geckos that have been
a low-level background presence throughout the
ventral city thin out here. They are present
in the upper section of the service corridor.
They are less present thirty meters in.
By the time the party reaches the deeper stone,
there are none.

Not because the conditions are wrong for geckos.
Because the geckos have decided, collectively,
across years of venturing and retreating,
that this is where they stop.

The Pathfinder notices the absence before she
notices what it means.

---

## THE SERVICE JUNCTION — A PAUSE

Forty meters from the Ossivane, the service corridor
opens into a junction — a small square room where
the maintenance passage intersects with two others.

One passage leads east — toward the remains of
a maintenance facility that served the canyon-facing
structural elements of the deep residential.
It is blocked approximately ten meters in by
a collapse. Old collapse — the stone has settled
and is not moving.

One passage leads west — toward the structural
support systems that anchor the palace to the bridge
above. This passage is intact.

One passage leads south — down.
Specifically down. The passage ahead is not level.
It drops at approximately a fifteen-degree angle
for as far as the glow rods reach.

> *Down is darker than behind.*
> *This is not a meaningful observation —
> darkness is not thicker in any direction,
> the physics of light do not work that way.*
> *But something about the passage south
> reads as darker regardless.*
> *Your glow rods reach the same distance.*
> *What they illuminate looks different.*

In the junction itself, on the east wall,
someone scratched something into the stone.
Not during the war — the scratching is old,
older than the war, possibly as old as the
building's construction.

In Pak Pak: *"From here, you are going somewhere important."*

Not threatening. Not ominous. A statement of fact
written by a worker who was building a passage
to a diplomatic complex and felt the weight of
what the passage connected to.

From here, you are going somewhere important.
They are.

> *Sigg reads it. He can read Pak Pak — not fluently,
> but enough. He translates it.*
> *He does not comment further.*
> *He starts down the slope.*

---

## THE SLOPE — FIFTEEN DEGREES FOR TWO HUNDRED METERS

The descent is not dramatic. Fifteen degrees is
a gentle slope — shallow enough that walking it
requires no special technique, steep enough that
the party is aware of going down in a continuous
and cumulative way.

The passage is lit by their glow rods and nothing else.
The construction here is at its most functional —
bare stone, no decorative work, the precision of
the joins visible in the rods' directional light.
The ceiling is two and a half meters — adequate,
not generous.

Two hundred meters is not a short distance in
a narrow passage going down. The party has time
to think.

**What they may think about:**

The passage is not a place for distraction.
There is nothing to look at except stone and the
circle of light moving ahead of them.
This tends to resolve thought into its simplest forms.

Sigg, approximately seventy meters in:

> *"N'got said the delegation came down in good faith.*
> *Tacker said Warde cared about the people under his command.*
> *The gatekeeper said he was good at his best.*
> *Warde wrote that he was sorry before he did it.*
>
> *I keep trying to find the point where he stopped
> being one thing and became another.*
> *I cannot find a specific point.*
> *It seems like it was gradual.*
> *It seems like the gradual is the most frightening part."*

He does not expect a response.
He may or may not get one.

At one hundred and twenty meters, the passage
bends slightly left — a course correction from
when the original survey realized the initial angle
would miss the junction point below.
The bend is the one moment of visual interruption
in the entire slope.

Beyond the bend, the passage continues.
And the Pathfinder notices something change
in the Force.

Not dramatically. Not a sudden shift.
Like a change in air pressure that the body
registers before the mind identifies it.
The residue she was warned about — the quality
that stopped the stalker — begins to be present.
Not strongly. Not yet.
But present.

She is not imagining it.

> *Sigg, without being told, slows his pace slightly.*
> *He felt it too. Less clearly. But felt.*
> *"We're getting close," he says.*

They are.

## LEVEL 14 — THE THRESHOLD — DETAILED ARRIVAL

The passage ends in a door.

The door is the most formal thing they have encountered
since the Ossivane entrance: stone frame, carefully
fitted, with Trade Federation decorative work in
the lintel — the stylized triangle-and-circle motif
that appears throughout the diplomatic complex's
iconography. A repeating pattern that means, in the
visual language the Trade Federation used for its
formal spaces: *what happens here matters.*

The door is unlocked. It has a handle and the handle
turns and the door opens inward.

> *The air that comes through the door is different.*
>
> *Not colder. Not warmer. Different in a way
> that is not temperature.*
> *The ventilation in the diplomatic complex
> was built to higher standards than the residential
> levels above — the air is cleaner, processed
> by filters that have been running on backup power
> for fifteen years, doing their job without
> anyone to know they are doing their job.*
>
> *The cleaner air should be a relief.*
> *It is not, quite.*
> *It is too clean.*
> *It is the air of a place that has been sealed
> and preserved and kept exactly as it was.*
>
> *It smells like fifteen years ago.*

Level 14 is a wide corridor — wider than anything
above it in the ventral city, designed for the
movement of diplomatic parties. The ceiling rises
to four meters. The floor is paved stone with
a central runner of different material — darker,
more refined — that marks the formal path from
the service door to the reception area ahead.

**The Wall Murals:**

The walls of Level 14 are decorated.

Not with the abstract geometric patterns of the
residential districts or the practical information
of the commercial district. These are figurative
murals — painted onto plaster that was applied
to the stone specifically to receive them —
depicting scenes from Neimoidian diplomatic history.

The style is formal and celebratory: the founding
of the Trade Federation, the establishment of
the purse worlds, the great trade agreements
that connected Neimoidian commerce to the wider galaxy.
Each scene is labeled in Pak Pak and Basic.
The figures are idealized — the Neimoidian diplomats
in their finest robes, the counterpart species
in their own formal dress, everyone making
the gestures of successful negotiation.

They are beautiful in the way of things designed
to celebrate an idea rather than record a reality.

The party walks past fifteen hundred years of
Neimoidian diplomatic history, rendered in pigment
on a wall that no one has looked at in fifteen years.

> *The last panel is the largest.*
> *It shows the Trade Federation's peak period:
> trade routes radiating from Cato Neimoidia
> across the galaxy, the accumulated commerce
> of three hundred years, all of it flowing through
> this planet, this city.*
>
> *In the lower right corner of the panel,
> at the foot of the composition, there is a small
> detail that would be easy to overlook:
> a single figure, separate from the celebration,
> looking outward from the painting rather than
> at the event being depicted.*
>
> *It is hard to tell if this is intentional.*
> *It looks intentional.*
> *The figure looks like someone who knows
> that what is being celebrated will not last.*

**The Force Quality Here:**

The Pathfinder feels it clearly now.

It is not painful. It is not the dark side in
the active sense — it is more like the impression
left by the dark side in the specific way that
wood holds the impression of something heavy
placed on it for a long time.

What she feels most clearly is not the event itself.
It is the approach to the event. This corridor
was where the delegation walked — where Warde walked
where Moll and Durael walked — toward what happened
in the room below.

The anticipation is still here somehow.
Hope, from the delegation's side.
Dread, from Warde's.
The two things walking toward each other
through this corridor.

> *She stops.*
>
> *Sigg stops behind her immediately.*
> *He does not speak.*
> *He waits.*
>
> *After a moment she starts walking again.*
> *She does not explain what she felt.*
> *He does not ask.*

## THE EXTERNAL WALKWAY — THE CANYON FACE

Before reaching the staircase to Level 15,
the formal route through Level 14 passes
through an external section — a portion of
the diplomatic complex that projects beyond
the bridge structure and is supported instead
by the palace's own cable system.

The passage from the internal corridor to the
external walkway is marked by a change in
the floor and walls — the interior stone gives way
to a transitional material, then to the open air.

> *The walkway extends ahead of you for thirty meters.*
> *Beyond the edge of the bridge above,
> supported by cables that disappear into the
> structure overhead and into the canyon walls below.*
>
> *The railing on the right side is intact.*
> *The railing on the left side is missing —
> not broken, the attachment points are clean,
> someone removed it deliberately.*
> *For what purpose is not clear.*
>
> *Below the grating underfoot: the cables,
> and below the cables, the mist.*
> *The canyon floor is not visible.*
> *The canyon floor has not been visible from here
> for fifteen years because the mist has thickened
> since the war and the ventilation patterns
> that used to clear it have changed.*
>
> *What is visible: the other external platforms
> of the diplomatic complex, extending at
> different levels from the palace structure,
> connected by external staircases and bridges
> some of which are intact and some of which
> are not.*
>
> *And, at the edge of the furthest visible platform,
> the Canyon Manta.*

The manta is not in the exhibition hall today.
It is here, resting on a platform three levels
below the walkway the party is on — close enough
to see clearly, far enough that its full scale
is apparent.

It is enormous. Both of them, actually —
the pair is together today, resting side by side
on the platform, their wingtips overlapping.

> *From this distance and this angle,
> with the mist below and the bridge above
> and the canyon going down around them,
> they do not look like animals that moved in
> after the people left.*
> *They look like they belong here.*
> *They look like they have always been here.*
> *They look like the canyon's response
> to the question of what fills a space
> when the previous occupants are gone.*

The Pathfinder can feel them from the walkway —
the deep, slow warmth of the bond extended across
the distance. They are aware of her. They are
unconcerned by her. They are content in a way
that the palace above them is not.

> *Sigg stands at the walkway railing and looks down
> at them for a long moment.*
>
> *"How long do they live?"*
>
> *He is asking the Pathfinder.*
> *Not because he expects a precise answer.*
> *Because he wants to know if they were here
> before the war.*

The answer, through the bond, is yes.
These specific animals — this pair — have been
here for more than forty years. They were here
when the palace was built. They watched the
diplomatic parties arrive. They watched the fighting.
They watched the people leave.

They have been here, in the canyon, on their platforms,
through all of it.

> *"Good," Sigg says.*
> *He says it quietly.*
> *"That's good."*

## THE STAIRCASE DOWN — LEVEL 15

The main staircase connecting Level 14 to the
palace proper descends in a single sweeping flight —
wide, formal, designed for the full width of a
diplomatic party moving in ceremony.

The handrails are still present on both sides.
The stone is intact. The steps are broad enough
that descending requires no particular care.

It should feel easy.

It does not feel easy.

The Force quality changes on the staircase.
Not suddenly — gradually, step by step.
The way temperature changes as you descend
into a space that has been sealed from
external climate. Not the temperature of the
Force — that is not a meaningful concept —
but the density of it. The accumulated weight
of significant events, getting heavier
as they get closer to the significant events.

> *The stairs are wide enough for all of you
> to walk abreast.*
> *You do not walk abreast.*
> *Without discussing it, you have formed a line.*
> *This is not tactical.*
> *This is the architecture doing something
> to how you move through it.*
> *Some spaces invite ceremony.*
> *This staircase does.*

At the midpoint of the staircase there is a window —
a large formal window in the canyon-facing wall,
one of the windows that gave the dining hall on
Level 15 its view. The glass is still intact here.
Through it: the canyon, the mist, the external
platforms below, the pair of mantas now visible
from a different angle.

And, in the glass, the party's reflection.
Themselves, descending.

> *It is the first mirror the deep ventral city
> has offered.*
> *The first time, descending through all of this,
> that you can see yourselves in it.*
>
> *The reflection is accurate.*
> *It shows what you look like in this place:
> people with glow rods going down into something
> that has been waiting.*

## LEVEL 15 — THE CHEMICAL LIGHTS

The floor at the base of the staircase has
a pressure sensor. When the first foot touches it,
the chemical lights ignite.

Not dramatically — sequentially, as described.
But the experience of it is worth taking time with.

> *The light comes from your left first.*
> *A soft ignition, like something remembering
> how to burn. The sconce at the corridor's entrance.*
> *Then the next one, eight meters further.*
> *Then the next.*
> *And the next.*
> *Each ignition carries a brief chemical smell —
> sharp, specific, not unpleasant but distinctive —
> that dissipates almost immediately.*
>
> *The light is purple-white.*
> *Not the warm amber of the residential levels.*
> *Not the blue-white of Imperial facilities.*
> *Something between and neither.*
> *Trade Federation diplomatic lighting.*
> *Chosen forty years ago by a designer who wanted
> the lighting in this space to feel neither warm
> nor cold, neither intimate nor institutional.*
> *Chosen to be appropriate for any delegation
> from any species under any circumstances.*
>
> *In the absence of delegations and circumstances,
> it is the lighting of a place that is ready
> for something that is not going to arrive.*

The lights extend down the main corridor of Level 15,
through the reception area, past the preparation suites,
and — triggered by the same propagating signal —
to the staircase that descends to Level 16.

The palace is lit.
After fifteen years of darkness, the palace is lit.

## THE GRAND ENTRY — LEVEL 15 — DETAILED

**The Reception Desk:**

The reception desk is to the right of the entrance,
in an alcove designed to allow for discreet checking-in
without the reception process being visible
from the main corridor.

The administrative droid is still here.

Not functional — it powered down fifteen years ago
when the palace's main power system failed and
its backup cell depleted. But it is still here,
in the specific stillness of powered-down droids
that have no damage, no visible reason for their
condition other than time.

It is seated at the desk in the position it
would have occupied to receive visitors.
The data terminal in front of it is dark.
The reception ledger — physical, paper,
the diplomatic complex used both digital and
physical records as a matter of protocol —
is open in front of it.

The ledger is open to the last page of entries.

> *The handwriting is precise and administrative —
> the handwriting of a system built for recording
> rather than expressing.*
>
> *Seven entries above the last.*
> *Seven delegations.*
> *The last entry:*
>
> *"Separatist Diplomatic Delegation.*
> *Party of seven, plus support staff.*
> *Purpose: Cease-fire Negotiations.*
> *Arrival: [time]*
> *Departure: —"*
>
> *The departure line is blank.*
> *The pen that would have filled it is beside the ledger.*
> *The droid that would have picked it up
> has not moved in fifteen years.*
>
> *The departure line will remain blank.*
> *There is no one to fill it in.*

**The Trade Federation Map Mosaic:**

The mosaic is everything the description promised.
Thirty meters across in the main atrium.
The purse worlds at the center. Trade routes
extending outward like the roots of an enormous
tree — or the branches, depending on which direction
you consider the center.

> *Cato Neimoidia is marked with a symbol
> the others do not have.*
> *Larger. More detailed.*
> *The symbol for a world that is not just
> a point in a network but the center of a network.*
> *The symbol for somewhere that everything
> runs through.*
>
> *The mosaic is intact.*
> *Fifteen years of sealed air and chemical lighting
> have not damaged it.*
> *It is exactly as it was when it was laid.*
> *Every trade route, every connection, every name.*
>
> *You are standing on a map of an empire
> that no longer exists.*
> *The map does not know this.*
> *The map shows the empire as it was
> at the moment of its greatest confidence.*
>
> *Every route on this map has been changed
> or closed or redirected.*
> *Some of the worlds named on this map
> are under Imperial control.*
> *Some of them were destroyed in the war.*
> *One of them — visible in the outer ring —
> does not exist in any current database.*
> *The Empire erased it.*

That world is Moraband.
It is on this map because the Trade Federation
knew it existed even if they did not trade with it.
It is on the map with a specific marker — not a trade route,
a reference marker. A notation.

The notation, in Pak Pak at the base of the marker:
*"Restricted. No commercial activity.*
*Historical significance only.*
*Consult diplomatic records before contact."*

Moraband was on the Trade Federation's maps
with a warning not to go there.
The Trade Federation has been gone for fifteen years.
The party is going there.

**The Preparation Suites:**

The western suite — the Separatist delegation's room —
is as described. Chairs positioned where people sat.
The personal items left behind.

> *In the center of the table: a small object.*
> *Stone, smooth, the size of a closed fist.*
> *Worn smooth by long handling — this is an object
> that has been carried and held many times.*
> *A worry stone, possibly. Or a significant stone.*
> *Or simply a stone that someone picked up once
> and kept.*
>
> *One of the seven members of the delegation
> set it on the table before the meeting.*
> *They expected to pick it up again afterward.*
>
> *It has been on this table for fifteen years.*

The eastern suite — Warde's room — is as described.
The notation board with his writing.

Take significant time here. Read his words aloud
if the party examines the board. All of it.

When you reach the last section — *I am sorry* —
let the silence after it be as long as it needs to be.

This is the emotional core of everything.
The investigation has been leading here.

> *Sigg reads it over the Pathfinder's shoulder.*
>
> *He does not speak for a long time.*
>
> *Then:*
> *"He wasn't surprised by himself.*
> *That's the part I keep coming back to.*
> *He knew. He wrote it down. He went into the room anyway.*
>
> *I don't know if that makes it worse or better.*
> *I've been trying to decide since we found the notation board.*
> *I haven't decided."*
>
> *He looks at the last line.*
> *"I am sorry.*
> *I don't know who I am apologizing to.*
> *I am sorry anyway."*
>
> *He looks at the Pathfinder.*
> *"He was apologizing to us.*
> *Or to people like us.*
> *He knew someone would come eventually.*
> *He was leaving it here for them.*
>
> *For us."*

## THE MAIN STAIRCASE — LEVEL 15 TO 16

The staircase between the reception level and the
conference level is less ceremonial than the one above.
Still formal — this is a diplomatic facility —
but designed for internal movement rather than
arrival. The lighting here is more functional,
the chemical sconces spaced further apart.

The staircase descends past a wall installation —
a large frame, approximately two meters by three,
that would have held a holographic display.
The display is inactive. The frame is still there.
In the frame, behind the inactive display medium,
the wall has been written on.

Not recent writing — old writing, careful handwriting,
in the space behind the holographic display where
no one would see it unless the display was removed.

The writing is in Basic. It is addressed to no one.

> *"If you are reading this, the display is down.*
> *This means something has gone wrong.*
> *I am leaving this here because things going wrong
> is a possibility I have to account for.*
>
> *My name is Eleth Sarrino.*
> *I was the facility manager of Sankar Palace
> from Year 12 of the Federation's Fourth Expansion
> to the evacuation.*
> *I lived here for eleven years.*
> *I loved this building.*
>
> *If something has gone wrong, I want someone to know
> that this was a good building.*
> *That people met here and talked and sometimes reached
> agreements that made things better.*
> *That the mosaics in the atrium took two years to lay
> and the mosaic artists were paid well and they were proud.*
> *That the canyon view from the conference level
> is the most beautiful view I have ever seen.*
>
> *I want someone to know that before the building
> becomes whatever it becomes next.*
>
> *I am sorry if things went wrong.*
> *I hope they did not.*
> *I hope this is never read."*

## LEVEL 16 — THE CONFERENCE LEVEL

The conference level opens from the base of the staircase
into a wide landing that gives onto the main corridor.

The chemical lights have extended here — the propagating
signal reached this level and the sconces are lit.

The corridor is wider than Level 15's. Higher ceiling.
The canyon-facing wall is almost entirely window
at this level — large, formal panels that give
the conference rooms on this side their characteristic
view. Some panels are broken and open to the air.
Through the intact ones, the canyon is visible:
the cables below, the mist, the distant suggestion
of depth.

Through one of the broken panels, the sound of
the canyon enters. Not wind specifically — the canyon
has its own sound at this depth, a low harmonic
produced by the interaction of the bridge structure
and the canyon walls at the specific frequencies
of this section of the span. It is almost musical.
Almost, but not quite.

> *The corridor is approximately fifty meters long.*
> *Conference rooms on the left.*
> *The canyon on the right.*
> *The meeting hall at the far end.*
>
> *You can see the meeting hall doors from the landing.*
> *They are closed.*
> *Large doors, formal, with Trade Federation motifs
> on the panels.*
> *They are closed and the corridor is lit and the canyon
> is audible through the broken windows and
> the meeting hall is at the end and you are here.*
>
> *Take a moment if you need one.*

**The Conference Rooms:**

The conference rooms are worth pausing in before
the meeting hall. They are the anteroom to the
main event — the places where the careful, technical
work of diplomacy happened before the formal
negotiation began.

Room 1 — the largest preliminary conference room:

> *Tables pushed back against the walls.*
> *Not overturned — pushed back deliberately,
> the way you push back tables when you need
> to change the configuration of a space.*
> *Whatever was happening in here when the
> evacuation came, someone took time to move the furniture.*
> *This is striking, given what was apparently
> happening on the floor below.*
> *Someone in this room, at the moment of crisis,
> moved the furniture.*
> *Some habits do not break even in extremity.*

On the largest table, still in the configuration
of an interrupted working session:
Position papers. Draft language. A seating chart.
The cease-fire terms in their preliminary form.

The seating chart is a single page, handwritten,
with eight positions marked around the main table
and one marked off to the side.

The off-side position is labeled: *Tactical Support — Republic Military.*

N'got's embedded position.
He sat here during the preliminary discussions.
He listened and reported and watched.
This is where he was.

> *Sigg looks at the off-side position.*
> *"He watched everything from there.*
> *He had the best angle on the room.*

> *He saw the decision when it was forming.*
> *He saw the two seconds.*
>
> *And he ran."*

> *He is not judging N'got.*
> *He is acknowledging that N'got ran and
> N'got survived and the people who did not run
> did not survive.*
>
> *"Running was the right thing to do," he says.*
> *"I'm just noting it."*

**The Corridor to the Meeting Hall:**

From the last conference room to the meeting hall
is approximately twenty meters of formal corridor.

The party will notice, in this section,
something that has not been present in the ventral
city above: sound from within the building.

Not voices. Not movement. The building settling —
the specific sounds of a structure adjusting
to temperature changes and its own accumulated
weight over fifteen years. Old buildings make
these sounds. The diplomatic complex is not as old
as the residential districts but the canyon environment
creates more movement in the materials.

But also: the soft sound of the chemical lighting.
It is not silent. The chemical reaction that produces
the light produces a very faint, very continuous
sound — a resonance at the edge of hearing.

> *The corridor has sound.*
> *Quiet sound, but sound.*
> *The palace is not silent.*
> *It has been waiting in its own sound
> for fifteen years.*

**The Meeting Hall Doors:**

The doors at the end of the corridor are the largest
the party has encountered in the palace.
Approximately three meters high. The decorative work
on them is the most elaborate in the building:
the Trade Federation's full formal iconography,
rendered in metal inlay against the dark stone.

The symbols mean, in the visual language of the Federation:
*This is the place where important things are decided.*
*What enters here matters.*
*What is said here matters.*
*What is agreed here will be kept.*

The doors are closed.

There is no handle visible on this side.
The doors open from the inside — a design decision
that placed the control of entry in the hands of
whoever was already inside, giving the meeting
room's occupants authority over access.

To open them from the outside: there is a mechanism
in the wall to the right of the doors, covered by
a panel. The panel opens easily. The mechanism
inside is a simple manual release — pull, and
the doors' internal mechanism unlocks.

> *The pull is smooth.*
> *The mechanism has not been used in fifteen years
> but it was well-made and well-maintained
> and it works.*
>
> *The doors open inward.*
>
> *And the light that was already lit in the corridor
> extends into the room as the doors swing back.*
>
> *But the room already has its own light.*
> *The sconces in the meeting hall are lit.*
> *They lit with the others when you entered the
> reception level above.*
> *The meeting hall has been lit for the hour
> or so you have been in the palace.*
> *The room has been waiting in its own light.*
>
> *The doors open.*
> *You go in.*

## THE MEETING HALL — THE ROOM ITSELF

Do not rush this.

The party has been descending toward this room
through this entire Act. Through every conversation,
every found document, every animal encounter,
every step of the ventral city. This room is what
all of it was pointing at.

Give it the space it deserves.

> *The room is large.*
> *Not enormous — large in the way of a room
> designed for serious work rather than spectacle.*
> *The ceiling is six meters.*
> *The walls are the same dark stone as the rest
> of the conference level but finished here to
> a higher degree — smooth, polished, the joins
> between stones barely visible.*
>
> *The lighting makes everything equally visible.*
> *Purple-white, chemical, steady.*
> *No shadows.*
> *Everything in this room is visible.*

Pause here. Look at what they see:

> *The table.*
>
> *Keloythian oak, the module says.*
> *Keloythian oak is a hardwood from a world
> that was heavily contested in the Clone Wars.*
> *Trade Federation records suggest they paid
> an extraordinary sum for this table.*
> *It was chosen because Keloythian oak is
> almost impossible to damage.*
> *It resists heat. It resists moisture.*
> *It was chosen because they wanted a table
> that would outlast everything.*
>
> *It has.*
>
> *The lightsaber cuts in its surface are the
> deepest damage the oak has sustained in
> a century of existence.*
> *They do not go all the way through.*
> *Nothing has gone all the way through.*
> *The table is still the table.*
> *Cut and burned and witnessed and still here.*

The chairs:

> *Some overturned.*
> *Not all — some remain upright.*
> *In the upright chairs you can see exactly
> where people were sitting when the room
> changed from a room where negotiations
> were happening to a room where something else
> was happening.*
> *The people in the upright chairs had time
> to understand what was happening but not
> time to move.*

The Separatist side of the table:

> *Here.*
> *The position papers still on the table.*
> *A cup, tipped over, the liquid long dried
> to a dark stain on the Keloythian oak.*
> *Personal effects.*
>
> *And at the near end of the Separatist side,
> on the floor, face down, the remains of
> what the chemical analysis would have
> identified as a BX-series commando droid.*
> *Its partner is three meters to the right.*
> *They went down fast.*
> *They were built for combat and they went down fast.*
> *Which tells you something about the person
> who put them down.*

The Republic side:

> *Warde's chair.*
> *Pulled back.*
> *Not overturned.*
> *Not pushed back in flight.*
> *Pulled back deliberately.*
> *The way you would pull back a chair
> if you were getting up to do something
> you had decided to do.*
>
> *The lightsaber cuts in the table begin
> near Warde's position.*
> *The first cut goes across the table —
> a single arc, from his side toward the center.*
> *The subsequent cuts are different in character:
> not arcs, not sweeps, but the marks of
> a combat that was no longer one-sided.*
> *The table tells the story of the fight
> in the geometry of the damage.*

The windows:

> *The canyon-facing wall is mostly window.*
> *Some panels intact.*
> *Some open to the air.*
> *Through the intact ones: the canyon.*
> *Through the open ones: the canyon and the sound.*
>
> *From this depth, the canyon sounds different.*
> *The harmonic that you noticed in the corridor
> is clearer here.*
> *The canyon is a resonant chamber and
> you are at the bottom of the portion
> of it that the bridge spans.*
> *The sound is continuous and low and
> not quite music and not quite wind.*
> *It has been this sound for the fifteen years
> the palace has been empty.*
> *It was this sound during what happened here.*
> *The canyon did not stop for it.*

## THE FORCE IN THE MEETING HALL

The party is Force-sensitive. They are standing in
the room where Suljo Warde fell to the dark side.

This is not a passive experience.

The residue that has been building since Level 14,
since the staircase, since the corridor, is concentrated
here. It is not dangerous. It is not active.
It is the emotional weight of a significant event
that the Force has not entirely absorbed.

What each character experiences:

**The Pathfinder:**

> *It hits differently than the approach.*
> *The approach was accumulation.*
> *This is arrival.*
>
> *Not a vision. Not images.*
> *Impressions.*
>
> *Fear, from the side of the table across from her.*
> *Not individual fear — collective, seven people's
> fear simultaneously, the specific flavor of fear
> that happens when something you did not expect
> becomes real in front of you.*
>
> *And from Warde's side: something that is not fear.*
> *Something that is harder to name.*
> *Certainty and grief and tiredness and
> the specific quality of someone who has
> made a decision and is executing the decision
> and cannot feel anything about the decision
> in the moment of execution because feeling
> anything would prevent the execution.*
>
> *She is not seeing him.*
> *She is feeling the imprint he left.*
> *The Force does not keep video records.*
> *It keeps something more intimate and less precise.*

If she uses Foresight here:

> *The power reads the room.*
> *The room has too much past to read future.*
> *What comes back is not what might happen.*
> *What comes back is what did happen.*
>
> *Ten seconds.*
> *Maybe fifteen.*
> *The meeting in progress.*
> *The faces of the delegation — she cannot
> see them clearly, they are not the focus,
> the Force was not attending to them the way
> it was attending to Warde.*
>
> *Warde at the head of the table.*
> *Standing.*
> *He is already standing.*
> *The decision has already been made.*
>
> *And then: two seconds.*
> *Two seconds where he is standing and
> the decision is made and something in him
> is asking whether the decision is right.*
> *She cannot see his face clearly.*
> *She can feel the two seconds.*
>
> *They are the most human two seconds
> she has felt since she entered the palace.*
> *In those two seconds he is uncertain.*
> *In those two seconds he is someone who
> could have chosen differently.*
>
> *And then the two seconds end.*
> *And what happened, happened.*

**Sigg:**

He does not have the Pathfinder's sensitivity.
What he feels is less specific and more atmospheric.

> *The room is heavy.*
> *Not with darkness exactly.*
> *With something that darkness contributed to.*
>
> *He stands in the center of the room and
> he can feel it the way you feel a room
> where something significant happened even
> when you don't know what it was.*
> *Except he knows what it was.*
>
> *He looks at the table.*
> *He looks at Warde's chair.*
>
> *"He sat here," he says.*

> to see whether they were going to betray the cease-fire.*

> *He is quiet for a moment.*

> *"I keep thinking about what he would have

> *What would the entry in the gatekeeper be?*
> *What would the gatekeeper know about him

> *The ventral city would not be empty.*
>
> *Everything we have found would not exist."*

> *He looks at her.*
> *"That's the version where he made a different choice.*
> *In those two seconds.*
> *Everything we have found is the version
> where he didn't."*

## THE BALCONY — JIV DURAEL

The balcony doors are open. Have been open for fifteen years.
The canyon air comes through them — cooler than the
interior, carrying the low harmonic more clearly.

> *The balcony floor is intact near the doors.*
> *Further out, toward the canyon, the flooring
> has given way — the structural support that
> extended beyond the main building has
> not survived fifteen years of canyon weather
> without maintenance.*
> *Do not go past the midpoint.*
> *The midpoint is clear.*
> *The midpoint is stable.*
>
> *Against the right wall.*
> *In the corner where the railing still holds.*
>
> *Jiv Durael.*

He is still here. In the specific stillness of
fifteen years. In robes that have become
what old fabric becomes in the canyon air —
faded, structural integrity compromised,
but present. Still present.

His posture, even in the state he is in,
is readable: he is propped against the wall.
He did not fall — he sat. He sat in the corner
where the railing was still intact and he sat
and he did not move after that.

The lightsaber hilt is nearby in two pieces.
The crystal housing, empty.

His right hand:

> *The hand is closed.*
> *Has been closed.*
> *The fingers have maintained their grip through
> everything that time does to things.*
> *Or rather, the grip was strong enough
> and long-lasting enough that it became
> the settled position.*
>
> *In the hand: the kyber crystal.*
> *Visible through the closed fingers.*
> *Gleaming, because kyber crystals gleam.*
> *They do not corrode.*
> *They do not deteriorate.*
> *They endure.*
>
> *He held this after he could no longer hold anything.*
> *He held it because it was the thing to hold.*
> *Because whatever the crystal would eventually
> do — whatever it was meant for — he was
> going to make sure it survived.*
>
> *He succeeded.*
> *Here it is.*
> *Fifteen years later.*
> *He succeeded.*

The left hand:

> *Open.*
> *Palm upward.*

> *A Jedi at the end.*

Taking the crystal:

> *The crystal comes free from his hand.*

>
> *The crystal is warm.*
> *Not ambient temperature warm.*
> *Warm.*
> *Kyber crystals are Force-attuned.*
> *This one has been in a Jedi's grip through his death.*
> *In a room saturated with a significant event.*
> *For fifteen years.*
>
> *What it carries is not a wound.*
> *It is not tainted.*
> *It is a crystal that has been through something
> and has endured.*
>
> *The warmth is not comfortable.*
> *It is honest.*
> *It is the warmth of something that knows

Sigg, watching:

> *Not to the Pathfinder.*
> *To the room.*
> *To Durael.*

> of the people who were here.*

Going back up is not the same as coming down.

The party has what they came for. The investigation

The notation board. The hesitation. Tacker's face

The geckos in Haido's office.
The Rusted Stalker sweeping the mosaic it didn't know it loved.
The calendar that someone kept for six months
in an apartment they couldn't go back to.
Durael holding the crystal after he couldn't hold anything.

They carry all of this out.

**The Ascent:**

The palace's corridors on the way back.
The notation board in Warde's preparation suite

pass the doorway it is there.
The ledger at the reception desk.
The mosaic.
The staircase.

Level 14 — the murals.
The figure in the corner of the last panel who
seems to know the celebration will not last.

The junction. The service corridor.

Sitting. Waiting. Exactly where she left it.

> *It does not ask where they went.*

> *The step is confirmation.*

> *They continue up through the ventral city.*
