# SP16 — Cavern Breach: The Names They Kept

**Suggested placement:** After SP15; connects toward SP17  
**Session length:** 3–4 hours  
**Primary encounters:** Investigation, document puzzle, preservation dispute  
**Story purpose:** Show people preserving information when official systems stopped answering them

## The Story

The palace’s service architecture wraps around a mass of rock connected to the bridge-city’s anchorage.

Damage opened a sheltered ledge beyond the formal corridor.

During the occupation, families used it as an unofficial waiting place. Someone kept copies of requests, detention numbers, transfer notices, and release slips.

The cache survived because it was outside the normal office route.

Now a damaged drainage line threatens it.

The party can recover useful information and decide whether the larger collection deserves preservation.

## Hooks

- Route marks from SP15 lead into the breach.
- A copied record points to the Emergency Processing Hall.
- Vessa recognizes the district seal on a container.
- A maintenance diagram shows access toward the flooded service cavern.

## Arrival

> Dressed stone ends against rough rock.
>
> Pipes turn around the intrusion.
>
> Beyond them, a sheltered ledge holds old containers and the remains of seating pads.
>
> A narrow channel carries water along the wall.
>
> It has begun spilling over the edge of one storage box.

## The Space’s History

The waiting place contains evidence of repeated use:

- More than one repair to a water vessel.
- Names written over earlier faded names.
- A lamp niche blackened by older use.
- A cloth screen replaced with different material.
- Small spaces kept clear for sleeping.
- An improvised shelf supported by office furniture.

It was not a comfortable permanent settlement.

It was somewhere people could remain near the offices without being in the offices.

## The Cache

Three containers survive.

### Box A — Family Requests

Names, addresses, descriptions, and requests for information.

### Box B — Administrative Copies

Detention references, review notices, and transfer codes.

### Box C — Outcomes and Corrections

A smaller collection of releases, revised numbers, returned mail, and handwritten comparisons.

Box C is particularly important because it shows someone tried to close cases rather than merely collect names.

## Handout: The Keeper’s Note

> These are copies.
>
> Keep the family name with the number.
>
> Numbers are corrected.
>
> People return with the old number.
>
> Do not tell them there is no record until you have checked both.

The author is unidentified.

## Encounter One: Water and Paper

The drain is sending water toward the cache.

This is urgent enough to require a choice, but not so fast that the documents dissolve during discussion.

### Options

- Move the boxes to the dry ledge.
- Redirect the shallow channel.
- Clear the obstruction.
- Use spare containers.
- Photograph the most exposed pages first.

### Mechanics

Routine careful work succeeds with suitable tools and time.

Use Average Mechanics for an improvised diversion under difficult access.

Use a relevant check for rapid document triage if needed.

### Failure

Some already damaged pages become harder to read, a container tears, or the party needs another drying surface.

Do not destroy every important clue.

## Puzzle: The Record That Looks Like Two People

A person appears under two slightly different written names.

The first document uses a family name in one order. A later copy reverses it and assigns another temporary reference.

### Clues

- Same address.
- Same hearing accommodation.
- Same relative’s signature.
- A baggage receipt linking both numbers.
- A handwritten correction in Box C.

### Resolution

The party can establish that the records concern one person.

If using Keth Anno’s case, this becomes an earlier step toward the Processing Hall’s release record. It does not contradict that outcome.

If Keth’s case is already solved, use an unnamed second case whose final outcome remains incomplete.

### Player Reward

They obtain a reliable link that someone else could use.

Incomplete information can still move a family’s search forward.

## Warde’s Connection

A routing sheet bears:

> COMMAND REVIEW — WARDE.

Another field states:

> RELEASE CONDITIONS AWAITING CLARIFICATION.

### What It Establishes

The case entered a process associated with Warde’s authority.

### What Remains Unknown

- Whether he personally reviewed the individual.
- Who wrote the later instruction.
- Whether the missing clarification existed elsewhere.

Keep those distinctions available without repeating them after every clue.

## Encounter Two: The Dry Container

Nemi or another current traveler needs a dry container for equipment that must reach Rennik.

The best intact box contains records.

This is not a choice between saving history and saving a life.

Possible solutions include:

- Repacking the records.
- Lending another container.
- Using waterproof cloth.
- Carrying the papers personally.
- Returning for the box.
- Asking the traveler to help transport the archive.

The scene rewards practical cooperation.

## The Household Memorial

A small arrangement sits in a rock niche:

- A bead.
- Embroidered cloth.
- A written name.
- An empty place where something has been removed.

There is no automatic Force manifestation.

The arrangement may remember someone absent rather than dead.

If Vessa is present:

> “Someone wanted the name to stay here.”
>
> “We should not assume we know the rest.”

## Optional Force Impression

A character deliberately reaching into the place’s residue may sense:

- Relief at finding shelter.
- Repeated waiting.
- Recognition when a name is spoken correctly.
- A person deciding to copy a document before returning it.

Do not reveal an all-knowing ghost who explains every file.

## Hidden Maintenance Niche

Following the pipes reveals:

- A usable inspection lamp.
- Two sealing clamps.
- An old service diagram.
- A note reading “Leave one lamp.”

The tools can help in SP17.

Taking them is allowed. Record what the party leaves for the next traveler.

## Optional Encounter: A Claim of Ownership

A current resident recognizes a family seal on one container and claims the whole cache.

Their family contributed records, but the boxes contain many families’ documents.

### Approaches

- Separate identifiable personal property.
- Make copies.
- Agree on custody.
- Ask the claimant to preserve the whole collection.
- Refuse exclusive ownership.

A successful social resolution should produce an arrangement, not merely a persuaded NPC.

## Ending

The party chooses:

- What to carry.
- What to copy.
- What to protect in place.
- Who should receive the information.

### Closing Description

> The boxes are no longer sitting in the water.
>
> The ledge is still a rough place beside old pipes.
>
> But the names inside the boxes have somewhere to go again.
