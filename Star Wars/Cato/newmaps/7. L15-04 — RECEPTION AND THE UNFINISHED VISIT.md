# 7. L15-04 — RECEPTION AND THE UNFINISHED VISIT

## 7.1 Read aloud

> The reception droid sits behind its desk, hands folded beside an open ledger.
>
> Its eyes are dark.
>
> The page is divided into precise columns: delegation, purpose, arrival, departure.
>
> Earlier entries have been closed.
>
> The last has not.

### The ledger entry

> SEPARATIST DIPLOMATIC DELEGATION  
> PARTY OF SEVEN, PLUS SUPPORT STAFF  
> PURPOSE: CEASE-FIRE NEGOTIATIONS  
> ARRIVAL: RECORDED  
> DEPARTURE: —

Do not invent an exact historical date or time if the campaign has not established one.

## 7.2 What can be found without a roll

Searching the desk reveals:

- The ledger.
- An emergency service key with a physical grip.
- A public room directory.
- A maintenance card showing the reception droid’s external power socket.
- A drawer of ordinary visitor badges.
- An unsigned end-of-shift form.
- A replacement nib wrapped in tissue.

The visitor badges are identification aids, not universal security clearance.

## 7.3 The unsigned end-of-shift form

**New handout:**

> BEFORE CLOSING RECEPTION:
>
> Confirm all delegations have departed.
>
> Return unclaimed belongings to secure storage.
>
> Record staff still on duty.
>
> Leave the arrival lights in standby.
>
> Do not close a visit on assumption.

That last instruction becomes important if the droid is restored.

## 7.4 Restoring the reception droid

This is optional.

### What is wrong

The droid’s onboard cell is exhausted. Its body remains seated because the chair’s docking connector supplied power while reception was active.

A surviving local memory module retains reception records. There is no complete recording of the massacre.

### Restoration options

| Approach | Requirement | Outcome |
|---|---|---|
| External power | Suitable supply and a few minutes | The droid operates while connected. |
| Repair docking connector | Average Mechanics; tools | Restores a stable connection if the service bus is active. |
| Remove memory module | Average Mechanics | Makes local reception records available through a compatible reader. |
| Carry it out | Suitable assistance or trolley | Allows later repair without delaying the session. |

A failed repair does not erase the ledger or the core story.

Suggested failure consequence: the connector needs a replacement lead, available in the works.

Do not require a second identical roll after the party obtains the missing part.

## 7.5 First words

> “Welcome to Sankar Palace.”
>
> The droid’s gaze moves between you and the corridor.
>
> “Your appointment does not appear in the current schedule.”
>
> A pause.
>
> “One delegation remains in session. Would you prefer to wait?”

## 7.6 Personality

Use a courteous, observant administrator.

It has habits:

- Straightens the ledger before answering a difficult question.
- Asks names before discussing personal belongings.
- Distinguishes observations from assumptions.
- Notices whether the party is cold, wet, or carrying someone injured.
- Offers practical help before discussing institutional procedure.

It is not a joke about bureaucracy. Its rules once protected visitors.

## 7.7 What it knows

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| Who used the west suite? | The visiting delegation. |
| Who used the east suite? | Suljo Warde. |
| Did the delegates arrive armed? | Reception did not conduct security screening. It cannot answer. |
| Did they expect to stay? | Their hospitality arrangements included a later meal and possible continued discussions. |
| Did Warde leave his room? | His preparation appointment ended before the conference session. |
| What happened below? | Its local records end before it received a verified conclusion. |
| Can it identify the red world? | Its geographic reference package is unavailable. It can preserve the inscription for later comparison. |
| Can it unlock security below? | No. Reception badges and military authorization are different systems. |
| Can it guide them? | It knows public rooms and staff access points on this level. |
| Where is the crystal? | It has no relevant inventory record. |

## 7.8 Encounter: “How should I record them?”

If the party explains that the delegation died:

> “I understand your statement.”
>
> Its fingers rest beside the empty departure column.
>
> “I have no departure to enter.”
>
> “How should I record them?”

There is no required answer.

### If the party says “deceased”

It asks whether they have identified all seven individuals.

If not, it proposes:

> “Reported deceased. Identification incomplete. Visit unresolved.”

### If the party says to leave the ledger unchanged

It complies and can attach the party’s statement separately.

### If the party invents a departure time

It asks what that time represents.

If they knowingly insist, it records their instruction as an annotation rather than silently converting it into observed fact.

### If they promise to return after Level 16

It requests:

- Names, if recoverable.
- Identification of personal property.
- A distinction between what the party saw and what they inferred.

### If they shut it down again

It asks:

> “Will someone return to finish the record?”

The players may answer honestly, reassure it, or say nothing.

**GM purpose:** Give the party responsibility for how the dead will be remembered without making one response the hidden correct solution.
