# Ventral Repair Court and Water Distribution Station

**Campaign:** Chronicles of the Gatekeeper — Cato Neimoidia  
**Suggested placement:** Introduce before the extended service route; revisit after recovering the regulator  
**Suggested length:** One session or two shorter visits  
**Map status:** Proposed new map  
**Content status:** New homebrew expansion  
**Primary themes:** Community, practical consequences, shared resources, and repair

## Session Premise

The repair court is a small inhabited pocket in the ventral city.

Its residents depend on a damaged water-distribution system.

The system still functions, but pressure is unreliable. Some taps receive water before others. Repairs are delayed because suitable components exist deeper in abandoned service spaces.

Rennik believes the auxiliary regulator in SP17 can improve the system.

The party can meet the people affected before retrieving it, then return to see what the recovery changes.

This location gives the palace expedition a consequence that can be touched, heard, and used.

## Continuity Boundaries

- This is a small community, not a previously undisclosed city.
- It lies outside the sealed ceremonial palace.
- Residents use lateral ventral routes.
- The regulator improves a local system; it does not restore all of Jorra.
- The residents do not know the final meeting hall’s secrets.
- The repair can succeed without solving Warde’s story.
- The party can investigate Warde without taking responsibility for every community problem.

## Story Hooks

### Before the Expedition

Rennik demonstrates the pressure fault and asks for help reaching the service cavern.

### After the Expedition

The party returns with the regulator, Vessa’s crew, or both.

### A Place to Ask Questions

The court provides:

- Local route knowledge.
- A place to repair equipment.
- Rumors that can be compared with evidence.
- A witness to the party’s preparations and return.

### A Promise Comes Due

If the party borrowed carrying frames, rope, or tools, this is where they return them.

## Map Layout

Include:

- A shared courtyard built within damaged suspended architecture.
- Several communal taps.
- A covered repair bench.
- A pump room with removable wall panels.
- Overhead pipes and accessible valves.
- Small homes opening onto shared circulation.
- A cargo landing or hoist platform.
- A store of reclaimed components.
- A raised walkway crossing part of the court.
- A safe meeting space away from machinery.

The space should feel maintained through work, not through pristine construction.

## Arrival

> Water runs from the first tap in a thin stream.
>
> At the next, someone waits with a container.
>
> A pipe knocks overhead.
>
> Across the court, Rennik has opened a machine housing and arranged its fasteners on a clean cloth.
>
> Nobody steps over the cloth.
>
> A person carrying food changes direction to go around it.

That small courtesy establishes a functioning community.

## The Court’s Daily Rhythm

### Early Work Period

- Containers are filled.
- People leave for salvage routes.
- The repair bench is busiest.
- Nemi carries messages and borrowed parts.

### Quiet Period

- Equipment is cleaned.
- Residents mend clothing.
- Someone checks the upper walkway.
- Vessa updates route warnings.

### Return Period

- Loads arrive.
- Tools are counted.
- Repairs are discussed.
- Missing people are noticed because someone expected them.

Use these rhythms to make the location change between visits.

## Recurring NPCs

### Vessa Tann

Coordinates routes and equipment.

She wants the repair to improve access without turning the station into private property controlled by whoever supplied one component.

### Rennik Sol

Understands the machine.

He can describe its limits clearly.

> “This part will help.”
>
> “It will not make the pipes younger.”

### Nemi

Moves between households and work sites.

Nemi knows who is waiting for a container, who can lift a component, and who lent which tool.

## New NPC: Oren Vask

**Species:** Neimoidian  
**Role:** Keeper of the communal parts store  
**Appearance:** Heavy work apron, careful hands, a narrow cloth cap worn for warmth  
**Immediate concern:** The repair must leave enough usable material for the next breakdown

Oren is cautious because spare parts are difficult to replace.

He is not secretly sabotaging the project.

### Dialogue

> “I believe it will work.”
>
> “I am asking what we do if the next part fails.”

## Opening Encounter: The Uneven Queue

Two residents disagree over filling containers.

One needs water for a household task that cannot wait.

The other has already spent a long time in the queue.

The disagreement is grounded and restrained.

The party can:

- Help carry water from another tap.
- Ask Nemi about the actual distribution.
- Offer a temporary container.
- Stay out of it.
- Make an impractical promise and discover its limits.

Do not turn the disagreement into an immediate riot.

## The System’s Actual Problem

The worn regulator causes inconsistent pressure between branches.

The lower branch also contains a partial obstruction.

Replacing the regulator without checking the branches improves the machine but leaves unequal flow.

This creates a diagnostic problem rather than a single “install item, receive applause” moment.

## Puzzle: The Part Is Not the Whole Repair

### Available Information

Rennik supplies a simple diagram showing:

- Intake.
- Regulator.
- Two distribution branches.
- A return line.
- A manual isolation valve.

### Observable Clues

- Pressure rises normally at the intake.
- One branch responds slowly.
- A pipe section vibrates differently.
- The old gauge sticks but can be checked against a portable meter.
- The obstruction is downstream from the regulator.

### Complete Repair

1. Isolate the station.
2. Warn users that flow will stop temporarily.
3. Replace the regulator.
4. Inspect the slow branch.
5. Clear the obstruction through its service point.
6. Restore flow gradually.
7. Check both branches under actual use.

### Partial Success

Replacing the regulator alone gives more reliable output but leaves one branch weak.

That is still an improvement.

The remaining symptom points toward further work.

### Checks

Use Average Mechanics for a routine installation with suitable tools.

Use a relevant observation or technical check to locate the obstruction if the players need assistance.

Do not require a roll for every bolt.

## Different Characters’ Roles

| Role | Useful contribution |
|---|---|
| Mechanic | Install and diagnose. |
| Social character | Explain the shutdown and coordinate temporary arrangements. |
| Strong character | Move housings and secure the component. |
| Scout | Inspect the branch route or locate an accessible service point. |
| Medical character | Help identify genuinely urgent water needs during shutdown. |
| Force-sensitive character | Use established abilities where useful; no special Force action is required. |

## Complication: The Missing Seal

The new regulator requires an intact seal.

Available options:

- Use the correct seal recovered earlier.
- Rework a compatible spare.
- Borrow one from a nonessential assembly.
- Trade for one.
- Install temporarily and schedule a proper replacement.

Oren objects to stripping the only spare without a plan.

He proposes a workable alternative if asked: a slower adaptation using available material.

The conflict is about maintenance judgment, not obstruction for its own sake.

## Optional Encounter: The Delivery Claim

If the party disputed salvage rights earlier, a claimant arrives.

They claim a share in the recovered component or payment for crossing their route.

Run this only if the earlier events justify it.

### Possible Resolutions

- Compare the actual agreement.
- Offer a different recovered part.
- Arrange repair assistance.
- Refuse an unsupported claim.
- Ask witnesses what was promised.

If no prior dispute exists, do not manufacture one automatically.

## Historical Storytelling

A reused pipe cover bears an old supply stamp.

Nearby, someone preserved the earlier repair request:

> REPLACEMENT AVAILABLE.
>
> TRANSPORT CLEARANCE PENDING.

The community lived with the consequence long after the offices that issued the forms stopped functioning.

If the party has read the cease-fire clauses:

Rennik can ask:

> “They were discussing repair windows?”
>
> “Then they knew this was happening.”

His statement refers to the issue, not proof that the delegates knew this exact present-day court.

## The First Successful Test

> Rennik opens the valve slowly.
>
> The first tap runs.
>
> Then the second.
>
> Someone at the far end calls that theirs is running too.
>
> Nobody cheers immediately.
>
> They wait to see whether it stops.
>
> It does not.

Give the party a moment before dialogue.

## Rewards

Offer practical, bounded rewards:

- Equipment repaired at reduced cost or as a favor.
- A reliable place to leave a message.
- Help moving recovered evidence.
- Accurate local route information.
- Returned borrowed equipment in better condition.
- A community willing to distinguish the party’s actions from rumor.

Do not reward the repair with an unrelated rare weapon.

## If the Repair Fails

The old assembly can usually be restored temporarily if it was removed carefully.

The community remains under strain but is not automatically doomed.

Rennik identifies what is still needed.

The party can choose whether to continue.

## Return After Level 16

The court asks about the people, not only the artifact.

Vessa:

> “Did you find anyone who could still be helped?”

The answer might include:

- A droid.
- A family awaiting information.
- Someone whose name can now be recorded.
- The party itself.

Do not dictate the response.

## Living-World Consequences

On later visits:

- Shorter queues free people for other work.
- A workshop task resumes.
- Oren labels the remaining spares.
- Someone adds the repair date.
- A different problem exists, because living places continue changing.

The repair mattered without ending the community’s history.

- Use lived-in clutter selectively.
