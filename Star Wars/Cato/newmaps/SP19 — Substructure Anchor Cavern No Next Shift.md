# SP19 — Substructure Anchor Cavern: No Next Shift

**Suggested placement:** Final maintenance-route location before Level 16  
**Session length:** 3–4 hours  
**Primary encounters:** Structural investigation, repair, negotiation over redundancy  
**Story purpose:** Show that the palace survives through maintained systems and decisions made by ordinary workers

## The Story

The party enters a cavern of primary supports, secondary braces, inspection bridges, and enormous anchorage machinery.

The primary structure is beyond what a small adventuring group can casually repair or destroy.

Their immediate problem is local: an access gantry has shifted because a sliding joint seized while a temporary brace remained engaged.

A warning indicator suggests a broad failure, but it is poorly calibrated.

The party must inspect the real structure, decide what it can responsibly fix, and leave a useful record.

An old inspection entry gives the location its human center:

> “Temporary support left in place.”
>
> “Next shift informed.”
>
> A later addition:
>
> “No next shift.”

## Hooks

- SP17’s maintenance route leads here.
- SP18 reveals a repeating local warning signal.
- The party seeks an alternate approach to SP11.
- Vessa needs a reliable cargo route.
- The recovered regulator must eventually pass through the service network.

The distinction in scale matters.

## What Can Actually Be Repaired

The encounter concerns:

- The local access gantry.
- Its sliding joint.
- A temporary secondary brace.
- An inspection jack.
- A manual retaining pin.
- The local warning indicator.

It does not concern resetting the palace’s entire suspension.

## Historical Life

The inspection station contains:

- Different generations of tool marks.
- Replacement labels written by hand.
- A cup ring on the edge of the log shelf.
- A carefully repaired safety strap.
- A checklist with practical corrections.
- Names repeated across years.
- A signature traced over after fading.

Someone valued the work enough to preserve its attribution.

## Encounter One: The Indicator Is Not the Structure

The warning panel reports excessive movement.

Physical inspection reveals a specific local fault rather than general collapse.

### Clues

- The indicator needle changes when the housing is tapped.
- Witness marks on the primary assembly still align.
- The local gantry’s marks do not.
- Mineral buildup surrounds the sliding joint.
- The temporary brace remains engaged.
- The inspection manual distinguishes primary and access loads.

### Resolution

The party can identify the local problem.

Average Mechanics can assist.

Failure means the character cannot safely diagnose it yet. Another inspection point or a specialist is needed.

Do not announce an imminent palace collapse merely to force action.

## Puzzle: Support, Release, Settle

### Safe Procedure

1. Inspect the local connections.
2. Position the inspection jack at its marked point.
3. Confirm it supports the gantry.
4. Clear the seized joint.
5. Ease the temporary brace gradually.
6. Let the gantry settle within its witness marks.
7. Insert the retaining pin.
8. Recheck the route under a light test load.

### Available Tools

- The installed jack.
- Manual controls.
- A retaining-pin rack.
- A maintenance lamp.
- Clamps from SP16, if recovered.
- Equipment the party carries.

### Character-Based Resolution

An Average Mechanics check handles a routine repair with suitable preparation.

Use Hard only for a materially harder approach, such as working without the necessary support or under an established active threat.

### Alternative

Leave the assembly untouched and return to the ceremonial route.

That is a valid decision.

## Failure Consequences

- The gantry settles onto the jack.
- A pin jams.
- The convenient bridge becomes temporarily inaccessible.
- A tool is trapped.
- A character suffers 2 strain from the effort or scramble.

The installed supports should do their job unless the party deliberately bypassed them.

## Key Optional NPC: Inspection Unit A-6

**New homebrew utility droid.**

A-6 remains connected to the inspection station.

Its mobility is limited to a short rail.

It knows:

- The local procedure.
- Its last completed inspection.
- The meaning of the witness marks.
- Which parts are temporary.

It does not know the current political situation or the final hall’s history.

### First Words

> “State the repair performed.”
>
> “State what remains unverified.”

### Personality

A-6 is precise without being inflexible.

It accepts competent improvisation if the party explains what the substitute does.

It dislikes being asked to mark untested work as complete.

## Encounter Two: The Valuable Brace

After repair, the portable brace is no longer carrying the gantry.

It is useful equipment.

Vessa’s crew could use it on the broken crossing.

However, taking it removes a backup from this route.

### Information Available

- The brace is currently unloaded.
- It provides redundancy.
- A smaller spare exists in a difficult storage rack.
- Recovering the spare takes additional work.
- The party can leave the route with a reduced safety margin if it chooses.

### Options

- Leave the main brace.
- Recover the spare.
- Substitute suitable equipment.
- Take the brace and document the limitation.
- Decide the route is no longer needed and mark it accordingly.

The droid asks:

> “Should the next user be told that the backup has been removed?”

It is not a hidden morality test. It is a practical responsibility.

## Historical Discovery: Inspection Access

A copied restriction bears Warde’s command designation.

It limited movement through support routes during a period of suspected sabotage.

An attached exception says:

> INSPECTION ACCESS MUST REMAIN AVAILABLE.
>
> FAILURE TO INSPECT DOES NOT MAKE THE STRUCTURE SAFE.

Later entries show that inspection continued.

### Story Meaning

Security and maintenance needs came into conflict.

Someone made and preserved an argument for continued access.

The records do not prove who ultimately accepted it.

## The Last Log

> Temporary support left in place.
>
> Permanent replacement did not arrive.
>
> Next shift informed.

Below, in another hand:

> No next shift.

There is no hidden code.

The words explain why a temporary arrangement lasted for years.

## Optional Present Conflict: Sign It Off

If a current salvage crew or companion wants to use the route immediately, they may ask the party to declare it safe before all checks are complete.

They are not necessarily reckless. They may be cold, exhausted, or carrying something difficult.

Possible compromises:

- Test a light load.
- Restrict use to one person at a time.
- Keep the jack installed.
- Assign someone to observe.
- Use the longer route for cargo.
- Record the remaining uncertainty.

The party can provide a bounded answer instead of absolute reassurance.

## Route to Level 16

The maintenance return joins before the BX Security Junction.

It may provide:

- A chance to hear activity before entering.

>

> “This access route has been assessed.”

## Return Payoff

The gantry holds as expected.

Someone checks the party’s new mark before crossing.

If the brace was removed, the warning remains visible.

If the spare was recovered, the broken-wing crossing may now have a better support without weakening this route.

## Connection to Level 16

The party enters the final confrontation having practiced:

- Distinguishing a warning from a complete diagnosis.
- Acting with incomplete information.
- Taking precautions.
- Preserving other people’s ability to choose.
- Recording what remains uncertain.

Those experiences give them concrete answers when the remnant later insists that fear of a possible future justifies certainty.
