# Lower Departure Hangar

**Campaign:** Chronicles of the Gatekeeper — Cato Neimoidia  
**Suggested placement:** Optional investigation near the end of the palace visit or as a brief branch before Level 16  
**Suggested length:** One session, approximately 2–4 hours  
**Map status:** Proposed new map  
**Content status:** New homebrew expansion built around Tacker’s established account of Warde departing  
**Primary themes:** Departure, witness perspective, unfinished choices, and the limits of physical evidence

## Session Premise

Tacker described Warde leaving through a lower entrance and departing in his starfighter.

The hangar gives that account a physical setting.

The fighter is gone.

The party finds the infrastructure around the departure:

- An empty berth.
- Maintenance equipment.
- A damaged flight-control booth.
- Local access records.
- A sheltered place where someone could have seen a departing pilot.
- A launch opening filled with fog.

The strongest revelation is not a hidden coordinate.

It is the realization that Warde left a room full of consequences and passed through another ordinary place where he could still have spoken, waited, or asked for help.

The chapter does not establish which of those possibilities he considered.

## Continuity Boundaries

- Warde’s departure comes from Tacker’s account.
- The hangar’s detailed layout is new homebrew.
- The starfighter is absent.
- No intact log supplies Moraband’s coordinates automatically.
- A local launch heading is not a complete hyperspace route.
- The hangar contains no message explaining Warde’s entire later life.
- It does not establish that the apparition in Level 16 is physically Warde.
- It does not replace Durael’s crystal or the gatekeeper’s onward revelation.

## Story Hooks

### Follow Tacker’s Account

The party wants to inspect the lower entrance and determine whether the layout fits his recollection.

### A Service Directory

The anchor route includes a maintenance connection to the departure facilities.

### After the Final Hall

The party asks where Warde went immediately after the confrontation.

### A Practical Exit

The hangar provides shelter and a possible cargo-handling route, but no conveniently waiting starship.

## Map Layout

Include:

- A broad launch opening into fog.
- One primary fighter berth.
- A secondary service or cargo position.
- A damaged control booth.
- Maintenance alcoves.
- Tool storage.
- A fuel or power-service connection appropriate to the setting.
- A pedestrian entrance.
- A protected observation position near that entrance.
- A cargo hoist or suspended handling rail.
- A maintenance exit back toward the palace route.

Keep the hangar suspended beneath the bridge-city.

No runway on a canyon floor.

## The Hangar’s Ordinary History

- Mechanics marked safe tool positions.
- A stool has one repaired foot.
- A hand lamp hangs beside an inspection checklist.
- Someone added a shelf within easy reach of a common service point.
- A launch warning has been repainted over older lettering.
- A small cabinet contains mismatched fasteners.
- An empty food wrapper sits behind a console.
- A maintenance log contains complaints about late departures.

This was a workplace before it became evidence.

## Investigation Goal

The party can test three questions:

1. Could Tacker have seen the person and fighter from his stated position?
2. Did the primary berth support a departure during the relevant period?
3. What can the remaining local record actually establish?

The party should leave with corroboration and limits.

## Area One: The Witness Position

A protected recess near the pedestrian entrance offers a view of:

- The primary berth.
- The route from the interior.
- Part of the launch opening.

It does not show every corner of the hangar.

### Player Activity

Place a token in the recess.

Ask:

- Can it see the pilot approach?
- Where would a service arm block the view?
- How long is the route between the interior door and berth?
- Where could two people briefly look at each other?

### Conclusion

The layout may support Tacker’s described encounter.

It cannot prove his exact remembered expression or what Warde intended to ask.

## Area Two: The Empty Berth

### Visible Evidence

- Landing-position guides.
- Wear from a craft using the berth.
- Retracted service connections.
- A completed local disconnect state.
- Later dust and debris.

### Important Limit

Do not describe ancient exhaust marks as an exact timestamp or unique identification without supporting records.

### Optional Supporting Record

A local service entry associates the berth with Republic use during the relevant occupation period.

The individual craft identifier is damaged.

This supports context rather than proving every detail.

## Puzzle: The Last Local Cycle

The control booth contains three incomplete records:

1. Service disconnect.
2. Launch-clearance request.
3. External tracking interval.

Their clocks drifted differently after power interruptions.

Players compare the event order using shared sequence numbers rather than treating the displayed times as perfectly synchronized.

### Clues

- A maintenance note warns that the external tracker clock ran late.
- The service record references the launch request’s sequence number.
- The tracking record begins after the launch opening clears.
- An unrelated later diagnostic has a newer date but no actual departure.

### Resolution

The party can identify the relevant departure sequence and separate it from later automatic tests.

### What It Establishes

- A craft departed from the primary berth.
- Local service connections were disengaged.
- Tracking continued briefly outside the hangar.
- The record ends before any reliable interstellar destination is established.

### Character-Based Solution

An Average Computers, Mechanics, or appropriate navigation-related check can organize the sequence.

Failure leaves the records available for later work.

### If Tacker’s Direction Is Known

The surviving local tracking fragment is consistent with his reported departure direction.

Treat this as corroboration.

Do not convert “consistent with” into an independent proof of Moraband.

## Area Three: The Maintenance Alcove

A routine service card remains clipped near the berth.

**New handout:**

> BEFORE DEPARTURE:
>
> Confirm service disconnect.
>
> Confirm launch path.
>
> Record unresolved faults.
>
> Notify ground support if assistance is required.

A blank assistance field sits beneath it.

This is an ordinary procedure.

It does not prove Warde consciously considered asking for help because the form existed.

It makes that possibility physically understandable.

## Optional Force Impression

A Force-sensitive character who deliberately examines the route from the interior may experience:

- Forward movement.
- Physical pain or strain without a clear diagnosis.
- Awareness of another person nearby.
- A pause.
- Movement continuing.

This echoes Tacker’s testimony without revealing Warde’s private thoughts.

### Read Aloud

> The impression reaches the berth.
>
> For a moment, attention turns back toward the entrance.
>
> There is someone there.
>
> Then the movement continues.

Do not add dialogue that Tacker never heard.

## Present Encounter: The Suspended Load

A cargo assembly hangs from an old handling rail.

Recent power restoration or physical disturbance has released its holding mechanism.

The load begins to drift toward a service platform.

### Warning

> The hook above you turns.
>
> A brake clicks twice.
>
> The suspended assembly moves a hand’s width.
>
> Then another.

The threatened area is visible.

### Objectives

- Keep people out of the path.
- Secure or lower the load.
- Protect useful equipment.
- Keep the exit clear.

### Solutions

- Engage the manual brake.
- Lower the load to its marked support frame.
- Tether it from a reliable anchor.
- Isolate the handling rail and secure the mechanism.
- Use suitable equipment or established Force abilities.

### Checks

Average Mechanics for the unfamiliar brake.

Average Athletics or Coordination for a risky physical intervention.

No roll is required to step out of the clearly warned path.

### Failure

- The load blocks one route.
- A service rack is damaged.
- A character suffers a modest strain cost during recovery.
- Another control point is needed.

Do not have the first failure sweep a character into the canyon.

## Optional Encounter: Someone Wants the Berth

A small current salvage crew considers the hangar a useful staging point.

Use an existing crew if the party already encountered one.

They want:

- A dry storage area.
- Access to the cargo mechanism.
- A defensible entrance.

Their occupation may threaten evidence through ordinary cleanup rather than deliberate historical erasure.

### Opening

> “If you want those records, take copies.”
>
> “We need somewhere to put the load.”

### Choices

- Agree on a protected evidence area.
- Help make a different berth usable.
- Document the scene before work begins.
- Refuse occupation.
- Negotiate shared use.
- Ignore the issue.

This turns preservation into a practical agreement.

## A Useful Recovery

The party may recover:

- A service schematic.
- A functioning inspection lamp.
- A cargo harness.
- A local launch-sequence copy.
- A tool useful for later restoration.

There is no free starfighter.

## Warde’s Story Contribution

The hangar establishes that the palace’s story continued after the room of violence.

Warde physically left.

Other people remained with what had happened.

The empty berth also preserves uncertainty: the party can follow his departure, but it cannot read his later choices from an empty floor.

## Conversation With the Gatekeeper

If the party shows the local record:

> “This tells us he left.”
>
> “It does not tell us what he became afterward.”

If the second crystal has already restored the intended onward knowledge, the gatekeeper can connect that knowledge with the departure evidence.

Use the campaign’s established destination reveal.

## Conversation With Tacker

If the party later returns:

>
> “The lower entrance?”
>
> He listens to the answer.
>
> “Then that part is still where I remember it.”

This gives him corroboration without declaring every memory exact.

## Map Generation Notes

- Primary berth should be visibly empty.
- Include a believable pedestrian route from the palace interior.
- Give the witness recess an actual view of that route and berth.
- Keep the control booth accessible through more than one approach if practical.
- Show cargo rails and support frames clearly.
- Exterior should be fog and suspended structural silhouettes.
- No starfighter, NPCs, or apparition baked into the artwork.
- Preserve room for later tokens, cargo tiles, and evidence overlays.
