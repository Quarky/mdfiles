# 13. L15-09 — WARDE’S PREPARATION SUITE

## 13.1 Read aloud

> The eastern suite is smaller.
>
> One chair. One table. A dark communications terminal.
>
> An empty cup stands precisely beside the terminal.
>
> There is no coat on the hook.
>
> On the wall behind the table, writing covers a notation board.
>
> Most has faded or run beneath old moisture stains.
>
> One sheltered section remains legible.

Let the party choose what to inspect.

Do not immediately read the entire board before they have engaged with the room.

## 13.2 What is immediately available

- The notation board’s surviving sections.
- The table and cup.
- The chair’s position.
- A communications terminal.
- A wall clock with no current power.
- The open doorway toward the corridor.
- A service-access vestibule on the utility side.

Do not add a secret diary that explains everything. The existing board already carries considerable weight.

## 13.3 Warde’s notation board

The following preserves the central text shared by the earlier Level 15 drafts.

### Section One

> They are sincere.
>
> They believe the cease-fire will hold.
>
> They intend to honor it.
>
> The delegation believes it.
>
> The command structure does not.
>
> The gap between what the delegates believe
> and what the command structure intends
> is visible.
>
> It is not certain.
>
> It shifts every time I look at it.

### Section Two

> The future branches here.
>
> I cannot hold all of them.
>
> There are too many variables.
>
> Every time one of them moves
> I lose the shape of the rest.
>
> I am very tired.
>
> I have been very tired for a long time.
>
> I should tell someone that I am very tired.
>
> There is no one to tell.

### Section Three

The pressure of the writing changes.

> I know what I am going to do.
>
> I have known for three days.
>
> I have been trying to see a version of this
> where I do not do it.
>
> There are versions.
>
> There are always versions.
>
> I cannot make myself believe in them.

### Section Four

> I am sorry.
>
> I do not know who I am apologizing to.
>
> I am sorry anyway.

After the last section, stop speaking.

Let the players react.

## 13.4 What the board establishes

Within the campaign continuity represented by these drafts:

- Warde regarded the delegates as sincere.
- He believed other intentions threatened the cease-fire.
- He acknowledged uncertainty.
- He recognized alternative futures.
- He anticipated his own action.
- He expressed remorse before the final meeting.
- He described exhaustion and isolation.

## 13.5 What it does not establish

The board does not independently prove:

- His interpretation of Separatist command intentions was correct.
- The future compelled him.
- No one offered assistance.
- His apology was addressed to these specific PCs.
- Remorse prevented or excused the harm.
- Every mark was written in a single sitting.
- The full sequence of the violence below.

Distinguish a historical person’s statements from an omniscient account of history.

## 13.6 Physical investigation

### The writing

A careful inspection reveals different pressure and more than one writing medium.

This supports revision or return to the board.

It does not provide exact timestamps.

### The protected section

The legible area sits away from the main water path. A shallow frame lip sheltered it.

The party can photograph or transcribe it without a check.

### The cup

A thin residue remains in its base.

It is ordinary. Do not turn it into a hidden poison plot without a separate campaign reason.

### The chair

The chair faces the board.

The party can reasonably infer that someone sat where they could repeatedly look at the writing.

Do not assign a precise duration from this alone.

### The door

The door is not locked.

No physical restraint in this room establishes that Warde had to remain alone.

The freedom to leave is part of the scene.

## 13.7 Preserving the board

Offer practical choices.

| Method | Requirement | Consequence |
|---|---|---|
| Photograph | Working imaging device | Fast, portable record. |
| Transcribe | Writing material and time | Clear textual record; physical writing details may be lost. |
| Scan | Suitable equipment | Preserves more surface detail. |
| Remove board | Tools and careful handling | Bulky artifact; risks damaging fragile sections. |
| Leave untouched | None | Preserves the room but leaves evidence vulnerable to future decay. |

Removing it is an **Average Mechanics check** if the mounting is unfamiliar.

On failure, a bracket sticks. The party can stop, bring better tools, or accept damage to an already illegible edge.

Do not erase the essential confession because someone tried to preserve it.

## 13.8 The communications terminal

The terminal retains a local record of an outgoing contact attempt to the Jedi Council.

This follows the earlier drafts.

### With no power

The display is dark. The party cannot read its electronic metadata merely by looking at it.

### With power

Recover:

- The destination category.
- A local transmission entry.
- An incomplete delivery status.
- A record that the terminal was used before the conference session.

### Payload

The message content is not available in this chapter.

Do not invent a Council reply.

A successful **Average Computers check** establishes that further payload recovery would require a surviving relay copy or another source, rather than repeatedly attempting the same local extraction.

The exact contents remain a manual-review point.

## 13.9 Additional discovery: an ordinary option

A hospitality strip beside the terminal has three mechanical request buttons:

- WATER
- ATTENDANT
- POSTPONE APPOINTMENT

The last button is unpressed.

This is a new environmental addition.

It does not prove that pressing it would have saved everyone.

It establishes that the institution contained ordinary ways to pause proceedings.

### Optional companion line

> “There was a button for asking them to wait.”

Do not follow with a speech explaining the lesson.
