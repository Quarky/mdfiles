# 5. L15-01 AND L15-02 — ARRIVAL AND AWAKENING

## 5.1 Read aloud

> The dark stone of the upper landing gives way to pale, dense slabs. A narrow seam of blue runs beneath the handrail.
>
> Halfway down, an intact window reflects your lights.
>
> Beyond it, fog moves around the silhouette of a support descending past the glass. You cannot see where the support ends.
>
> The stair continues downward.
>
> At its foot, a line of unlit sconces waits along the corridor.

If the seven were released:

> The gathered presence from the chamber above has fallen away.
>
> Something remains below, distinct from what you encountered there.

Do not identify that remaining presence here.

## 5.2 The reflection window

The window contains three useful observations.

### A. The structure is suspended

A support outside carries a maintenance plate. Its directional arrow points upward toward the bridge anchorage.

Anyone looking closely can establish that the support carries the palace from above.

### B. The window was maintained

A strip along its lower edge was replaced with a slightly different material.

A small handwritten mark on the inner frame reads:

> “Still clear enough. Replace the cracked corridor panels first.”

This is a new staff detail, not a hidden Warde clue.

### C. The outside is not a usable shortcut without preparation

A maintenance rail runs below the glass, but vanishes around the outer wall.

Fog conceals the next anchor point. Condensation runs along the metal.

Characters can investigate or plan an exterior route. Tell them the visible risks before asking for a traversal roll.

## 5.3 The pressure sensor

A broad threshold plate activates the arrival sequence.

It was designed for visitors and wheeled service equipment, not as a trap.

**If the party notices it first:**

Allow inspection. A visible mechanical linkage connects it to a relay box. A character can activate it by hand.

**If they step on it:**

> A click travels beneath the floor.
>
> The first sconce brightens with a faint chemical hiss.
>
> A second follows farther down the corridor. Then another.
>
> Purple-white light reaches the atrium and opens across pale stone, dark metal, and a floor threaded with gold.
>
> Near your feet, one fixture flickers twice and stays dim.

Pause and ask:

> “What do you look at first?”

## 5.4 Practical discoveries

No check is required to notice the uneven fixture.

An **Average Mechanics check** can identify:

- The chemical lamps are separate from the main electrical supply.
- Activation uses a small control system.
- One return line is restricted.
- The lights can function while terminals remain dead.

On failure, the character still locates a service symbol directing them toward the works. They simply cannot diagnose the problem from here.

**Suggested Advantage:** Identify which lamp bank is affected.

**Suggested Threat:** The inspection releases a sharp odor and exposes a minor leak. It becomes an obvious issue to repair, not immediate damage.
