# 8. L15-05 — THE WESTERN PREPARATION SUITE

## 8.1 Read aloud

> Six chairs surround a low table.
>
> A seventh, folded, rests beside a wall cabinet.
>
> Papers lie under a smooth stone near the center. Six cups remain on a tray; a smaller seventh cup stands by the chair nearest the door.
>
> A coat hangs from a hook.
>
> The arrangement feels temporary. People set these things down expecting to use them again.

The extra chair and cup reconcile the existing delegation count with the original room description.

## 8.2 Pelri Dass’s stone

Preserve the stone as an ordinary personal object.

- Smooth.
- Palm-sized.
- Worn by handling.
- No inherent Force power.
- No hidden mechanical key.

If the party learned its ownership above, recognition is immediate.

Otherwise, a nearby preparation sheet has a small note:

> “Pelri — your stone is holding the amendments again.”

The note establishes ownership without requiring psychic knowledge.

### Possible choices

**Leave it:**  
The party preserves the room as found.

**Take it for return:**  
Record who carries it and what they intend to do.

**Use it as evidence:**  
It helps connect the Level 14 impression to the physical preparation room.

**Treat it as valuable loot:**  
It has negligible monetary value. Let that fact emerge plainly.

Do not impose supernatural punishment for moving it.

## 8.3 The delegation’s work

The papers are damaged but contain several readable proposed clauses.

**New handout:**

> WORKING TERMS — NOT RATIFIED
>
> Civilian movement to resume through designated corridors.
>
> Medical consignments to be inspected without indefinite detention.
>
> Disputed prisoner identities to be reviewed by representatives of both parties.
>
> Local utility repairs to proceed during the suspension of hostilities.
>
> Implementation schedule to be agreed in session.

### What the papers reveal

The delegates prepared substantive terms addressing ordinary consequences of war.

The text does not prove every term would have succeeded.

A character familiar with administration, negotiation, or wartime logistics can recognize practical details without a check.

An **Average Knowledge (Education) check**, or another relevant approach, can reveal:

- The proposals account for specific implementation problems.
- Several changes would require concessions from both sides.
- These are working documents, not a finished propaganda statement.

**Suggested Advantage:** Identify an issue that directly echoes a person or hardship already encountered in the ventral city.

Only make that connection if the earlier campaign established it.

## 8.5 A private datapad

The original drafts include a locked personal datapad.

Expand it as an optional privacy and evidence choice.

### Visible without unlocking

Its outer sleeve contains a paper label:

> “Personal correspondence. Working draft copied to shared folder.”

The cease-fire evidence is already available on the table.

### Options

- Preserve the device unopened.
- Attempt an **Average Computers check** to access surviving data.
- Use a specialist tool or legitimate credential found later.
- Return it to an identified recipient if one is eventually established.

### Successful access

Recover three fragments:

1. A mundane packing reminder.
2. An unfinished message about the negotiations.
3. An appointment the writer expected to keep after returning home.

**New text for the unfinished message:**

> “We argued over the corridor wording again. I think we finally have something they can accept.”
>
> “I know you dislike it when I promise a return date. I have not promised one.”
>
> “Keep the evening free anyway.”

This is new homebrew characterization, not proof of all external military intentions.

### Failed access

The reader cannot reconstruct the file directory with available tools. The pad remains intact.

Suggested Threat: a read attempt drains its remaining emergency cell; further work requires external power.

Do not destroy the files merely because the first attempt failed.

## 8.6 The room’s absent seventh person

The folding chair offers a small reconstruction puzzle.

The public ledger lists seven delegates. Six chairs and cups are immediately apparent. The seventh cup is smaller and separately placed.

Clues:

- The folding chair has scuff marks matching a space beside the door.
- A preparation sheet includes seven separate initial marks.
- The pantry request says: “One guest prefers to stand during briefing; retain seat.”

**Resolution:** Seven people used the suite. One moved or stood during preparation.

**Purpose:** Establish that physical scenes require investigation before conclusions. The room is not evidence that the seventh delegate was absent or an impostor.

No reward requires solving this. It trains the method the party will need below.
