# 12. SECOND PUZZLE: WHO GETS THE REMAINING POWER?

This is a resource decision rather than a riddle with one correct answer.

Use it after the works are stabilized, or when the party inspects the service bus.

## 12.1 The capacity

The restored bus safely supports **three load units**.

These abstract units are an encounter device, not an official Star Wars equipment specification.

| Circuit | Load | Benefit |
|---|---:|---|
| Reception | 1 | Powers the reception droid and public directory. |
| Suite terminals | 2 | Allows deeper examination of surviving local records. |
| Drain pump | 2 | Dries the maintenance return near the lower stair. |
| Public information displays | 1 | Restores room names and visitor notices. |

Chemical sconces are not on this electrical budget.

## 12.2 The decision

The party can run:

- Reception and terminals.
- Reception and pump.
- Displays and terminals.
- Displays and pump.
- Reception and displays, with one unit unused.

They can switch circuits and perform tasks sequentially.

**Do not manufacture urgency to prevent this.** Planning an efficient sequence is a successful solution.

If there is already an active patrol or environmental problem, that existing situation may make the order matter.

## 12.3 Information available before choosing

The panel states:

> “Disconnect before reassignment.”
>
> “Stored data unaffected by ordinary power interruption.”
>
> “Reception unit requires notice before shutdown.”

The droid asks for time to finish its current annotation before power is removed.

The terminals do not erase themselves merely because the party chooses another circuit.

## 12.4 Overloading the bus

The load gauge shows the overload before activation.

If the party proceeds anyway, a breaker trips. Resetting it takes a few minutes.

A skilled character can propose an upgrade using carried equipment. Reward plausible engineering rather than insisting the puzzle remain unsolved.

## 12.5 Why this scene matters

People maintained the palace by deciding what needed attention first.

The party now does the same.

The question is practical:

- Speak to the surviving administrator?
- Recover more records?
- Make the route safer?
- Restore public information for whoever comes next?

None of these priorities is secretly immoral.
