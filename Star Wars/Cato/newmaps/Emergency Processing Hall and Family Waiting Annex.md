# Emergency Processing Hall and Family Waiting Annex

**Campaign:** Chronicles of the Gatekeeper — Cato Neimoidia  
**Suggested placement:** Before Level 15, connected to the diplomatic quarter or a lateral ventral route  
**Suggested length:** One session, approximately 3–4 hours  
**Map status:** Proposed new map  
**Content status:** New homebrew expansion  
**Primary themes:** Detention, incomplete records, institutional responsibility, and families seeking answers

## Session Premise

The party discovers a public hall converted into an emergency processing center during the occupation.

Its original purpose was practical: register arrivals, identify missing people, organize temporary accommodation, and connect families with public offices.

Security procedures gradually took over its spaces.

Benches became holding areas. Private interview screens became barriers. A room intended to help people move through the city became a place where they waited for permission to move at all.

The party arrives because the records may clarify what Warde’s authority meant outside the battlefield.

Someone living in the ventral city also needs an answer from those records.

The session should produce one meaningful piece of information, one consequential choice, and a clearer understanding of how a person could disappear into a process without everyone involved understanding what happened.

## Story Hooks

### A Family’s Question

Vessa Tann asks the party to look for a name: **Keth Anno**.

Keth was a relative of someone in her community. The family’s last document lists a detention number but no final destination.

Vessa does not demand rescue or claim Keth must still be alive.

> “We have had three explanations.”
>
> “I would settle for one piece of paper that means what it says.”

### A Number Already Found

If the party recovered copied requests in SP16, one carries the processing hall’s office stamp.

The hall offers a place to connect the number to a later record.

### Tacker’s Incomplete List

If Tacker’s list is already part of the campaign, several entries mention this office.

Do not have the hall resolve his entire notebook. It contains one surviving administrative branch, not a perfect duplicate of military records.

### A Practical Shortcut

The service exit once connected to a staff circulation route.

The party may enter simply to inspect that route and discover the human story while searching.

## Map Layout

Include:

- A broad public entrance.
- Intake desks with enough circulation space for queues.
- A waiting area with fixed benches.
- Temporary partitions visibly added after construction.
- A records counter and secure file room.
- Two interview rooms.
- A small staff rest area.
- A baggage cage.
- A family waiting annex.
- A concealed overflow space behind service partitions.
- A staff exit providing an alternate route.

The visual story should show conversion over time.

Permanent architecture is formal and civic. Later barriers are cheaper, mismatched, and easier to remove.

## Arrival

> The entrance opens into a public hall.
>
> Its decorative floor continues beneath rows of temporary barriers.
>
> Above the intake desks, an old sign reads:
>
> ARRIVALS AND ASSISTANCE.
>
> A newer panel covers the word “assistance.”
>
> On the benches, faded numbers mark places where people were expected to sit.
>
> Someone scratched a name beside one of them.

Give the party the visible choices: desks, benches, annex, interview rooms, and staff door.

## The Hall’s Living Details

Use a few rather than reading the entire list.

- A desk drawer contains different sizes of writing grips.
- One bench has been repaired where a leg was shortened to level it.
- A faded instruction asks staff to speak clearly to people using translation devices.
- A cup beneath the counter contains replacement styluses.
- Someone copied the office hours onto the outside wall so people could read them before entering.
- A queue barrier has cloth tied around a sharp edge.
- A staff notice complains that the baggage cage key is never returned.
- An improvised child’s game is scratched into a bench.

These details establish workers and visitors as people with habits, not anonymous components of an evil machine.

## Key NPC: Vessa Tann

Use Vessa if she accompanies the party. Otherwise, she supplies the name and remains outside.

**Immediate goal:** Obtain a reliable answer about Keth’s administrative fate.

**What she knows:**

- The family name.
- An old detention number.
- That the family received contradictory explanations.
- Which current resident should receive any answer.

**What she does not know:**

- Whether Keth survived after leaving custody.
- Who personally approved the detention.
- Whether the last explanation was a lie or a clerical mistake.

If the party finds something ambiguous:

> “Tell me what it proves.”
>
> “Then tell me what you think.”

## Area One: The Family Waiting Annex

### Read Aloud

> The annex is smaller and less formal.
>
> Benches face a closed service window.
>
> A blanket has been folded into the corner of one seat.
>
> On the wall, a list explains which documents to bring.
>
> Someone added:
>
> “Bring food if you can. They may not call you today.”

### Discoveries

Searching reveals:

- A copied request for Keth’s location.
- A baggage receipt.
- A correction to his detention number.
- A note indicating a hearing accommodation.
- A separate request from an unrelated family.

The unrelated request matters because Keth’s case was not the only life passing through this room.

### Keth’s Family Note

> REQUEST FOR LOCATION CONFIRMATION
>
> Keth Anno.
>
> Detention reference attached.
>
> Requires assistance hearing on the left side.
>
> Please send the answer to the family address as well as the district office.
>
> The district office says it has received nothing.

## Area Two: The Interview Rooms

One room is orderly.

The other contains a broken privacy screen and a chair repaired with a metal brace.

### What the Records Show

An early interview instruction says:

> Record observed behavior separately from suspected intention.

A later form combines both into one field:

> SECURITY CONCERN.

This is a concrete change in process.

It does not prove every interviewer used the field irresponsibly.

### Warde’s Connection

A routing sheet states:

> REVIEW UNDER COMMAND AUTHORITY: WARDE.

The surviving document shows where the case was routed.

It does not show a personal reply from him.

### Optional Force Impression

A Force-sensitive character who deliberately examines the room may sense:

- Repeated explanations.
- Frustration at not being understood.
- Relief when someone finally repeats a name correctly.

No clear vision identifies a hidden culprit.

## Puzzle: The Number Changed

### The Problem

Keth appears under two different detention references.

One is a temporary intake number. The other is a permanent case number.

The family copied the first onto later requests.

The party must connect:

1. The intake record.
2. The interview record.
3. The transfer record.

### Available Clues

| Document | Matching detail |
|---|---|
| Intake slip | Family address and temporary number. |
| Interview sheet | Hearing accommodation and corrected number. |
| Baggage receipt | Both numbers, with one crossed out. |
| Transfer list | Corrected number and later processing status. |

### Resolution

The records establish that Keth was transferred to a release-processing office.

A surviving completion slip shows that the transfer was closed as **released from custody**.

The later civilian destination is not recorded.

This is a real answer with a real limit.

### Character-Based Resolution

An Average Knowledge, Computers, or appropriate administrative check can organize the documents.

Success identifies the match.

Failure means more time or another corroborating document is needed. The essential papers remain available.

### If Players Solve It Directly

No check is required merely to confirm their correct comparison.

## Area Three: The Baggage Cage

Keth’s baggage claim was never collected at this office.

The cage contains ordinary abandoned property:

- A repaired outer garment.
- A sealed container of family documents.
- A small household tool.
- A set of worn travel straps.

Do not turn every item into valuable loot.

### The Useful Discovery

A baggage-return instruction states:

> Property remained at intake while person was transferred.
>
> Collection notice sent to district office.

The family never received the notice.

This explains one source of uncertainty without inventing a grand cover-up.

## Encounter: Who Gets the Records?

A small salvage pair arrives through the staff entrance.

They are stripping storage housings and usable power components.

They are not looking for Keth and do not understand the records’ importance.

### Opening

> “We’re taking the cabinets.”
>
> “You can have the paper if you move it.”

### What They Want

- Salvage they can carry.
- A clear route.
- No fight over material they consider abandoned.

### Available Resolutions

- Help separate records from reusable housings.
- Trade another salvage opportunity.
- Ask them to transport the records.
- Refuse access and defend the archive.
- Take only the relevant papers and leave the rest.
- Mark the records for later collection.

A failed social check can mean they demand help or payment. It need not trigger combat.

### If Violence Occurs

Use existing suitable scavenger profiles.

Their objective is escape with salvage, not death in an archive.

## A Small Mechanical Problem

The archive shutters have closed unevenly.

Opening both shutters simultaneously overloads their weak shared drive.

The manual mechanism allows one to be opened and pinned before the other moves.

Clues are visible:

- A maintenance instruction.
- A retaining pin.
- Wear around the manual crank.

An Average Mechanics check can handle the procedure.

Forcing the shutter may bend a track and remove the convenient exit, but should not destroy every record inside.

## Session Resolution

The party can leave with:

- A documented release from custody.
- An unresolved later destination.
- Keth’s unclaimed property.
- Evidence of administrative failure.
- A decision about the larger archive.

### Vessa’s Response

> “Released.”
>
> She repeats the word once.
>
> “We can tell them that.”
>
> “We can stop telling them he never came out.”

She does not immediately turn the answer into certainty that Keth lived happily afterward.

## Return and Living-World Consequences

If records were preserved, later residents begin identifying other cases.

If only one file was taken, that answer still matters.

If the archive was stripped, the party knows what information became harder to recover.

If the salvage pair helped, they may become practical allies rather than enemies.

## Link to Warde’s Story

This location shows how authority persists through forms, routing decisions, and unanswered reviews.

It gives the party a reason to distinguish Warde’s direct actions from everything done under his name.

It also makes his later insistence on certainty more troubling: people had already been living with the consequences of uncertain judgments.
