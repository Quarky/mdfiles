# SP17 — Flooded Service Cavern: The Part That Never Arrived

**Suggested placement:** After SP16 or directly from Level 15’s service works  
**Session length:** 3–4 hours  
**Primary encounters:** Rescue, engineering, cargo recovery, optional negotiation  
**Story purpose:** Connect the cease-fire’s utility clauses to a problem still affecting living residents

## The Story

Rennik Sol came to recover a regulator from a decommissioned auxiliary unit.

He found it.

Then a damaged outlet caused the chamber’s water level to rise, cutting off the route he used.

Rennik is alive on a stable platform with the regulator and his tools.

The party must decide how to get him out, whether to recover the heavy component, and how much of the chamber to restore.

The historical records reveal that this same category of repair once stalled because transport authorization never arrived.

## Hooks

- Vessa asks the party to find Rennik.
- Nemi leads them to his route marks.
- The Repair Court needs the regulator.
- SP10’s service diagram references this chamber.
- The party follows a repeating maintenance signal.

## Arrival

> Water covers the lower machine housings.
>
> Catwalks link a control station, a storage recess, and a platform on the far side.
>
> A hand lamp moves.
>
> “Use the upper route,” someone calls.
>
> “The lower ladder came away.”
>
> Beside the speaker, a heavy component is strapped into a carrying frame.

## Rennik Sol

A Neimoidian repairer with a clear understanding of the component and an incomplete understanding of the changed chamber.

He is embarrassed, but not foolish.

He left route marks, an estimated return time, and enough supplies for a delay.

### Dialogue

> “The extraction went well.”
>
> He looks at the water.
>
> “The return plan did not.”

## Objectives

The party can pursue these separately:

1. Rescue Rennik.
2. Recover the regulator.
3. Recover tools.
4. Restore a useful route.
5. Inspect the maintenance record.
6. Stabilize the chamber.

Rescuing him does not require accomplishing all six.

## Chamber Logic

- Leaks and retained drainage have filled the lower chamber.
- An automatic sump release will add more water.
- A damaged outlet prevents normal clearing.
- Upper platforms remain safe.
- Lower access will become harder to use.
- The whole palace is not about to flood.

## Visible Timing

A mechanical indicator advances toward the next release.

Explain the situation plainly:

> “You have time to inspect the chamber and carry out a plan. A prolonged search or major dismantling job may run past the next release.”

Advance time for significant actions, not player discussion about what their characters can see.

## Encounter One: Reach Rennik

### Upper Catwalk

A bent section needs bracing or careful crossing.

Average Mechanics can stabilize it.

Average Athletics or Coordination can resolve an uncertain crossing.

### Tethered Transfer

A line can move Rennik separately from the regulator.

Reliable anchors are visible on both platforms.

### Lower Route

Clearing drainage may expose the lower access again.

### Character Abilities

Suitable repulsor equipment, climbing devices, droids, and established Force abilities can provide alternatives.

## Puzzle: Hold, Drain, or Transfer

The control station offers:

- HOLD NEXT RELEASE.
- MANUAL DRAIN.
- NORMAL CYCLE.

A damaged interlock prevents holding the release while the manual drain runs.

### Clues

- A diagram shows the incoming release and outgoing drain.
- Debris near the outlet moves when the drain starts.
- The lower route lies across that current.
- The upper transfer avoids the water.

### Safe Plan A

1. Hold the release.
2. Secure an upper transfer.
3. Move Rennik.
4. Move the component.
5. Leave the chamber in a documented safe state.

### Safe Plan B

1. Clear the outlet.
2. Start manual drain.
3. Wait for the lower route to become usable.
4. Stop the drain before crossing its intake path.
5. Move people and cargo.

Other plausible plans are valid.

## Complication: The Carrying Frame Catches

The regulator’s frame catches on a bent rail.

The party can:

- Rotate it using a second line.
- Remove a nonessential handle.
- Lower it onto a stable platform.
- Disassemble the frame around the component.
- Leave the regulator temporarily and rescue Rennik first.

The component is valuable but replaceable. It is not the campaign’s quest crystal.

## Failure Consequences

- A tool drops into shallow, visible water.
- A line becomes caught.
- A character is soaked and suffers 2 strain.
- The release begins before cargo is moved.
- The lower route closes, requiring the upper route.

Avoid making one failed check kill Rennik or destroy the only possible solution.

## Historical Discovery: Approved in Principle

A protected maintenance sleeve contains:

> REPLACEMENT APPROVED IN PRINCIPLE.
>
> TRANSPORT AUTHORIZATION PENDING.
>
> CIVILIAN REPAIR WINDOW SUBJECT TO SECURITY CONDITIONS.

A later handwritten note says:

> “The part exists.”
>
> “The route is the problem.”

## Connection to the Peace Talks

The party may recognize the same problems addressed by the delegation’s utility-repair and civilian-movement clauses.

If they have not yet read those papers, this clue prepares the connection.

Rennik says:

> “People think a machine fails because the right part does not exist.”
>
> “Sometimes it fails because the part is somewhere else.”

## Optional Maintenance Droid

A dormant utility droid occupies the cradle bay.

It contains:

- A local schematic.
- A record of deferred maintenance.
- No recording of the massacre.
- A damaged mobility assembly.

Restoring it requires suitable power and an Average Mechanics check if the connection is damaged.

Its first question:

> “Has inspection access been restored?”

The schematic can be recovered without fully repairing the droid.

## Optional Social Encounter: Another Need

If the party needs a larger complication, a second repair crew arrives seeking the same regulator.

Their own system also needs work.

They can be negotiated with through:

- An alternate component.
- Shared repair labor.
- A loan arrangement.
- An honest comparison of urgency.
- A second recovery operation later.

Do not make them secretly malicious merely to simplify the choice.

## Resolution

Rennik checks the regulator after recovery.

Then he counts his tools.

Then he checks the people.

> “All right.”
>
> “Now we get it home.”

The Repair Court provides the later payoff.
