# SP18 — Observation Spire: The Second Look

**Suggested placement:** Optional branch from SP15; possible service connection toward SP19  
**Session length:** 2–3 hours  
**Primary encounters:** Observation puzzle, uncertain contact, optional social intrigue  
**Story purpose:** Let the party experience how incomplete information becomes dangerous certainty

## The Story

The observation spire once monitored local traffic, weather, and structural conditions.

Its scope still works imperfectly.

One fractured viewing panel creates a reflected light that appears to move outside the structure.

A historical report shows a similar problem of interpretation: an uncertain observation became an unqualified command summary.

The party also sees a current figure carrying a long object through the fog.

They must decide whether to observe, investigate, signal, hide, or act.

The encounter should present uncertainty honestly. It should not punish the players for believing a misleadingly certain GM description.

## Hooks

- A scope might reveal whether the next service route is intact.
- A maintenance signal repeats from the anchor cavern.
- Nemi mentions that the spire provides a view of nearby connections.
- The party wants to check whether someone follows it.
- An old report references observation from this station.

## Arrival

> A narrow room projects beyond the surrounding structure.
>
> Fog presses against damaged viewing panels.
>
> A fixed scope points through one intact section.
>
> Beside the eyepiece, someone scratched:
>
> WAIT FOR THE SECOND CLEARING.
>
> On the instrument shelf lies a notebook with a page held down by a broken calibration weight.

## Original Function

The spire supported:

- Local traffic observation.
- Weather reporting.
- Inspection of nearby supports.
- Communication with maintenance crews.

It was not a complete military surveillance center.

## Everyday Details

- The observer’s chair has been repaired twice.
- A footrest sits at a different height from the original fitting.
- Someone marked a preferred focus setting.
- An old cup fits perfectly into an unofficial bracket.
- A notebook contains weather complaints alongside measurements.
- One page records a maintenance crew’s successful return.

The operators watched ordinary work as well as danger.

## Puzzle: The Third Light

Three apparent reference lights are visible intermittently.

Only two are outside.

The third is a reflection from a damaged interior indicator.

### Clues

- The reflected light moves when the observer shifts position.
- It appears only through one part of the cracked panel.
- The notebook warns of a duplicate under certain angles.
- Two fixed structural references maintain the same relative position.
- Covering the interior indicator removes the false light.

### Solution

Identify the reflection, align the scope using actual fixed references, and wait for a useful clearing.

### Character-Based Resolution

Average Perception or Mechanics can calibrate the instrument.

Failure produces a low-confidence result and a clear reason for uncertainty. It does not give a confident false answer unless the character knowingly ignores the warning.

## Useful Current Observation

Choose the result most relevant to the party’s route:

- A service door remains accessible.
- A walkway has lost its outer rail.
- A maintenance bridge is occupied.
- An anchor-cavern indicator is flashing.
- A patrol uses a visible crossing.
- A supposed moving threat is stationary equipment shifting in the wind.

The observation should change an available choice.

## Encounter: The Figure in the Fog

The party sees a person carrying a long object.

Describe only what is visible:

> A figure crosses the distant platform.
>
> Something long rests against one shoulder.
>
> The fog hides the lower half of the body.
>
> You cannot yet tell whether the object is a weapon or equipment.

### Default Truth

The figure is Nemi or an established worker carrying a repair section.

If that would contradict current positions, use an unnamed worker.

### Options

- Wait for a clearer view.
- Signal.
- Use communication equipment.
- Follow by a known route.
- Take cover.
- Prepare a precaution without attacking.
- Fire, accepting that the target is unidentified.

Do not secretly change the figure into an attacker because the players were cautious.

## Historical Record

The original observation says:

> OBSERVATION CONFIDENCE: LIMITED.
>
> Intermittent movement beyond eastern support.
>
> Number and identity unconfirmed.
>
> Recommend second observation before classification.

A forwarded summary says:

> MOVEMENT DETECTED BEYOND EASTERN SUPPORT.

Its uncertainty is gone.

A routing stamp indicates it was sent to Warde’s command office.

## What the Evidence Means

The party can establish:

- An observer reported uncertainty.
- A later summary omitted it.
- The summary was routed to a command office.

They cannot establish from this alone:

- Who removed the qualification.
- Whether Warde read it.
- Whether the movement was hostile.
- Whether this report caused a particular action.

The discovery becomes powerful when compared with Warde’s own insistence on predicted threats.

## Optional Encounter: The Observer

If the previously established Syndicate observer remains active, they use the spire to watch routes.

They are surprised to find the party here.

### Opening

> “You can see more from this room than from the walkway.”
>
> “Not enough, usually.”

### Goals

- Learn the party’s route.
- Avoid being trapped.
- Preserve their cover.
- Possibly trade information.

### Negotiation

They can offer one true observation.

In return, they ask a limited question:

> “Are you returning through the broken wing?”

The party can answer, refuse, lie, bargain, or follow them.

A failed check might reveal evasiveness without revealing their employer.

## Environmental Complication

A viewing panel flexes in the wind.

The warning is visible: its seal pulls away and the frame vibrates.

The party can:

- Move back.
- Secure the interior shutter.
- Continue using a different panel.
- Risk a brief observation.

Failure damages the instrument’s convenient position or forces retreat from the window.

Do not turn a routine observation into an unavoidable fall.

## Reward

The party leaves with:

- One reliable observation.
- Better route knowledge.
- A calibrated instrument.
- A historical example of lost uncertainty.
- Possible information about the observer.

## Return Visit

If the scope was repaired, others can use it.

A new note may appear:

> REFLECTION COVERED.
>
> CHECK TWO REFERENCES BEFORE REPORTING.

The party’s method becomes part of the place.
