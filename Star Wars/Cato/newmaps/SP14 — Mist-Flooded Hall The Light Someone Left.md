# SP14 — Mist-Flooded Hall: The Light Someone Left

**Suggested placement:** A lateral branch from the diplomatic quarter or Level 15 service route  
**Session length:** 2–3 hours  
**Primary encounters:** Social investigation, environmental problem, optional confrontation  
**Connections:** Palace service corridor; SP15 Collapsed Cliffside Gallery  
**Story purpose:** Introduce the living people who still depend on the palace’s neglected spaces

## The Story

The Mist-Flooded Hall once received visitors waiting between appointments.

During the occupation, it became an overflow waiting area for families seeking information about detained relatives. After the palace was abandoned, occasional travelers used its sheltered interior wall as a resting place.

Now Vessa Tann’s salvage group maintains a small temporary shelter here.

Their lamp has disappeared.

That sounds insignificant beside the party’s investigation into Warde, but the lamp marked the safe route through the fog. Without it, someone unfamiliar with the hall could follow the wrong doorway toward a collapsed landing.

Vessa suspects another scavenger took it.

The truth is more complicated: an injured traveler moved the lamp to warn people about a new breach. The person then retreated into a service alcove and could not return it.

The session begins with an accusation and becomes an investigation into what people actually did.

## Continuity

- The shelter is temporary and accessed from the ventral city.
- Its occupants have not lived beside the final meeting hall for fifteen years.
- They have not found the quest crystal.
- Vessa’s knowledge is practical and local.
- The hall’s historical notices are new environmental evidence, not a complete account of Warde’s command.
- This chapter can introduce the request to find Rennik in SP17.

## Session Hooks

### The Missing Route Light

Vessa asks whether the party passed anyone carrying a lamp.

> “It was tied above the safe turn.”
>
> “Whoever took it knew what it was for.”

She believes this. The GM should not present it as established fact.

### Someone Is Overdue

Nemi has returned from checking the first crossing, but Rennik has not returned from the flooded works.

The missing lamp makes searching less safe.

### A Place to Rest

The party enters looking for a sheltered place to check equipment before continuing.

### A Historical Connection

An old waiting-area notice matches the office stamp on a document the party already carries.

## Arrival

>

>
> A cord hangs empty from a ceremonial bracket.
>

>
> A Neimoidian woman stands beside it.
>

## Key NPCs

### Vessa Tann — Route Organizer

Practical, tired, protective of shared equipment.

She wants the lamp returned, the route made safe, and her missing repairer found.

She responds well to clear plans and poorly to confident claims without evidence.

### Nemi — Courier and Climber

An adult Neimoidian who knows the first part of the route.

Nemi is willing to admit that the fog has changed since the last visit.

> “I know where the door was.”
>
> “I don’t know what is outside it now.”

### Leth Orra — Injured Traveler

**New homebrew NPC.**

An independent scavenger who moved the lamp after finding a broken landing.

Leth strained a leg while retreating. They are sheltering in an alcove with the lamp visible through a damaged grille.

Leth expects Vessa to be angry and has delayed calling out.

## The Hall’s Everyday Life

Show activity during conversation:

- Vessa shifts a container away from creeping moisture.
- Nemi counts returned fasteners.
- Someone has repaired a screen with material from two different garments.
- A cup is turned upside down over the clean water container.
- Boots are placed where dripping water will not reach them.
- A route sketch has corrections in more than one handwriting.

The shelter is a cooperative arrangement with imperfect habits.

## Encounter One: What Happened to the Lamp?

### Visible Clues

- The cord was untied, not cut.
- A fresh scrape leads toward the outer passage.
- One muddy footprint is shorter than the others, suggesting an uneven gait.
- Faint light appears intermittently through a service grille.
- A new warning mark has been scratched beside the dangerous doorway.

### Approaches

The party can:

- Follow the physical traces.
- Call out.
- Ask Nemi to identify recent route changes.
- Inspect the missing lamp’s attachment.
- Search the service alcoves.

An Average Perception or Survival check can organize unclear tracks. A sensible direct search finds the light without requiring repeated rolls.

### Discovery

> Behind the grille, someone shifts.
>
> “I didn’t take it away.”
>
> “I moved it.”
>
> “There’s no floor past the old marker.”

### Resolution

The party can:

- Help Leth out.
- Establish a new safe marker.
- Return the lamp after placing another warning.
- Ask Vessa and Leth to agree on how shared equipment may be moved.
- Ignore the dispute and continue.

No particular apology is required.

Vessa can revise her judgment:

> “You should have told us.”
>
> After looking toward the broken route:
>
> “But you were right to mark it.”

## Encounter Two: The Shelter Is Getting Wet

A damaged exterior panel redirects mist and condensation toward the supply area.

The residents cannot simply move farther inward because an old partition blocks the dry space.

### Options

- Remove the nonstructural partition.
- Adjust the cloth screens and drainage.
- Repair the panel’s interior gutter.
- Relocate the supplies.
- Leave the shelter temporarily.

With tools and time, ordinary work succeeds without a roll.

Use Average Mechanics if the party attempts a quick repair while the panel moves in the wind.

### Failure

A failed repair costs time, soaks a noncritical supply, or requires another attachment point.

It does not collapse the whole hall.

## Historical Discovery: The Waiting Room Beneath the Shelter

Moving the partition reveals an older public notice:

> FAMILIES AWAITING TRANSFER INFORMATION MAY REMAIN UNTIL THE NEXT REVIEW.
>
> DO NOT CLEAR THE ROOM SOLELY TO RESTORE CEREMONIAL APPEARANCE.

A later notice covers part of it:

> WAITING AREA CLOSED PENDING SECURITY REVIEW.

Beneath that, someone wrote:

> “Where do we wait now?”

### What It Reveals

The space changed from assistance to restriction.

The notices do not identify who personally made each decision.

If the party has Warde-related administrative records, these notices supply human context rather than a new confession.

## A Small Object

Behind a bench lies a folded paper bird made from an obsolete office form.

Its underside contains half a detention reference.

The object can become:

- A clue to compare with the processing hall.
- Something preserved as evidence of waiting families.
- A personal object whose owner remains unknown.

Do not guarantee a complete story for every artifact.

## Optional Confrontation

If the established Syndicate observer is active, Vessa notices someone examining the party’s route marks.

The observer claims to be checking safety.

They are also trying to learn where the party intends to go.

Possible outcomes:

- Exchange limited route information.
- Ask them to help repair the shelter.
- Follow them later.
- Refuse discussion.
- Expose their interest without identifying their employer.

They should not attack merely because noticed.

## Ending the Session

Vessa offers a route sketch and asks the party to check on Rennik.

> “He went for a regulator.”
>
> “The part matters.”
>
> “Bring him back first.”

## Return Visit

If the party helped:

- The dry space is larger.
- The lamp marks the correct turn.
- Leth has contributed another warning.
- Vessa keeps borrowed equipment ready for collection.

If the party exposed a false accusation, later disagreements begin with questions rather than immediate blame.
