# THE ZEL N'GOT SECTION
## A Living World Guide for Act 2 — Jorra, Cato Neimoidia

---

# PART ONE: THE RESIDENTIAL DISTRICT — UPPER LEVELS

---

## WHAT THE UPPER RESIDENTIAL DISTRICT IS

The upper residential district of Jorra occupies the middle third of the bridge-city
between the commercial district to the southwest and the factory district to the northeast.
It is the largest district by area and the most densely populated — approximately
sixty percent of Jorra's eight and a half million residents live here, in habitation
complexes that rise thirty to forty meters above the bridge surface and descend
in some cases through the bridge structure itself into the sub-bridge levels.

If the factory district announces itself through smell and sound, and the commercial
district announces itself through the density of business signage and foot traffic,
the residential district announces itself through a specific kind of domestic
complexity — the layered sound of people living in close proximity, the smell of
food preparation from ten thousand different kitchen vents, the sight of laundry
and personal objects on the balconies and exterior platforms that Neimoidians
use as extensions of their interior spaces when the weather permits.

It is, in other words, a neighborhood. Many neighborhoods, technically,
organized into loose sub-districts by the affiliation patterns of the families
that dominate each section. But from the street it reads as a single continuous
fabric of domestic life that has been interrupted — by the war, by the occupation,
by the Empire — and has continued anyway, because domestic life is harder to stop
than governance.

---

## THE HISTORY THE NEIGHBORHOOD CARRIES

### Before the Wars

The residential district was not a monolith before the Clone Wars.
It was organized around the same production house affiliations
that structured the factory district's labor — workers and their
families clustered near the facilities they were affiliated with,
creating sub-neighborhoods with distinct characters.

The northeastern residential blocks, nearest the factory district,
were working-class Neimoidian in the specific sense that the
Trade Federation used that term: comfortable, organized, with
access to the collective resources of the production houses
but without the wealth of the Trade Federation's upper echelons.
The southwestern blocks, nearest the commercial district,
were more mixed — merchants, logistics personnel, the service
workers who staffed the commercial district's businesses.

The middle sections, where N'got's building is located, were
neither one nor the other: lower-middle Neimoidian, the
kind of area where people had enough to be comfortable
and not enough to be notable. Which suited N'got perfectly,
both before the war and after it.

### During the Occupation

The residential district was where the occupation was felt most
personally. The factory district experienced the war as a
production conversion — traumatic but abstract, the violence
of policy rather than the violence of soldiers in corridors.
The commercial district experienced it as economic disruption.

The residential district experienced it as people with weapons
moving through spaces where people lived.

Warde's forces — Republic clone troopers under Jedi command —
were billeted throughout the residential district. They occupied
buildings. They established checkpoints at major intersections.
They conducted searches based on intelligence reports
that were sometimes accurate and sometimes the product of
Warde's increasingly unreliable foresight.

The residents adapted in the ways that occupied populations
always adapt: compliance in public, concealment in private,
and the development of a granular, street-level intelligence
network that the Republic forces never fully understood
because it operated through channels — family connections,
production house affiliations, neighborhood gossip — that
were invisible to outsiders.

N'got operated in this environment as a Separatist spy.
He was good at it because he was from this environment —
he understood the channels the Republic did not understand.
What he did not understand, until too late, was that
Warde's ability to foresee actions meant that some of
those invisible channels were not invisible to Warde.
N'got survived the war through a combination of careful
operational security and the fact that Warde's attention
was consumed by the cease-fire crisis before he could
follow N'got's trail to its conclusion.

### After the Wars — Living With What Happened

The residential district today is the place in Jorra where
the war's legacy is most present as lived experience
rather than architecture or economics.

In the factory district, the war left marks on walls.
In the ventral city, the war left empty buildings.
In the residential district, the war left people —
people who remember, people who lost someone,
people who did something they do not discuss,
people who were in a certain place on a certain day
and have been living with that ever since.

Sanna Olpan is here. Her son was arrested by Warde
and she does not know if he is alive.

Tieka Dalken is here. Warde saved her family and she
has been defending his memory against the neighborhood
consensus ever since.

N'got is here. He was a Separatist spy and Warde
never caught him and he has been waiting for fifteen years
for someone to come and catch him.

The neighborhood knows all three of these people exist.
It does not know all three of these stories. It has
its own stories about all of them, which are partially
accurate and mostly not.

## THE DISTRICT AT STREET LEVEL

### The Architecture

The residential district's buildings are older than the
factory district and more varied in style. The Trade Federation
period produced a standard residential architecture — curved
facades, organic balcony structures, the amber-lens window
treatment that was the Neimoidian aesthetic preference
of that era — but buildings were added and modified over
three centuries and the result is a layered visual complexity
that the factory district's standardization lacks.

The streets are narrower than the factory district's corridors —
designed for pedestrians and personal speeders rather than
cargo vehicles. The overhead cargo track network does not
extend into the residential district. What takes its place
is a network of personal speeder lanes at varying heights,
the Neimoidian solution to density being to add vertical
layers of transit rather than widen horizontal paths.

At street level the residential district is shaded for
most of the day by the buildings above, creating a
cooler, dimmer ground-level environment than the upper
bridge surface would suggest. The amber-lens windows
of the traditional buildings collect what light there is
and distribute it in warm pools that make the street-level
character of the district feel warmer than it is.

### The Population

The residential district's population is almost entirely
Neimoidian. This is not policy — it is the result of
the same clustering patterns that have always organized
Neimoidian residential choice, combined with the economic
pressure that the Empire's control of the Trade Federation's
assets has created. Non-Neimoidians can afford to live
in the district. Most choose not to, for reasons that
the Neimoidian population does not encourage examination of.

The residents of the northeastern sections — N'got's section —
are factory workers and their families, production house
affiliates who have maintained their neighborhood identity
even though the production house system no longer functions.
They know each other. They watch each other's buildings.
They are aware when something unusual happens on their block.

A party of non-Neimoidians moving through this section of
the residential district is unusual. The district sees offworlders
occasionally — Imperial personnel, trade contacts, the odd
visitor — but they move through rather than stopping,
and they do not ask questions. A group that stops and
asks questions, or that appears to be looking for
a specific building, will be noticed.

This is significant for the N'got encounter.
N'got has informants in his building's neighborhood —
not formal informants, just people who have learned
to notice things and tell him. By the time the party
locates his building, he will almost certainly know
they are coming.

## FINDING N'GOT

### The Address

Haido gives them a location: third building east of
the intersection with Service Corridor 7, residential district.

This is accurate but not as specific as it sounds.
The building in question — a twelve-story habitation
complex called the Terrath Block — is large enough
to contain approximately two hundred residential units
distributed across twelve floors. N'got has access
to several of these units. He sleeps in a different
one each night.

The building's address is publicly visible. Finding it
requires simply navigating the residential district,
which is straightforward if slightly disorienting —
the district's street layout is organic rather than
gridded and the Service Corridors (industrial access routes
that predate the residential development) run at angles
to the main streets that create irregular intersections.

An Average Streetwise or Navigation check gets the party
to the right intersection without delay. A failure adds
time — additional wandering, wrong turns — but not
permanent confusion. The district is not that large.

### What the Party Sees Approaching

The Terrath Block is a twelve-story habitation complex
from approximately eighty years before the Clone Wars —
old by Jorra standards, built in the traditional Neimoidian
residential style with amber-lens windows and curved
exterior balconies. The facade shows its age: the amber
lenses have darkened with time, the organic curves of
the balcony structures have been repaired in places
with materials that don't quite match, the lower floors
have accumulated the grime of decades in the specific
way that old urban buildings accumulate grime.

It is not a distinguished building. It is the kind of
building that has always housed the same kind of people —
workers with stable jobs and modest aspirations — and will
probably continue doing so indefinitely.

The notable thing about the Terrath Block is not visible
from the street. What is notable is above the fifth floor,
where N'got's secured territory begins: the windows on
the upper floors are less consistent than those below.
Some are boarded. Some are covered with internal panels
that do not look original to the building. One has a
piece of equipment attached to the exterior that could
be a sensor array or could be a modified ventilation unit.

A Perception check (Hard) from street level notices these
inconsistencies. Average Vigilance notices that someone
is watching from a sixth-floor window — not obviously,
not continuously, just a presence that moves and then
is not visible and then is present again.

N'got knows they are here. He has known they were coming
for approximately fifteen minutes. He has been deciding
what to do about it since he found out.

## THE TERRATH BLOCK — THE BUILDING

### Ground Floor and Lower Levels

The building's ground floor is communal — an entrance lobby,
a management office (unstaffed, the building manager
works mornings only), access to the building's shared
storage areas, the staircase and lift shaft that serves
all twelve floors.

The lobby is occupied during the day by residents passing
through. At the time the party arrives it will have
a small number of people: an older Neimoidian woman
waiting for someone, two workers still in factory clothes
from the day shift, a juvenile Neimoidian sitting on
the lobby bench with the specific quality of attention
that suggests they have been asked to watch the entrance.

That last one is not casual. N'got pays the building's
children in small amounts to watch the entrance and
report unusual arrivals. He has been doing this for
years. It is not sophisticated. It works.

The juvenile looks at the party. Goes back to whatever
they were doing. This look lasts approximately two
seconds longer than casual indifference would require.

### Floors One Through Four — Normal Residential

The lower four floors of the Terrath Block are ordinary
residential space. Approximately eight units per floor,
all occupied, all containing the sounds and smells of
domestic Neimoidian life. The staircase here is used
regularly. The lift functions. The corridors are maintained.

Nothing significant happens on these floors for the party's
purposes. What these floors establish, atmospherically,
is what the building is supposed to be — domestic, functional,
unremarkable. This is the building N'got is hiding inside.

### Floor Five — The Transition

The fifth floor is where the building changes.

The transition is not dramatic. It is simply that when the
party reaches the fifth floor via the staircase, something
shifts. The corridor is quieter than the floors below.
There are eight units on this floor as on the others,
but fewer sounds come from behind the doors. The lighting
in the corridor is functional but one unit's door — Unit 5F —
has a piece of equipment attached to the frame that is
definitely a sensor of some kind.

The sensor detects the party crossing the fifth-floor
landing. Somewhere above, N'got knows they have reached
his secured territory.

**The Pale Spider Warning System:**
N'got discovered, approximately four years ago, that
the Pale Spider colony in the building's upper floors
responds to unfamiliar vibration patterns in ways that
are predictable and useful. He did not bond with them —
he is not Force-sensitive and has no particular connection
to animals — but he observed their behavior carefully
enough to understand that they tighten their webs
and become still when they detect unfamiliar heavy
footfall in the structure.

He uses this. Not precisely — he cannot tell from
spider behavior whether it is one person or six —
but he can tell when something has entered his territory
that has not been there before. The spiders tell him
what his sensor tells him, which is that something
is on the fifth floor. Both sources confirming the
same information gives N'got a higher confidence
level than either alone.

**For the Pathfinder:**
The Pale Spider colony is immediately present to her
as she reaches the fifth floor. Not the bonded colony
from the ventral city — a different group, larger,
older, and significantly more anxious. The entire colony
is in a state of alert. She can feel it clearly:
the collective tightening that N'got has learned to
read from behavior, she reads directly as experience.

The spiders are not afraid of the party specifically.
They are in a permanent state of low-level anxiety
because N'got's modifications to their environment —
the sensor equipment anchored to walls, the modified
ventilation creating unusual air currents, the irregular
human activity at all hours — have disrupted the
normal rhythms of their territory. They have adapted
but the adaptation is incomplete and they are perpetually
slightly stressed.

This is useful information. If N'got is reading
the spiders' behavior as a warning system, he does not
know that the spiders are already stressed before
the party arrives. His warning system has a significantly
higher false-positive rate than he believes.

The Pathfinder could, theoretically, calm the colony
enough that N'got's spider-based early warning fails.
This requires an Average Animal Empathy check
and requires her to be on the fifth floor or above.
If successful, the spiders settle — not completely,
but enough that N'got's readings from their behavior
become ambiguous rather than alarming.

This does not disable his electronic sensors.
But it removes half his warning system.

## FLOORS SIX THROUGH TWELVE — N'GOT'S TERRITORY

N'got has occupied the upper seven floors of the Terrath Block
through a combination of factors:

- He has been here for fifteen years and has used money
  — some saved, some from his pre-war Separatist compensation
  — to quietly acquire the leases on approximately eighteen units
  across floors six through twelve, holding them under
  various Neimoidian names in a chain of beneficial ownership
  that would be extremely difficult to trace

- Several of the units he controls are not officially his —
  they are vacant units whose leases have lapsed and whose
  building management, in the understaffed and underfunded
  way of Imperial-era Neimoidian building management,
  has not gotten around to reassigning

- The remaining units on these floors are occupied by
  legitimate residents who are not aware that their neighbor
  in several of the other units is the same person
  in different contexts, and who have over time grown
  accustomed to the odd sounds and sensor equipment
  that characterize the upper floors of their building

### What N'got Has Done to His Territory

Fifteen years is a long time to fortify a residential building.
N'got has been methodical and has had almost nothing else
to do. The upper floors of the Terrath Block have been
transformed from standard residential space into something
that is neither exactly a bunker nor exactly a trap
but combines elements of both.

**Sensors:**
Motion sensors at every stair landing from five upward.
Vibration sensors anchored to the staircase structure
that detect footfall pattern and weight. A rudimentary
camera system with displays in his current sleeping unit
and in two secondary positions. All powered by
a modified independent power cell that is not connected
to the building's main power — N'got does not trust
systems he cannot disconnect instantly.

**The sensors have significant flaws:**
- The camera system has two blind spots that N'got knows
  about and has not been able to address (the turn in
  the staircase at floor nine, and the east end of
  the floor ten corridor)
- The vibration sensors cannot distinguish between
  single heavy individuals and multiple lighter ones
  if they are moving in coordinated fashion
- The power cell for the independent system runs
  low approximately every three months and N'got
  has learned to live with a period of reduced
  sensor coverage until he can recharge it.
  It is currently at about sixty percent.

**Traps:**
The module establishes these and they are real.
Gas dispensers, stun charges, and at least one detonite
charge that N'got has been reconsidering the wisdom of
including for approximately seven years but has not removed.

The traps are concentrated at specific chokepoints:
floor transitions (the staircase landings at floors
seven, nine, and eleven), corridor intersections
on floors eight and ten, and the approach to
whichever unit N'got is currently sleeping in.

The traps are not elegantly integrated. They are
the work of a paranoid non-engineer who has been
reading equipment manuals and improvising with
available components. They are functional but obvious
to anyone who knows what to look for — a Perception
check at each trap location, difficulty based on
how well the trap has been concealed (the gas dispensers
are well-hidden, Hard; the stun charges are Average;
the detonite charge is so poorly concealed that
anyone looking at the wall it is attached to will
notice it immediately).

**The Pale Spider Webs:**
Across the upper floors, N'got has — unintentionally —
created ideal conditions for Pale Spider web installation.
His sensor equipment provides anchor points.
His irregular schedule means the spiders have learned
the difference between his footfall pattern and others.
His independent power system creates a specific electromagnetic
signature that the spiders have incorporated into their
environmental mapping.

The result: the upper floors of the Terrath Block are
extensively webbed. Not visibly — the webs are in corners,
across the upper portions of doorways, along ceiling
junctions — but present. A character who moves without
care through these spaces will disturb the webs,
which creates vibration, which the spiders register,
which changes their behavior, which N'got reads.

For the Pathfinder, the webs are visible in another sense:
she can feel the colony's web-map as a kind of spatial
awareness. The spiders' collective territory sense
gives her a rough layout of which spaces have been
recently disturbed (lower web tension in the disturbed areas)
and which have not (high tension, tight webs).
This is not a floor plan. It is more like a heat map
of recent activity across the upper floors.

## THE TRAPS — DETAILED

### How to Navigate Them

The party has several options for navigating N'got's traps:

**Direct navigation:**
Move carefully through the upper floors, checking
for traps before proceeding. The GM should treat
this as a series of checks interspersed with the
atmospheric description of the building. The trap
difficulties are as described above (Hard for gas
dispensers, Average for stun charges, Easy for
the detonite charge). A failure means the trap activates.

**The spider guidance:**
If the Pathfinder has attuned to the colony's web-map,
she can feel which areas of the floor have undisturbed
webs and which have been recently walked. N'got's
regular route through his own territory has created
areas of permanently lower web tension — she can
navigate his path. His path avoids his own traps.
This does not give her explicit trap locations but
it gives her N'got's safe route, which is more useful.

**The beetle guidance:**
If the Drone-Beetle has been brought from the factory
session, it can enter the building through exterior
ventilation and navigate the upper floors before
the party enters. The beetle cannot identify traps
specifically but it can identify N'got's location
with high precision — the beetle follows heat signatures
and N'got is the only warm-bodied creature on the
upper floors larger than a spider. This tells the
party which unit he is currently occupying.

**N'got's own response:**
If the party makes significant noise or triggers
a sensor before triggering a trap, N'got will flee
to his current sleeping unit and lock the door.
If they trigger a trap, he will flee to a secondary
position (there are two, both on floor eleven,
both connected by the narrow maintenance corridor
that runs behind the residential units on that floor).
If they trigger the detonite charge, the structural
damage to that section of the corridor buys N'got
approximately thirty seconds and a lot of dust.

## ZEL N'GOT — THE MAN

### Who He Is

N'got was a spy in the way that some people are spies —
not from ideology or adventure but from a specific
combination of aptitude and circumstance that made
it the path of least resistance at a particular moment.

He was, before the war, a logistics coordinator for
a Neimoidian commercial house that had close Trade
Federation ties. He understood supply chains, routing,
the movement of goods and information through networks.
The Separatist intelligence service recruited him
because people who understand networks are useful
to organizations that operate through networks.

He was good at it. He was good at it in a technical
sense — the information he gathered was accurate,
his analysis was sound, his operational security
was above average — but he was not good at the
human elements. He did not understand that the
people he observed were watching him back. He did
not understand that Warde's ability to foresee actions
was operating at a level his training had not prepared
him for. He survived not because he outmaneuvered
Warde but because events overtook the pursuit.

Since the war, N'got has been waiting for consequences
that have not arrived. This is worse than if they had arrived.
A consequence is defined. An absence of consequence is
infinite possibility distributed across every moment.
He has been living inside that infinite possibility
for fifteen years. It has made him a specific kind of person:
meticulous, paranoid, physically diminished by the accumulated
weight of sustained vigilance, and possessed of a sharp
intelligence that has had nothing to do for fifteen years
except refine its own anxiety.

He is not a villain. He is a frightened old man
who did things that were reasonable in their moment
and has spent fifteen years waiting to be held accountable
for them. The fact that no one has come to hold him accountable
has not relieved the fear. It has intensified it,
because the waiting is worse than the reckoning would be.

### His Physical Condition

N'got is in his late sixties, which for a Neimoidian
is solidly old. His skin is the mottled gray-green
of Neimoidian advanced age. He moves slowly,
partly from age and partly from fifteen years of
restricted movement in a space that is comfortable
but not large. His hands are precise — the hands
of someone who has spent years working with
small mechanical components — but his legs are
stiff and he does not navigate the stairs of his
own building quickly.

He sleeps badly. This is visible in his face
and in the way he operates his environment —
the lights in his current sleeping unit are always on,
because darkness is where the fears are most active.
He has not slept in genuine darkness for approximately
twelve years.

He carries a holdout blaster that is too large for him
to comfortably operate. He has practiced with it
just enough to know that practice has not made
him confident with it.

## CATCHING N'GOT — APPROACHES

### The Module's Framework

The module establishes that N'got flees when he detects
the party and that catching him involves either
moving before he can flee or pursuing him.
The full living world treatment expands this into
a set of distinct approaches with different textures.

### Approach 1 — The Quiet Entry

Move through the building without triggering sensors
or traps, reach N'got's current unit before he is
aware of the party's presence, and knock.

**Requirements:**
- Navigate five floors of traps without triggering them
  (series of Perception/Skulduggery/Stealth checks)
- Optionally: Pathfinder calms the spider colony (Average Animal Empathy)
  to disable N'got's behavioral early warning
- Optionally: Drone-Beetle scouts N'got's current location

**What happens:**
The party reaches N'got's door. They knock.
A long silence. Then:

> *"Who is there."*

Not a question. A test. He is listening to how
they answer, not just to what they say.

If they answer honestly — that they have questions
about Suljo Warde — there is another long silence.
Then the sound of locks being undone. Not all of them.
The door opens to the width of a security chain.

> *"You are not Imperial. You are not Syndicate.*
> *I do not know who you are.*
> *This is already unusual."*

He is looking at the party through the gap.
His one visible eye is doing rapid calculations.

> *"Come in. Slowly. And tell your one with the
> animal sense to please not interfere with the webs.
> They took three years to establish."*

He knows about the Pathfinder. He does not explain how.
(The spiders' behavior changed when she attuned to them.
He reads the behavioral change as something unusual about her.
He has had fifteen years to observe his spiders carefully.)

### Approach 2 — The Confrontation

Trigger N'got's sensors or traps and pursue him.
This is the module's primary encounter structure.

**The Pursuit:**
N'got is old and slow. He knows this. His pursuit
strategy is not to outrun the party — it is to create
enough complications and delays that the distance
between them remains viable.

**Floor Seven — The First Contact:**
If the gas dispenser on the seventh landing is triggered,
N'got hears it and begins moving. He is already in
his secondary position on floor eleven — he has not
been in his sleeping unit when the party was below
floor five. He has been watching them on his cameras.

**Floor Nine — The Stair Blind Spot:**
The turn in the staircase at floor nine is the camera
blind spot. N'got knows the party passes through it
but cannot see them during it. He uses this moment
of his own uncertainty to make a decision: which exit.

The roof hatch (floor twelve) leads to the building's
external maintenance platforms. He can potentially
exit via the exterior and cross to an adjacent building.
This is his preferred escape route and he has maintained
it against the possibility of exactly this situation.

The secondary stair (a maintenance access he has
concealed behind a false panel on floor ten) leads
down to the fifth floor via a narrow service route
that bypasses floors six through nine entirely.
From the fifth floor he can exit through a unit
whose window opens onto an adjacent rooftop.

He chooses based on where the party seems to be:
- If they are below him: roof hatch
- If they are above him (if somehow the party splits
  or approaches from the roof): secondary stair

**The Rooftop:**
If N'got reaches the roof hatch, the encounter
moves to the building's roof — a flat utility surface
with ventilation equipment, the service access to
the building's antenna array, and a partial railing
on the street-facing side. The adjacent building
is close enough to cross to via a fixed maintenance
bridge (the kind buildings share for equipment access).

N'got crosses this bridge. He does not look down.
He crosses it every time he practices his escape route,
which he does twice a year, because the bridge is
over a thirty-meter drop to the street and he is
terrified of heights and he does not allow fear
to make decisions for him on this particular point.

The bridge is not rated for combat. It will hold.
But a result of Threat or Despair on any action
taken while crossing it creates a moment where
the bridge swings or one side detaches slightly,
requiring the active character to make an Average
Coordination check to maintain their footing.

**The External Walkway:**
The roof of the adjacent building is lower than
the Terrath Block by approximately two meters.
Getting from the maintenance bridge to the adjacent
roof requires a small jump (Easy Athletics) or
a longer route via an exterior ladder.

N'got has done this jump before. He lands badly
every time. He lands badly this time too —
he favors his left knee, which has not been good
for years — but he stays on his feet and keeps moving.

If the party has the Pathfinder bonded with any
aerial animal — a Canyon Manta, or the Kreehawk
from the commercial district session — the rooftop
pursuit changes character entirely.

**The Module's Resolution:**
N'got stops when cornered, when injured (he will not
be deliberately attacked without provocation by
any party that understood what they came here for),
or when the party demonstrates that they know
enough about his situation that running is pointless.

The last option is available from any pursuit position.
If a party member, at any point during the pursuit,
calls out something N'got could not expect them to know —
Hasar's name, Tacker's name, the specific detail
of his mission to monitor Warde specifically —
N'got stops.

> *"How do you know that."*

Again not a question. A statement of the impossibility
of his situation becoming more impossible.

If they know that much, running will not help.
He stops running. He decides — in the specific way
that he makes all decisions, by calculating what
gives him the best odds of survival — that talking
is now the least dangerous option.

### Approach 3 — The Setup

Rather than finding N'got, let N'got find them.

If the party has been in the residential district
for any length of time — helping residents, asking
questions, creating any kind of reputation —
N'got knows who they are and what they want.
He has been watching since they arrived on Cato.

The setup approach involves making the party's
presence and purpose visible enough that N'got
decides the risk of meeting them is lower than
the risk of not knowing what they know.

This requires the party to have done several things:
- Established a visible pattern of behavior in the residential district
- Made contact with at least two of the module's NPCs
- Not made themselves appear to be Imperial agents or Syndicate operatives

If these conditions are met, N'got sends a message.
Not directly — through the building's juvenile watcher,
who appears near the party in a public space and
hands them a folded note without making eye contact.

The note, in cramped handwriting:

> *"Third floor, unit 3F, Terrath Block. Tonight, one hour
> after curfew bell. Come without weapons if you want
> information. Come with weapons if you want a fight.*
> *I will not be there for a fight.*
> *I am too old for a fight and I suspect you are
> better at it than I am in any case.*
>
> *Come alone if possible. The one who feels the spiders
> may come. She is already known to me.*
> *Do not bring the cerulean one unless necessary.*
> *He has the look of someone who has been on Cato before
> and I prefer to talk to people whose histories I do not know."*

He is right about Sigg. He has not identified Sigg specifically
but the cerulean skin color is memorable and N'got's
surveillance network is more extensive than the party realizes.

**Unit 3F:**
A unit N'got maintains on the third floor — below his
secured territory, in the ordinary residential section.
It is furnished simply. It has no traps. It has a back
window that opens onto a maintenance platform and from
there to a route N'got has used so many times it is
worn smooth.

He is there when the party arrives. He is sitting at
the table with both hands visible. There is a light
on. He does not look like a spy. He looks like a
tired old man who has decided to stop running for
one evening and see what happens.

## THE CONVERSATION WITH N'GOT

### His Manner

N'got speaks precisely. Not formally — precisely.
Every sentence is constructed to convey the minimum
necessary information while revealing the minimum
necessary about his internal state. This is a habit
so deep that he no longer notices he is doing it.
He has been doing it for thirty-five years.

He does not apologize for anything. He does not
preemptively excuse himself. He describes what
happened from his perspective with the specificity
of someone who has been reviewing the same memories
for fifteen years and has catalogued every detail.

He is not indifferent to the moral weight of what
he describes. He is simply not going to perform
guilt for strangers. He has enough of his own.

### What He Knows — And How He Says It

**On his mission:**

> *"I was assigned to monitor Suljo Warde specifically.*
> *Not the Republic presence generally — Warde specifically.*
> *The Separatist intelligence service understood that
> his ability to foresee actions was more valuable
> to the Republic than ten thousand clone troopers.*
>
> *My assignment was to understand the mechanism.
> How far ahead could he see. What information he needed.
> What his error rate was. Whether there were conditions
> under which his foresight failed.*
>
> *I gathered significant data on the first three questions.*
> *On the fourth, I had a preliminary conclusion
> that I never delivered."*

**On the error rate:**

> *"He was not infallible. He was close to infallible
> about immediate actions — what someone would do in
> the next few minutes. He was less reliable about
> decisions that were contingent on other decisions.*
>
> *What I observed was that his error rate increased
> when the people he was predicting were in emotional states.*
> *Fear, specifically. People who were very frightened
> made choices that his foresight did not anticipate.*
>
> *He knew this. He adapted his methods to account for it.*
> *The adaptation was to increase the fear.*
>
> *I am not certain he understood the implications
> of what he was doing. I am not certain he did not."*

**On watching Warde deteriorate:**

> *"I watched him change from a competent Jedi
> conducting a difficult occupation into something else.*
> *I do not have a clean term for the something else.*
>
> *He became someone who had decided the future was fixed.*
> *Which it is not. Nothing I observed in his ability
> suggested the future was fixed — it suggested that
> the most probable futures clustered around certain
> decision points, and that his ability let him identify
> those points. That is not the same as certainty.*
>
> *But he began treating it as certainty.*
> *And when you treat probable futures as certain,
> the choices you make to address those futures
> become self-fulfilling. He created the conditions
> his foresight predicted by acting on the prediction.*
>
> *I observed this in real time. I found it fascinating
> and horrifying simultaneously. I did not know
> how to report it in a way that would be believed."*

**On the cease-fire talks:**

This is what the party has come for. N'got knows this.

He is quiet for a moment before speaking.

> *"I was in the Sankar Palace. Not as a delegate —
> as an observer, embedded in the diplomatic support staff.*
> *I was there to assess whether Warde's foresight
> would identify me. He had been getting close.*
>
> *The negotiations were genuine. I want to say that clearly
> because it matters and because the history that exists
> says something different. The Separatist delegation
> was there in good faith. I knew the delegation personally.
> I know what their orders were.*
>
> *They intended to honor a cease-fire if one was reached.*
> *Whether the Separatist high command would have honored it —
> that I cannot speak to. But the delegation was sincere."*

He pauses.

> *"Warde saw something. I do not know what he saw —
> I could not access his vision, only observe his behavior.*
> *What I observed was that he became certain, in the
> space of approximately ten minutes at the negotiating table,
> that the delegation was going to betray the cease-fire.*
>
> *He may have been right. He may have been wrong.*
> *The dark side was in him by then — I did not know
> the terminology at the time, but what I observed
> was a man whose perception was compromised by his
> own emotional state in ways he was not accounting for.*
>
> *He moved before anyone else understood what was happening.*
> *I was close enough to the door to run.*
> *Others were not."*

Another pause. Longer.

> *"I ran. I want to be clear that I ran.*
> *I did not attempt to stop him. I did not warn the delegation.*
> *I ran because I was going to die if I stayed
> and I made the calculation that staying was not worth it.*
>
> *I have made that calculation many times in my life.*
> *This is the one I cannot make satisfactory peace with."*

**On what he knows that no one else does:**

> *"There is one thing I observed that I have not
> told anyone because no one has asked.*
>
> *When Warde moved, he hesitated.*
>
> *One moment. Perhaps two seconds. He had his lightsaber
> drawn and he hesitated. He looked at the delegation
> and he hesitated. And then he did not hesitate anymore.*
>
> *I do not know what happened in those two seconds.*
> *I do not know if he saw something in his foresight
> that confirmed his course of action or if he made
> a choice to override a doubt.*
>
> *I think about those two seconds frequently.*
> *I think they are the most important two seconds
> in the history of what happened here.*
> *I think they might have gone differently.*
> *I cannot know."*

## N'GOT'S ANIMALS

The upper floors of the Terrath Block have a specific
animal ecology that the Pathfinder will be aware of
throughout the encounter.

**The Pale Spider Colony:**
The dominant animal presence, distributed across
floors six through eleven. Approximately forty individuals,
organized into three territorial sub-groups that have
negotiated border arrangements over years of cohabitation.

N'got has studied their behavior extensively as a
survival tool. The Pathfinder experiences them differently —
as a collective intelligence with a spatial awareness
of the entire upper building, as an anxiety state that
has been elevated for approximately fifteen years and has
become their baseline, and as a specific response
to her own presence that is different from their
response to N'got.

They are not afraid of her. They are curious.
A bonded Pale Spider from the ventral city, if the
Pathfinder has one, resonates with this colony —
they are the same species, their web-language is
mutually intelligible, and the bonded spider's calm
has a measurable settling effect on the colony.

N'got notices this if he is present. He watches
the spiders settle and then looks at the Pathfinder
with an expression of someone adjusting their model
of a situation.

> *"I did not know they could do that.*
> *I have been reading them for three years.*
> *They have been anxious for three years.*
> *You are here for twenty minutes and they settle."*

He is quiet.

> *"This is useful to know. I do not know why it is
> useful to know but I have found that useful information
> tends to become relevant eventually."*

**Bridge Geckos:**
A small population on the exterior of the upper floors.
They do not come inside — N'got's modifications to the
building have sealed most of the access points that
geckos would use. But they are present on the exterior
walls, visible through the windows. The Pathfinder
can feel them — uncomplicated, content, entirely
uninterested in the complicated human situation inside.

**A Single Rusted Stalker Patrol:**
The Rusted Stalker from the ventral city, if bonded,
occasionally patrols the service corridor at the base
of the Terrath Block. N'got knows about this animal.
He has observed it for approximately two years.
He does not know it has been in the ventral city.
He knows it is territorial and he knows it does not
bother him specifically, which he has filed as
useful information without being able to explain why.

If the Pathfinder has bonded the Stalker and it is
present during the N'got encounter, N'got notices
the animal's behavior toward her. He files this.

## SIGG IN THE N'GOT SECTION

### On the Approach

Sigg's note in N'got's message — *do not bring the cerulean one
unless necessary* — establishes something important:
N'got has been tracking Sigg's presence in the city.

This means N'got knows about Sigg's previous posting.
Not specifics — not why Sigg was here or who he was
working for — but that a cerulean Guardian-type was
in Jorra a year ago and is in Jorra again.

Sigg, when told about the message, does not react
with alarm. He reacts with the specific stillness
of someone who has just been told that their
operational security has a gap they did not know about.

> *"A year ago. He was watching a year ago.*
> *I need to know what he saw."*

He says this quietly. It is not a directive.
It is him thinking out loud, which he does rarely.

**If Sigg comes to the meeting:**
N'got acknowledges him with a nod.

> *"You are more careful than you were a year ago.*
> *You were careful then. You are more careful now.*
> *Something happened between your visits."*

He is not asking. He is stating an observation.

Sigg does not confirm or deny this. He sits.
The conversation proceeds. But something has shifted
in the room's dynamic — N'got is the more experienced
intelligence operative and both of them know it.

**After the meeting:**
Sigg is quiet for longer than usual. On the walk back:

> *"He knew I was here last year. He watched me
> for what — weeks? Months? And I did not know.*
>
> *I thought I was being careful.*
> *He was being careful too.*
> *He was better at it."*

A pause.

> *"He is going to keep watching. Even after this.*
> *It is what he does. It is the only thing that
> still works the way he expects it to."*

## AFTER THE N'GOT MEETING — AFTERMATH

### What N'got Wants

N'got does not make explicit demands. He is not in
a position to make demands. But he has a need that
he expresses obliquely and the party can address
if they choose.

He wants to know if anyone is going to come for him.

Not the party — after this conversation, he is
relatively confident about the party. But the Empire.
The Syndicate. Anyone who might have been watching
the same threads he has been watching.

> *"I have been waiting fifteen years.*
> *I have developed a theory about waiting.*
> *The danger is not when someone comes.*
> *The danger is in the waiting, which is continuous,
> and which does not end regardless of what happens.*
>
> *If you find Warde — if he is where the crystal says
> he went — I would like to know what happened.*
> *Not for any operational reason.*
> *I spent three years watching him and I should like
> to know how the story ends.*
>
> *If it is not too much trouble."*

He is asking them to come back and tell him.
He does not know if they will. He is asking anyway.

### Leads Generated

N'got provides the following leads:

**On Tacker:**
> *"TH-313 was present for the events at the palace.*
> *He was stationed outside with the primary clone
> trooper contingent. He saw Warde leave.*
> *He saw the direction he went.*
> *He knows things about that night that I cannot
> tell you because I was running in the other direction."*

**On Hasar:**
> *"The only surviving delegate. She was badly injured.*
> *Commando droids retrieved her — she was still alive
> when they carried her out.*
> *I have followed her career since. I find it instructive.*
> *She is not a person who forgives.*
> *She is looking for him. She has been looking for him.*
> *If you find Warde before she does, I suggest you do so quietly."*

**On Warde's direction of flight:**
> *"He went south. His starfighter was in the southern
> landing bay of the Sankar Palace — the ventral side,
> lower levels. The direction suggests he was not
> heading for any inhabited system in this sector.*
> *I looked at the charts afterward.*
> *There is very little to the south that anyone
> would choose to go to voluntarily."*

He pauses.

> *"I could not identify a destination.*
> *The gatekeeper probably knows what I could not work out.*
> *Ask it about worlds to the south that a Jedi
> who believed he was dying might choose.*
> *I suspect you will find your answer quickly."*
