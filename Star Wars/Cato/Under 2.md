# TACKER AND THE VENTRAL PALACE
## A Living World Guide for Act 2 — Jorra, Cato Neimoidia
## Sessions: TH-313, The Descent, and Sankar Palace

---

# PART ONE: FINDING TACKER

---

## WHO TACKER IS IN THIS CITY

TH-313 — known to the people who knew him as Tacker —
is the last living witness to the events at Sankar Palace.

He is also the last living witness to Suljo Warde as a person
rather than a story. Everyone else the party has spoken to
knew Warde through his effects: the ventral city that bears
his damage, the residents who fear his name, Haido who worked
for him at a careful distance, N'got who watched him from
further still. Tacker served under him. Tacker ate with him.
Tacker watched him make decisions and watched him change
and watched him leave and has been carrying the weight
of knowing what he saw for fifteen years.

He is, in the specific way of old soldiers, a man who has
organized his life around the discipline of continuing.
He continues because continuing is what he was trained to do.
He continues because the alternative requires a kind of
deliberate choice he has never been able to locate in himself.
He continues because somewhere in the back of his mind,
at a level he does not examine, he is still waiting for
the situation to resolve — for Warde to come back,
for someone to explain what happened, for the thing that
happened at Sankar Palace to make sense in a way it has
never made sense.

The party arriving and asking about Warde is, for Tacker,
the closest thing to that resolution that fifteen years have offered.

---

## THE INTELLIGENCE SITUATION

### Who Knows About Tacker

The party has his name from two sources:
- Haido mentioned him with the specific detail about the list of names
- N'got confirmed he was outside Sankar Palace and knows Warde's direction of flight

Neither source knows exactly how to reach him.
N'got knows he is under Irbian's command.
Haido knows he is a stormtrooper captain.

This is enough to locate him in theory. In practice,
approaching a stormtrooper captain under Duke Irbian's
command requires navigating a surveillance environment
that has been getting more attentive to the party
since the factory encounter.

### Irbian's Awareness

By the time the party is looking for Tacker, Duke Irbian
knows they exist. His informant network in the factory
district flagged offworlders connected to the Kreehawk attack.
The surveillance cameras at the district boundaries have
their images. He has been building a picture.

He does not know what they want. He knows they have
been asking about Warde. He knows they were at DE-13.
He knows they are not Rebel operatives in any pattern
he recognizes — Rebel operatives do not typically ask
about fifteen-year-old Jedi history. He is suspicious
but his suspicion is not yet directed enough to act on.

Approaching Tacker risks changing this. If the party
contacts Tacker openly and Irbian's people see it,
the duke will begin connecting pieces and the picture
will become clearer faster than is safe.

Tacker is aware of this. He has been aware of this
for fifteen years in the general sense that any
former clone trooper with Jedi loyalties is aware
that those loyalties constitute a permanent risk.

### What Tacker Is Doing

Tacker is on shift rotation at Southside Prison
for the next two days. This is standard rotation —
he oversees security procedure at the prison for
forty-eight-hour blocks on a three-week cycle.

This creates an access problem: the prison is not
easily approached, it is heavily monitored, and
a group of offworlders attempting to reach a
stormtrooper captain inside an Imperial prison
facility would attract the exact kind of attention
that ends the investigation quickly and badly.

It also creates an opportunity: the prison rotation
has a known schedule, which means Tacker's movements
between the prison and the Imperial barracks are
predictable. The party can find the window
if they are patient.

---

## FINDING THE WINDOW

### The Magrail Terminal

The Southside Prison is accessible from the upper city
only via the magrail tunnel that runs through the bridge
core to the prison hanging below the southern tension cables.
The terminal is in the bridge core level — below the
factory district floor, accessed via a dedicated elevator
from the factory district edge.

The magrail runs on a schedule. Shift changes at the
prison happen every twelve hours. The transition period —
when the outgoing shift is at the terminal waiting
for the magrail while the incoming shift is in transit —
creates a ten-to-fifteen-minute window when the terminal
is occupied by off-duty stormtroopers who are not
technically on duty but are also not yet dispersed
to their off-duty locations.

This is Tacker's window.

He has been using it for three years. During the
transition period, he sits in the terminal's civilian
waiting area — technically off-limits to civilians
but informally tolerated during the shift change period
because no one has specifically prohibited it —
and he keeps a small notebook and he makes entries.
The entries are his list.

The party, if they have learned about the list from
Haido and have correctly inferred Tacker's schedule
from asking careful questions around the barracks area,
can be at the terminal during the transition period.

They will find an old man in a stormtrooper captain's
uniform sitting in a civilian waiting area writing
in a small notebook. He will look up when they arrive.
He will look at them for a long moment.

He will not be surprised.

> *"I wondered when you would find me.*
> *Sit down. The magrail does not run for another
> twelve minutes and I find I would like to use them."*

---

## THE MAGRAIL TERMINAL — THE LOCATION

The terminal is a functional space with no pretension
to aesthetics. A platform, a track, a waiting area
with fixed seating, a security checkpoint at the
far end that is currently unmanned (the checkpoint
operates only when the magrail is actively running).
The lighting is the white-blue of Imperial functional spaces.

It is also, at this hour, almost entirely empty.
The off-duty stormtroopers who transition through here
disperse quickly — they have places to be, they are
off duty, they do not linger. Tacker is the exception.

The terminal has three cameras. Tacker knows where
all three are. He is sitting in the blind spot between
two of them. Whether this is deliberate or habit
is indistinguishable at this point.

---

## TACKER — THE MAN

### His Appearance

The module describes him accurately: an aging face,
close-cropped hair going white in places, the body
of a man who has maintained physical fitness through
discipline because discipline is the thing he has.
His bad knee is visible in how he distributes his
weight when sitting — a slight favor, the kind of
accommodation you make for pain that has been present
long enough that working around it has become automatic.

He has the stormtrooper captain's uniform on but the
helmet is beside him on the seat rather than on his head.
This small non-compliance — being in a quasi-public
space with his uniform incomplete — says something
about where he thinks he is in the hierarchy of risk
at this particular moment.

### His Manner

Tacker is the most direct NPC the party has encountered
in Jorra. He does not evade. He does not perform.
He answers what he is asked with the precision of
someone who has been organizing his recollections
for fifteen years against the possibility of exactly
this conversation.

He is also the most emotionally present NPC
the party has encountered. Haido managed his emotions
into a controlled performance. N'got had compressed
his into a focused point of surviving dread.
Tacker's emotions are on the surface in the specific
way of old soldiers who have stopped performing toughness
because they have simply run out of reasons to perform it.

He is not crying. He is not dramatic. He is simply present,
and the weight of what he carries is visible in the
same way that structural stress is visible in old metal —
not as dramatic failure but as the accumulated evidence
of force applied over time.

### His Relationship to What Happened

Tacker has never believed Warde was simply wrong.

This is the thing that separates him from every other
NPC the party has encountered in Jorra. The residents
of the ventral city hate Warde. Haido managed and suppressed
his complicated relationship to what he observed.
N'got assessed Warde as a subject of study who exceeded
his parameters. Even Tieka Dalken's defense of Warde
is rooted in a single formative childhood experience.

Tacker served under him for over a year. He saw the
good and the bad in continuous sequence without the
mercy of distance. And his conclusion — held for
fifteen years against the weight of everything that
happened — is that Warde was a good man who broke.

Not a monster. Not a failure. A good man who was
given a task that exceeded what any good man could
sustain and who broke under it in a specific, visible,
preventable way that no one with the power to intervene
chose to prevent.

This belief costs Tacker something. He knows how it
sounds. He knows what the residents of Jorra think
of anyone who defends Warde. He has never said it
to anyone because there has been no one to say it to.

The party is the first people in fifteen years who
are asking the right questions for him to say it to.

## THE CONVERSATION — DETAILED

### The Opening

Tacker does not wait for the party to introduce themselves
or explain their purpose. He already knows enough.

> *"You have been asking about Warde. You have been
> to the factory district. You have been to the
> residential blocks. You have been asking the right
> questions in the right order.*
>
> *That means you have something that belongs to him.*
> *A holocron, I would guess, based on what I know
> about what he left behind.*
>
> *I have been wondering for fifteen years if someone
> would come with that. I had mostly stopped wondering.*
> *I am glad I was wrong to stop."*

He looks at them.

> *"I will tell you what I know. I ask one thing in return.*
> *When you find him — if he is alive, if he is dead,
> if there is anything left of him to find —
> tell me. Send word somehow. I do not need details.*
> *I need to know the story ends.*
>
> *I have been waiting for it to end for fifteen years.*
> *I would like that."*

### On Warde as a Commander

> *"He was the best tactician I served under and I served
> under Jedi in four campaigns. He did not make mistakes
> about battle. He saw the shape of things before
> they resolved into shapes anyone else could see.*
>
> *He cared about us. That is the thing people here
> do not know or do not say. He cared about the
> clone troopers under his command in a way that
> was unusual — we were not furniture to him,
> we were not resources, we were people he was
> responsible for and he felt that responsibility.*
>
> *He knew our names. Not our designations — our names.*
> *The names we had given ourselves. He asked us
> what we wanted to be called and he used those names.*
> *This sounds like a small thing.*
> *Among the commanders I served under, it was not a small thing."*

### On the Deterioration — Tacker's Version

What Tacker saw is different from what Haido saw
and different from what N'got saw. He was closest.
He saw the private version.

> *"It started with the sleep. He stopped sleeping
> the way people sleep. He would sit in the command
> center for hours after everyone else had rotated
> to rest and when we came back on shift he would
> be in the same position, reading the same data,
> looking at the same projections.*
>
> *Then the orders changed. Not dramatically —
> in small increments. A patrol that covered an area
> we hadn't covered before. An arrest made on
> information he would not show us. A person held
> longer than the regulations allowed.*
>
> *I asked him about it once. About one of the arrests.*
> *A man named Olpan — I believe he had a son.*
> *I asked what evidence we had for the arrest.*
>
> *He looked at me for a long time before answering.*
> *What he said was: 'I have seen what he will do.
> That is sufficient.'*
>
> *I did not argue. I was a clone trooper under a
> Jedi commander. Arguing was not my role.*
> *I should have argued anyway.*
> *I have thought about that many times."*

### On Sanna Olpan — If the Party Has Met Her

If the party has encountered Sanna and mentions her or
mentions looking for information about her son Gren,
Tacker stops. A full stop, in the middle of whatever
he was saying.

> *"Olpan.*
>
> *The name is on my list. Gren Olpan, arrested
> on Warde's order, no formal charges.*
> *Held at Southside for seven months during the
> occupation. Released when the Republic forces
> withdrew from Jorra.*
>
> *I do not know where he went after that.*
> *I tried to find out. The records from that period
> are — the Empire reorganized the records.*
> *They do not have his file.*
>
> *If his mother is still in the city, she does not
> know he was released.*
> *She has been looking for information about
> a man who walked out of Southside Prison
> eight months into the occupation.*
> *He may be anywhere."*

He is quiet for a moment.

> *"That is a piece of information I have been
> carrying for fifteen years with no one to give it to.*
> *Please tell her."*

This is not a side quest. It is simply a piece of
information that has been waiting in Tacker's list
for fifteen years for someone to carry it to the
person who needs it. The party now has it.
What they do with it is their choice.

### On the Cease-Fire Talks

> *"I was stationed at the palace entrance with
> a contingent of twelve. Standard protocol for
> diplomatic meetings — presence but not intrusion,
> enough force to deter intervention, not enough
> to threaten the process.*
>
> *The meeting was inside for approximately forty minutes
> before we heard it.*
>
> *Not the lightsaber — that came after.*
> *What we heard first was silence.*
> *The kind of silence that happens when every
> mechanical and ambient sound in a space stops
> simultaneously because something has changed
> in the fundamental nature of what is happening.*
>
> *I have heard that silence three times in my career.*
> *I knew what it meant each time."*

### On the Fight

> *"The two Jedi came out first. Moll and Durael.*
> *They were not injured yet. They were — I had never
> seen Jedi look like that. Afraid. Not of physical harm.*
> *Afraid of what they were about to have to do.*
>
> *They told us to hold position. That Warde had —
> that something had happened. That they needed to
> contain the situation. They went back in.*
>
> *What happened inside, I know from the reports.*
> *From my position at the entrance I heard the fight.*
> *Lightsabers are loud when they're used in earnest.*
> *People do not realize this. They are very loud.*
>
> *It went on for longer than you would expect.*
> *He fought both of them. Grievously wounded.*
> *He still fought both of them.*
>
> *I do not say this to excuse what he did.*
> *I say it because it is true and because the man
> who fought two Jedi while dying was the same man
> who asked me what I wanted to be called
> on my first day under his command.*
> *Both of those things are the same person.*
> *I have never been able to resolve that."*

### On Warde's Departure

This is the information N'got confirmed Tacker has —
the direction of flight.

> *"He came out of the south entrance. The ventral entrance —
> the lower levels of the palace, closest to the canyon.*
> *His starfighter was in the landing bay there.*
>
> *I saw him. He saw me.*
>
> *He stopped. Just for a moment. He looked at me
> the way a man looks at someone he is about to
> ask for something and has decided not to ask for.*
> *I do not know what he would have asked.*
> *He got in his ship and left.*
>
> *The direction was south-southwest.*
> *I tracked his departure vector for as long as
> my equipment could maintain it.*
> *There is nothing to the south-southwest of this
> system that any sane person would fly toward on purpose.*
>
> *I looked at the charts afterward.*
> *Many times.*
> *There is one place.*
> *I do not know its name.*
> *I think your gatekeeper does."*

He looks at them.

> *"Go find him. Whatever he has become.*
> *Go find him and tell me what you find.*
> *That is all I ask."*

### The List

At the end of the conversation, whether or not
the party asks about it, Tacker closes his notebook.

> *"The list. You know about it.*
>
> *Everyone who was arrested under Warde's orders
> during the occupation. As complete as I could
> make it — one hundred and forty-three names.*
> *For sixty-one of them I have subsequent information:
> released, transferred, died in custody, current location.*
> *For eighty-two I have only the arrest.*
>
> *I have been keeping this list because someone should.*
> *Warde did what he did and then he left and then
> the Empire came and the records were reorganized
> and those hundred and forty-three people became
> administrative artifacts.*
>
> *I was there. I know they existed.*
> *Someone should know."*

He holds the notebook for a moment.

> *"Gren Olpan is number forty-seven.*
> *Tell his mother."*

## THE PATHFINDER IN THE TACKER SCENE

Tacker reads to the Pathfinder very clearly.

He is the most emotionally open person she has encountered
in Jorra. Not the most emotional — Sanna Olpan's grief
is deeper, Haido's exhaustion is more pervasive — but
the most open. What he feels is on the surface and has
been on the surface for so long that it has simply
become the texture of his presence.

What she feels from him is primarily: loyalty.
Not to the Empire — that is a surface layer,
a structure he operates within because structures
allow him to continue. The loyalty underneath is to
Warde specifically. Not to what Warde became —
to what Warde was. To the man who asked him
what he wanted to be called.

She may also feel, if she is attentive: relief.
He has been waiting for someone to come and ask
these questions for fifteen years. The asking is,
for Tacker, something closer to resolution than
he has had access to in a very long time.

**Sigg's note:**
Sigg watches Tacker throughout the conversation
with the attention of someone cross-referencing
what they hear against what they already know.
He does not participate much. At one point,
when Tacker describes the silence before the fight,
Sigg looks at the floor. Just for a moment.
He does not explain this.

# PART TWO: THE DESCENT TO SANKAR PALACE

---

## SETTING UP THE DESCENT

The party now has what they need:
- From Haido: N'got's lead, Tacker's lead
- From N'got: the hesitation, Hasar's search, the flight direction south
- From Tacker: the exact departure vector, the south entrance of Sankar Palace,
  the confirmation that Moraband is the destination

They need to get to Sankar Palace.

Sankar Palace is in the ventral city. Specifically, it is
in the deepest part of the ventral city — below the Ossivane,
below the deep residential, extending further down toward
the canyon than any of the residential buildings reach.
It was built as a diplomatic facility during the height
of the Trade Federation's Jorra presence: a place large
enough to host significant delegations, prestigious enough
to signal the Trade Federation's wealth and power,
private enough to conduct the kind of negotiations
that did not benefit from public visibility.

The party has been to the ventral city. They have been
to the Ossivane. They know the descent routes.
What they do not know is what is in the palace
and what is between the Ossivane level and the palace level.

---

## THE APPROACH — WHAT HAS CHANGED

The ventral city has changed since the party's last visit.

Not dramatically. But:

**The Rusted Stalker is more present.**
If bonded, the stalker has been tracking the party's
movements in the ventral city and has established
a patrol that covers the route between the Ossivane
and the deeper levels. It knows something is different
in the deep ventral — it has been avoiding the lower
levels for a reason it cannot articulate in any language
the Pathfinder has for understanding.

What the stalker knows, communicated through the bond
in the specific non-verbal way of animal knowledge:
something below is active that was not active before.
The stalker has been hearing sounds from the deep levels
for approximately the past week. It does not go to
investigate. It is not afraid, exactly — it is old
and experienced and fear is not the right word.
It is simply aware that the deep levels are not
currently the safe stillness they were when it
established its territory.

What is making sounds in the deep levels: the droids.
The BX commandos in the commerce level have begun
expanding their patrol patterns, possibly in response
to the disruption of the party's previous ventral city visit,
possibly for reasons of their own internal scheduling.
They have not reached the palace level. They are
moving in that direction.

**The Syndicate knows the party went down.**
Hasar has received a report: the offworlders descended
to the ventral city, spent a significant amount of time,
and returned with items that appeared to belong to
a specific residence. This confirms they know the city
better than she expected. It also confirms they are
not simply passing through.

She has sent one person down to assess the situation.
Not Lor Keln — Keln is recognizable and the party
has met her. A different operative, careful and quiet,
who has been in the ventral city before and knows
the routes the party does not know.

This operative is currently in the commerce level,
having descended via a Syndicate-exclusive route
that bypasses the shaft and the external descent.
They are not blocking the party. They are watching.
Hasar wants to know where the offworlders are going
in the ventral city more than she wants to stop them.

## THE LEVELS BETWEEN — EXTENDED VENTRAL TERRITORY

Between the Ossivane at Level 11 and Sankar Palace
at Level 15-18 there are levels the party has not visited.
These levels were, before the war, the most exclusive
addresses in Jorra's ventral city.

### Level 12 — The Exhibition Level

A single large building that occupied the entire Level 12
footprint: the Jorra Trade Federation Exhibition Hall.
A permanent installation showcasing Trade Federation
commerce, technology, and history, used for corporate
events and diplomatic hospitality.

**Condition:**
The exhibition hall is structurally intact but has been
comprehensively looted — not quickly, but methodically,
over years. The fixtures are gone, the display cases
are empty, the sophisticated lighting systems have been
stripped for components. What remains is the architecture
itself: a large, high-ceilinged space with excellent
canyon views and the ghosts of an aesthetic ambition
that was formidable in its day.

**What can be found:**
The exhibition hall's permanent interpretive plaques —
mounted directly to the walls, not portable, therefore
not stolen — are still readable. They tell the story
the Trade Federation wanted told about itself:

> *"The Trade Federation exists to serve the galaxy's
> commercial needs through the application of Neimoidian
> organizational genius and the free movement of
> goods across the stars. We are not an army.*
> *We are not a government.*
> *We are the invisible hand that makes commerce possible."*

The plaque below this one, smaller and added later,
reads in handwritten Basic: *"And then they sent the droids."*
The handwriting looks like a child's. It is not vandalism —
it is too considered, too placed. It is commentary.

A second plaque, in Pak Pak only, reads (translated):
*"Founded in the year of the Fourth Expansion.*
*Built by Neimoidian hands.*
*To serve Neimoidian interests.*
*May it outlast everything else we built here."*

It has outlasted everything else they built here.

**The Canyon Manta:**
One of the pair from the deep residential platforms
is present at Level 12 when the party passes through.
It has entered through a collapsed section of the
exhibition hall's canyon-facing wall — a gap approximately
three meters wide — and is resting on the exhibition
floor, wings folded, an enormous creature filling
a third of the main hall.

It is completely calm. It has been here before.
It will leave when it is ready and not before.

The party will need to pass through the exhibition hall
to reach the lower levels. The manta is blocking
the most direct route through. It is not aggressive.
It is simply present and large and will not move
on request.

**For the Pathfinder:**
The manta registers clearly — old, deeplywa calm,
the specific quality of animal calm that comes from
being a creature with very few natural predators
and a very long life. It is not afraid of them.
It is not particularly interested in them.y
If she communicates through the Force — not a bond,
just presence — it will acknowledge her existence
and return to whatever it was doing.

She can ask it to move. It will consider this
request at its own pace, which is approximately
the pace at which large canyon fauna consider
anything. After a minute or so it will shift
its position enough to clear the passage —
not leaving, just accommodating. It does this
with the unhurried dignity of something that
has never needed to be quick.

Sigg, watching this, says nothing.
He steps through the cleared passage.
He does not comment on what just happened
because he has decided that some things
do not require comment.

### Level 13 — The Diplomatic Quarter Approach

Level 13 was the approach level to the diplomatic
facilities below — the level that visitors to
the Trade Federation's Jorra diplomatic complex
would transit through before reaching the reception
and conference levels below.

It is wider than the residential levels, designed
for the movement of delegations rather than individuals.
The corridors here are broad, the ceilings are high,
and the decorative work — now weathered and partially
damaged — is more elaborate than anything the
residential levels offered.

**The Canyon Edge:**
Level 13 is built closer to the canyon edge than
any level above it. On the south face of the building
structure, the exterior walkways extend beyond the
structural mass of the bridge and are supported instead
by the palace's own support system — a network of
cables and struts that attach to the bridge above
and the canyon wall below.

Walking the south exterior of Level 13 means walking
on platforms that are, below your feet, nothing —
just air and mist and the canyon far below.
The platforms are solid. They have been solid
for thirty years and the cable support system,
while showing weathering, is not compromised.

But the experience of being on them is distinctive.

> *The platform extends beyond the edge of the bridge
> above. Underfoot: metal grating, through which
> you can see the support cables and, through those,
> the mist. The canyon is below. How far below depends
> on the mist — on a clear day the forest floor
> is visible as a distant green suggestion. Today
> the mist is thick and the bottom is invisible.*
>
> *The platform sways very slightly in the canyon wind.*
> *Not dangerously. Just enough to feel.*

**The Diplomatic Archive:**
On Level 13, in a room that was once a pre-meeting
briefing space for delegates, there is a sealed cabinet
that was either overlooked by looters or considered
not worth the effort of opening.

Hard Skulduggery opens it. Inside:
Printed briefing documents for the cease-fire negotiations.
The actual documents prepared for the Separatist
delegation that came to Jorra — their background
on the Republic presence, their briefing on Warde
(listed as "Jedi Knight Suljo Warde, prognosticative
ability assessed HIGH, recommend no deception-based
negotiating positions"), their objectives for the talks,
and the draft cease-fire terms they intended to propose.

The draft terms are detailed, specific, and fair.
They represent a genuine attempt at ending the
Jorra conflict.

The last page of the delegation briefing package,
under the heading "Risk Assessment":

> *"Primary risk: Jedi Knight Warde's prognosticative
> ability may identify tactical elements of any
> position taken. Recommend complete transparency
> in negotiating positions.*
> *Secondary risk: Warde's psychological assessment
> indicates potential instability under prolonged
> operational stress. Recommend keeping negotiations
> brief and conclusive.*
> *Note: Secondary risk assessment provided by
> embedded observer [DESIGNATION REDACTED].*
> *Assessment dated [DATE REDACTED]."*

The embedded observer was N'got.
The redaction is the Separatists' operational security.
His assessment — that Warde was unstable — was accurate.
It was also in the briefing documents for the delegation.
They knew he was unstable and they came anyway.

This does not resolve anything about what happened.
It adds weight to N'got's statement that the delegation
was sincere. It also suggests that the delegation
may have been taking a calculated risk — trusting
that the talks could be concluded before Warde's
instability became an active factor.

The calculation was wrong by approximately forty minutes.

### Level 14 — The Threshold

Level 14 is the last level before the palace begins.
It was, before the war, a security and reception level —
the checkpoint through which visitors to the diplomatic
complex would pass after being received at Level 13.

It is now a threshold in a different sense.

**The Architecture Changes:**
The building style changes at Level 14 in a way that
is immediately visible. Below this level, the Trade Federation
built in the same organic Neimoidian aesthetic as the rest
of the ventral city. Level 14 downward was designed to impress
foreign delegates — it was built in a more formal,
less distinctly Neimoidian style, the kind of architectural
language that was intended to read as cosmopolitan
and powerful rather than specifically cultural.

More stone. Higher ceilings. More deliberate use of
the canyon views. The building becomes grander and
more formal as you descend.

**The Feeling Changes:**
Force-sensitive characters notice something at Level 14.

Not the dark side specifically — not yet, that is the
palace below. Something more ambiguous: the residue of
significant events. The Force does not remember history
the way a datapad records it, but events that carry
sufficient emotional weight leave something in the
places where they happened. Level 14, as the last
thing the cease-fire delegation passed through alive,
carries weight.

The Pathfinder feels this most clearly: not fear exactly,
not pain exactly. More like the emotional equivalent
of a stain — something was soaked into this place that
has not fully dried in fifteen years.

If she has been to any other Force-sensitive location —
if she has had experience with vergences or sites of
significant events — she recognizes this quality.

Sigg feels it too, less clearly, as a kind of
atmospheric change in the Force that makes him
more alert without a specific reason.

> *"Something happened here,"* he says.*
> *Not a question.*
>
> *"Something important. Something the Force
> didn't entirely let go of."*

He does not elaborate. He does not need to.

# PART THREE: SANKAR PALACE

## THE PALACE — OVERVIEW

Sankar Palace is a six-level structure occupying the
lowest position in the ventral city, extending from
Level 15 at its upper face to Level 18 at its lowest
point — approximately thirty meters below the Ossivane,
approximately fifty meters above the canyon floor at
this point along the bridge span.

It was built in the Trade Federation's third expansion
period, approximately forty years before the Clone Wars,
by a Neimoidian architect whose name does not appear
in any surviving record but whose work is distinctive:
the palace combines the formal cosmopolitan aesthetic
of diplomatic architecture with a Neimoidian awareness
of space and light that creates something that reads
as grand without reading as cold.

Or it did. Before the war.

### What the War Did to the Palace

The palace was the site of the most significant single
event of the Jorra occupation: the moment when Suljo Warde
moved from a troubled Jedi Knight making increasingly
problematic decisions into something that had no name
in Jedi terminology.

The fighting at the palace was not extensive — it was
concentrated in the meeting hall and the adjacent
corridors, and the combatants were four: Warde,
the diplomatic delegation (brief), Moll, and Durael.
But the intensity was extreme and the Force presence
involved was significant enough that the building
carries marks that are not architectural.

In the physical sense: the fighting left the meeting hall
and certain corridors extensively damaged. Lightsaber
cuts in floors and walls. Blaster damage from the
droid bodyguards' return fire. Structural collapse
in the section where Durael fell.

In the Force sense: Warde's slide into the dark side
was an event of sufficient magnitude to leave a
residue in the place where it happened. Not a vergence —
not a sustained energetic phenomenon — but a stain.
The same quality the Pathfinder felt at Level 14,
concentrated and intensified.

### The BX Commando Droids — Updated Situation

The two BX commando droids from the commerce level
have, since the party's last ventral city visit,
extended their patrol to include the upper levels
of Sankar Palace.

This is new. They have never done this before.
What changed: the party's presence in the ventral
city during the Ossivane session disturbed the
established patterns of the lower city enough that
the droids' patrol algorithm extended their territory
assessment. They have been running level-by-level
checks of the lower ventral city and have, within
the last forty-eight hours, established that the
palace is structurally accessible from the commerce
level via a collapse point the party did not use.

They are currently on a four-hour patrol cycle
that includes the palace's upper level and the
Level 14 approach. They are not at the palace
when the party arrives — their patrol timing
puts them in the commerce level junction —
but they will be at the palace within two hours
of the party's arrival.

This creates a time constraint for the palace exploration.

**GM Note on the Droids:**
The party has options. They have already encountered
the droids at the junction. If they resolved that
encounter through conversation rather than combat,
the droids know their transponder signatures and
may not engage immediately on seeing them again.
If they resolved it through combat, the remaining
droid (or droids, depending on the outcome) has
logged them as hostiles.

The droid situation at the palace level is something
the GM should determine based on what happened
at the previous encounter and what serves the current session.

## ENTERING SANKAR PALACE

### The Upper Level Entrance

The palace's public entrance — the grand reception
doors that welcomed delegations from across the galaxy —
is on Level 15. The doors themselves are still present:
enormous durasteel panels, ornate with Trade Federation
symbolic work, standing approximately four meters high.
They are sealed. The mechanism that would open them
has been without power for fifteen years.

Getting through:
- The doors can be forced (Daunting Athletics — they are heavy)
- The mechanism can be restored (Hard Mechanics —
  the power conduit still exists, it just needs reconnection)
- A side access — a service entrance to the left of
  the main doors, half-concealed behind a decorative
  column, designed for maintenance personnel —
  is sealed with a standard lock (Average Skulduggery)

The Rusted Stalker, if bonded and present, sits at the
service entrance and waits. It has been inside the palace.
It does not like the inside of the palace.
It is telling the Pathfinder this as clearly as it can
without language: I have been in there and I am out here
and I recommend out here.

It will not go in. The bond does not compel it.
The Pathfinder can feel why: the Force stain that
she noticed at Level 14 is concentrated inside the palace.
The stalker does not perceive the Force. What it perceives
is that this is a place where the ordinary animal confidence
that comes from being the top predator in an environment
does not function as expected. The palace is wrong
in a way the stalker cannot identify and therefore
cannot defend against. It will not enter.

### The Torches

The module establishes that torches in the palace walls
spring to life when the party enters.

This is not a mechanical effect. Explain it as follows:
The palace's lighting system was not entirely power-dependent.
The Trade Federation, aware that diplomatic facilities
must be functional even during power disruptions,
installed a chemical lighting system in the wall sconces —
slow-burn compounds that ignite from a trigger mechanism
and burn for approximately six hours before requiring
replacement.

The trigger mechanisms are pressure-activated.
The moment someone crosses the threshold of the palace's
main interior corridor, pressure sensors in the floor
send a signal through a still-functional mechanical
relay system that ignites the wall sconces.

The light that results is not electric and not fire exactly.
It is the specific purple-tinged illumination of the
Trade Federation chemical lighting compound — chosen
for aesthetic reasons forty years ago, now creating
an atmosphere that was not intended and is not comfortable.

> *The doors open and the light comes on.*
>
> *Not all at once — sequentially, from the entrance
> inward, each sconce igniting from the next as the
> signal propagates through the walls. The light is
> purple-white, chemical, completely steady.*
> *There is no flicker. There are no shadows.*
> *Everything the light touches is equally illuminated,
> which means everything has the same quality
> of visibility, which creates its own kind of unease:
> nothing is hidden, nothing is shadowed, and it is
> not reassuring.*

### The Grand Entry — Level 15

The entry level of Sankar Palace is the most intact
section of the building. The fighting did not reach
here — the damage is all in the lower levels.
What the entry level shows is what the palace was
before it became what it is.

High ceilings. A wide corridor that bifurcates
after twenty meters into two sweeping staircases —
one to the formal reception level above (now inaccessible,
the upper section of the staircase having been damaged),
one to the conference levels below. Between the staircases,
in the entry atrium, a large decorative element:
a stylized map of the Trade Federation's reach at its peak,
rendered in stone and metal inlay in the floor.

The map is still legible. At its center, the Neimoidian
purse worlds. Extending outward in trade route lines
that cover a third of the known galaxy. Cato Neimoidia
marked with a specific symbol — larger than the others,
the acknowledged center of the network.

At the bottom of the map, in Pak Pak and Basic:
*"We built this. We will maintain this. We will protect this."*

The map is correct. The Trade Federation built this
and maintained this and ultimately failed to protect it.
The map does not know this. Maps do not know the
things that happen to them.

**The Mosaic Floor:**
The floor surrounding the map is a mosaic of the same
quality as the Ossivane lobby — perhaps better quality,
certainly more elaborate. It has been partially damaged
by the droid activity in the building: droid footprints
tracked through sections of the mosaic, several panels
cracked where heavy equipment was dragged across them
during the war.

What survives is extraordinary. Neimoidian artisans
worked this floor for two years before the palace opened.
The designs are not abstract — they are narrative,
telling the story of Neimoidian civilization from
the founding of the homeworld settlements to the
establishment of the purse worlds. It is the most
complete visual history of Neimoidian culture
in any single location on Cato Neimoidia.

The Empire does not know it exists. The ventral city
prohibition has kept everyone away.

**Found here:**
In the reception desk alcove off the main entry —
a desk where a reception droid would have checked
in arriving delegates — there is a physical ledger.
A guest book. Open to a specific page.

The last entries, in neat administrative handwriting:

> *"Separatist Diplomatic Delegation, 7 members.*
> *Purpose: Cease-fire Negotiations.*
> *Arrived: [time]*
> *Departed: —"*

The departure line was never filled in.

## THE PALACE LEVELS — DETAILED

### Level 15 (Upper) — Reception and Support

The entry level, described above. Also contains:

**The Preparation Rooms:**
Small suites where delegations would prepare before
entering the meeting hall. Each suite is furnished
with Neimoidian diplomatic hospitality in mind:
comfortable seating, refreshment preparation stations,
private communication equipment (now dead).

The Separatist delegation used the western preparation suite.
It is still in use-condition: chairs positioned where
people sat, a few personal items left behind
(a coat, a communication device, a small personal object —
a good luck token, perhaps, or a religious item —
that someone set down and did not pick back up).

The Republic diplomatic team — Warde's position —
used the eastern preparation suite. It is empty.
Warde prepared alone. There is nothing personal in the room.
The furniture is arranged precisely, the communication
equipment has a single logged transmission —
a message to the Jedi Council before the meeting,
content not recoverable — and there is a single cup
on the preparation counter, empty, positioned with
the same precision as Haido's datapad.

**In the eastern preparation suite, on the wall:**
A standard diplomatic facility notation board —
the kind used for notes during complex negotiations.
It is still covered in Warde's handwriting.
Lines of Force notation intermixed with tactical
assessments in Basic. Most is not recoverable —
water damage, fifteen years of chemical lighting
exposure. But one section, protected by its position
against the interior wall:

> *"They are sincere. They believe the cease-fire
> will hold. They intend to honor it.*
>
> *The delegation believes it.*
> *The command structure does not.*
> *The gap between what the delegates believe
> and what the command structure intends is visible.*
> *It is not certain. It shifts every time I look at it.*
>
> *The future branches here. I cannot hold all of them.
> There are too many variables. Every time one of them
> moves I lose the shape of the rest.*
>
> *I am very tired.*
> *I have been very tired for a long time.*
> *I should tell someone that I am very tired.*
> *There is no one to tell."*

Below this, in different ink, written with the pressure
of someone pressing very hard:

> *"I know what I am going to do.*
> *I have known for three days.*
> *I have been trying to see a version of this
> where I do not do it.*
>
> *There are versions. There are always versions.*
> *I cannot make myself believe in them.*
>
> *I am sorry.*
> *I do not know who I am apologizing to.*
> *I am sorry anyway."*

**GM Note:**
This is the emotional core of the entire palace visit.
Everything else — the fight evidence, the bodies,
the droid encounter — is aftermath. This is the moment
before. This is what Warde knew and what he couldn't
stop himself from doing and the fact that he knew
he was about to do it and he was sorry before he did it.

Read this aloud if the party finds it.
Give it the silence it deserves after.

### Level 16 — The Conference Level

The main working level of the palace: conference rooms,
support offices, the primary meeting hall.

The conference rooms are intact. They were used for
preliminary discussions before the main negotiation —
working through logistical details, establishing
communication protocols, the administrative scaffolding
of formal diplomacy. The paperwork from these
preliminary sessions is still on the conference tables.
Draft language for the cease-fire terms.
Position papers.
A seating diagram for the main meeting hall.

The seating diagram, specifically: seven seats for
the Separatist delegation, three for the Republic
(Warde and the two other Jedi), with a neutral
observer position off to one side. The neutral
observer position is labeled "Tactical Support —
Republic Military." This position was probably N'got's
embedded cover identity.

**The Primary Meeting Hall:**

This is the room. The room where it happened.

The party has been working toward this room for
the entire Act 2 investigation. Every NPC they have
spoken to has pointed here. Every document they have
found has circled here. Every piece of the story
leads to this room.

The room needs to deserve that buildup.

> *The meeting hall is large enough for the formal
> purpose it was built for and not larger.*
> *The ceiling is high — perhaps six meters —
> but not cathedral. The canyon-facing wall is
> mostly window: floor-to-ceiling panels, several
> of which have been broken and are now open
> to the air and the sound of the canyon below.*
>
> *The furniture is still here.*
> *A central table — Keloythian oak, the same wood
> the module describes. It has been slashed and burned.
> The lightsaber cuts are not the clean lines of
> controlled weapon use. They are the cuts of combat:
> irregular, some deep, some shallow, some that
> began in one direction and changed.*
>
> *The chairs are overturned. Not all of them —
> some are upright, positioned where people were sitting
> when they stopped being people who were sitting.*
>
> *On the table: the residue of refreshments.*
> *Cups that were filled and not drunk.*
> *A datapad, cracked, dark.*
> *A personal effect — small, unidentifiable from
> across the room.*
>
> *At the head of the table: a chair that is not
> overturned. It is pulled back slightly.*
> *Deliberately, not in flight.*
> *As if someone stood up from it carefully.*
> *Warde's position.*

**The Bodies:**

The module establishes that Neimoidian remains are
still here — the delegation who did not survive.
Also the body of Jiv Durael.

The Neimoidian remains are in the main hall.
What happened to them is visible in the physical evidence —
the speed and completeness of Warde's attack, the
positions of the fallen, the evidence of the droid
bodyguards' response in the form of blaster damage
on the walls and on the Republic side of the room.

The droid bodyguards did not survive the initial attack.
Their remains are in pieces near the Separatist side
of the table — BX-series commando droids, the same type
the party has already encountered in the commerce level.
Whether the commerce level droids' odd behavior —
the fifteen-year loyalty to orders no one countermanded —
has anything to do with having been manufactured in
a facility that produced the droids that died here
is not something anyone can answer. It is worth noting.

**Jiv Durael:**

On the balcony. The module is specific about this.

> *The balcony extends from the meeting hall's
> canyon-facing wall through a pair of doors
> that are now permanently open — the mechanism
> has corroded in the open position.*
>
> *The balcony is in ruins. The flooring at the
> far end has collapsed. The railing is gone
> on the left side.*
>
> *Against the right wall, in the corner where
> the railing still holds, there is a skeleton
> in Jedi robes.*
>
> *The robes have the color and texture of fifteen years
> of exposure — they are faded, they are damaged,
> they are in the specific state of things that have
> spent a long time being rained on and dried
> and rained on again. But they are recognizably
> Jedi robes. And in the skeleton's right hand,
> fingers still curled around it in the grip
> of someone who held on even after holding on
> was no longer possible, is the next kyber crystal.*
>
> *The left hand is empty.*
> *Open.*
> *The lightsaber hilt is in two pieces nearby.*
> *The crystal housing has been removed.*
> *The hilt is otherwise intact — well-made,
> elegant in its proportions.*
>
> *Jiv Durael died here.*
> *Died fighting a friend.*
> *Held the crystal until he could not.*
> *Held it after that.*

**The Force in This Room:**

The Force stain the party has been feeling since Level 14
is concentrated here. Not overwhelmingly — it is not
a dark vergence, it is not Moraband, it is not the
kind of Force presence that breaks people.

It is more like a chord that has been struck and
has not stopped resonating. The events in this room
were significant enough that the Force has not
fully absorbed them. They persist as a kind of echo.

Force-sensitive characters who spend time in this room
experience this differently:

The Pathfinder experiences it primarily as emotional residue —
the specific quality of what was felt in this room
by the people in it. Fear from the delegation.
Rage and grief and certainty from Warde.
Horror from Moll and Durael as they understood
what was happening. She does not hear these as voices.
She feels them as impressions, the way you feel the
shape of a room that has been empty for a long time.

Sigg experiences it as a kind of disorientation —
the Force around him in this room has a texture
that interferes with his normal connection to it.
Not painfully. Like trying to see through slightly
imperfect glass. He is more alert than usual and
cannot identify what he is alert about.

If the Pathfinder has the Foresee power or the
Warde's Foresight power at any level, and she
uses it in this room, the images she receives
are not of the future. They are of the past.
The room is so saturated with the residue of what
happened that Foresight, which reads the Force's
relationship to possible events, reads this room's
history instead of its future.

She sees approximately ten seconds of what happened.
Not the violence — the moment before. Warde at
his position at the head of the table. The delegation
across from him. His face. His decision.

And the two seconds N'got described — the hesitation.

She sees the hesitation. She sees his face during it.
She does not know what he saw in that moment.
She sees that he was present and that something
in him was not committed and that something in him
overcame that and committed.

She can tell Sigg what she saw, or not.
What she cannot do is explain what the hesitation contained.
That is the one thing the Force in this room does not show.
The interior of those two seconds belongs to Warde alone.

## THE KYBER CRYSTAL — THE RECOVERY

The crystal is in Durael's hand. The module is clear
about this and the recovery is straightforward
in a mechanical sense and significant in every other sense.

The Pathfinder, specifically: if she takes the crystal
from Durael's hand, she feels the kyber's resonance.
Kyber crystals are Force-attuned. This one has been
in a Jedi Knight's grip through his death and has been
sitting in the Force stain of this room for fifteen years.
What it carries is complex. The Genesys/Force and Destiny
mechanic is that the crystal unlocks the next tier of
the Foresight power tree. The narrative texture is that
the crystal has absorbed something of what happened here
and the Pathfinder, as someone who reads through the Force,
may feel that.

Not trauma exactly. Not damage. Something that has been
through something significant and has survived it.
There is a quality of endurance in the crystal
that is almost animal in the way she can read it.

She might hold it for a moment before putting it away.
She might not. This is her character's choice.

## THE DROIDS — PALACE ENCOUNTER

If the BX commando droids arrive during the palace visit —
which they will if the party spends significant time here —
the encounter has a different texture here than it did
at the commerce level junction.

At the junction, the droids were in their established
territory and the encounter had the quality of a border
challenge. Here, the droids are in new territory —
the palace expansion of their patrol is recent —
and they are, in a subtle way, also responding to
the Force stain of the room.

Droids do not feel the Force. But they are programmed
to respond to significant events and the indicators
of significant events. The palace has a residue that
even non-Force-sensitive observers find unsettling.
The droids' behavior in the palace is slightly different
from their behavior at the junction — marginally less
certain, marginally more watchful.

This may be nothing. It may be that the droids, on
some level of their programming that was never intended
for this application, are responding to something
that their sensors cannot categorize.

The module's resolution options for the droids apply here.
The additional option, specific to the palace:

**The third resolution — the truthful place:**

If the party has resolved the junction encounter through
conversation and has explained that the war ended,
and if the droids are here at the palace:

The droids stop at the entrance to the meeting hall.
They look into the room. They look at Durael's remains.
They look at the Neimoidian remains. They process.

Then one of them says, in the flat affect of BX
commando droid speech:

> *"We were to protect these proceedings.*
> *We did not protect these proceedings.*
> *The individuals in this room are deceased.*
> *Our function in this location is complete.*
> *Our function is complete."*

A pause. The droids' sensor arrays continue scanning.

> *"The war is over.*
> *Our orders are expired.*
> *We have no current orders.*
> *What do we do."*

This question is directed at the party.
It is not rhetorical. They are actually asking.
They have been asking this question in their own
programming for fifteen years and have not been
able to answer it. They are asking the people
who told them the war is over because those people
might know what comes after the war is over
for entities who were built for the war.

The party can answer or not. The answer matters.

If they have no answer, the droids state:
> *"We will await further orders.*
> *We will return to the junction.*
> *If orders come, we will be there."*

And they return to the junction. This is not
a resolution. It is a deferral. But it is what
they have.

## LEAVING THE PALACE

### The Return

Getting back from the palace to the upper city
is the return of the full descent journey in reverse.
The party knows the route now. The stalker is
at the Level 15 threshold, where it waited.
The manta is still in the exhibition hall, or gone.
The droids are wherever the encounter left them.

What changes on the return journey: the party knows
what is in the room at the bottom. They know what
Warde wrote on the preparation suite wall. They know
what N'got's hesitation looked like from inside
the Force. They know Durael died on that balcony
and held the crystal until he couldn't and then held it longer.

The ventral city on the way back is the same
ventral city it was on the way down. The school mural
is the same. The community center inscription is the same.
The apartments with their frozen lives are the same.

It looks different anyway. It always does,
after you have been somewhere that changes the light.
