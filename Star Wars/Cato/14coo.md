# LEVEL 4: THE DIPLOMATIC CORRIDOR — LEVEL 14

---

## GM OVERVIEW

**Purpose:** This is the cultural and historical heart of the deep route. Level 14 represents the pinnacle of Trade Federation power and influence—a corridor designed to impress delegations, convey authority, and celebrate the Federation's dominance. Here, the party walks through fifteen hundred years of Neimoidian diplomatic history, culminating in a poignant detail that foreshadows the Federation's fall.

**Tone:** Grand, historical, tinged with melancholy. The corridor is beautiful and imposing, but it is also a monument to a power that no longer exists. The murals celebrate an empire that has crumbled. The party is walking through a museum of a dead civilization.

**Key GM Goals:**
- Immerse the party in Trade Federation culture and history
- Convey the grandeur and hubris of the Federation at its peak
- Foreshadow the Federation's fall through environmental storytelling
- Build the Force residue as a growing presence
- Establish the emotional weight of approaching Sankar Palace
- Keep the narrative engaging for players with attention challenges through varied sensory details

| Element | Details |
|---------|---------|
| **Location** | Level 14, Diplomatic Corridor, Sankar Palace Complex |
| **Length** | Approximately 50 meters of formal corridor |
| **Features** | Murals depicting 1500 years of Trade Federation history |
| **Time Since Evacuation** | 15 Years |
| **Key NPCs** | Sigg (present), The Force Ghost (encounter) |
| **Atmosphere** | Grand, historical, melancholic, weighted with significance |
| **Primary Challenge** | Processing the weight of history, resisting the growing Force residue |
| **Duration** | 45-60 minutes of session time |

## ENVIRONMENTAL DETAILS — THE DOOR

### The Threshold

The passage from the slope ends at a door—the most formal thing the party has encountered since leaving the Ossivane.

**GM Description:**
> *"The passage ends in a door. It is not like the rough-hewn doors of the maintenance corridor. This door is formal, crafted with care, designed to be seen."*

> *"The frame is stone, carefully fitted, with decorative work in the lintel—the stylized triangle-and-circle motif that appears throughout the diplomatic complex. The pattern repeats, a visual rhythm that draws the eye upward."*

> *"The symbol means, in the visual language the Trade Federation used for its formal spaces: 'What happens here matters.'"*

> *"The door is unlocked. It has a handle, and the handle turns. The door opens inward."*

### The Air

The air that comes through the door is different—cleaner, processed, preserved.

**GM Description:**
> *"The air that flows out of the doorway is different from the air you have been breathing. It is cooler, cleaner, filtered. The ventilation system in the diplomatic complex was built to higher standards than the residential levels above. It has been running on backup power for fifteen years, doing its job without anyone to know it is doing its job."*

> *"The cleaner air should be a relief. It is not, quite. It is too clean. It is the air of a place that has been sealed and preserved and kept exactly as it was."*

> *"It smells like fifteen years ago."*

## THE TRADE FEDERATION MURALS

### The Corridor

Level 14 is a wide corridor—wider than anything above it in the ventral city, designed for the movement of diplomatic parties.

**GM Description:**
> *"You step through the door, and the corridor opens before you. It is wide—four meters across, with a ceiling that rises to four meters. The floor is paved stone with a central runner of different material—darker, more refined—that marks the formal path from the service door to the reception area ahead."*

> *"The walls are decorated. Not with the abstract geometric patterns of the residential districts or the practical information of the commercial district. These are murals—painted onto plaster that was applied to the stone specifically to receive them."*

> *"They depict scenes from Neimoidian diplomatic history."*

### The Murals — A Walking History

The murals line both walls of the corridor, telling the story of the Trade Federation's rise and dominance.

**GM Description:**
> *"The murals begin at the door you entered through and continue all the way to the far end of the corridor. They are painted in rich colors—deep blues, golds, reds, greens—that have faded slightly over the years but remain vibrant."*

> *"Each scene is labeled in both Pak Pak and Basic. The figures are idealized—the Neimoidian diplomats in their finest robes, the counterpart species in their own formal dress, everyone making the gestures of successful negotiation."*

> *"These are not records of what happened. They are celebrations of what the Federation wanted to be."*

### The Scenes

| Scene | Description | Significance |
|-------|-------------|--------------|
| **The Founding** | Neimoidian diplomats signing the first trade agreements | The Federation's origin story |
| **The Expansion** | Trade routes spreading across the galaxy | The Federation's growth |
| **The Purse Worlds** | Cato Neimoidia and the other purse worlds at the center | The Federation's power base |
| **The Great Agreements** | Delegations from across the galaxy meeting in harmony | The Federation's influence |
| **The Peak** | Trade routes radiating from Cato Neimoidia across the galaxy | The Federation's dominance |

**Sigg's Observation:**
> *"Sigg walks beside you, his eyes moving over the murals. 'They built this to impress,' he says. 'They built this to last. They wanted everyone who walked here to know: this is who we are. This is what we have built.'"*

> *"'And now it's empty. No one walks here. No one sees it. The murals are just... here. Waiting for someone who will never come.'"*

### The Last Panel

The last panel is the largest, depicting the Trade Federation at its peak.

**GM Description:**
> *"The last panel is the largest. It shows the Trade Federation's peak period: trade routes radiating from Cato Neimoidia across the galaxy. The accumulated commerce of three hundred years, all of it flowing through this planet, this city."*

> *"The composition is triumphant. Figures celebrate in the foreground. Ships cross the starfield in the background. The Federation's symbol is prominent at the center, a sunburst of power and wealth."*

> *"And in the lower right corner, at the foot of the composition, there is a small detail that would be easy to overlook."*

### The Watching Figure

A single figure, separate from the celebration, looking outward from the painting rather than at the event being depicted.

**GM Description:**
> *"You almost miss it. It is small, tucked into the corner, easy to overlook. But once you see it, you cannot unsee it."*

> *"A single figure, standing apart from the celebration. The robes are simple, not the ornate robes of the diplomats. The posture is quiet, contemplative. And the figure is looking outward—looking at you, looking at the viewer, looking at the world outside the painting."*

> *"The expression is impossible to read. It might be sadness. It might be resignation. It might be the knowledge that what is being celebrated will not last."*

**Sigg's Observation:**
> *"Sigg stands in front of the panel, his eyes fixed on the figure. He is quiet for a long moment."*

> *"'Someone knew,' he says. 'Someone knew it would end.'"*

> *"'Or someone was worried it would end,' you say. 'And wanted to record that worry.'"*

> *"'Fifteen years ago, they were right. It ended.'"*

## THE FORCE RESIDUE

### The Growing Presence

The Force residue that the party has been feeling since the slope is stronger here.

**GM Description:**
> *"The Force residue is stronger here. You felt it on the slope, but now it is unmistakable. It is not painful. It is not the dark side in the active sense—it is more like the impression left by the dark side in the specific way that wood holds the impression of something heavy placed on it for a long time."*

> *"What you feel most clearly is not the event itself. It is the approach to the event. This corridor was where the delegation walked—where Warde walked, where Moll and Durael walked—toward what happened in the room below."*

> *"The anticipation is still here somehow. Hope, from the delegation's side. Dread, from Warde's. The two things walking toward each other through this corridor."*

### Sigg's Response

**GM Description:**
> *"Sigg stops behind you. He does not speak. He waits."*

> *"After a moment, you start walking again. You do not explain what you felt. He does not ask."*

**Sigg's Observation:**
> *"'I can feel something,' Sigg says. 'I can't feel it the way you do—it's fainter, less specific. But I can feel it. Like the weight of something that happened here. Something that is still happening.'"*

## NPC INTERACTIONS — STORY AND CULTURE

### Sigg in the Corridor

Sigg is moved by the murals. He has seen the Trade Federation's power and its fall, and he recognizes the hubris on display.

**Sigg's State:**
- Quiet, reflective, thoughtful
- Moving slowly through the corridor
- Taking time to look at each mural
- Speaking thoughtfully, when he speaks

**Sigg's Observations:**

**On the Murals:**
> *"'They built this to celebrate themselves. They wanted everyone who walked here to know: we are powerful. We are permanent. We will last forever.'"*

> *"'And now they're gone. The power is gone. The permanence is gone. All that's left is what they built and the damage they did.'"*

**On the Watching Figure:**
> *"'Someone knew. Someone in the corner, watching. Someone who understood that it wouldn't last.'"*

> *"'I wonder who they were. A worker? An artist? Someone who saw what the Federation was becoming?'"*

**On the Force Residue:**
> *"'This place feels heavy. Like the air is thick with something that happened a long time ago.'"*

**If the Party Asks About the Trade Federation:**
> *"'The Trade Federation was the most powerful force in the galaxy. They controlled trade, influence, wealth. They thought they were untouchable. And then the war came. And the Empire came. And now they're nothing.'"*

> *"'These murals are their monument. A monument to a power that no longer exists.'"*

### The Force Ghost — A Hint of What's to Come

The Force Ghost does not appear fully in this level, but the party may feel its presence—a whisper at the edge of perception.

**GM Description:**
> *"You feel something. A presence, at the edge of your awareness. Not threatening, not yet—just... present. Like someone is watching from the shadows."*

> *"You turn, but there is nothing there. The corridor is empty. The murals are still. The shadows are still."*

> *"But you know something was there. Something that has been waiting."*

**If the Pathfinder Reaches Out:**
> *"You reach out with the Force, and you feel a whisper of something—cold, ancient, patient. It does not respond. It simply... waits."*

> *"It is not hostile. Not yet. But it is watching."*

## ATMOSPHERE NOTES

### Running This Level

- **Pace:** Slow, contemplative, allowing time to absorb the murals
- **Tone:** Grand, historical, melancholic
- **Player Engagement:** This level is about discovery and absorption; the players should feel the weight of history and the sadness of what was lost
- **Narrative Importance:** This level establishes the Trade Federation's culture and hubris, setting up the tragedy of its fall

### Attention-Span Considerations

For players with attention challenges:
- Break the corridor into sections—describe two or three murals at a time
- Use Sigg's dialogue to break up descriptions
- Include sensory details (sight, sound, smell) to maintain engagement
- Allow players to ask questions and explore at their own pace

### Music Suggestions

- Grand, orchestral pieces with a melancholic undercurrent
- Music that suggests loss and fading glory
- Subtle ambient sounds (echoing footsteps, distant wind)
- Low strings or brass that build tension without drama
