# Master GM Continuity - Ventral City to Sankar Palace

## The physical truth players should eventually understand

Sankar is not underground and it is not a conventional tower. Jorra's bridge-city is above it. The palace drops from that massive structure into the canyon like a chandelier whose lower pieces have broken away. Levels 12-16 are progressively farther from the bridge foundation. The palace's formal spine is crossed by service arteries, exterior galleries, anchor machinery, and later damage from the battle that followed Warde's attack.

Make the geometry legible through play:

- At the approach, let the PCs see the palace **below** walkways they have already crossed.
- On Level 13, show support cables going upward toward the bridge and downward/sideways toward anchor masses.
- On Level 14, a broken service schematic should use a vertical diagram with the bridge at the top and the palace hanging below it.
- On Level 15, pressure sensors wake lights **ahead and below** the party.
- On Level 16, a broken exterior window reveals only fog, nearby trusses, and occasional silhouettes of lower palace structures.
- In SP19, the anchor cavern finally explains how the whole impossible structure stays attached.

## The story Sankar tells

Sankar's story is not "Warde went evil here." It is a sequence of narrowing human scale.

### Public culture - Level 12

The party meets the Neimoidians as a civilization. The Exhibition Hall has been carefully stripped of portable valuables, but the mosaic floor, plaques, wear marks, labels, children's traces, and architecture remain. The lesson is that a culture was here before the war reduced it to a stereotype.

### Institutions and choices - Level 13

The Diplomatic Quarter shows that hospitality was engineered. Rooms were built for different bodies and customs. The archive demonstrates that the cease-fire delegation possessed real authority, had been warned of danger, and came anyway. The party begins separating **the delegates' intent** from **the command structure's intent**.

### History becomes memory - Level 14

The mural gallery tells the version of history the Trade Federation wanted remembered. Small flaws, later scratches, missing names, and the red unlabelled world turn propaganda into archaeology. The Ghost Chamber then stops being about what the institution said and becomes about what people experienced. The party learns that memory can persist even when records fail.

### Warde becomes a person - Level 15

The palace wakes around the party through pressure sensors and chemical lights. In Warde's preparation suite the story contracts to one chair, one cup, a dead terminal, and a notation board. His notes show that he understood more than the witnesses realized: the delegates were sincere, the larger military situation was not, the future branched, and he had alternatives he could no longer make himself trust. He knew the act he was preparing to commit and apologized before doing it.

### Consequence becomes evidence - Level 16

Conference rooms preserve process. The final approach preserves old orders. The meeting hall preserves geometry: chair positions, attack angles, blaster scars, lightsaber cuts, bodies, droid wreckage, and Jiv Durael on the balcony. The PCs should be able to reconstruct the event before any Force vision confirms it.

### The thematic conclusion

Warde's foresight did not force him to fall. He treated a feared possibility as certainty, reduced people to components of a future event, and then chose to create the future he feared. The palace should leave the PCs with a practical warning: **seeing a possible future is not the same as being entitled to decide for everyone living inside it.**

## Warde timeline used by this pack

1. Warde serves on Cato Neimoidia and is respected by troops, but accumulated losses and responsibility wear him down.
2. His foresight repeatedly shows betrayal after the cease-fire. He warns the other Jedi and becomes increasingly certain that catastrophe is inevitable.
3. Evidence in Sankar suggests the delegates themselves are sincere even if forces above them may intend to exploit the cease-fire.
4. Warde prepares alone. He records branching futures, fatigue, his intent to act, and an apology.
5. In the meeting hall he hesitates for approximately two seconds, then attacks the Separatist delegation.
6. Kaddo Moll and Jiv Durael intervene. Both are mortally struck in the confrontation; Durael dies in the palace. Moll survives long enough to send warning and dies nearby. Warde is also badly wounded.
7. Warde flees Jorra. Witnesses can supply a departure direction, but **the palace crystal/Gatekeeper provides the final reliable confirmation of Moraband as his destination.**
8. Jiv Durael dies with Warde's kyber crystal in hand, preserving the evidence and the next stage of the Gatekeeper quest.

## Three simultaneous "voices" of the palace

Use these on every level so the location feels alive.

| Voice | What it communicates | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Institution** | What the Federation wanted people to believe | mosaics, plaques, seating protocol, formal architecture |
| **Workers and visitors** | What daily life was actually like | comfort notes, maintenance shorthand, worn cushions, personal objects, guest-book errors |
| **Aftermath** | What war, looting, weather, and abandoned systems did | blast scars, stripped cases, scavenged power cells, water paths, droid repairs, fog intrusion |

A good room contains at least two voices. A great room contains all three.

## Environmental clue rule

For every major revelation, provide three evidence types:

1. **Physical evidence** - something visible in the room.
2. **Document/system evidence** - a record, terminal, schedule, maintenance log, or archive.
3. **Emotional/Force evidence** - an impression, resonance, ghostly presence, or Warde's Foresight reaction.

Players can miss any one channel and still reconstruct the story.

## Droids as a living-world faction

Use the published force as the available baseline pool: surviving BX commandos with a droideka as the final heavy threat, scaled for the party. Do not deploy them all at once unless the PCs deliberately create that situation.

The droids should behave as if they have spent years maintaining an obsolete mission:

- scavenged power cells labeled by date;
- repair patches made from incompatible battle-droid parts;
- patrol routes that still protect doors no one uses;
- challenge phrases that reference a government that no longer exists;
- hesitation when confronted with proof that the war is over;
- fallback routes toward original security posts rather than arbitrary tactical retreat.

A non-combat resolution should be possible if the PCs can prove the proceedings ended and the old command structure no longer exists. If the droids cannot accept that proof, their failure should feel like tragic programming rather than stupidity.

## Fog behavior

Roll or choose a fog state whenever the party moves onto an exterior/broken-wing map:

| d6 | Fog state | Effect |
|---:|---|---|
| 1 | Thin veil | long shapes become visible; expose a route or distant support |
| 2 | Drifting bands | visibility changes every few rounds; conceal/reveal alternates |
| 3 | Wet fog | surfaces become slick; add setback to rushed movement |
| 4 | Sound-carrying | distant machinery/droids seem closer than they are |
| 5 | Dead quiet | sound is muffled; Perception by hearing becomes unreliable |
| 6 | Pressure surge | fog pushes through breaches and moves loose debris; reveal airflow and hidden openings |

Use fog to reveal architecture, not just obscure it.
