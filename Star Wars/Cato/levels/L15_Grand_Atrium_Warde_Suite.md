# Level 15 - The Palace Wakes; Warde Becomes Personal

**Maps:** SP08 Grand Atrium & Preparation Suites; SP09 Warde's Preparation Suite; SP10 Service Gallery & Chemical-Light Works  
**Core question:** *What did Warde know before he stood up from the table?*  
**Tone:** reawakening, intimacy, controlled dread.

## The arrival - reflection and pressure sensor

Use the stair/reflection beat from the custom drafts: before the party sees Warde's room, let them see themselves in the dark glass. The palace is becoming a story about people making choices in rooms; the PCs are also people making choices in rooms.

A pressure sensor wakes one light, then another. Do not turn every light on instantly. The building should appear to remember a routine.

## Chemical-light ignition sequence

Each lit zone tells a small story:

1. stair/threshold - a route remembered;
2. reception - someone expected visitors;
3. atrium mosaic - institutional pride;
4. preparation wing - private work before public performance;
5. service indicator - the hidden machinery behind ceremony.

If the party repairs SP10 first, they can choose the order and thereby change the tone of the level.

## Grand atrium

### Reception desk

The guest book should contain arrivals without matching departures. An administrative system may still be waiting for checkout/closure. If you use an inactive droid, let it awaken confused but helpful rather than immediately hostile.

### The mosaic

Use a more formal political mosaic than Level 12. Repeat motifs so players recognize the same culture at a different institutional scale. The red restricted-world marker may recur here as a visual callback, but do not make it the scene's focus.

## Western suite - the delegation's room

The suite should feel occupied in absence:

- several seats used differently;
- a small homeworld stone set where its owner could see it;
- unfinished notes;
- comfort adjustments requested by staff;
- a personal message or childish "come home soon" tucked among formal materials.

The function is to make the people Warde killed specific without requiring full biographies.

## Eastern suite - Warde's room

Keep it sparse. The custom draft's strength is its restraint.

- one chair;
- one table;
- one dead communication terminal;
- one cup;
- exact alignment;
- notation board.

The room should feel like someone trying to impose order on themselves.

## The notation board - revelation ladder

Do not dump all of it at once if the players want to investigate.

### Layer 1 - analytical truth

Warde distinguishes the delegates' sincerity from the danger he sees in the larger future. This is the critical complication: he was not simply fooled into thinking every person across the table was lying.

### Layer 2 - cognitive overload

He records branching futures and an inability to hold all of them. He is exhausted and aware of his exhaustion.

### Layer 3 - premeditation

He records that he has decided what he will do and has been searching for a branch where he does not do it. Alternatives exist. He no longer trusts them.

### Layer 4 - apology

He apologizes before the event without knowing who the apology is for. This does not absolve him. It establishes that conscience survived long enough to understand the act.

## Dead Council transmission

The terminal should show that Warde attempted or prepared a message to the Jedi Council before the meeting, but the full content is unrecoverable. Keep it fragmentary so the notation board remains the direct private evidence.

Possible recoverable metadata:

- transmission time;
- destination channel;
- unusually long draft duration;
- multiple canceled send attempts;
- final status ambiguous: queued, failed, or deleted.

## Puzzle - Three-Reservoir Chemical Light Works

Use Puzzle 5 in SP10. Let success affect later scenes: improved light can remove setback, reveal evidence, or expose the party to droid sightlines.

## Distant movement

After the Warde board lands, introduce one mechanical sound below. This is pacing, not a jump scare. It reminds the table that the palace has physical danger and prevents the emotional scene from becoming static exposition.

## What the party should leave knowing

- Warde was exhausted, not mindless.
- He could see more than one future.
- He believed the larger military future was dangerous.
- He also knew the delegates were sincere.
- He prepared to act anyway.
- He was sorry before he acted.
- Something below is still moving.
