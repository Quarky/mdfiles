## Expanded GM Session Pack: Grand Atrium, Preparation Suites, and Chemical-Light Works

# 1. PURPOSE AND CONTINUITY

Level 15 is where Warde stops being a historical name and becomes a person whose choices can be examined.

The levels above have introduced the palace’s culture, politics, victims, and supernatural aftermath. Here, the party enters the rooms used immediately before the final negotiations.

They discover people preparing to continue their lives:

- Staff arranged meals for after the meeting.
- A delegate left a coat because the conference was supposed to be brief.
- An administrator maintained a departure ledger.
- Someone repaired a chair instead of replacing it.
- Warde sat alone, examining futures he increasingly refused to believe could end differently.

The level’s central question is:

> **What happens when someone can imagine several futures but begins treating only the worst one as real?**

Do not answer that question through a single speech. Let the rooms, records, choices, and players’ own disagreements develop it.

# 2. RUNNING THE SESSION

## 2.1 Three things players should understand before leaving

1. **The delegation expected negotiations and a return to ordinary life.**
2. **Warde recognized uncertainty and alternatives, yet anticipated taking the action that would destroy those people.**
3. **The final meeting must still be examined below. Level 15 establishes preparation and intention, not a complete forensic account of the confrontation.**

These discoveries are not locked behind checks.

If characters inspect the obvious papers, ledger, and board, give them the essential information. Checks reveal additional context, improve preservation, or resolve risks around the investigation.

## 2.2 Suggested session structure

### One-session version: approximately 3–4 hours

| Segment | Approximate time | Purpose |
|---|---:|---|
| Arrival and lights | 10–15 minutes | Reawaken the palace and establish the new atmosphere. |
| Atrium and reception | 30–40 minutes | Discover the unfinished visit; choose whether to restore the droid. |
| Western suite | 30–40 minutes | Meet the delegates through their belongings and unfinished work. |
| Service gallery | 35–50 minutes | Solve a practical problem and discover the people who maintained the palace. |
| Warde’s suite | 35–50 minutes | Read, investigate, and discuss the central evidence. |
| Consequences and descent | 15–25 minutes | Resolve player choices and establish the Level 16 approach. |

These are pacing guides, not mandatory timers.

### Two-session version

**Session 15A — The People Who Expected to Leave**

Arrival, reception, western suite, and service works. End with the eastern suite available for exploration and a question raised by its silence.

**Session 15B — The Man Who Expected the Worst**

Warde’s suite, deeper investigation, an optional encounter, preservation choices, and the descent.

If the players enter Warde’s room early, let them. Do not lock it to enforce the preferred order.

## 2.3 Three entry states

### A. The seven were released on Level 14

The absence of their collective presence is perceptible to a character who previously sensed them. The remaining presence feels distinct.

### B. The seven were encountered but not released

Their residue remains behind. The presence below is still distinguishable, but the party has not brought closure to the earlier encounter.

Do not punish them with a combat encounter for leaving. Their unfinished business is enough.

### C. The Ghost Chamber was bypassed

Pelri’s stone is initially an unidentified personal possession. Her name can be connected to it through the western suite’s papers.

Sigg does not know its owner by intuition.

## 2.4 Character agency

- Ask what the characters notice and do.
- Describe Force impressions without dictating the character’s emotional response.
- Use Sigg’s dialogue as optional prompts, not required speeches.
- If Sigg is player-controlled, give his player relevant observations and let them choose his words.
- The Pathfinder may share or conceal impressions.
- Respect earlier decisions about whether the Rusted Stalker enters the palace. If it remains above, record who is attending it and where the party intends to reunite.

# 3. THE LEVEL AS A REAL PLACE

## 3.1 What Level 15 used to do

Level 15 handled the transition between public ceremony and closed negotiations.

Visitors arrived through the Grand Atrium. Reception recorded their presence and directed them into preparation suites. Staff distributed briefing materials, checked translation equipment, adjusted hospitality arrangements, and coordinated the conference level below.

The architecture expresses two different kinds of work:

**The ceremonial route:**

Wide corridors, formal sightlines, comfortable waiting spaces, immaculate public finishes.

**The service route:**

Narrower passages, replaceable panels, drainage channels, tool access, delivery hatches, and places where employees could sit without being watched by delegates.

The service route explains how the ceremonial route functioned.

## 3.2 Why so much survives

Different materials survived differently.

- Sealed chemical cartridges remained dormant until activated.
- Physical ledgers survived in a dry, sheltered reception alcove.
- Exterior fabrics rotted; protected folded papers survived unevenly.
- Moisture damaged writing near outer walls.
- Dark terminals retained only limited local records.
- Cabinet doors protected their contents from drifting dust.
- Staff repairs outlasted some expensive decorative fittings.

Do not describe every object as pristine or every system as broken.

A palace becomes believable when damage follows its construction.

## 3.3 What is happening now

The party’s arrival changes the level.

- Dormant lights begin consuming their remaining cartridges.
- Old relay mechanisms transmit local signals.
- A failing drain starts carrying newly released condensation.
- A restored terminal adds load to an unreliable electrical bus.
- Fresh footprints cross spaces that have been undisturbed for years.
- If connected security droids survive below, certain signals may attract attention.

The palace is responsive through machinery and environment. It does not need to be secretly conscious.

## 3.4 Small signs of ordinary life

Use one or two per room.

| Detail | What it suggests |
|---|---|
| A polished indentation beneath the reception desk | Someone repeatedly hooked a foot around the same support while working. |
| A drawer labeled “VISITOR STYLUSES” containing mismatched pens | Official presentation depended on ordinary improvisation. |
| A cup marked “NOT FOR SERVICE” | A staff member wanted one object to remain theirs. |
| A replacement chair foot cut from darker material | The room remained presentable because someone repaired it. |
| A small tally of shifts scratched inside a service cupboard | Work continued in a place visitors never noticed. |
| A translator earpiece wrapped for repair | Delegates depended on imperfect equipment and patient technicians. |
| A sleeve protector beside handwritten forms | Someone spent long hours copying records. |
| A folded cushion behind a console | A person rested here without a proper break room. |
| A joke crossed out on a staff notice | The staff disagreed about what was funny. |
| Two versions of a menu | Someone remembered that an important guest could not eat the first one. |

These details do not all become quests. Their value is that the world contains lives beyond the investigation.

# 5. L15-01 AND L15-02 — ARRIVAL AND AWAKENING

## 5.1 Read aloud

> The dark stone of the upper landing gives way to pale, dense slabs. A narrow seam of blue runs beneath the handrail.
>
> Halfway down, an intact window reflects your lights.
>
> Beyond it, fog moves around the silhouette of a support descending past the glass. You cannot see where the support ends.
>
> The stair continues downward.
>
> At its foot, a line of unlit sconces waits along the corridor.

If the seven were released:

> The gathered presence from the chamber above has fallen away.
>
> Something remains below, distinct from what you encountered there.

Do not identify that remaining presence here.

## 5.2 The reflection window

The window contains three useful observations.

### A. The structure is suspended

A support outside carries a maintenance plate. Its directional arrow points upward toward the bridge anchorage.

Anyone looking closely can establish that the support carries the palace from above.

### B. The window was maintained

A strip along its lower edge was replaced with a slightly different material.

A small handwritten mark on the inner frame reads:

> “Still clear enough. Replace the cracked corridor panels first.”

This is a new staff detail, not a hidden Warde clue.

### C. The outside is not a usable shortcut without preparation

A maintenance rail runs below the glass, but vanishes around the outer wall.

Fog conceals the next anchor point. Condensation runs along the metal.

Characters can investigate or plan an exterior route. Tell them the visible risks before asking for a traversal roll.

## 5.3 The pressure sensor

A broad threshold plate activates the arrival sequence.

It was designed for visitors and wheeled service equipment, not as a trap.

**If the party notices it first:**

Allow inspection. A visible mechanical linkage connects it to a relay box. A character can activate it by hand.

**If they step on it:**

> A click travels beneath the floor.
>
> The first sconce brightens with a faint chemical hiss.
>
> A second follows farther down the corridor. Then another.
>
> Purple-white light reaches the atrium and opens across pale stone, dark metal, and a floor threaded with gold.
>
> Near your feet, one fixture flickers twice and stays dim.

Pause and ask:

> “What do you look at first?”

## 5.4 Practical discoveries

No check is required to notice the uneven fixture.

An **Average Mechanics check** can identify:

- The chemical lamps are separate from the main electrical supply.
- Activation uses a small control system.
- One return line is restricted.
- The lights can function while terminals remain dead.

On failure, the character still locates a service symbol directing them toward the works. They simply cannot diagnose the problem from here.

**Suggested Advantage:** Identify which lamp bank is affected.

**Suggested Threat:** The inspection releases a sharp odor and exposes a minor leak. It becomes an obvious issue to repair, not immediate damage.

# 6. L15-03 — THE GRAND ATRIUM

## 6.1 Read aloud

> The atrium opens roughly thirty meters across, beneath a ceiling six meters above the floor.
>
> A mosaic covers the central space. Trade routes spread outward from the purse worlds in fine bands of metal and colored stone.
>
> Reception stands to the right. Two broad corridors lead toward preparation rooms on opposite sides.
>
> Along one wall, a narrow line interrupts the decorative symmetry: a service door, painted to disappear.
>
> A trolley stands beside it with one wheel turned sideways.

## 6.2 The mosaic

Preserve the red, restricted world marking from the earlier drafts.

Let characters inspect:

- The purse worlds at the center.
- Trade routes and commercial relationships.
- Areas left outside the trade network.
- Repairs to tiles along a frequently used path.
- The restricted marking at the outer edge.

### Player-facing inscription

> RESTRICTED  
> NO COMMERCIAL ACTIVITY  
> HISTORICAL SIGNIFICANCE ONLY  
> CONSULT DIPLOMATIC RECORDS BEFORE CONTACT

### What it establishes

The palace’s makers included a restricted location in their institutional geographic display.

### What it does not establish

It does not prove:

- Every employee knew Sith history.
- The delegates knew Warde would travel there.
- The floor provides a safe hyperspace route.
- The palace was built around a secret Sith agenda.

If the party already identified the location as Moraband, this is corroboration.

If they have not, record it as **the restricted red world**. Its identification remains a later discovery.

## 6.3 The repair that interrupts the grandeur

One trade-route line contains a tile of the wrong shade.

Beside it, nearly invisible unless someone kneels, are tiny workshop marks.

A maintenance slip in the reception drawer explains:

> “Replacement batch rejected by ceremonial office. Installed anyway. Original fracture was catching walking aids.”

The slip is signed with initials, not a famous name.

This is the first clear sign that the palace’s staff sometimes chose usefulness over appearance.

## 6.4 The stranded trolley

The trolley carries:

- A sealed package of clean writing sheets.
- Translation earpiece housings.
- An empty hot-drink carrier.
- Seven folded place cards with the names no longer readable.
- A maintenance tag identifying the jammed wheel.

The wheel can be repaired without a roll if the party takes time and has basic tools.

The trolley can then carry recovered evidence, a disabled droid, or supplies.

**Future consequence:** If used for evidence, it is cumbersome on the service stair. The party must repack or choose the broader ceremonial route.

## 6.5 Optional conversation prompt

If a companion is needed to start discussion:

> “They were still bringing things in.”

Stop there. Let the party decide what that means.

> ARRIVAL: RECORDED

## 7.2 What can be found without a roll

- An emergency service key with a physical grip.
- A public room directory.
- A maintenance card showing the reception droid’s external power socket.
- A drawer of ordinary visitor badges.
- An unsigned end-of-shift form.
- A replacement nib wrapped in tissue.

## 7.3 The unsigned end-of-shift form

**New handout:**

> BEFORE CLOSING RECEPTION:
>
> Confirm all delegations have departed.
>
> Return unclaimed belongings to secure storage.
>
> Record staff still on duty.
>
> Leave the arrival lights in standby.
>
> Do not close a visit on assumption.

That last instruction becomes important if the droid is restored.

## 7.4 Restoring the reception droid

This is optional.

### What is wrong

The droid’s onboard cell is exhausted. Its body remains seated because the chair’s docking connector supplied power while reception was active.

A surviving local memory module retains reception records. There is no complete recording of the massacre.

### Restoration options

| Approach | Requirement | Outcome |
|---|---|---|
| External power | Suitable supply and a few minutes | The droid operates while connected. |
| Repair docking connector | Average Mechanics; tools | Restores a stable connection if the service bus is active. |
| Remove memory module | Average Mechanics | Makes local reception records available through a compatible reader. |
| Carry it out | Suitable assistance or trolley | Allows later repair without delaying the session. |

A failed repair does not erase the ledger or the core story.

Suggested failure consequence: the connector needs a replacement lead, available in the works.

Do not require a second identical roll after the party obtains the missing part.

## 7.5 First words

> “Welcome to Sankar Palace.”
>
> The droid’s gaze moves between you and the corridor.
>
> “Your appointment does not appear in the current schedule.”
>
> A pause.
>
> “One delegation remains in session. Would you prefer to wait?”

## 7.6 Personality

Use a courteous, observant administrator.

It has habits:

- Straightens the ledger before answering a difficult question.
- Asks names before discussing personal belongings.
- Distinguishes observations from assumptions.
- Notices whether the party is cold, wet, or carrying someone injured.
- Offers practical help before discussing institutional procedure.

It is not a joke about bureaucracy. Its rules once protected visitors.

## 7.7 What it knows

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| Who used the west suite? | The visiting delegation. |
| Who used the east suite? | Suljo Warde. |
| Did the delegates arrive armed? | Reception did not conduct security screening. It cannot answer. |
| Did they expect to stay? | Their hospitality arrangements included a later meal and possible continued discussions. |
| Did Warde leave his room? | His preparation appointment ended before the conference session. |
| What happened below? | Its local records end before it received a verified conclusion. |
| Can it identify the red world? | Its geographic reference package is unavailable. It can preserve the inscription for later comparison. |
| Can it unlock security below? | No. Reception badges and military authorization are different systems. |
| Can it guide them? | It knows public rooms and staff access points on this level. |
| Where is the crystal? | It has no relevant inventory record. |

## 7.8 Encounter: “How should I record them?”

If the party explains that the delegation died:

> “I understand your statement.”
>
> Its fingers rest beside the empty departure column.
>
> “I have no departure to enter.”
>
> “How should I record them?”

There is no required answer.

### If the party says “deceased”

It asks whether they have identified all seven individuals.

If not, it proposes:

> “Reported deceased. Identification incomplete. Visit unresolved.”

### If the party says to leave the ledger unchanged

It complies and can attach the party’s statement separately.

### If the party invents a departure time

It asks what that time represents.

If they knowingly insist, it records their instruction as an annotation rather than silently converting it into observed fact.

### If they promise to return after Level 16

It requests:

- Names, if recoverable.
- Identification of personal property.
- A distinction between what the party saw and what they inferred.

### If they shut it down again

It asks:

> “Will someone return to finish the record?”

The players may answer honestly, reassure it, or say nothing.

**GM purpose:** Give the party responsibility for how the dead will be remembered without making one response the hidden correct solution.

## 8.1 Read aloud

> Six chairs surround a low table.
>
> A seventh, folded, rests beside a wall cabinet.
>

>

The extra chair and cup reconcile the existing delegation count with the original room description.

## 8.2 Pelri Dass’s stone

Preserve the stone as an ordinary personal object.

- Smooth.
- Palm-sized.
- Worn by handling.
- No inherent Force power.
- No hidden mechanical key.

If the party learned its ownership above, recognition is immediate.

Otherwise, a nearby preparation sheet has a small note:

> “Pelri — your stone is holding the amendments again.”

The note establishes ownership without requiring psychic knowledge.

### Possible choices

**Leave it:**  
The party preserves the room as found.

**Take it for return:**  
Record who carries it and what they intend to do.

**Use it as evidence:**  
It helps connect the Level 14 impression to the physical preparation room.

**Treat it as valuable loot:**  
It has negligible monetary value. Let that fact emerge plainly.

Do not impose supernatural punishment for moving it.

## 8.3 The delegation’s work

The papers are damaged but contain several readable proposed clauses.

**New handout:**

> WORKING TERMS — NOT RATIFIED
>
> Civilian movement to resume through designated corridors.
>
> Medical consignments to be inspected without indefinite detention.
>
> Disputed prisoner identities to be reviewed by representatives of both parties.
>
> Local utility repairs to proceed during the suspension of hostilities.
>
> Implementation schedule to be agreed in session.

### What the papers reveal

The delegates prepared substantive terms addressing ordinary consequences of war.

The text does not prove every term would have succeeded.

A character familiar with administration, negotiation, or wartime logistics can recognize practical details without a check.

An **Average Knowledge (Education) check**, or another relevant approach, can reveal:

- The proposals account for specific implementation problems.
- Several changes would require concessions from both sides.
- These are working documents, not a finished propaganda statement.

**Suggested Advantage:** Identify an issue that directly echoes a person or hardship already encountered in the ventral city.

Only make that connection if the earlier campaign established it.

## 8.4 The coat and drawing

## 8.5 A private datapad

The original drafts include a locked personal datapad.

Expand it as an optional privacy and evidence choice.

### Visible without unlocking

Its outer sleeve contains a paper label:

> “Personal correspondence. Working draft copied to shared folder.”

The cease-fire evidence is already available on the table.

### Options

- Preserve the device unopened.
- Attempt an **Average Computers check** to access surviving data.
- Use a specialist tool or legitimate credential found later.
- Return it to an identified recipient if one is eventually established.

### Successful access

Recover three fragments:

1. A mundane packing reminder.
2. An unfinished message about the negotiations.
3. An appointment the writer expected to keep after returning home.

**New text for the unfinished message:**

> “We argued over the corridor wording again. I think we finally have something they can accept.”
>
> “I know you dislike it when I promise a return date. I have not promised one.”
>
> “Keep the evening free anyway.”

This is new homebrew characterization, not proof of all external military intentions.

### Failed access

The reader cannot reconstruct the file directory with available tools. The pad remains intact.

Suggested Threat: a read attempt drains its remaining emergency cell; further work requires external power.

Do not destroy the files merely because the first attempt failed.

## 8.6 The room’s absent seventh person

The folding chair offers a small reconstruction puzzle.

The public ledger lists seven delegates. Six chairs and cups are immediately apparent. The seventh cup is smaller and separately placed.

Clues:

- The folding chair has scuff marks matching a space beside the door.
- A preparation sheet includes seven separate initial marks.
- The pantry request says: “One guest prefers to stand during briefing; retain seat.”

**Resolution:** Seven people used the suite. One moved or stood during preparation.

**Purpose:** Establish that physical scenes require investigation before conclusions. The room is not evidence that the seventh delegate was absent or an impostor.

No reward requires solving this. It trains the method the party will need below.

## 9.5 A practical cache

A sealed maintenance drawer contains:

- Two insulated leads.
- An intact hand lamp.
- A small mirror on an articulated stem.
- Absorbent cloths.
- A seal suitable for the works’ damaged return coupling.

These are tools, not high-value treasure.

If the party searched carefully, give them the drawer without a roll.

## 9.6 A glimpse of Warde before the final day

Inside an older maintenance folder is a copied service request.

**New homebrew handout:**

> REQUEST: Temporary extension of civilian waiting area.
>
> Existing seating insufficient during checkpoint reviews.
>
> Authorization appended: S. Warde.
>
> Note: Provide water before further interviews.

A later sheet uses the same administrative format:

> REQUEST: Additional holding-space partitions.
>
> Release conditions awaiting command clarification.

The later request is not signed clearly enough to attribute it to Warde.

### Interpretation

The earlier record supports a concrete act of care by Warde.

The later record shows a worsening institutional situation but does not, by itself, establish who ordered it.

This adds complexity without converting a sympathetic detail into an excuse for the killings.

### Connection to Tacker

If Tacker described Warde’s early concern for people, this independently supports that portion of his testimony.

If the party never met Tacker, it works on its own.

# 11. L15-08 — PUZZLE: THE LIGHTS NEED SOMEWHERE TO GO

## 11.1 Purpose

This is an infrastructure puzzle with a practical history.

The lights have awakened, but their shared return assembly is restricted. The party can stabilize the affected bank, restore useful electrical service, and improve the route.

They do not need to solve it to read Warde’s board or descend.

## 11.2 Physical explanation

For this campaign’s fictional machinery:

- The sconces use sealed chemical cartridges.
- A service system regulates activation and collects waste condensate.
- A separate low-voltage bus powers controls, terminals, and reception equipment.
- The affected bank’s condensate return is obstructed.
- Continuing to cycle the bank without clearing the return causes leakage.

Describe labels in plain language. The challenge is understanding the safe order of operations, not guessing invented chemistry.

## 11.3 Read aloud

> Three large controls occupy the panel.
>
> ADMISSION.
>
> RETURN.
>
> SERVICE RELEASE.
>
> A fourth lever is covered by a transparent guard marked MANUAL BYPASS.
>
> The admission indicator is lit.
>
> The return indicator is dark.
>
> Inside the housing, something knocks once.
>
> A bead of fluid forms beneath a coupling.

## 11.4 The starting state

| Component | Starting condition |
|---|---|
| Admission | Open. |
| Return | Obstructed by a swollen seal. |
| Service release | Closed. |
| Manual bypass | Closed; usable as a temporary return route. |
| Drain trough | Partly obstructed by fallen plaster. |
| Reserve collection vessel | Empty and intact. |
| Electrical service bus | Disabled by a protective interlock. |

## 11.5 The clues

### Clue A — The inspection tag

> RETURN FIRST.

This means the system must have a clear return path before admission resumes.

It does not mean “open the jammed return while everything is pressurized.”

### Clue B — The maintenance diagram

A simple diagram shows:

- Admission feeding the lamp bank.
- Return leading to collection.
- Service release opening into the trough.
- Manual bypass connecting around the damaged return section.

Any character studying it understands the connections.

### Clue C — Eleth’s handwritten procedure

Found inside the panel door:

> “Close the feed before opening the housing.”
>
> “Clear the trough. Then release.”
>
> “If the seal has swollen again, use the bypass until replacement.”
>
> “Do not let anyone restart admission before you call clear.”

### Clue D — The physical fault

A mirror, light, or careful inspection reveals the distorted seal.

No roll is needed once a character looks into the relevant access space.

## 11.6 Safe solution

1. Close admission.
2. Clear the drain trough.
3. Open the service release and allow the affected section to empty.
4. Replace the swollen seal, or open the manual bypass.
5. Close the service release.
6. Confirm the return path is open.
7. Reopen admission.

The lamps stabilize. The interlock releases. The electrical service bus becomes available.

## 11.7 Alternate solutions

### Mechanics-led repair

An **Average Mechanics check** with suitable tools allows a trained character to diagnose and perform the safe sequence.

Players do not have to solve the puzzle personally if their character plausibly would.

### External power

The party may power individual terminals and the reception droid from its own equipment.

This bypasses the electrical problem but leaves the leaking lamp bank unresolved.

### Shut the bank down

Closing admission prevents further leakage.

The party continues with its own lights. This is a valid solution.

### Replace the assembly

A well-equipped group can remove the affected coupling and install a substitute.

Judge time and materials from what they actually carry.

## 11.8 If they use the wrong order

The machinery gives warnings before consequences.

### Opening the housing while admission remains active

A hiss becomes a spray. The character can step back or close the feed.

If they insist on working in the spray, use an **Average Coordination or Mechanics check**, according to approach.

Suggested failure: 2 strain and soaked equipment requiring cleaning.

### Reopening admission without a return route

A visible pressure indicator climbs. The bank shuts down and vents into its collection vessel.

The party must drain and reset it.

Do not destroy the level or the historical evidence.

### Ignoring the blocked trough

A shallow spill makes a short section of gallery slippery.

It remains clearly visible. Warn characters before anyone moves quickly through it.

## 11.9 Reward

The party gains:

- Stable lighting in the affected spaces.
- Access to the electrical service bus.
- A dry route through the works.
- A usable collection of maintenance tools.
- A clearer understanding of the palace’s infrastructure.

A character who performed the repair can explain it to another visitor later.

That knowledge is a meaningful reward in a place where survival depends on understanding old systems.

# 12. SECOND PUZZLE: WHO GETS THE REMAINING POWER?

This is a resource decision rather than a riddle with one correct answer.

Use it after the works are stabilized, or when the party inspects the service bus.

## 12.1 The capacity

The restored bus safely supports **three load units**.

These abstract units are an encounter device, not an official Star Wars equipment specification.

| Circuit | Load | Benefit |
|---|---:|---|
| Reception | 1 | Powers the reception droid and public directory. |
| Suite terminals | 2 | Allows deeper examination of surviving local records. |
| Drain pump | 2 | Dries the maintenance return near the lower stair. |
| Public information displays | 1 | Restores room names and visitor notices. |

Chemical sconces are not on this electrical budget.

## 12.2 The decision

The party can run:

- Reception and terminals.
- Reception and pump.
- Displays and terminals.
- Displays and pump.
- Reception and displays, with one unit unused.

They can switch circuits and perform tasks sequentially.

**Do not manufacture urgency to prevent this.** Planning an efficient sequence is a successful solution.

If there is already an active patrol or environmental problem, that existing situation may make the order matter.

## 12.3 Information available before choosing

The panel states:

> “Disconnect before reassignment.”
>
> “Stored data unaffected by ordinary power interruption.”
>
> “Reception unit requires notice before shutdown.”

The droid asks for time to finish its current annotation before power is removed.

The terminals do not erase themselves merely because the party chooses another circuit.

## 12.4 Overloading the bus

The load gauge shows the overload before activation.

If the party proceeds anyway, a breaker trips. Resetting it takes a few minutes.

A skilled character can propose an upgrade using carried equipment. Reward plausible engineering rather than insisting the puzzle remain unsolved.

## 12.5 Why this scene matters

People maintained the palace by deciding what needed attention first.

The party now does the same.

The question is practical:

- Speak to the surviving administrator?
- Recover more records?
- Make the route safer?
- Restore public information for whoever comes next?

None of these priorities is secretly immoral.
