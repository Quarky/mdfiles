# Level 12 - Exhibition Hall: Culture Before Catastrophe

**Maps:** SP01 Exhibition Hall; SP02 Cultural Vault & Manta Passage  
**Core question:** *Who were these people before the war decided what history would remember about them?*  
**Tone:** wonder, melancholy, absence without immediate threat.

## GM goals

By the time the party leaves, they should understand that the Trade Federation was a civilization with children, artists, explorers, administrators, and self-mythology. They should also understand that the ruin has had a second life: looters, animals, moisture, and failing systems have all changed it.

Do not foreground Warde yet. Level 12 earns the emotional weight that makes later deaths matter.

## Arrival - the threshold that animals respect

The known ventral-city fauna should behave differently at the palace threshold. Bridge geckos that were casual elsewhere stop at a line they have learned not to cross. Do not explain whether this is Force residue, predator behavior, chemical smell, or generational habit.

Let the Pathfinder read only the fact of the decision: *they stop here.* The uncertainty is more useful than an answer.

## Living-world discoveries

### 1. The stripped museum

The cases are intact. The contents are gone. Hinges show careful tool use. Dust patterns show that objects of many different sizes were removed at different times. This was not a single wartime smash-and-grab; it became a market over years.

**Optional clue chain:**

- mount label for the Sakoyan Navigator;
- packing-catalogue serial in SP02;
- upper-city rumor/collection later identifying the same serial;
- moral choice: expose, steal back, negotiate return, or leave it.

### 2. The mosaic floor

Use the eight panels as a walkable history. Avoid reciting all of them unless the players want it. Ask what they inspect.

Recommended emphasis:

- homeworld/hatcheries - care and origin;
- first ships - curiosity rather than conquest;
- first bridge-city - survival and engineering;
- Federation founding - unity as self-image;
- Cato Neimoidia center - the canyon mantas existed before the institution and remain after it;
- decline - the institution was capable of representing fatigue and burden even in official art.

### 3. The child's handprint

This is the best small-scale humanizing beat on the level. It should be discoverable by light angle or condensation, not glowing conveniently. There is no need to identify the child. The point is that a child stood here long enough to press a hand to glass and look at something now stolen.

### 4. Staff maintenance marks

Add tiny mundane details:

- a maintenance sticker written over twice because the first date was wrong;
- a floor tile whose color mismatch shows a repair nobody bothered to disguise;
- a "do not polish" note on a mosaic edge that someone ignored for years;
- a lost stylus trapped behind a display rail;
- a staff initials game on the underside of a service drawer.

These details prevent the level from feeling like a curated lore museum created for the PCs.

## The Curator - optional Force event

Earlier drafts include a Curator-like presence. If used, keep it ambiguous: a repeated museum memory, an old caretaker droid projection, or a Force impression of someone who loved the collection. It should never become a mandatory boss fight.

The best use is to ask a question the PCs cannot solve with violence: *"If you take a thing from the place that gave it meaning, what exactly did you take?"*

## The canyon manta

The manta is an environmental resident, not a monster. It blocks the direct passage because it is enormous and comfortable. The Pathfinder can communicate presence/intent through the Force; patient PCs can wait, coax, route around, or use the staff passage.

**Do not reward aggression.** If attacked, it flees through the breach and the map becomes more dangerous: broken railing, downdraft, and structural stress. The party still progresses, but they have learned nothing from the creature.

## FFG checks and spend ideas

- **Average Perception:** identify systematic rather than chaotic looting.
- **Average Xenology/Education:** interpret one mosaic detail correctly.
- **Average Mechanics:** open the maintenance-mosaic interface.
- **Force use:** receive animal calm or a faint sadness echo; do not hand out plot facts.
- **Advantage:** find a staff note, provenance tag, or safer route.
- **Threat:** spend time, stir dust/fog, or draw a remote droid sensor ping.
- **Triumph:** identify a specific stolen object's chain of custody.

## Exit image

The party leaves with the manta, mosaic, or empty cases behind them. Nothing dramatic needs to happen. The important transition is that the next level is no longer about culture displayed to visitors; it is about **the rooms where visitors actually came to negotiate.**
