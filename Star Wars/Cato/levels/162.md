
# LEVEL 16 — THE ROOM WHERE THE FUTURE CLOSED
## Expanded GM Session Pack: Security Junction, Conference Suites, Final Meeting Hall, and Durael’s Balcony

**Campaign:** Chronicles of the Gatekeeper — Jorra, Cato Neimoidia  
**System:** Star Wars FFG  
**Placement:** Immediately after Level 15  
**Primary maps:** SP11, SP12, SP13  
**Suggested length:** Two sessions, or one extended session with selected optional scenes  
**Visibility:** GM-only, except individually revealed handouts

**Scope:** This chapter completes Level 16. Additional locations and encounters on the route toward Level 16 are reserved for a separate expansion.

---

# 1. THE PURPOSE OF LEVEL 16

Level 15 showed people preparing for a meeting.

Level 16 shows what became of that preparation.

The party should leave with more than a recovered crystal and a description of a massacre. They should understand the relationship between:

- A genuine attempt to negotiate.
- Warde’s growing reliance on his own predictions.
- The difference between anticipating danger and proving guilt.
- The choices made by people who could not see the future.
- The orders and institutions that continued after their original purpose disappeared.
- The responsibility of those who arrive afterward.

The central question is:

> **When you believe you know what someone will do, what do you still owe them before you act?**

There is no required answer.

The palace provides evidence. The players decide what that evidence means for their characters.

## 1.1 The emotional progression

Run the level through five changes in perspective:

1. **Procedure:** These were working rooms with real administrative purposes.
2. **Uncertainty:** The surviving evidence is incomplete and sometimes misleading.
3. **Violence:** The final hall contains the physical consequences.
4. **Confrontation:** Something wearing Warde’s face offers a dangerously simple explanation.
5. **Responsibility:** Durael’s remains, the crystal, and the victims’ belongings require present-day decisions.

Do not begin at maximum emotional intensity.

A crooked translation microphone and a half-corrected schedule give the later discoveries something ordinary to interrupt.

## 6.2 Immediate information

Before any encounter begins, reveal:

- The nearest cover.
- The visible side doors.
- Any obvious damaged floor.
- The sound or absence of mechanical movement.
- A service route marker, if the party has not already used that route.

Do not make danger depend on failing to ask for the one correct perception check.

## 6.3 The canyon’s sound

The structure produces a low, irregular resonance.

It changes when:

- Wind moves through broken panels.
- A nearby cable shifts.
- A door opens between interior spaces.
- A pressure change moves fog through a breach.

This is a physical sound.

The Force encounter may later borrow its rhythm, but the sound does not begin as evidence of a monster.

## 6.4 A practical sign

A cracked notice beside the windows reads:

> “During high crosswind, use inner circulation.”

The inner route is the conference-room side of the corridor.

Anyone following it avoids unnecessary exposure.

# 7. L16-02 — THE SECURITY JUNCTION

## 7.1 Choose the actual security state

### State A — No surviving hostile droids

Use the junction as an investigation scene.

The party finds:

- A docking recess.
- Patrol marks.
- A local status panel.
- Evidence of old military occupation.

Do not introduce replacements.

### State B — Previously persuaded droids

The droids recognize the party or their established agreement.

Their unresolved question is what to do with the final hall and the missing delegation.

### State C — Unaware surviving patrol

The party can observe it before contact.

### State D — Alerted surviving patrol

The droids have prepared around what they know.

They do not know the party’s full abilities or intentions unless previously demonstrated.

## 7.2 First sight

> A droid stands near the junction with its weapon lowered but ready.
>
> Its head turns toward the conference corridor, then toward a dark docking recess.
>
> A second shape moves behind a damaged partition.
>
> On the wall, an old status display shows one phrase:
>
> SESSION SECURITY ACTIVE.

For a lone patrol, remove the second shape.

## 7.3 What the droids are protecting

**New encounter-specific standing order:**

> Maintain the conference security perimeter.
>
> Prevent unauthorized armed entry.
>
> Preserve access for verified support personnel.
>
> Report interruption of proceedings.
>
> Await command confirmation before terminating assignment.

These orders explain behavior. They do not make the droids omniscient.

## 7.4 Signs of long survival

- A charging cable has been repaired with a mismatched connector.
- Patrol routes avoid a collapsed section.
- One droid uses a replacement hand with different plating.
- Weapon handling is precise, but movement includes small pauses.
- Their current patrol history contains long inactive intervals.

This explains persistence better than claiming every machine walked continuously for fifteen years.

## 7.5 Reconnaissance

Give useful information for sensible observation.

| Approach | What it reveals |
|---|---|
| Watch a full patrol circuit | One position becomes unobserved briefly. |
| Inspect the public directory | Support access is distinct from the main armed checkpoint. |
| Listen to a droid exchange | They are waiting for command confirmation that has not arrived. |
| Examine the service route | It joins behind a partition, not behind every defender. |
| Display an existing recognized credential | The droids pause to evaluate its actual authority. |
| Ask what they are guarding | They identify the conference assignment. |

An **Average Perception, Computers, or relevant Knowledge check** may reveal extra tactical detail where uncertainty remains.

Failure should not erase obvious doors or cover.

# 8. SECURITY NEGOTIATION — “THE ASSIGNMENT HAS NOT ENDED”

## 8.1 Opening challenge

> “Identify your authorization.”
>
> “Conference security remains in effect.”

Allow the party to speak before assuming combat.

## 8.2 Useful approaches

### A. Establish that the political situation changed

The droids can compare:

- Current, independently dated records.
- Their last authenticated command timestamp.
- Evidence of long system isolation.

This can establish that their command context is obsolete.

It does not automatically persuade them to abandon an assignment.

### B. Request a supervised inspection

The party offers to determine the status of the delegation.

The droids may permit limited entry while retaining weapons or monitoring the doorway.

Characters can negotiate those conditions.

### C. Invoke maintenance access

This is strongest if the party actually repaired palace systems and can demonstrate relevant work.

A visitor badge alone does not make them engineers or officers.

### D. Present reception’s unresolved ledger

If reception was restored, its testimony or signed local record supports:

- A delegation arrived.
- No verified departure was recorded.
- Their status requires investigation.

Reception does not outrank military security. It supplies evidence.

### E. Deceive the patrol

A plausible false order may work temporarily.

Decide what the droids can verify. Failure creates suspicion tied to the specific deception.

Do not have them detect every lie automatically.

## 8.3 Suggested resolution

Use **Average** difficulty for a well-supported request to inspect under supervision.

Use **Hard** difficulty for persuading an uncooperative patrol to suspend its orders entirely without command authority.

Use an opposed check if using the droid’s full social profile.

Strong evidence may remove the need for a roll about a simple fact. A separate roll can still determine whether the droids accept the proposed course of action.

## 8.4 A failed negotiation

Failure can mean:

- The party must accept an escort.
- The droids demand weapons remain secured.
- Access is limited to two characters initially.
- The patrol checks another record.
- The party must choose another route.

Combat begins when the parties actually escalate, not merely because a social check failed.

## 8.5 If the party says “the war is over”

> “That statement has been entered.”
>
> “The conference assignment has not been terminated.”
>
> “Who is authorized to terminate an assignment when its command structure no longer exists?”

Let the party answer.

The question can become the droids’ story for the rest of the level.

# 9. IF SECURITY BECOMES COMBAT

## 9.1 Give the encounter an objective

Choose one:

- Get through the junction.
- Reach the local control recess.
- Withdraw with an injured companion.
- Prevent a scout from reporting.
- Disable a defender without destroying it.
- Hold a doorway while another character opens a route.

Defeating every machine is one possible outcome.

## 9.2 Terrain functions

| Feature | Use |
|---|---|
| Damaged partition | Cover; blocks one firing angle. |
| Conference doorway | Local retreat or flanking access. |
| Docking recess | Defender position and visible power equipment. |
| Service door | Alternate route; may be manually secured. |
| Window-side corridor | Exposed route with clearly visible structural risks. |
| Heavy document trolley | Movable cover if the map supports it. |
| Public status panel | Can interrupt local alerts, not remotely control every droid. |

## 9.3 Droid behavior

BX defenders:

- Use cover.
- Exchange concise information.
- Attempt to deny passage.
- Reposition rather than stand in the open.
- Avoid destroying protected conference records unless their orders or damage justify a change.

A droideka, if already part of the encounter:

- Holds a visible lane.
- Forces the party to change position.
- Must relocate to respond to a successful flank.
- Does not cover every entrance simultaneously.

## 9.4 Environmental interaction

Examples:

- Closing a fire door separates defenders temporarily.
- Cutting a local alert cable prevents that cable from transmitting.
- Turning off a light affects sight, not droid existence.
- Disconnecting a charging cradle does not disable a mobile droid’s internal power.
- Destroying a control panel removes its useful functions too.

Tell players what their proposed action can plausibly accomplish before they commit.

## 9.5 Avoiding excessive difficulty

Do not stack a full droid battle, a second reinforcement wave, and the remnant’s harshest manifestation by default.

If the junction is a demanding combat, run the remnant primarily through dialogue and choices.

# 11. L16-04 — THE PRELIMINARY CONFERENCE ROOM

## 11.1 Read aloud

> Tables stand against the walls.
>
> Their placement leaves a broad central space, with chairs arranged in smaller groups.
>
> One table still holds draft language, translation notes, and a schedule.
>
> A line of small adhesive marks crosses the floor.
>
> The room was rearranged.
>
> The surviving objects do not yet tell you when.

This corrects an unsupported assumption in the earlier draft: moved furniture alone does not prove that someone tidied during the massacre.

## 11.2 Reconstruction puzzle: why were the tables moved?

The players can compare three clues.

### Clue A — Floor plan

A paper diagram shows the room’s furniture moved to the perimeter.

Its heading reads:

> PRELIMINARY WORKSHOP CONFIGURATION

### Clue B — Access note

> “Leave central passage clear for interpreters and document exchange.”

### Clue C — Furniture marks

Dust and older wear are consistent with the tables having occupied these positions before the final abandonment.

### Resolution

The room was deliberately configured for preliminary discussions.

It was not necessarily rearranged during the crisis.

## 11.3 What this teaches

The party has corrected an appealing but unsupported story by comparing evidence.

Do not frame this as a trick.

The room’s initial appearance supported more than one explanation.

## 11.4 Working terms

The documents expand the Level 15 proposals.

**New handout:**

> IMPLEMENTATION QUESTIONS
>
> Who verifies that a medical convoy has reached its destination?
>
> What happens if a checkpoint receives contradictory orders?
>
> Which official can authorize urgent utility repairs?
>
> How are missing detainees distinguished from prisoners transferred elsewhere?
>
> When does a temporary suspension become a breach of the agreement?

These are unresolved problems, not proof that negotiations were fraudulent.

## 11.5 A small success before the disaster

A separate page contains a completed agreement about exchanging medical-supply inventories.

Two sets of initials appear beneath it.

The concession is limited but real.

The meeting did not consist only of people repeating slogans. They had already accomplished something.

## 11.6 Optional personal detail

A translation note explains that one phrase sounded insulting in its first rendering.

Beside the correction:

> “They laughed. Keep the second version.”

This lets the room contain a moment of relief without inventing a major historical event.

# 17. THE HALL’S EVIDENCE STATIONS

## E16-A — THE TABLE

The table is Keloythian oak, retained from the campaign drafts.

It survived with substantial damage.

### Obvious

- Several cuts cross its surface.
- Some are deeper than others.
- Scorching affects more than one area.
- A cup lies on its side.

### Careful inspection

A character can distinguish at least two different damage directions.

That supports movement during violence.

It does not identify every attacker from blade marks alone.

### Useful check

**Average Mechanics or a relevant combat-informed examination** can distinguish obvious weapon damage from ordinary decay.

**Advantage:** Identify an overlapping mark that helps establish relative sequence.

**Failure:** The character cannot confidently separate two marks. The broader fact of violent damage remains obvious.

### Ordinary detail

A faint rectangle shows where a writing pad rested through years of use.

The table had a long history before this meeting.

---

## E16-B — WARDE’S CHAIR

### Obvious

The chair at the principal Republic position is pulled back and remains upright.

### Supported inference

Someone could have stood from it without overturning it.

### Stronger campaign interpretation

The draft describes Warde rising deliberately.

# 18. RECONSTRUCTION PUZZLE — ONE ROOM, SEVERAL EVENTS

## 18.1 Purpose

Give the party a way to assemble its findings without requiring a single perfect interpretation.

The puzzle is arranging evidence into broad phases and marking uncertainty.

## 18.2 Provide these evidence cards

### Card 1 — Working papers

Drafts and implementation questions show active negotiations.

### Card 2 — Unfinished hospitality

Refreshments and personal objects were left in use.

### Card 3 — Warde’s notes

He acknowledged sincere delegates, uncertainty, alternatives, and anticipated action.

### Card 4 — Chair and table damage

Someone rose; violent movement followed.

### Card 5 — Bodyguard response

Droids responded to danger near their protected people.

### Card 6 — Durael’s remains

A Jedi died on the balcony during the larger confrontation.

### Card 7 — Later damage

Some structural damage occurred after earlier marks.

### Card 8 — Departure testimony

If obtained, Tacker saw Warde leave the palace.

## 18.3 Reasonable reconstruction

A supported broad sequence is:

1. Preparations and negotiations took place.
2. Warde entered the meeting with the state of mind described in his notes.
3. Warde attacked the delegation.
4. Defenders responded during the violence.
5. The confrontation involved Moll and Durael; Durael died.
6. Warde departed, according to Tacker’s testimony.
7. Further damage and long abandonment altered the scene.

The precise ordering of individual defensive movements is not required.

## 18.4 Successful play

The group succeeds when it can distinguish:

- What it observed.
- What multiple sources support.
- What remains uncertain.

There is no bonus for inventing missing details confidently.

## 18.5 If they reach a mistaken conclusion

Offer an available contradictory observation.

Example:

If they claim the bodyguards unquestionably began the incident:

> “How does that fit with Warde’s notes about what he expected himself to do?”

If they claim every broken window came from one duel:

> “One fracture crosses a surface that had already weathered.”

Ask questions. Do not have an NPC recite the correct answer.

## 18.6 Mechanical assistance

A relevant **Average Knowledge, Perception, or Mechanics check** can:

- Identify which two clues should be compared.
- Separate an observation from an unsupported inference.
- Establish a likely relative sequence.

The check helps the characters reason. It does not replace all investigation with omniscient narration.

# 21. RESOLVING THE REMNANT

## 21.1 Three valid outcomes

The party may:

1. **Break its argument and release the manifestation.**
2. **Disperse it through a structured confrontation.**
3. **Refuse engagement and recover the crystal while containing or avoiding it.**

No exact phrase is mandatory.

No character must forgive Warde.

## 21.2 The three patterns holding it here

Use three visible markers:

- **CERTAINTY**
- **ERASURE**
- **REPETITION**

These are encounter markers, not physical objects or official game statistics.

### Certainty

The remnant treats a feared possibility as an unavoidable fact.

### Erasure

It reduces the delegates and others to roles in its justification.

### Repetition

It insists that the present party must repeat the same logic.

## 21.3 Resolving Certainty

Examples:

- Read the line acknowledging other versions from Warde’s board.
- Ask it to distinguish what happened from what it feared.
- State a different present course of action and carry it out despite uncertainty.
- Use an established Force ability to steady perception while another character presents the evidence.

If the party supplies a clear, relevant contradiction and engages with its response, resolve the marker without requiring a speech roll.

If success depends on uncertain delivery or control under pressure, use an **Average Discipline, Charm, or appropriate check**.

## 21.4 Resolving Erasure

Examples:

- Identify a victim by name.
- Connect the working papers to a concrete civilian need.
- Preserve a personal object and explain its significance.
- Invite a surviving droid to describe the people it was assigned to protect.
- Refuse to let the argument ignore Durael’s death.

This is acknowledgment, not a requirement for forgiveness.

## 21.5 Resolving Repetition

The remnant offers a present-day version of its argument.

> “The machine may attack you later.”
>
> “The witness may lie.”
>
> “The companion may fail.”
>
> “Act while you can still prevent it.”

It refers only to things it can actually observe or learn.

The party resolves this marker by rejecting the demand to treat uncertainty as sufficient cause for preemptive harm.

Possible actions:

- Secure a weapon without destroying its owner.
- Ask a question before attacking.
- Let a companion choose for themselves.
- Establish a practical precaution instead of demanding perfect certainty.
- Leave an exit open.

The action matters more than polished dialogue.

## 21.6 Peaceful resolution

Once all three patterns are addressed:

> The figure looks toward the delegates’ side of the table.
>
> For the first time, it seems unable to keep its attention on Warde’s chair.
>
> “Then I might have been wrong.”
>
> The words do not sound like an answer it wanted.
>
> Its outline loosens.
>
> The windows behind it become visible again.

The remnant’s release does not establish that the living Warde has been redeemed.

It ends this manifestation’s hold on the room.

# 22. IF THE REMNANT BECOMES HOSTILE

This is a homebrew environmental confrontation, not the contradictory Rival stat block in `Phatnasm.md`.

It gives every character a role without making conventional characters useless.

## 22.1 When hostility begins

Use when:

- The party deliberately attacks the manifestation.
- A character accepts its demand to harm someone and begins acting on it.
- The remnant’s argument is challenged and the GM chooses a more active response.
- The players clearly want a physical confrontation.

Give a warning before the first harmful effect.

## 22.2 Structure

Use normal turn order if helpful.

Keep the three markers:

- Certainty.
- Erasure.
- Repetition.

Resolve each through a distinct action or supported argument.

The party can change tactics during the encounter.

## 22.3 The remnant’s environmental response

At the end of each round, choose one effect. Do not stack them all.

### Cold pressure

A visible frost-like pattern spreads across the nearby table edge.

One character who remains in the concentrated area makes an **Average Discipline or Resilience check**, according to their response.

Failure: suffer 2 strain.

Moving away before the pulse avoids it.

### Displaced object

A loose chair or metal fitting begins to lift.

Characters see which object is moving.

They can:

- Leave its path.
- Secure it.
- Catch or deflect it using appropriate equipment or abilities.

If ignored, resolve an **Average Coordination check** for the threatened character.

Failure: suffer 2 strain from the scramble and impact.

Do not use this to destroy essential evidence.

### False movement

A shape seems to move behind a companion.

The affected character adds one Setback to their next relevant check unless they spend a moment verifying what they saw.

It does not force an attack.

### Narrowed attention

The remnant insists that one danger is the only thing that matters.

A companion can interrupt, name a present fact, or physically guide the character toward another viewpoint.

If nobody intervenes, an **Average Discipline check** avoids 2 strain.

## 22.4 Limits

- No automatic Conflict from suffering an effect.
- No compulsory betrayal.
- No blanket immunity that excludes most of the party.
- No repeated strain loss merely for standing anywhere on the map.
- No destruction or disappearance of the quest crystal.
- No escalation beyond what the party can see and respond to.

## 22.5 Conventional combat contributions

A conventional attack against the visible figure may scatter its outline briefly.

Treat a successful appropriate combat check as **suppression**:

- Cancel the next environmental response.
- Do not claim the attack has resolved the remnant’s identity or argument.

Each round allows only one suppression cancellation.

Characters can also:

- Secure loose objects.
- Shield a companion.
- Maintain an exit.
- Recover evidence.
- Help someone reach the balcony.
- Restore useful light.

## 22.6 Force contributions

Use the character’s actual purchased abilities when applicable.

For this special encounter, an **Average Discipline check** made as an action can represent stabilizing their perception of the residue and engaging one relevant marker.

A clear connection to evidence or present action is still needed.

This encounter permission does not grant a new permanent Force power.

## 22.7 Dispersal

If the party resolves all three markers while resisting the manifestation, it disperses as above.

If the party wants a forceful containment rather than a conversation:

- Establish the nature of the distortion.
- Anchor companions to present facts.
- Disrupt its attempt to repeat the old choice.

These can be physical, technical, social, or Force-assisted actions.

## 22.8 Withdrawal

The party can leave.

The remnant remains bound to the hall and balcony vicinity for this version of the encounter.

It does not pursue them through the entire campaign.

If they recover the crystal and withdraw, record the manifestation as unresolved.

That is a valid outcome with an unfinished local story.
