# Level 13 - Diplomatic Quarter and Records

**Maps:** SP03 Diplomatic Quarter & Canyon Walkway; SP04 Suspended Canyon Crossing; SP05 Sealed Archive  
**Core question:** *Did anyone here actually believe peace was possible?*  
**Tone:** institutional archaeology, openness giving way to exposure.

## GM goals

Level 13 should overturn the easiest version of the story. The cease-fire was not merely a room full of villains setting a trap. The delegation had real authority and came despite a credible warning. Hospitality architecture and paperwork should prove this before any ghost or Force scene does.

## The diplomatic corridor

Use seals, door widths, furniture, wear patterns, and climate controls to show that Sankar was designed for bodies unlike Neimoidians. The room types should make diplomacy feel like engineering: someone measured how visitors rested, stood, breathed, or communicated and built for them.

### Room A - Banking/financial delegation suite

A round table with no dominant head position. An old ledger contains both numbers and a handwritten note for successors: a reminder that a relationship cannot be reduced to a balance sheet.

### Room B - Unknown-seal suite

Low platform, cushions, different access height, and a carved or scratched winged figure. Do not identify the species unless the campaign already has an answer. Mystery makes the galaxy feel larger.

### Room C - Preparation library

A physical or projected globe includes the red unlabelled world. This is **foreshadowing**, not Warde's destination reveal. The room also contains etiquette references, pronunciation guides, and small evidence of anxious staff preparing for unfamiliar guests.

## Puzzle - Hospitality Protocol

Use Puzzle 2. The players learn the archive's security logic by understanding the building's hospitality logic.

## The sealed archive

### Mandatory record set

1. **Authorization:** the delegation had authority to make meaningful cease-fire commitments.
2. **Objectives:** reduction/cessation of fighting mattered more than symbolic victory.
3. **Republic assessment:** Warde's command was strained; security concerns were real.
4. **Risk warning:** the delegation had reason to fear manipulation or violence and came anyway.

The exact wording can vary, but these facts should survive missed rolls.

### Living-world records around the important documents

- lunch/refreshment requisition with one allergy correction;
- a translator's complaint about a mispronounced title;
- a staff request for two more cushions;
- a security clerk noting that one door keeps sticking in wet weather;
- a half-completed form requesting replacement transplas after a minor prewar incident.

These boring papers are valuable. They make the important papers believable.

## The red world clue

Run Puzzle 3 if the players are interested. The key revelation is that the Trade Federation marked some knowledge as "known but not for ordinary routing." If the PCs already know Moraband, they may identify it. If they do not, retain the red marker as a recurring visual language.

## Exterior walkway and suspended crossing

This is where the party should physically understand the palace's relationship to the bridge. Put cables above them disappearing into Jorra and structural members below/sideways vanishing into fog. Let the walkway feel engineered and reliable in some places, damaged in others.

### Environmental event - fog breathing

During the crossing, fog thins for only a few seconds. The party sees lower palace masses hanging beneath them and realizes how much farther down the complex extends. Then the fog closes.

### Optional complication - Syndicate observer

The operative following the PCs may appear only as evidence:

- fresh boot print in dry dust;
- a recently opened service latch;
- a distant comlink click;
- a silhouette that uses a route the party does not know.

Do not force a chase unless the players pursue.

## Fail-forward crossing

A failed Athletics/Coordination check should put a PC on a lower maintenance ledge, dangling from a safety line, or separated by a small gap - not kill them. The rescue becomes a short scene and may reveal a hidden service access.

## What the party should leave knowing

- Sankar was built to accommodate difference.
- The cease-fire delegation had genuine authority and reason to hope.
- They were warned and came anyway.
- The institution knew of a red/restricted world but did not publicly route to it.
- Something deeper in the palace feels increasingly aware of the party.
