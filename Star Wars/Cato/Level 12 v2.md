
# LEVEL 12: THE EXHIBITION HALL
## *"They built this to last. They were right."*

---

## GM OVERVIEW

**Purpose:** This is the first contact with the deep ventral diplomatic complex. The Exhibition Hall represents the Trade Federation's cultural heart—a place where they displayed their history, their achievements, and their identity. The party encounters the Federation as a people with a culture, not just a faceless empire. This level plants the seeds of what was lost and foreshadows the darkness that consumed it.

**Tone:** Wonder with melancholy underneath. Beautiful and abandoned. Not threatening yet, but with a growing sense of loss and the first whisper of something darker. The space is grand but empty, preserved but dead.

**Key GM Goals:**
- Establish the Trade Federation as a civilization with culture and history
- Show the scale of what was lost through environmental storytelling
- Create an emotional connection through small, human details
- Introduce the first subtle thread of the dark side presence
- Allow players to learn about Neimoidian culture through discovery
- Keep pacing engaging for players with attention challenges

| Element | Detail |
|---------|--------|
| **Location** | Level 12, Exhibition Hall, Deep Ventral Diplomatic Complex |
| **Features** | Mosaic floor, display cases, plaques, child's handprint, dark corner |
| **Time Since Evacuation** | 15 Years |
| **Key NPCs** | Sigg (present), The Curator (optional), Canyon Manta (obstruction) |
| **Atmosphere** | Wonder with melancholy, beautiful abandonment |
| **Primary Challenge** | Processing the weight of loss, navigating the manta obstruction |
| **Duration** | 45-60 minutes of session time |

## GM MODE: RUNNING THIS LEVEL

### For Players with ADHD/Autism

| Challenge | GM Strategy |
|-----------|-------------|
| **Maintaining Attention** | Break the hall into sections. Describe 2-3 things, then pause for player interaction. Keep descriptions concise and vivid. |
| **Processing Information** | Use Sigg as a "translator" for complex historical details. He can summarize what the plaques mean. |
| **Sensory Overload** | The hall is huge and empty—this is actually calming. Emphasize the quiet, the stillness, the sense of space. |
| **Emotional Engagement** | The child's handprint is the key emotional beat. Let the player discover it naturally. Don't rush it. |
| **Pacing** | Keep moving. Short, sharp beats. Let the player discover rather than be told. |

## THE THRESHOLD — BEFORE THEY OPEN THE DOOR

### The Door

The service passage from Level 11 ends at a door that stops them.

**GM Description:**
> *"It's not a maintenance door. Stone frame. Carved border—geometric, precise, the kind of work that takes weeks. Someone cared about this door. This was the back entrance to somewhere important."*

**GM NOTE:** Pause here. Let them look at it. Then say:

> *"You notice the walls beside the door. Back in the Ossivane—back up in the residential levels—the Bridge Geckos were everywhere. Every surface. Every corner."*

> *"They are not here. The wall beside this door is bare. They stopped. Right here. And did not come further."*

### The Geckos' Decision

**If the Pathfinder reaches through the bond:**

> *"She can feel where they stopped. Not why. Just—the edge. A line they decided not to cross. Years ago. And have not crossed since."*

**Sigg's Observation:**
> *"Sigg looks at the bare wall. 'Geckos are smart. They know what they're doing.' He does not sound comforted."*

**Beat:** Let that sit for one second. Then:

> *"The door opens inward. The air that comes through is different. Not colder. Not warmer. Cleaner. Like a room that has been sealed and has had nothing happen in it for a very long time."*

## ENTERING THE EXHIBITION HALL

### The First Look

The party steps through. The lights do not ignite here—this level runs on the palace's secondary backup, which has been slowly dying for fifteen years. Three quarters of the hall is lit by what remains. One quarter is dark.

**GM Description:**
> *"The ceiling is eight meters above you. You know this because your glow rod reaches the top and finds it. The space is enormous."*

> *"And it is empty. Not damaged-empty. Stripped-empty. Display cases with their glass intact and nothing inside them. Mounting brackets on the walls where objects used to hang. Outlines on the floor where plinths stood—you can see the marks, slightly darker stone where the bases sat for years and the light could not reach."*

> *"Whatever was here is gone. Taken. Methodically. Not smashed. Taken. Someone came here and took everything they could carry."*

### Sigg's First Reaction

> *"Sigg stands in the center of the hall, turning slowly. He takes in the empty cases, the mounting brackets, the outlines on the floor."*

> *"'Someone came through here with a shopping list,' he says. 'They knew exactly what they wanted. They knew what was valuable. They didn't break anything—they just... took it.'"*

> *"He looks at a mounting bracket where something large once hung. 'I wonder if they knew what they were taking. I wonder if they knew what any of it meant.'"*

### The Looting — A Deeper Look

**If the party examines the empty cases:**

> *"The display cases are intact. The glass is not broken. Whoever took the contents opened the cases carefully, removed the objects, and closed them again. This was not vandalism. This was theft. Systematic, deliberate, professional."*

> *"You can see where the objects were mounted—the brackets are still in place, the outlines of their bases visible. Some objects were large—you can see the full footprint of where they sat. Others were small. There are dozens of empty spots."*

**If they check the mounting brackets:**

> *"The brackets are labeled. Each one has a small plaque that identifies what was mounted there. The plaques are still present. The objects are gone."*

**What This Reveals:**
- The looting was professional and systematic
- The objects had significant cultural and historical value
- The plaques remain as ghostly reminders
- The objects exist somewhere, disconnected from their meaning

## THE MOSAIC FLOOR

### The Centerpiece

**GM Description:**
> *"But they couldn't take the floor."*

> *"Look down."*

> *"The floor of the Exhibition Hall is a mosaic. The entire floor. Every centimeter. Neimoidian artists worked this for two years. You can tell because it is not one thing—it is a hundred things, each panel connecting to the next, telling a story across the whole surface."*

### Panel 1 — The Homeworld

**GM Description:**
> *"Near the entrance: the Neimoidian homeworld. The great hatchery lakes. Tiny figures tending the grub pools. The colors are extraordinary—deep greens and silvers, the lake surfaces catching light that no longer comes through these windows."*

**What This Reveals:**
- Neimoidian civilization began with hatcheries
- Water was central to their culture
- They tended their young with care
- The homeworld was beautiful

**Sigg's Observation:**
> *"Sigg stops at this panel. He crouches to look at the tiny figures."*

> *"'They're tending the grubs. That's what they did back then—they raised their young in the water. It was the center of their lives. Everything came from the water.'"*

> *"He looks up at the empty cases. 'I wonder if the people who took everything knew that. I wonder if they knew what they were taking from them.'"*

### Panel 2 — The First Ships

**GM Description:**
> *"Moving inward: the first ships. The first trade routes. A Neimoidian captain at a viewscreen, stars ahead of him, the homeworld behind. His expression is not greedy. It is curious."*

**What This Reveals:**
- The Neimoidians were explorers
- Their expansion was driven by curiosity
- They built ships to reach the stars
- They did not leave their homeworld out of desperation

**Sigg's Observation:**
> *"Sigg stands at this panel for a long moment. 'His expression is curious,' he says. 'Not hungry. Not greedy. Curious. They left their homeworld because they wanted to see what was out there.'"*

> *"'And then they found Cato Neimoidia. And the canyon. And the mantas. And they built all of this.'"*

### Panel 3 — The Arrival

**GM Description:**
> *"Further in: the arrival on Cato Neimoidia. The first ships descending through the canyon mist. The bridge-cities are not yet built—instead, the first settlers are shown constructing their initial shelters on the canyon rim."*

> *"The scene is humble. Tents. Simple structures. The Neimoidians working together to build their new home."*

**What This Reveals:**
- The first settlers were pioneers
- They built Cato Neimoidia from nothing
- They worked together
- The bridge-cities came later

### Panel 4 — The First Bridge-City

**GM Description:**
> *"The next panel shows the first bridge-city. It is small compared to what Jorra became—a single span between two rock arches, simple structures built along its length. But it represents something important."*

> *"The plaque beneath the panel reads: 'The First Bridge-City — Year 47 of the Neimoidian Expansion. The first permanent settlement on Cato Neimoidia. It survived. We survived.'"*

**What This Reveals:**
- Building the bridge-cities was difficult
- The Neimoidians were determined
- They saw themselves as survivors
- Their civilization grew from this foundation

### Panel 5 — The Trade Federation

**GM Description:**
> *"The next panel is a shift in scale. Where the previous panels showed specific events, this panel shows an institution: the founding of the Trade Federation."*

> *"Neimoidian delegates from across the purse worlds gather in a great hall. They are signing documents, shaking hands, embracing. The mood is one of unity."*

**What This Reveals:**
- The Trade Federation was originally a unifying institution
- It brought the purse worlds together
- It represented cooperation, not domination
- The original intention was different from what it became

### Panel 6 — The Expansion

**GM Description:**
> *"The expansion panel is enormous—covering half the floor. It shows trade routes spreading across the galaxy, ships traveling in all directions, Neimoidian influence reaching everywhere."*

> *"In the background, smaller figures in different styles represent other species: the Muun, the Sakoyans, the species from unknown seals. The Federation did not work alone. They built alliances."*

**What This Reveals:**
- The Federation grew rapidly
- They built trade routes across the galaxy
- They formed alliances with other species
- Their power was built on relationships

### Panel 7 — The Center Panel: Cato Neimoidia

**GM Description:**
> *"The center panel: Cato Neimoidia. The bridge-cities shown from above—you can see Jorra in the design, the specific span of this bridge. The canyon below it."*

> *"And on the canyon floor, barely visible because the artist wanted you to look closely: the Canyon Mantas. In the mosaic. They were already here when this was made."*

**If the Pathfinder is paying attention:**

> *"Through the bond, distantly, she can feel the actual mantas. Somewhere below. Warm. Slow. Present. The mosaic knew they were here. The mosaic was right."*

**Sigg's Observation:**
> *"Sigg stands at the center panel, looking at the mantas in the mosaic. Then he looks up, as if trying to see through the floor."*

> *"'They're still there,' he says. 'The mantas. They were here when this was built. They're still here. The Federation is gone. The art is gone. The objects are gone. But the mantas—they're still here.'"*

> *"He says it like it matters. Like it means something."*

### Panel 8 — The Decline

**GM Description:**
> *"The final panel near the south exit shows a more somber scene. A Trade Federation diplomat at a table, head in hands. The caption reads: 'The Weight of Responsibility.' There is no celebration here. Only fatigue."*

**What This Reveals:**
- The Federation's later years were difficult
- The weight of power was heavy
- They could see decline coming
- There is sadness in this panel

## THE DISPLAY PLAQUES

The display cases are empty but the plaques that identified their contents are still mounted. The party can read them.

**GM NOTE:** Read these as found objects. The player is learning Trade Federation history through what they chose to display about themselves.

---

### PLAQUE 1 — The First Trade Agreement

**GM Description:**
> *"First Trade Agreement — Neimoidia to Cato Neimoidia. Signed in the year of the Third Expansion. Original document. The founding of the purse world system. For the first time, Neimoidians lived among the stars. They did not forget where they came from."*

**What This Reveals:**
- The Neimoidians valued agreements and contracts
- They remembered their origins
- The purse worlds were their greatest achievement
- They saw themselves as pioneers

**Sigg's Observation:**
> *"Sigg reads the plaque aloud. Then he says: 'They didn't forget where they came from. They wanted to remember. That's what this hall was for—remembering.'"*

---

### PLAQUE 2 — The Sakoyan Navigator

**GM Description:**
> *"The Sakoyan Navigator. Gift from the Sakoyan people to the Trade Federation upon the signing of the Outer Rim Commerce Accord. We did not only trade goods. We traded trust."*

**GM NOTE:** The Sakoyan Navigator was a sculpture, approximately one meter tall, considered one of the finest examples of cross-species diplomatic art in the sector. It is currently in Duke Irbian's private quarters. He acquired it from a Syndicate contact three years ago for a price he considers a bargain. He does not know its provenance. He likes the way it looks on his shelf.

**What This Reveals:**
- The Federation valued diplomacy
- They created alliances with other species
- They took pride in their relationships
- Trust mattered to them

---

### PLAQUE 3 — Personal Effects

**GM Description:**
> *"Personal effects of the founding families. Objects carried from the homeworld on the first colony ships. Not valuable. Not decorative. Carried because they were home."*

> *"The cases are empty. Someone took objects that were carried from the Neimoidian homeworld on the first ships. Someone took them and sold them and they are somewhere now with no one who knows what they were."*

**What This Reveals:**
- The Neimoidians were sentimental
- They valued continuity with their origins
- Their history has been scattered and lost
- The loss is not just material—it is spiritual

**Sigg's Reaction:**
> *"Sigg reads this plaque twice. Then he says: 'These were the things people carried from their homeworld. Not valuable. Not decorative. They carried them because they were home.'"*

> *"'And someone took them. Someone sold them. And now they're in a warehouse or a private collection and no one knows what they are. No one knows what they meant.'"*

> *"He is quiet for a moment. 'That's the worst part. The taking—that's bad. But the not knowing—that's worse.'"*

---

### PLAQUE 4 — The Battle of the Purse Worlds

**GM Description:**
> *"The Battle of the Purse Worlds. First major engagement of the Federation's defense forces. The ships depicted are the original defensive fleet—small, outnumbered, but they held. They held for three weeks until reinforcements arrived. This mosaic panel is dedicated to those who fought. They were not soldiers. They were traders, administrators, and engineers. They fought anyway."*

**What This Reveals:**
- The Federation was not a military power initially
- Its people fought when necessary
- They held, even when outnumbered
- They were not soldiers by nature, but became warriors out of necessity

> *"The case is empty. The objects that were inside it—objects meant for children, meant to teach them their history—are gone."*

## THE CHILD'S HANDPRINT

### Discovery

On the far side of the hall, in the section that is partially dark, there is a display case that was not fully looted.

The case is large—floor to ceiling, built into the wall. The objects that were inside it were too heavy to move easily and whoever looted the hall gave up on this one. What they left: fragments of what were once scale models of Neimoidian ships. Broken. The models were damaged in the war and were being held here for restoration. They were never restored.

But that is not the important thing about this case.

**GM Description:**
> *"At the base of the case, on the outside of the glass, at the height a child would reach: a handprint."*

> *"Not painted. Not decorative. Pressed. A child's hand, pressed against the glass hard enough and long enough that the oils from the skin left a permanent mark."*

> *"Fifteen years of sealed air have preserved it perfectly."*

> *"A child stood here. Looked at whatever was in this case. Put their hand on the glass. The way children do. The way children always do."*

**GM NOTE:** Do not explain this. Do not contextualize it. Just describe it and stop. Let it land.

### Examining the Handprint

**If the player wants to examine it further:**

> *"The hand is small. Four fingers and one opposable digit—Neimoidian anatomy. Young. Maybe five, six years old. They were pressing their face close too, probably. Looking at the ships. Looking at the stars their people sailed."*

> *"You can see the texture of the palm. The ridges of the fingerprints. This was a real child, standing here, looking at something they loved."*

**What This Reveals:**
- Children lived here
- They visited this exhibition hall
- They were curious about their history
- The Neimoidians were a people, not just a government
- Someone loved this place enough to bring their child

**Sigg's Response:**
> *"Sigg comes to stand beside the player. He looks at the handprint for a long time."*

> *"He doesn't speak. He just looks."*

> *"Finally: 'A child stood here. Just stood here, like any child anywhere. Looking at ships. Looking at stars.'"*

> *"'And then the war came. And the child—I don't know what happened to the child. No one knows. They're gone now. Somewhere. Their parents, too. And all that's left is this handprint.'"*

> *"He looks at the player. 'That's what we're here for. This. The handprints. The things that were left behind.'"*

## THE DARK SIDE FORESHADOWING — FIRST SIGN

**GM NOTE:** This is subtle. One beat only. Do not linger. Do not explain. Plant the seed and move on.

### The Shadow

It happens while the party is examining the hall—whenever feels natural. Possibly at the child's handprint. Possibly at the center mosaic. Whenever the player is most engaged with something beautiful.

**GM Description:**
> *"For just a moment—less than a second—the light in the far corner of the hall does something it should not do."*

> *"Not flicker. The chemical lights don't flicker. Something else. Like a shadow moved where there was nothing to cast a shadow."*

> *"And then it is gone. The corner is empty. The light is steady. Whatever you saw—or thought you saw—is not there."*

### Investigating the Corner

**If anyone investigates the corner:**

> *"Nothing. Empty floor. The stone is cold. The air is still."*

> *"But the Pathfinder—if she reaches through the Force—finds something she cannot quite name. Not a presence. The echo of a presence. Something that was here once and is not here now but has not entirely left."*

> *"It does not feel dangerous. It feels sad."*

**Sigg's Reaction:**
> *"Sigg watches the corner. He does not approach it."*

> *"'There's something there,' he says. 'I can't feel it the way you can. But there's something there.'"*

> *"'Is it dangerous?' he asks."*

> *"The answer, if the Pathfinder reaches out: Not yet. But it is present. It is waiting."*

**GM NOTE:** This is the first thread. By Level 13 it will be a rope. By Level 14 it will be a chain. For now—just this. Just the corner. Just something that moved.

## THE CURATOR — OPTIONAL FORCE GHOST ENCOUNTER

### Overview

The Curator is a Force ghost that appears in the Exhibition Hall—a remnant of the Trade Federation's curator, who stayed behind when the war came.

| Element | Detail |
|---------|--------|
| **Trigger** | Party examines the child's handprint, the mosaic, or the dark corner |
| **Type** | Force Ghost — Remnant of a Trade Federation official |
| **Nature** | A wound in the Force left by the Federation's fall |
| **Tone** | Sad, mournful, weighted with regret |
| **Combat** | Optional — the ghost can be angered into attacking |

### Trigger Conditions

**GM Description:**
> *"The air in the hall changes. It grows cold—not a physical cold, but the cold of something that has been waiting in darkness for a very long time."*

> *"You hear it before you see it. A voice—dry, rustling, like old parchment being crumpled."*

> *"'Visitors. After all these years.'"*

> *"You turn. In the corner where the shadow moved earlier, a figure is standing. Translucent. Flickering. It wears the robes of a Trade Federation official—a high-level administrator, from the quality of the cloth. Its face is gaunt, its eyes sunken."*

> *"It is looking at the empty display cases."*

> *"'They took everything,' it says. 'Every piece of our history. Every object we preserved. They took it and sold it and scattered it across the galaxy like ash.'"*

> *"It turns to look at you. Its gaze is ancient."*

> *"'And you come now, looking for what is left. What do you hope to find?'"*

### The Curator's Dialogue

**Opening:**
> *"'I was the Curator of this hall. For thirty-two years. I oversaw every object, every display, every story we chose to tell about ourselves.'"*

> *"'And then the war came. And the people came. And they took everything I had spent my life preserving.'"*

> *"'I stayed. I stayed because I could not leave. This hall was my purpose. Now it is my grave.'"*

**If the party asks about the Federation:**
> *"'We were not a faceless empire. We were a civilization. We had art, history, culture. We had children who pressed their hands against the glass to see the ships of their ancestors.'"*

> *"'We were not perfect. We did terrible things in our pursuit of profit. But we were not monsters.'"*

> *"'We were just people. People who built too high and fell too far.'"*

**If the party asks about the looting:**
> *"'The Syndicate came first. They took the most valuable objects—the art, the cultural artifacts, the things that could be sold. They did not care what they took, only what it was worth.'"*

> *"'Then the Imperials came. They took the records, the documents, the histories. They wanted to erase us, to make it so no one would remember what we were.'"*

> *"'They succeeded. Look around you. What is left?'"*

**If the party asks about the dark side:**
> *"'The darkness was already here. It had been here long before we arrived. We built our palace on top of it, pretending it was not there, pretending that we could control it.'"*

> *"'When the war came, the darkness woke. It fed on the fear, the anger, the death. It grew strong. And then the Jedi came—the one called Warde.'"*

> *"'He brought the darkness with him. He was already infected, even if he did not know it. And when he fell, the darkness had its home.'"*

**If the party shows respect:**
> *"'You see it. You understand. There are so few who do.'"*

> *"'Remember what you saw here. The handprint. The mosaic. The history that someone tried to erase. Remember that we were people, not just an empire.'"*

> *"'That is all I ask.'"*

## THE CANYON MANTA — THE OBSTRUCTION

### The Blockage

The south exit of the Exhibition Hall—the passage that leads to Level 13—is blocked.

**GM Description:**
> *"You see it before you reach it. The south passage is approximately four meters wide. Something is in it. Something large."*

> *"It is one of the Canyon Mantas. In the passage. Wings folded. Resting. It takes up the entire width of the corridor and most of the height. Its eyes—set wide on a flat, ancient head—are open."*

> *"It is looking at you. It is not alarmed. You are in its space. It is not particularly interested in that fact."*

**GM NOTE:** This is not a combat encounter. This is a presence encounter. The manta will not move on command. It will not move on request. It will move when it decides to move.

### Attempting to Pass

**If anyone tries to push past it:**

> *"The manta does not move. It shifts its weight slightly—the way something very large shifts its weight when it has been mildly inconvenienced. The floor vibrates. You reconsider."*

**If anyone draws a weapon:**

> *"The manta's eye shifts to the weapon. Then back to the person holding it. It does not react defensively. It does not react aggressively. It simply watches."*

> *"You get the distinct impression that it has seen weapons before. Many weapons. And it is not impressed."*

### Approaching the Manta

**If the Pathfinder approaches:**

> *"She can feel it clearly. Not the bond—she has not bonded this one. Just its presence."*

> *"Old. Very old. Older than the building around it. This manta was alive when this hall was built. It has been in this canyon since before the Trade Federation arrived. It was here before any of this. It will be here after."*

> *"It is not frightening. It is humbling. There is a difference."*

**Communicating with it:**

The Pathfinder can reach toward it through the Force—not a bond attempt, just a presence acknowledgment. No roll required. She simply extends attention and it registers her.

> *"It turns its head. Slightly. The way something turns its head when it has decided you are worth a second look."*

> *"What comes back through that attention is not words and not images. It is something older than either. It is the manta's relationship to this space: I was here. I am here. I will be here. You may pass when I am ready."*

### The Shift

After a moment:

> *"The manta shifts. Slowly. With the unhurried dignity of something that has never needed to be fast. It moves its body to the left, folding its wings tighter, creating a gap along the right wall."*

> *"Not large. Enough."*

> *"It does not look at you again. It has done what it decided to do. You may go."*

**Sigg:**
> *"He watches the Pathfinder and the manta. He does not comment. He steps through the gap. He pauses on the other side and looks back."*

> *"'It was here before the war,' he says. 'Wasn't it.'"*

> *"Not a question. He already knows."*

## BEFORE LEAVING — THE LAST LOOK

### The Final View

As the party passes through the gap and exits:

**GM Description:**
> *"Look back."*

> *"The Exhibition Hall behind you: the empty cases. The mounting brackets. The plaques with nothing left to describe. The floor mosaic—all of Neimoidian history laid in stone, intact, unlooted, because you cannot carry a floor."*

> *"And the child's handprint. Still there. Still exactly the size it was."*

> *"The manta settles back into the passage behind you. It is already closing its eyes."*

### The Manta's Final Presence

> *"Through the bond, distantly, she feels the manta's attention fading. It is no longer interested. It has seen you. It has acknowledged you. It has let you pass."*

> *"Its thoughts, if they can be called thoughts, are simple: You are here. You are not a threat. You will leave. I will remain."*
