# ACT 2 LIVING WORLD SCENES
## Cato Neimoidia — Chronicles of the Gatekeeper

---

## SCENE 1: THE MIST SHELF
**Location: Exterior platform on the bridge underside, accessible from a maintenance hatch Sigg remembers**

The party needs to get their bearings. Sigg knows a spot — a broad maintenance shelf that juts from the underside of the bridge, hidden in the fog layer, where he used to sit alone when he was stationed here a year ago. He brings them there quietly.

From above it looks like nothing. From below, standing on it, the entire ventral city hangs overhead like an inverted skyline. The mist rolls through in slow waves. It is completely silent except for wind.

### Living World Texture
A school of Fog Rays drifts through at close range — they are enormous up close, each one the size of a speeder, completely silent. They ignore the party entirely.

The Pathfinder can feel them through the Force before they arrive. She knows they are coming maybe thirty seconds early. She doesn't say anything, she just steps to the railing and waits. When they emerge from the mist she is completely still and one brushes close enough that its trailing edge passes over her outstretched hand.

Sigg watches this happen and says quietly, almost to himself:

> *"I never saw one up close when I was here. I used to see them from above and wonder what they looked like from down here."*

### Story Function
Establishes Sigg's prior presence on Cato. He begins volunteering small memories — the cafe he used to go to, a guard he recognized on the way in. He is orienting the party and himself simultaneously. He knows this place but it has changed. The Syndicate has more visible presence. Irbian has tightened things. He is recalibrating what he knows against what is current.

### Optional Beat
A Tension Cable Bat roost is visible in the junction above them. The Pathfinder can attempt a bond during the quiet of this scene. If she succeeds the bat colony becomes a navigational asset for the ventral ruins later.

## SCENE 2: TORNA'S CAFE — THE WRONG TABLE
**Location: Commercial district, Torna's Cafe**

Sigg brings the party here because he knows it and trusts the anti-Imperial undertone. He has been here before. He remembers the proprietor Hinv Opan as someone who would talk if you were careful.

But when they arrive someone is already at the table Sigg always used — a middle-aged Neimoidian woman sitting alone with a cold drink, watching the door. Her name is **Vetha**, a former Trade Federation logistics clerk who recognized his cerulean coloring when he came through a year ago. She did not report him then. She is not sure why.

She does not approach. She waits.

### Living World Texture
A pair of Bridge Geckos are running along the ceiling above the bar. The proprietor Opan absently tosses them a food scrap without looking up — they are clearly regulars. The Pathfinder notices them immediately. If she is paying attention to the animals rather than the room she actually clocks Vetha watching Sigg before Sigg does, because she notices the geckos freeze and look at a specific person.

### The Scene
Sigg wants to make contact with Opan for information. The Pathfinder will probably want to be anywhere but a crowded cafe. There is a back courtyard — a narrow maintenance yard with a single dead tree growing through a crack in the bridge surface, somehow alive despite everything. The Pathfinder can sit out here.

Sigg comes out briefly to check on her. This is a rare moment where he is not performing his function — he is just checking on her. He sits on a crate and is quiet for a moment and then says:

> *"Opan is going to talk but he wants us to do something first. There is a kid named Tohn Kreiwic who works the block outside. Kreehawk runner. His father comes in every morning and Opan is tired of watching it."*

He does not push. He frames it as information, not a directive.

### Story Function
Introduces Tohn Kreiwic as a rescue/intervention thread. Gives the party a reason to interact with the Syndicate at street level before the bigger confrontations. Sigg's check-in with the Pathfinder establishes their dynamic — he gives her space but keeps contact.

## SCENE 3: THE DEAD GARDEN
**Location: A collapsed palace district courtyard, accessible from a side alley**

Between two crumbling Trade Federation estates is a gap that used to be a private garden. The walls are still standing. The garden is not exactly dead — moss and a few stubborn flowering vines have come back in the years since the war. There is a cracked stone basin that collects rainwater. Someone has been leaving food scraps here.

The party finds this place because the Pathfinder follows a Neimoidian Hound that has been paralleling them for two blocks.

### Living World Texture
The hound is semi-feral, lean, clearly once domesticated. It does not approach but it does not flee. It leads them here and then sits at the basin and drinks and watches them. There are three other animals already here — two stone hoppers and what appears to be a juvenile arch serpent coiled in the warm moss near the south wall.

This is someone's informal sanctuary. That someone is **Sanna Olpan**, the woman whose son was arrested by Warde. She comes here every morning. She is not here right now but she has clearly been here recently — there is a worn spot where she sits, a small wrapped bundle of food for the animals.

### Story Function
Introduces Sanna Olpan through her absence and through the animals she cares for. The Pathfinder will recognize immediately what kind of person tends a place like this — and why. When Sanna arrives later (she comes at dusk) the Pathfinder has already been sitting here for hours. Sanna is startled but not threatened. The shared language of caring for animals is the entire basis of their interaction. Sanna is the first person in Jorra who talks to the party openly, without fear or agenda, because the Pathfinder was just sitting quietly with her hound.

### Sigg's Role
He stays near the entrance, leaning against the wall, not intruding. When it is clear Sanna is going to talk, he drifts away entirely and waits in the alley. This is him understanding exactly what he should not do.

## SCENE 4: THE FACTORY FLOOR APPROACH
**Location: Outside and approaching Factory DE-13**

Before the party reaches Haido they pass through the factory district at shift change. Thousands of Neimoidian workers moving between facilities, the air heavy with industrial smell, automated cargo haulers running on fixed tracks overhead.

### Living World Texture
Scavenger Drone-Beetles are everywhere here — dozens of them on every surface, maintaining the infrastructure. No one pays them any attention. The Pathfinder can bond with one during this transit almost incidentally, in the crowd, while everyone else is focused on not being noticed. It takes maybe two minutes. A worker glances at her crouching next to a beetle on a wall and gives her the look people give someone doing something mildly strange but harmless.

### Story Function
The bonded beetle becomes the surveillance asset for the factory and potentially for later locations. This scene also shows the Pathfinder doing her thing in a crowd — which is to say, ignoring the crowd entirely and paying attention to the one small creature on a wall. Sigg notices and quietly adjusts their pace so she has time to finish.

Sigg also clocks a Syndicate watcher in the crowd — the same posture, the same idle-but-tracking body language he saw a year ago. He doesn't react visibly. He just moves the party through a different exit and explains afterward:

> *"We have maybe a few hours before they figure out we went to the factory. Let's not waste them."*

## SCENE 5: N'GOT'S BUILDING — THE APPROACH
**Location: Residential district, the abandoned tower**

It is raining when they find the building. The Pathfinder senses something before they reach the door — a Pale Spider colony in the superstructure, massive, territorial, running thread across every interior surface. She can feel their collective anxiety about the intruder who lives in their space and the traps he has put in their anchor points.

She relays this in her way — probably not a full briefing, more like:

> *"The building is full of big spiders. They are unhappy about all the metal wire he put in their web."*

And then she might give a count and rough locations because the spiders' own territorial mapping is essentially a floor plan.

### Story Function
The Pathfinder's animal empathy becomes the party's most valuable intelligence asset for this entire encounter. She is not solving the scene for the party — she is giving them information that they still have to act on. But it is information that could not come from any other source.

### Sigg's Role
He has looked up N'got's file from what Haido told them and knows which floor he likely sleeps on. He combines his intel with the Pathfinder's spider-map and the result is a reasonably accurate picture. He says:

> *"Top three floors. Traps on every transition point between five and eight. He sleeps somewhere between nine and twelve and changes it every night."*

He pauses.

> *"She's right about the spiders. That's going to be unpleasant."*

He means for N'got, not for them. The Pathfinder might be completely fine with the spiders.

## SCENE 6: THE VENTRAL EDGE
**Location: The boundary point between the upper city and the ventral ruins**

Before the party descends to Sankar Palace they stand at the point where the inhabited city ends and the ruins begin. There is a physical threshold here — a cracked walkway where the lights stop, where the structural maintenance stops, where the air smells different.

A Rusted Stalker is sitting on the far side of the threshold, watching them.

It has been here before. It knows this edge. It has been watching them for two days, actually — the Pathfinder has been aware of it as background presence since the Dead Garden. This is the moment it decides.

It does not come to her. It sits and waits.

### The Bond
This is the empathy roll for the Rusted Stalker if the Pathfinder attempts it. The difficulty is Hard. The flavor is that the stalker has been alone in the ruins for years and it has opinions about who is allowed in. It is not testing her strength — it is reading whether she understands what is down there. A Force-sensitive Pathfinder who has been on Cato for days and has touched the grief and fear of this city through its animals has a natural edge on this roll that should be reflected in the narrative regardless of the dice.

If she bonds with it, it stands, turns, and walks into the darkness. It is not waiting for them to be ready.

### Sigg's Role
He watches this for a moment and then picks up his pack.

> *"I guess we're going."*

He does not comment on the animal bond itself. He just treats it as information: they have a guide. He respects this completely.

## SCENE 7: TIEKA DALKEN'S APARTMENT — THE UNEXPECTED WELCOME
**Location: Residential district**

Tieka Dalken's apartment is small and slightly chaotic. She has two Trade Federation Parrots in a large cage by the window — old birds, inherited from a family that couldn't take them when they left Jorra after the war. She has had them for fifteen years. Their names are **Preet** and **Ossian**.

When the party enters, Preet says something in a voice that sounds uncomfortably like a middle-aged Neimoidian man giving orders. Tieka laughs, embarrassed.

> *"He picked that up somewhere. I have no idea who. It was before my time."*

The Pathfinder and the parrots have an immediate moment. This is not a roll — it is just a beat. The birds are interested in her. She is interested in the birds. Tieka notices and relaxes visibly. She is warmer in this conversation than she would have been with strangers, because the birds like this one.

### Story Function
Tieka provides the Haido lead. But the scene has texture because of the parrots. If the Pathfinder asks about them — who Preet is mimicking — Tieka thinks about it and says she always assumed it was someone from the Trade Federation period. The voice says things like:

> *"Move them before they arrive."*
> *"The southern approach is compromised."*

It could be nothing. It could be a fragment of something the bird heard during Warde's time. This is not a puzzle. It is ambient. But it is the kind of thing that makes the world feel lived in and the animals feel like they carry history.

## SCENE 8: SIGG'S QUIET SCENE
**Location: The dead garden, late at night**

Sigg does not sleep much. The Pathfinder probably knows this because her animals have noticed him — the hound watches him when he is moving around at night.

He is sitting on the low wall at the edge of the garden, not doing anything. If the Pathfinder approaches he does not perform ease — he just makes space on the wall.

After a while he says:

> *"I was here for six weeks last year. I spent most of it trying not to be noticed. I think I did a decent job. But I also think someone noticed and decided not to do anything about it. I don't know who. It has been bothering me since we arrived."*

He is not asking for help. He is just saying it out loud because he trusts her more than he trusts most people, possibly because she does not have a social agenda.

> *"I keep thinking the city remembers me. Which is — I know that's not how cities work. But still."*

The hound is sitting between them. This is fine.

### Story Function
This scene gives Sigg dimensionality outside his guide role. It also foreshadows that someone on Cato has already clocked him — possibly Tacker, possibly one of Hasar's people. It plants something the GM can use or not use. It also gives the Pathfinder a moment where she is the more comfortable one, which is unusual for her and worth playing.
