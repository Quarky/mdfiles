# Sankar Palace Puzzle Toolkit

These puzzles are designed for Star Wars FFG play. None of them can permanently block the main plot. A failed check changes time, strain, Threat/Despair consequences, or the route taken.

## Puzzle 1 - The Mosaic Path of Continuity (Level 12 / SP01-SP02)

**Purpose:** turn the Exhibition Hall's history into play rather than narration.

Eight mosaic panels contain small bronze inlays at their borders. The looters removed the portable exhibits but did not understand that the floor itself is a maintenance interface. A strip of five inlays can be pressed in historical order to open a narrow cultural-vault access door.

### Clues

- The homeworld panel contains the symbol for **origin**.
- The first ships and first bridge-city establish **departure** and **settlement**.
- The founding of the Federation establishes **union**.
- Cato Neimoidia at the center establishes **continuity**.
- The decline panel is not part of the original five-key sequence; its inlay was added later and has a different alloy.

### Solutions

Players can solve by reasoning, an Average Knowledge (Education/Xenology) check, or an Average Mechanics check to read circuit continuity. A failed check does not lock them out: it opens a service panel, vents dust, costs time, and reveals a bypass with two setback dice for the next check.

### Reward

The vault contains no priceless treasure. It contains **museum operations**: packing foam, display mounts, inventory tags, and a partial pre-looting catalogue. This creates provenance clues showing that at least one famous cultural piece now sits in an upper-city collection. The reward is a future social choice, not credits.

## Puzzle 2 - Hospitality Protocol (Level 13 / SP03-SP05)

**Purpose:** make the architecture's accommodation of different species mechanically relevant.

The sealed archive door has three worn selector plates matching three diplomatic suite designs: conventional chairs, floor cushions/low platform, and standing braces. The archive was meant to grant delegates access according to their registered body plan and security protocol.

### Puzzle

Three visitor seals appear along the corridor. Each seal must be matched to the room that was physically adapted for its users. The clues are wear marks, height, hand/appendage contact points, and the unknown winged figure scratched on a standing brace.

### Resolution paths

- observation and logic;
- Average Perception + Xenology;
- Average Computers/Skulduggery to bypass;
- a recovered staff key from an optional room.

### Consequence of a wrong answer

The door does not explode. It politely resets and activates an obsolete privacy field, adding setback to conversation/comlink use for a few minutes. Three wrong entries wake a tiny archival security droid that asks for credentials and can be reasoned with.

### Reward

Cease-fire authorization, risk assessment, working drafts, and a staff routing map to SP06. The main documents are mandatory; the puzzle only determines whether the party gets them cleanly, quickly, and with extra metadata.

## Puzzle 3 - The Missing Route (Level 13-14 / SP05-SP06)

**Purpose:** connect the red world clues without prematurely revealing Warde's destination.

The preparation-library globe and mural map both show the same red point with no public route. A navigation legend has a missing category. Players can compare the two displays and a damaged route ledger.

**Correct inference:** the marker is **restricted historical significance / do not route automatically**, not a secret Trade Federation destination.

This may identify the world if the PCs already possess the relevant knowledge, but it should not establish that Warde went there.

**Bonus reward:** a hidden maintenance note explains that the display system intentionally suppresses the world's label on public-facing screens. This supports the theme of institutions acknowledging danger while refusing to speak plainly about it.

## Puzzle 4 - Witness, Not Exorcism (Level 14 / SP07)

**Purpose:** make the Ghost Chamber interactive without turning it into a combat encounter.

The seven presences cannot be "defeated." The room changes only when the party demonstrates that the story has been witnessed.

### Three acts of witnessing

The party must perform any three of the following:

- identify an object/person from earlier evidence (the small homeworld stone, a seal, a communications initial);
- speak one fact proving the delegates came in good faith;
- acknowledge the two-second hesitation without excusing what followed;
- promise to carry the record out of the palace;
- physically restore a fallen memorial strip or straighten a nameplate;
- allow a Force-sensitive PC to accept a memory fragment without trying to control it.

No combination is "correct." The puzzle tests attention and empathy, not trivia.

### Failure state

If the PCs treat the room as hostile, the presences withdraw and the temperature remains cold. They can return after learning more. The main plot continues, but they lose a clearer memory of Warde's hesitation.

## Puzzle 5 - Three-Reservoir Chemical Light Works (Level 15 / SP10)

**Purpose:** explain why the palace can still awaken and give technical PCs a meaningful job.

The light system uses three sealed chemical reservoirs feeding a pressure-driven mixing manifold. One reservoir is overpressured, one is partly crystallized, and one sensor lies about its level.

### GM physical layout

- **Reservoir A - activator:** high pressure, safe concentration.
- **Reservoir B - luminophore:** partly crystallized; flow is low.
- **Reservoir C - stabilizer:** normal, but its sensor reads empty.

The control panel suggests opening A first, but the maintenance stencil under the panel warns: **"STABILIZER BEFORE ACTIVATOR AFTER LONG SHUTDOWN."**

### Resolution

Correct manual sequence: C -> clear B -> A. PCs can also solve with Mechanics, Education, or careful physical inspection.

### Threat/Despair ideas

- harmless but blinding flare in one zone;
- chemical vapor adds setback until ventilation cycles;
- a light line activates behind rather than ahead, silhouetting the party to a distant droid sensor;
- old pressure causes a service hatch to pop open, revealing a bonus route.

### Reward

Players can choose which zones to re-light. Lighting the formal spine makes navigation easy but increases visibility to droids; lighting service galleries reveals hidden routes and staff evidence. The system becomes a strategic tool rather than a one-time switch.

## Puzzle 6 - The Table Tells the Story (Level 16 / SP12-SP13)

**Purpose:** let the PCs reconstruct the massacre from evidence before a vision confirms it.

Give the players six evidence cards (see `handouts/Evidence_Cards_GM.md`) and ask them to place them in likely chronological order.

Core evidence:

1. Warde's chair is pulled back deliberately.
2. Delegate-side injuries show surprise and a first strike.
3. Separatist bodyguard fire impacts the Republic side after the initial cuts.
4. Crossed lightsaber scars appear later and move away from the table.
5. Warde's blood and damage continue toward the exit route.
6. Durael's final position is on the balcony with the kyber crystal.

The exact sequence is not a pass/fail exam. Each sensible placement earns a clearer inference. Contradictions prompt a new Perception/Lore clue rather than punishment.

### Bonus conclusion

If the players combine this puzzle with the Level 15 apology, they can state the thematic truth themselves: **Warde did not lose control in a single instant. He prepared, hesitated, chose, and then paid for the choice.**

## Puzzle 7 - Anchor Load Balance (Broken Wing / SP19)

**Purpose:** make the inverted architecture a playable system.

Three anchor pylons are carrying unequal load after wartime damage. A service lift to a hidden bypass will not move while the load sensors report dangerous torsion.

Players can:

- redistribute tension through three winches;
- brace a cracked support with salvage;
- temporarily shut down a dead pylon and accept movement in the whole structure.

A failed roll creates motion, noise, or a changed route - never an instant fatal fall. Success can stabilize one region of the palace for the return trip, creating a visible consequence of the PCs improving the world.
