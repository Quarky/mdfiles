## Opening the scene

> The palace rises from the desert on columns broad enough to be towers. Eroded guardians stand watch, and one narrow thread of smoke leaks from a high window.

Pause after the first two sensory details and ask what each character is doing. Do not read the whole situation as exposition. Establish one physical danger, one ambiguous sign, and one person or companion who needs a decision.

### Entry states

- The palace emerges from the storm after the overlook decision.
- Warde runs ahead after being accused.
- The party approaches without him while doors open on their own.

### Questions before dice

- Who is physically leading, and who is watching the rear or the companions?
- What is the group trying to preserve: time, secrecy, supplies, trust, or evidence?
- Which earlier fear, injury, bargain, rescued enemy, or damaged system is relevant now?
- What would make a character stop and investigate instead of pressing ahead?

## The world is already moving

- Statues rotate toward whoever carries a taken relic.
- Pursuers choose whether to attack, withdraw, or honor prior agreements.
- The vergence offers each character a fast personal route separated from the group.

Run one motion beat when the scene opens, another after the first meaningful delay, and a third when the group commits to an exit. These are not punishments for roleplay. They show that weather, rivals, creatures, companions, and the vergence continue to act.

## Cast, agendas, and reactions

### Suljo Warde / the Wanderer

Before his reveal, Warde observes courtesy, compassion, certainty, treatment of weakness, and willingness to revise a conclusion. He wants evidence that the group will kill him because that would make his vision safe and comprehensible. He fears being offered a future he cannot predict. If he is not physically present, the environment can still test the same questions without granting him impossible knowledge.

### The gatekeeper

The gatekeeper distinguishes memory, inference, and hope. It knows Warde’s former habits but cannot automatically recognize the person Moraband has shaped. When contradicted, it should admit limits rather than become an answer machine.

### Rivals and companions

Rivals follow their current agenda, injuries, morale, and agreements; they do not teleport into encounters. Companions react as people and animals, request help, disagree, notice sensory contradictions, and remember how the party treats them. Never use a companion merely as a hostage without first establishing their own choice and risk.

### Lines and likely questions

| Speaker | Use at the table |
|---|---|
| Wanderer | “Beyond those doors, every answer will cost you the question that led here.” |
| Gatekeeper | asks the party to preserve Warde if possible but admits it cannot command them. |
| Rival ally | demands the terms of entry be restated. |
| Palace whisper | promises certainty in a loved one’s voice. |

When players ask something outside the table, answer from the speaker’s wants and fears. A lie should protect an agenda; it should not exist only to block progress.

## Full living-world walkthrough

1. Deliver the published monumental description.
2. Let players reconnoiter before the guide urges entry.
3. Reveal Warde’s camp smoke and at least one alternate breach.
4. Give a final chance to resolve pursuers outside.
5. Have the guide move more easily as he nears the palace.
6. Use statues and courtyards for a mobile standoff.
7. Let Force users feel the vergence concentrate inside.
8. Record entrance, faction order, and who carries the holocron.

9. After the immediate objective is clear, ask what the characters inspect, protect, leave behind, or tell one another. Give the place time to respond.
10. Advance only the clocks justified by what happened. Record changed trust, rival position, evidence, temptation, supplies, injuries, and open promises before moving on.
11. Offer at least two exits. One can be safer and slower; one faster and more exposed. Failure opens a costly route instead of repeating the same check.
12. Close on a changed fact—a new ally, damaged route, recovered object, revealed contradiction, or altered belief—not merely “the party goes to the next map.”

## Clues and no-bottleneck logic

- The structure is a palace, not a burial complex.
- The doors have been used regularly.
- Smoke and water containers prove habitation.

Present each essential conclusion through at least three vectors: visible terrain or behavior, a recoverable object or record, and a person/creature/environmental reaction. Rolls control clarity, ownership, and cost—not whether the campaign can proceed.

## Optional encounters and interruptions

- Pursuers demand Warde before entry.
- A guardian statue speaks in a PC’s voice.
- Warde offers to enter alone and “prepare the tomb.”

Use one interruption when pacing needs a new pressure or when players deliberately explore. Do not stack every optional encounter merely because a map has space. An encounter should reveal character, alter a resource, change an allegiance, or produce evidence.

## Meaningful choices

- Enter openly, use the side breach, establish a retreat point, or wait for rivals.
- Reject or accept separated “truth” routes.
- Disarm, negotiate with, or race the final rival faction.

Tell players when a choice risks Conflict. Do not conceal moral stakes behind GM knowledge. A dark-side shortcut can be genuinely useful; its price should be visible enough to be a choice.

## Environment and pressure

- Palace awakening: silent, watching, whispering, manifest. Entry advances at least one step.

Use three layers: physical change, doubtful perception, and personal emotional pressure. Not every manifestation attacks. Silence, misplaced familiarity, and evidence that contradicts certainty are often more effective than constant damage.

## Branches, consequences, and clocks

- Reconnaissance grants alternate starting positions.
- Violence outside shapes Warde’s certainty about the PCs.

Record changes to:

- **Expedition:** route, fatigue, water, shelter, medical state, and return path.
- **Pursuit:** rival location, injuries, morale, evidence, bargains, and witnesses.
- **Warde Trust:** courtesy, compassion, threats, proven revisions, and surrendered opportunities to strike.
- **Temptation:** accepted instruction, dark-side comfort, certainty claims, and unresolved fear tags.
- **Palace Awakening:** disturbed relics, broken oaths, violent Force use, and protective/light-side acts.

## GM-only truths

- The side breach is Warde’s ordinary entrance and contains mundane proof of his life.
- The main doors amplify fear but do not lock behind the party.
- A grounded brazier weakens the first palace apparition.

Do not reveal a truth merely because it is written here. Reveal evidence, behavior, and contradictions; let the group decide what those facts mean.

## Handouts and reveal timing

- Reveal a portrait/dossier only after the subject is clearly observed or identified.
- Reveal an evidence artifact after it is physically recovered or copied; never reveal its keeper notes.
- If the scene produces a prophecy, record, route sketch, or translated inscription, state whether it is a player-held fact, an uncertain interpretation, or a GM-only connection.
- Keep exact clock thresholds, hidden identities, manifestation triggers, and rival private goals GM-only.
