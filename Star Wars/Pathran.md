# Pathran Meeting – Full Scene (PCs have helped significantly)

## Entering the Root Tunnels

The entrance is a dark cleft between two massive roots, hidden behind a collapsed mushroom stall in the lowest level of Quolas. A resistance sympathiser nods once and pulls aside a hanging moss curtain.

> *“The air grows cool and damp as you step inside. The tunnel slopes downward, walls woven with living roots that pulse faintly with bioluminescent fungi. Your footsteps echo on packed earth. After a few minutes of winding descent, the tunnel opens into a cavern – the roots arching overhead like the ribs of an ancient beast. A small fire burns in a stone ring, and figures in red-trimmed cloaks rise to meet you.”*

## Meeting Pathran (PCs have helped the city significantly)

In the low-ceilinged root chamber, a young Sathari with blue plumage marked by a shock of white feathers on more Sathari stand off to the side. They are armed, but their slugthrowers and diiros are holstered and stowed away on their persons. He parts his beak in what you now recognize as a smile and gestures to the chairs across from him. 

Pathran Helshar steps forward, his blue plumage bright in the firelight, the white streak across his face catching the glow. He spreads his hands in welcome, a genuine smile on his beak.

> *“You came. I’ve heard about everything you’ve done. Paying old Kellin’s taxes when the guards would have taken his stall – he’s been feeding his family because of you. And after that brawl near the elevator, you stayed to heal the wounded. My own cousin told me you bound her arm when the guards just walked past. You didn’t have to do that.”*

He gestures to a wooden table covered in a rough map of Quolas.

> *“Then there’s Jyl Korlis. She says you harvested two weeks of mushrooms in a single day. She’s been able to send food to the lower levels for the first time in months. And before you even reached Quolas – saving that crashed ship’s crew from assassins? The whole city heard about that. Some say it was the Empire’s work. You made enemies that day, but you also made friends.”*

His voice lowers, earnest.

> *“The people speak of you. They don’t say your names – they don’t dare – but they whisper about strangers who fight back. We’ve been waiting for someone like you. Marcolf has ruled through fear for too long. The Helshar and Tumris are too busy hating each other to see what we could do together. But you – you’re not bound by our feuds. Will you help us?”*

## Pathran’s Sister Interrupts

Before the PCs can answer, a figure steps out of the shadows: a younger Sathari female, her plumage a lighter blue, her facial feathers shorter. She crosses her arms and glares at Pathran.

> *“Our mother is seriously upset with you for earlier. Your whole fight with the Tumris distracted the guards from their watch job and a bearsloth came in and injured many people and tarvixes before the guards could take it down.”*

Pathran’s expression hardens. He turns to her, voice tight.

> *“It was not my fault. They started it.”*

She shakes her head, then looks at the PCs.

> *“Also I have some news. Some of your supporters have started a riot against the defacing of the Pillar of Ancestors and it looks like it is about to get violent. If you do not do something soon a lot of people are going to get hurt.”*

Pathran’s face drains of colour. He looks from his sister to the PCs, then back to the map.

> *“The Pillar… They wouldn’t. Not yet.”* He grabs a cloak from a hook. *“I have to go. But I can’t stop a riot alone. Will you come with me?”*

## Transition to the Pillar Scene

The sister leads the way back up through the root tunnels. The group emerges near the mid‑level platforms and hurries upward. Already they can hear the sounds of shouting, the crash of tools, and the heavy tread of guards.

# Pillar of Ancestors – Approaching the Riot

You climb the last spiral ramp, the sound of shouting growing louder with each step. The wooden platform of the Pillar of Ancestors opens before you – a wide circle carved from the living trunk, ringed by hanging lanterns that swing wildly as the crowd surges.

The pillar itself rises hundreds of meters, its ancient surface covered in carved reliefs of Sathari ancestors: beaked faces, outstretched wings, scenes of hunting and harvest. But the lower third has been scarred – chisel marks gouge into the stone, and crude new shapes are taking form: the stern, angular face of a Mirialan man, unmistakably Marcolf.

At the base, a crowd of Sathari – perhaps fifty or more – presses against a thin line of guards. The guards wear padded armor, slugthrowers raised, their facial feathers bristling. Their captain shouts orders, but the mob drowns him out.

Artisans' tools lie scattered on the ground. A young Sathari stands on the lowest carved figure, a hammer in his raised fist, his four eyes wild. “They are killing our ancestors!” he screams. “If they will not stop, we will!”

Behind the guards, more reinforcements are climbing from lower levels – at least a dozen more, weapons ready.

The air is thick with fear and rage. A single spark – a shove, a shout, a misfired slug – will turn this into a bloodbath.

Pathran, at your side, grips his cloak. His voice is barely a whisper.

> *“By the roots… we are too late. Unless we do something now.”*

He looks at you, desperate.

> *“Please. I cannot stop this alone.”*


> *“You climb stairs and ramps, following the noise. When you reach the platform of the Pillar of Ancestors, you see the crowd – dozens of Sathari, young and old, faces twisted with rage. Artisans have been knocked aside; their chisels lie on the ground. A line of guards with raised slugthrowers faces the mob. At the center, a young Sathari stands on the base of the pillar, a hammer in his hand, screaming at the crowd to tear down the carvings. The guards’ captain is shouting for backup. Any second, someone will fire.”*
