# Extended Session — The Shard Moon Incident

**Campaign:** Chronicles of the Gatekeeper  
**System:** Star Wars FFG / Force and Destiny  
**Canonical status:** Repaired extended-session master, 2026-10-03  
**Placement:** After Delriss and immediately before Moraband proper  
**Supersedes for session running:** `Chronicles_Gatekeeper_PreSession_Vergence_v3.md` + `Chronicles_Gatekeeper_Session1_ShardMoon_Full_v2.md` as separate run documents. Those files remain preserved as older versions.

---

## GM use: what this document is

This is the session-time version of the Shard Moon adventure. It keeps the established Project Ash Warde concepts, maps, adversaries, Sigg continuity, and the required opening line, but turns the older outline into a living-world walkthrough. The moon is not a sequence of disconnected encounters. It is a failed Sith research site whose old systems still react to intruders, whose surviving experiments move between locations, and whose dark-side vergence changes what the party experiences as they go deeper.

Run it as a place with three simultaneous pressures:

1. **The ship is damaged.** The party can leave only after stabilizing or repairing critical systems, and the needed answers or parts are inside the ruins.
2. **The facility is waking.** Old sensors, containment routines, droids, and experimental subjects become more active as the PCs restore power or cross restricted zones.
3. **The vergence notices people.** It does not simply cause spooky visions. It studies fear, guilt, ambition, attachment, and the desire to control outcomes. The Red Whisper uses what characters reveal through their choices.

The adventure should work whether the party moves carefully, aggressively, technically, socially, or through the Force. Failed checks should change cost, position, injury, suspicion, available information, or later threats rather than simply stop progress.

## Campaign continuity before play

Before the hyperspace exit, record:

- current ship hull trauma, system strain, Critical Hits, and repair state;
- which companions and animals are aboard;
- Sigg's current injuries and emotional state;
- any enemies who may still be tracking the party;
- who currently carries the holocron and recovered Delriss information;
- unresolved fear, Conflict, promises, or personal doubts that the vergence can echo.

The active ship is the newer upgraded vessel with enough room for the party's animals and companions. Do not revert to the earlier tiny fighter-sized interior. Only the crash-landing battle map should visibly contain the ship.

## Persistent clocks

### Ship survival — 0 to 4
Start at **1** after the crash unless the emergency descent was exceptionally successful.

- **0:** ship is stable; departure requires only ordinary checks and time.
- **1:** grounded but safe; one significant repair or replacement is needed.
- **2:** power or coolant instability; leaving without repair risks another Critical Hit.
- **3:** cascading faults; the ship cannot safely lift until the party finds parts/data or performs difficult repairs.
- **4:** emergency; a major system must be cannibalized, replaced, or bypassed before departure.

Good emergency checks can reduce the starting value. Despair, abandoning stabilization, or a major vergence event can increase it. Do not destroy the ship merely because the clock reaches 4; make the problem concrete and solvable.

### Facility wakefulness — 0 to 6
Start at **0**. Advance when the party restores power, triggers security, opens sealed research zones, uses loud explosives, fails restricted-access checks with Threat/Despair, or allows an alarm to propagate.

- **0–1:** dormant systems, isolated movements.
- **2:** DR-9 or local systems detect activity.
- **3:** patrol routines begin; Branch H creatures start tracking.
- **4:** Vesh-Null activates and containment doors change state.
- **5:** Vael Serrin can address the party through active systems; W-Prime begins waking.
- **6:** full containment/failsafe logic; the complex tries to seal, purge, or redirect intruders.

Lower wakefulness by isolating networks, deliberately powering zones down, convincing DR-9 to help, or using facility credentials discovered in play.

### Vergence pressure — 0 to 5
Start at **1** when the ship is pulled from hyperspace. Advance after significant fear, deliberate dark-side use, major cruelty, accepting the Red Whisper's bargains, or entering the ritual/core zones. Reduce it through genuine acts of trust, mercy, acceptance of uncertainty, or deliberately refusing a tempting shortcut.

At higher pressure, use more personal manifestations, but never use the clock to dictate a character's emotions or choices.

# Scene 0 — Wrong Exit from Hyperspace

## Purpose

Start in motion, establish the vergence as a physical and spiritual emergency, injure or complicate the ship without making the crash predetermined in detail, and remove Sigg from the role of instant exposition source.

## Opening

The ship tears out of hyperspace already rotating. Stars smear sideways across the canopy. Moraband is visible farther in-system, but the navigation solution is wrong by an impossible margin. An outer volcanic moon fills too much of the forward display. Warning lights stack faster than anyone can clear them.

Sigg looks once at the sensor distortion and understands enough to be frightened.

> “Oh crap! It's a vergence—”

Then he collapses.

## What is happening without the PCs

The ship's nav computer is trying to reconcile mutually inconsistent position data. The moon is not exerting impossible ordinary gravity; the problem is that the party emerged along a distorted hyperspace solution influenced by the dark-side nexus. Old Shard Moon systems briefly detect the ship and issue an ancient automated challenge on frequencies the crew may not notice during the emergency.

The Red Whisper is not yet speaking in sentences. It is testing what images make individual characters hesitate.

## Run the emergency as a team problem

Ask each player what system or person they take responsibility for. Useful approaches include:

- **Piloting (Space):** stop the tumble, choose a survivable descent corridor, use terrain rather than fight the entire vector.
- **Mechanics:** keep engines, coolant, landing systems, or power distribution alive.
- **Computers:** filter sensor ghosts, isolate corrupted inputs, recover the ancient challenge packet.
- **Astrogation:** prove the ship did not merely make a normal navigational error and identify the moon's orbit.
- **Medicine:** stabilize Sigg while restraints, gravity, and equipment are shifting.
- **Discipline/Cool:** distinguish supernatural pressure from actionable warnings.
- **Athletics/Coordination:** secure cargo, companions, animals, or damaged internal systems.

Success should buy something specific: lower Ship Survival, a better crash location, preserved medical gear, working sensors, intact communications, or a first piece of facility data. Threat creates costs. Despair should create a later problem, not erase the party's agency: a landing strut fails, a sensor mast shears away, a repair part is destroyed, or an old beacon locks onto the ship.

## Personal vergence flashes

Use at most one brief image per PC during the crisis. Tie it to something already established rather than inventing secret backstory. Examples:

- the cockpit reflected as a sterile observation chamber;
- a companion heard calling from behind a sealed door;
- rows of pale bodies in red-lit fluid;
- a version of the character calmly choosing the ruthless solution;
- Moraband's red horizon behind a face that should not be here.

Never tell the player what the image “means.”

## Exit

The party commits to a crash corridor. Move directly into Scene 1.

# Scene 1 — Crash Landing Zone

## Map

**01 — Shard Moon Landing Zone.** This is the only moon battle map that should show the active ship.

## Immediate description

Black volcanic stone has been crushed into glassy fans around the wreck. Red mineral dust hangs in low gravity eddies. The ship has carved a scar through an old paved approach whose geometry is too regular to be natural. Beyond the impact trench, broken marker pylons form a path toward higher ruins. Several are dark. One flickers as the ship's reactor settles into an unstable rhythm.

## What is happening now

The crash has awakened a low-power perimeter circuit. It is trying to classify the ship using credentials that have not existed for centuries. A damaged subterranean relay begins transmitting a repeating containment-status request. Nearby scavengers initially keep their distance. If Facility Wakefulness reaches 2 here, a remote sensor establishes the party as “unverified biological inventory.”

Sigg can be stabilized here, but he should not wake with all the answers. When conscious, he remembers the sensation of the vergence and recognizes Sith design language in the marker pylons. He does not know Project Ash Warde.

## Full walkthrough

First, let the players define priorities. Common priorities are people, animals, medical stabilization, ship integrity, perimeter security, and finding a way off the moon. Reward them for making the crash site feel lived in. Companions should react like people, not inventory: frightened animals need calming, an injured ally may insist they are fine, loose cargo may block access to a panel.

Second, assess the ship. Do not make a single Mechanics roll answer everything. Present two or three concrete faults based on the emergency result: power regulation, sensor damage, landing gear, coolant, or engine alignment. A successful inspection tells them what can be repaired locally and what requires a part, calibration file, or high-output power component likely found in the ruins.

Third, let them notice the old road. From elevated wreckage or a sensor sweep they can identify at least two destinations: a route toward the volcanic ruins and a higher silhouette consistent with an observatory. A weak encrypted signal originates deeper underground.

Fourth, trigger the first vergence pulse after the party has had time to make choices. The pylons glow one after another toward the ruins. An inactive speaker inside the ship whispers half a second of a human voice before returning to static. A containment code appears in the sensor log even if no one was listening when it arrived.

## Optional threat

If the scene needs action, use scavengers, a damaged probe, or a single Branch H spoor encounter rather than a major boss. The purpose is to prove the moon is occupied by things that adapted to its ruins, not to consume the session at the ship.

## Failure forward

- Failed repairs identify the fault but consume parts or time.
- Failed Computers checks expose the party to the old network and advance Facility Wakefulness, but still recover the direction of the transmitting relay.
- Failed survival/perception checks still find the route, but the party misses evidence that something large has been following old patrol paths.

## Reveals

The party can learn that the moon held a controlled installation, that the crash woke it, and that some systems still treat biological beings as tracked subjects.

## Exit

The party leaves the ship as a temporary safe point and enters the Volcanic Ruins Traverse.

# Scene 2 — Volcanic Ruins Traverse

## Map

Use **02 — Volcanic Ruins Traverse** as the primary map. **09 — Volcanic Ruin Transit**, **12 — Ruined Landing Approach**, and **13 — Temple Route** can be inserted as travel or optional encounter spaces.

## World state

The traverse was once both a service route and an evacuation corridor. It has partially collapsed into lava-cut ravines. Old signs point in contradictory directions because later Sith personnel repurposed civilian-looking infrastructure for restricted research traffic. Branch H hunter creatures use the same chokepoints because the old conditioning beacons still emit traces they associate with escaped subjects.

## Opening description

The road leaves the crash basin and becomes a series of basalt shelves joined by fractured bridges. Some walls are natural. Others are black blocks cut so precisely that ash has nowhere to settle in the seams. Here and there, painted evacuation arrows remain beneath soot. Several point toward doors that were deliberately welded shut from the outside.

## What happens without the PCs

A Branch H hound has noticed the crash but is not charging blindly toward it. It circles downwind and follows elevated routes. A disabled caretaker droid farther ahead repeatedly attempts to report a checkpoint failure. If Wakefulness is already 3 or more, an old probe begins moving toward the observatory to confirm intrusion.

## Play sequence

Let exploration establish three stories at once:

1. people once tried to leave in a hurry;
2. security tried to stop some of them;
3. something biological escaped anyway.

At the **evacuation lane**, the party finds abandoned cases, restraint hardware, and a wall where emergency directions were overwritten by a red restricted-access symbol. A careful search can distinguish ordinary staff evacuation from captive movement.

At the **failed checkpoint**, a security station contains damaged authorization logs. Technical success can recover a partial designation: `ASH / WARD-...` or a Branch letter. Threat can awaken a local alarm while still granting the data.

At the **shrine den**, a creature has nested among ritual fragments because the structure remains warm. If the party approaches carefully, they can find evidence of the Branch H conditioning tags before combat begins. The creature does not need to fight to the death. Food, distraction, environmental control, Force influence, or retreat can all resolve the situation.

## Branch H Hunter Hound

Run the hound as a tracker, not a random monster. It is drawn toward Force activity and old conditioning cues. It tries to isolate a straggler, test defenses, then withdraw and follow if hurt. If captured or examined, it bears signs of surgical modification and an old control implant.

## Choices that matter later

- If the party kills every altered creature on sight, W-12 later assumes they are another purge team.
- If they preserve or disable the hound nonlethally, they may learn how to spoof Branch H control signals.
- If they restore checkpoint power, they get useful data but advance Wakefulness.
- If they destroy the checkpoint, they reduce local surveillance but lose an easy credential path.

## Exit

The route climbs toward the ruined observatory, the first place with a chance to explain why the moon exists.

# Scene 3 — Ruined Observatory over the Lava Abyss

## Map

**03 — Ruined Observatory.** Optional approach or overflow spaces can use **08 — Sith Necropolis** if a broader exterior is needed.

## Scene purpose

Give the party its first reliable historical framework. Introduce Survey Custodian DR-9 as a character rather than a lore vending machine. Reveal that the moon's “observation” mission changed over time into experimentation.

## Opening

The observatory is built across a split in the mountain. Below, lava light moves like a slow pulse through the abyss. The primary dish has collapsed, but several interior rings still rotate a few degrees at a time whenever power surges. Star charts cover one wall. Another wall has been physically cut away where a later installation connected downward into the rock.

## DR-9

DR-9 begins with fragmented authorization logic. It may address the party as inspectors, emergency personnel, or incorrectly identified research staff depending on what credentials they recovered.

Its goals are:

- restore enough observatory function to complete an interrupted survey report;
- obey ancient restrictions it no longer fully understands;
- avoid acknowledging that the site's mission was changed after its original programming;
- determine whether the party is here to “resume,” “audit,” or “terminate” the program.

Useful lines and behaviors:

> “Observation mandate remains valid. Supplemental biological mandate is... not within my original remit.”

> “There were no prisoners assigned to this facility. There were subjects. The distinction was administratively important.”

> “Project name corrupted. I retain: ASH. WARD. CROWN. That is insufficient for a proper index.”

If treated with patience, DR-9 becomes a cautious ally. If threatened, it does not heroically fight; it locks data, transmits an intrusion report, or retreats into another terminal.

## Information ladder

Do not dump everything on one Computers check. Let the party uncover layers:

**Public/old mission:** observe the Moraband-system vergence, stellar anomalies, and hyperspace distortions.

**Restricted expansion:** measure how the vergence affects living Force-sensitive subjects.

**Black program:** produce repeatable, controllable traits associated with a powerful target/template designated Warde.

**Hidden branch references:** W, H, L, V, C.

**Core location:** lower facility/Crimson Core accessed through ritual and laboratory security, not from the observatory alone.

## Sigg

If conscious, Sigg can interpret the Force implications but should be disturbed by the scientific language. He can say that observing a vergence is plausible; using people to calibrate it is something else. He may realize the facility was trying to turn destiny-like phenomena into a repeatable process.

## Red Whisper first clear contact

Use the first full sentence here, ideally through a dead speaker or reflected in the rotating star map:

> “They measured what the Force would give them. You could measure what it would give you.”

The Whisper should offer a method, shortcut, or certainty rather than generic evil. It might tell the truth about a hidden door in exchange for the party restoring a subsystem. Accepting the help is not an automatic fall; it advances Vergence Pressure and gives the Whisper more context.

## Failure forward

A failed data recovery still yields the branch designations, but corrupts a map sector, advances Wakefulness, or gives Vael Serrin notice that someone is reading restricted files.

## Exit

The party can take a direct route toward the ritual plaza or a more technical lower-service route if they have recovered enough facility data. Both ultimately lead toward the Clone Vaults.

# Scene 4 — Ritual Plaza and Temple Ruins

## Map

**04 — Ritual Plaza**, with **07 — Obsidian Ritual Annex** as an optional side area.

## Scene purpose

Show that Project Ash Warde was never merely cloning. The Sith tried to combine biological replication, conditioning, and ritual alignment to the vergence.

## What the players see

The plaza is open to a red-black sky. Shallow channels radiate from a central dais and disappear into the structure below. Some carried fluid. Others held conductive or alchemical material. Broken restraint fittings are arranged with ceremonial precision around the outer ring. The architecture resembles a temple until closer inspection reveals calibration marks, numbered positions, and observation windows.

## What is happening without the PCs

The vergence is strongest here. The Red Whisper can produce clearer sensory substitutions. If Wakefulness is high, Vael Serrin uses the site's projectors to stage a controlled “orientation” rather than immediately attack. An old ritual sentry or Sith Acolyte Experiment may be present as guardian, survivor, or awakened test subject.

## The ritual truth

The researchers believed Warde's significance could not be copied from tissue alone. They tried to manufacture compatible bodies, expose them to carefully shaped fear and choice, and use the vergence to force a repeatable spiritual pattern.

The result was not a line of obedient Wardes. It was broken identity, unstable foresight, conditioned violence, and entities that remembered things they had never lived.

## Run the plaza as a choice scene

Present at least three ways forward:

- **Ritual understanding:** interpret the alignment and open the lower route without fully powering the site.
- **Technical bypass:** use observatory credentials, repair a control station, and command the access mechanism.
- **Physical breach:** force a sealed route, faster but loud and likely to raise Wakefulness.

A character using the Force may feel several possible outcomes at once. Do not turn this into a “correct vision.” Offer incompatible futures and let the player decide what matters.

## Guardian encounter

If using the Sith Acolyte Experiment, give it traces of training but no stable Sith identity. It may recite conditioning phrases, defend a marked boundary, and become confused if addressed by a name or treated as a person. This can become combat, conversation, or a grim clue.

## Reveal

The party should leave knowing that Branch C — Crown was intended to produce the stable culmination of the program, and that the lower facility still contains at least one Crown-status chamber.

## Exit

Access opens to the Clone Vaults and research levels.

# Scene 5 — Clone Vat Gallery

## Map

**14 — Clone Vat Gallery**.

## Opening

The first lower laboratory is colder than the ruins above. Rows of vertical tanks stand behind cracked transparisteel. Most are empty or opaque with residue. A few contain collapsed support frames sized for humanoid bodies. Emergency lights still mark each station with a branch letter and serial number.

## World motion

Med-droids and broken attendants repeat partial maintenance cycles. If the party restored power above, several tanks attempt to warm. If Wakefulness is 4+, Vesh-Null begins routing doors to funnel intruders away from protected core areas.

## Discoveries

Let the party find evidence through objects, not only terminals:

- growth records showing repeated physical viability but psychological collapse;
- deliberately altered training schedules;
- tissue samples labeled from several unrelated donors, proving “Warde replication” was not simple genetic cloning;
- a log noting that memory imitation was less useful than reproducing decision pressure;
- transfer records moving selected subjects to Conditioning and Branch C.

## Moral pressure

One chamber may still support a barely viable subject or preserved biological material. The party can shut it down, attempt rescue, leave it untouched, or use it to obtain data. There is no universally correct answer. Make the consequences concrete and knowable where possible.

## Failure forward

Failed Medicine/Mechanics checks may save the subject but leave them unstable, or recover data while damaging the chamber. Failed Computers checks may disclose the party to Serrin while still revealing W-12's last known location.

## Exit

The gallery connects to Conditioning, Branch L containment, and deeper fortress corridors.

# Scene 6 — Conditioning Ward and Specimen Archive

## Map

Use **16 — Specimen Archive & Conditioning**.

## Purpose

Turn the abstract program into personal evidence. This is where the party learns how subjects were taught, punished, rewarded, and rewritten.

## Environment

Training rooms are arranged around an archive core. Some contain ordinary furniture. Others have restraint points or projection walls. The archive includes recorded emotional responses, combat trials, social tests, and “choice convergence” experiments in which subjects were repeatedly pushed toward a desired decision.

## What is happening

W-12 is nearby unless earlier events moved them. They know the ward better than anyone alive but do not initially know whether the party has come to kill, retrieve, or replace them. The Red Whisper tries to make the party mistake W-12's confusion for deception.

## Failed Subject W-12

W-12 should be tragic, dangerous, and capable of choice.

They have fragments of implanted expectations rather than Warde's actual memories. They may recognize names they have never met, anticipate a gesture a second early, or react violently to conditioning phrases.

Possible opening lines:

> “Which version are you here for?”

> “They said he always came back to the same choice. I didn't.”

> “If you know Warde, tell me something they couldn't put in a file.”

W-12 wants proof that the facility no longer owns their identity. They fear both being left here and being taken away as evidence. If the party treats them only as an object, they become defensive. If offered agency, they may provide a core route, security phrase, or warning about W-Prime.

## Archive revelations

The archive can reveal:

- Branch W: body/template replication and identity imitation;
- Branch H: Force-sensitive pursuit animals;
- Branch L: parasitic suppression of Force-sensitive captives;
- Branch V: host bodies for imprint/possession research;
- Branch C: a “Crown” candidate intended to combine stable body, conditioned identity, foresight response, and vergence alignment.

Also reveal that the project was never completed cleanly. The final containment event was partly a purge by staff and partly a revolt/failure inside the program.

## Branching outcomes

W-12 can accompany the party, hide, flee toward the surface, or remain to free other subjects. Record the choice. If they travel with the party, Vesh-Null gives them priority as escaped inventory.

# Scene 7 — Branch L Containment

## Map

**15 — Branch L Containment**.

## Purpose

Provide horror and a practical danger that directly interacts with Force-sensitive characters without turning off their abilities by fiat.

## Environment

Containment cells line a humid service chamber. Transparent tubes once carried nutrient fluid. Many are dry. Some are not. The ceiling has too many maintenance cavities and too many of them show fresh movement.

## Force Leeches

Branch L creatures were designed to weaken captives and make ritual/conditioning results easier to control. Run them as ambush hazards that latch, drain, and separate rather than as bags of hit points.

Players can solve the encounter through combat, fire/heat control, sealing ducts, environmental manipulation, bait, chemical knowledge, or restoring a containment field. A technical solution may be cleaner than a fight but can raise Wakefulness.

## Important discovery

The leech program's notes reveal the researchers' underlying fear: they could not reliably control a strong Force-sensitive subject while that subject retained full access to the Force. Suppression was a workaround, not mastery.

This undermines Vael Serrin's later claim that the program was close to success.

## Consequence

If leeches escape this zone, note it. Do not automatically turn them into a future campaign problem, but give the party a chance to recognize that leaving a live containment breach has consequences.

# Scene 8 — Beast Pens / Fortress Catacombs

## Map

Use **06 — Fortress Catacombs** and, if needed, portions of the fortress outer wings.

## World motion

At moderate/high Wakefulness, Branch H creatures are now actively coordinating through old beacons. Vesh-Null may release them to herd rather than kill. The point is to direct intruders toward controlled kill zones or away from the Crimson Core.

## Scene play

Give the party evidence that some animals were born here while others were modified after capture. Pens include scent-training tracks, Force-response test chambers, and handler stations.

If the party previously spared or studied a hound, they can spoof a beacon, redirect a pack, or pass through a territory with reduced hostility. Pay off earlier choices.

A surviving handler log can identify the Warden-Construct Vesh-Null as the final containment authority and note that it cannot distinguish “protect the project” from “preserve the people inside it.”

## Exit

The catacombs lead to the ritual calibration controls and then the fortress core.

# Scene 9 — Ritual Calibration Control

## Map

**17 — Ritual Calibration Control**.

## Purpose

Put the party in control of systems that can change the finale. This is the decision point where they can choose to wake, isolate, purge, expose, or partially shut down the core.

## Environment

The chamber mixes engineering consoles with ritual geometry. Mechanical indicators track power, atmosphere, door state, and containment. Another set of instruments records emotional and Force-response measures using symbols rather than numbers.

## Vael Serrin contact

If not already encountered, Serrin speaks through the system here. Decide the form based on campaign tone: preserved holographic intellect, ritual imprint, or half-living remnant sustained deeper in the facility. The important point is that Serrin is **facility-bound** and has incomplete control.

Serrin's argument should be coherent:

- Warde represented an unacceptable concentration of unpredictable power.
- The Sith needed a way to reproduce and control such potential.
- Failed subjects were regrettable but necessary iterations.
- The PCs are standing in a site that could, with their help, finally provide answers the original researchers lacked.

Serrin should offer something real: safe ship-repair schematics, a route to Moraband, information about Warde, or a way to stabilize W-12. The price is restoring core access, providing a Force reading, or preserving part of the program.

## Systems the party can influence

- isolate the Crimson Core from upper security;
- disable Branch H beacons;
- re-enable or disable Branch L containment;
- open evacuation routes;
- recover a high-output component compatible with the ship;
- wake or keep dormant the Crown chamber;
- initiate a controlled data purge;
- overload the facility, with consequences.

No single roll should decide all systems. Let the party choose priorities under pressure.

# Scene 10 — Hidden Fortress / Crimson Core

## Map

**05 — Hidden Fortress & Clone Vaults** for the central complex and core confrontation.

## Opening

The Crimson Core is not a throne room. It is a research heart built to look sacred because the people who designed it believed measurement and ritual were the same act. A central chamber contains the Branch C apparatus. Observation stations ring it. Emergency blast doors show evidence that someone once tried to seal the chamber from both sides.

## What is happening

At Wakefulness 4+, Vesh-Null is active. At 5+, W-Prime begins waking or has already awakened. At 6, containment logic is actively changing doors and atmosphere to force resolution.

## Warden-Construct Vesh-Null

Vesh-Null is not malicious. Its directives are irreconcilable:

1. protect the project;
2. suppress contamination;
3. preserve viable subjects;
4. prevent unauthorized removal.

A clever party can exploit the contradictions. Presenting W-12 as a person with agency may not persuade it morally, but can break an inventory-based rule if credentials or records have been altered. Technical sabotage can isolate its network control. Combat remains valid and dangerous.

Useful fragmented lines:

> “Viable subject cannot depart. Viable subject must be preserved. Departure creates non-preservation condition.”

> “Contamination includes unauthorized memory.”

## W-Prime Crown Prototype

W-Prime is the program's closest approach to a stable Crown subject, but should not simply be “evil Warde.” They are a person created under impossible assumptions.

On waking, W-Prime experiences the party as evidence that the project has failed to predict reality. They may be furious, curious, terrified, or conditioned into immediate violence depending on how the chamber was opened and what Serrin has told them.

W-Prime has broken foresight-like flashes and conditioned combat responses. During confrontation, let them predict obvious tactics once, then become destabilized when players choose empathy, retreat, sacrifice, or an irrational-looking but human choice.

Possible lines:

> “You're not in the sequence.”

> “He was supposed to choose this.”

> “Tell me which part of me is mine.”

## Three-way conflict

The strongest finale has at least three agendas:

- **Serrin** wants the project preserved or completed.
- **Vesh-Null** wants containment logic satisfied.
- **W-Prime** wants identity, escape, certainty, or destruction of the system that defines them.

The party can fight, negotiate, split systems, rescue subjects, destroy data, preserve selected research, or trigger evacuation.

## The Red Whisper

At high Vergence Pressure, the Whisper offers the easiest solution: certainty. It tells one PC exactly what to destroy, whom to kill, or which switch will “end this.” Some of the information may even be true. The temptation is surrendering judgment, not merely doing something violent.

## Finale outcomes

### Controlled shutdown
The party isolates Serrin, disables Vesh-Null, stabilizes W-Prime or leaves them contained by choice, and removes critical power. Best for preserving evidence and minimizing collapse.

### Rescue and escape
The party frees W-12/W-Prime/other viable subjects and prioritizes evacuation over records. The facility remains partly intact and may continue to be dangerous.

### Destruction
The party overloads or collapses the core. Make this a timed escape with specific routes opened or closed by earlier choices. Do not erase all evidence automatically; allow the party to carry what they deliberately secured.

### Bargain
The party preserves part of the program in exchange for information or repairs. Record exactly what remains active and what Serrin/the Whisper gained.

### Defeat without death
If the party loses a confrontation, use capture, separation, forced testing, or emergency containment rather than a mandatory campaign-ending kill. The facility wants subjects and data.

# Scene 11 — Escape / Facility Failure

## Trigger

Use this scene if the finale causes overload, purge, security collapse, or a mass release. If the party achieved a calm shutdown, run it instead as a tense withdrawal with doors and systems dying behind them.

## World motion

The complex's systems are no longer coordinated. Some doors fail open, others lock. Released experiments move toward heat, exits, or familiar conditioning signals. DR-9 may attempt to preserve observatory records. Vesh-Null, if still functional, prioritizes whichever directive is currently dominant.

## Make the escape about previous decisions

- spared hounds may ignore or even follow a spoofed signal;
- escaped leeches create a hazard if containment was neglected;
- W-12 can guide the party through service routes if trusted;
- DR-9 can hold a door or transmit a map if allied;
- destroyed checkpoints remove security but also route guidance;
- preserving too much power makes the core more dangerous during overload;
- taking bulky evidence or artifacts costs time and carrying capacity.

Avoid a generic series of identical Athletics checks. Give each PC a job and let success in one area create options in another.

# Scene 12 — Return to the Crash Site

## Opening

The landing basin looks different after the facility. The ship is still damaged, but it is recognizably theirs: light, noise, companions, and ordinary problems after a place built to turn people into categories.

## Ship repair resolution

Pay off the original Ship Survival clock. The party may now have:

- a compatible high-output regulator;
- calibration data;
- observatory navigation corrections;
- salvaged Sith-era power components;
- DR-9's technical assistance;
- knowledge of exactly which damaged ship system can be bypassed.

Repairs should consume time and possibly parts, but this is not the place for another arbitrary dead end. The adventure has earned departure.

## Sigg

Sigg's reaction depends on what the party brings back. He is most disturbed by evidence that the project tried to manufacture not simply Force power but predictable choices. If W-12 or W-Prime is present, Sigg should address them as a person and ask what they want before discussing what they are.

## Companions and animals

Resolve any crash injuries, stress, separation, or protection choices. If an animal stayed aboard, it may react strongly to a rescued subject, artifact, or lingering vergence contamination. Use this as texture, not a forced supernatural detector.

# Scene 13 — Aftermath and Moraband Handoff

## Record before leaving

Write down:

- final Ship Survival and any remaining Critical Hits;
- Sigg's condition;
- whether DR-9 survives and whether it left the moon;
- W-12's fate;
- W-Prime's fate;
- Vesh-Null's fate;
- Serrin's state: destroyed, isolated, preserved, escaped in data, or bargained with;
- whether Branch L specimens remain loose;
- whether Branch H control systems remain active;
- which Project Ash Warde records were recovered or destroyed;
- any artifact or component removed from the site;
- promises made to surviving subjects;
- notable dark-side choices, mercy, betrayal, or trust;
- whether pursuers could detect the crash or facility activation.

## What the party should know

By departure, the party should understand that:

- the moon was a Sith auxiliary research and ritual site;
- the vergence was studied and exploited;
- Project Ash Warde attempted to reproduce/control qualities associated with Warde;
- the program split into multiple branches and failed repeatedly;
- the Crown effort came closest to a stable result but did not prove that identity or destiny could be manufactured;
- the moon's history complicates rather than replaces the party's reason to go to Moraband.

## Transition

Once the ship is spaceworthy, resume the Moraband approach. Use the published/campaign Hard Astrogation procedure only if the Shard Moon arrival did not already adjudicate the same navigation failure. Do not punish the party twice for one distorted jump.

The tonal transition is important: Shard Moon was a place where people tried to control the Force through systems. Moraband is a place where the Force itself presses back.
