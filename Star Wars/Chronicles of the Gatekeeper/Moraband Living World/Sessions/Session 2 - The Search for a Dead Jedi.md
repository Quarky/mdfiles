# Session 2 — The Search for a Dead Jedi: Full GM Walkthrough

## Opening — the landing site disappears

Begin with the expedition already walking. The ship is still visible behind them, but each ridge hides more of it. Ask who looks back last. When the vessel finally disappears, say:

> The red horizon closes over the only machine on this world that knows your names. Ahead, the ground breaks into miles of cracked stone and toppled monuments. Nothing marks a road. Every direction contains ruins, and every ruin looks old enough to have waited specifically for you.

The immediate objective is Warde’s Delta-7B, detected as a faint metal contact. If the group did not find that signal, the gatekeeper proposes searching for a tomb complex while scouts discover the same trail through tracks and architectural evidence. Choose a different character to lead each travel leg. A failed search check still reaches the next event, but the group arrives tired, exposed, separated from a useful vantage, or after the pursuers gain ground.

## The broken causeway

The first route crosses the remains of a monumental causeway spanning a dry ravine. Statues once stood shoulder to shoulder along it; now only stone feet remain. The center has collapsed, leaving a gap above darkness and a lower route through the ravine.

Give the party a real choice. Crossing the broken span is fast and visible. Descending is slow but sheltered. Searching the statues may reveal history but costs daylight. If rivals are allied, they argue for the choice matching their agenda. Hasar favors the fastest path and calls caution “another word for allowing Warde to choose the ground.” A wounded hunter favors the ravine and resents being treated as expendable.

At the midpoint, place evidence of Warde’s passage: a lightsaber-cut handhold made when the causeway was less damaged, dried fabric caught in a crack, and a boot impression protected beneath fallen stone. These establish that he survived the landing and traveled under his own power. The gatekeeper first says, “He was dying,” then corrects itself: “He had been wounded. I do not know what happened afterward.”

If the party crosses above, a slab breaks after the heaviest character passes. Let Athletics, Coordination, Move, cables, teamwork, or engineering solve it. Failure leaves a lost supply case or an injured companion, not a dead campaign. If they take the ravine, they find a wall carved with a procession of kneeling figures. One figure has been scratched away recently, and a whisper offers a character the power to ensure nobody ever has to kneel again.

## The half-buried Delta-7B

Near the end of the travel leg, the metal signal resolves into the curved edge of a cockpit protruding from red sand. Reveal it slowly: a wingtip, scorched Republic-era paint, then the shape of an old Jedi interceptor without its hyperspace ring.

> The fighter lies nose-down as if the desert tried to swallow it and stopped midway. Wind has polished the exposed hull but cannot erase the old impact scars. The cockpit is closed. Beneath the dust, one handprint has been burned into the metal from the inside.

Let the players treat this as an investigation rather than a single Computers check. Power can be restored. The canopy can be opened mechanically. Flight records can be reconstructed. Footprints and discarded medical supplies show that Warde dragged himself from the wreck. The emergency kit is empty, but one sealed ration remains because he deliberately left it. That small choice becomes redemption evidence: even desperate and alone, Warde did not consume everything available.

The navicomputer contains fragments:

- The landing was controlled until the final seconds; Warde chose this region rather than crashing randomly.
- His hyperspace ring remains in stable orbit and can be found later.
- The last cockpit recording contains breathing, a warning alarm, and Warde saying, “It happens here. I have seen it.”
- A corrupted sensor image shows a distant structure where current scanners show only dunes.

If someone repairs more of the system, play a second fragment: Warde addresses the gatekeeper prototype by name and apologizes for leaving it incomplete. The present gatekeeper becomes quiet and asks to retain a copy.

Allow the fighter to be repairable with time and parts. It cannot carry the whole party, but restoring it creates an emergency asset and a symbol that the future need not match the wreckage. If the party strips it, note exactly what they take. Warde later recognizes those components.

## The false oasis

On leaving the fighter, the route passes through rising heat distortion. Make the refuge plausible before making it supernatural. The characters see green growth in a sheltered hollow, hear water, and detect a temperature drop. Sensors confirm humidity. A thirsty companion reacts before any roll.

> Beyond the next ridge, red gives way to green. Reeds stir around a clear pool beneath dark-leaved trees. For the first time since landing, the air smells alive. Someone has set stones around a small fire, and a familiar figure sits beside it with their back toward you.

Choose the figure from character history—a lost friend, teacher, relative, or victim. Do not announce that every character sees someone different. Let them speak and discover the contradiction. The figure knows intimate emotional truths but gets one factual detail wrong. It offers rest first, then certainty: abandon the search, resurrect what was lost, or take enough power that nobody can be taken again.

Ask what each character wants to be true before calling for Hard Discipline or an appropriate Cool check. Failure means acting as if the refuge is real, taking the published strain and Conflict, but another character can intervene. Success exposes an inconsistency without instantly dissolving the scene. A character may knowingly remain because the conversation matters; treat that as a choice, not another failed roll.

Give the apparitions actual dialogue:

> “You have crossed half the galaxy for a dead man’s permission. Sit down. Let the dead speak for themselves.”

> “You call it temptation because you are afraid the answer might work.”

> “If you leave now, nobody has to know you were not strong enough.”

When someone rejects the offer for a stated reason, the oasis decays around that reason. Water becomes dust between their fingers, leaves blacken, and the familiar face asks one last sincere-sounding question. Preserve that answer for the palace apparitions.

Afterward, leave ambiguous residue: damp cloth, a recording containing only wind, or a footprint made by someone who was never there. The party should understand that Moraband can manufacture evidence as well as visions.

## The final movement

As the illusion collapses, the route ahead becomes visible: a canyon whose stone openings moan in the wind. The sun has not set—Moraband barely has a day—but the red light is weakening. The gatekeeper confirms that the Delta-7B proved Warde survived his landing. It cannot prove he died afterward.

If pursuers trail the party, reveal one of their markers at the false oasis. If they travel together, one rival secretly accepted an apparition’s promise. Do not announce this; show a changed expression, hidden object, or sudden certainty.

Close with the party choosing whether to enter Bonewind Canyon before the light fades or camp on exposed ground. Session 3 begins with the cost of that choice.

## Alternate paths

- **Repair the fighter fully:** spend the remainder of the session on salvage, defense, and a test flight. The oasis manifests around the repair site that night.
- **Avoid the oasis:** its familiar voices follow at the edge of hearing and reappear at camp. Reward caution with position, not immunity from the theme.
- **Split the group:** run the fighter investigation and oasis temptation in alternating cuts. Each group hears fragments of the other group’s conversation through static.
- **Turn back:** the route is still dangerous. Let the characters return and make a new plan without erasing what they learned.

## Carry forward

Record the state of the Delta-7B and its ring, the evidence that Warde survived, which apparition each character saw, the offer made, who accepted or rejected it, and the exact reason given. Those details are ammunition for both the Sith phantasms and the effort to redeem Warde.
