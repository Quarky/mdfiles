# Session 6 — The Palace and the Unfixed Future: Full GM Walkthrough

## Opening — a building that expects violence

Begin at the threshold with the party’s answer to Warde’s question still hanging in the air. The palace reacts to how they enter. Weapons drawn cause statues to turn their faces toward the group. Open hands cause the purple flames to retreat into their sconces. If someone carries a rescued enemy, repaired relic, or item preserved through hardship, the ancient script beneath their feet briefly glows around it.

> Torches ignite one after another without heat, filling the hall with sickly purple light. The ceiling arches more than a dozen meters overhead. Stone tiles bear letters in an ancient language, and alcoves hold robed figures whose faces have been chiseled away. Above the center of the hall hangs a chandelier made from the bones of many species. Empty corridors open in every direction. The old man walks inside as though returning home.

Do not rush directly to initiative. Let the party explore enough to understand that Warde has lived here. They find a repaired heater, water condensers, training marks, old blood cleaned from the floor, and pathways worn by one person pacing for years.

## The great hall and the evidence of a life

Warde leads toward the central chamber, but the characters may stop him or search. Present three discoveries:

1. **The prophecy wall:** hundreds of future events carved into plaster. Many are crossed out. Some failed. Some happened differently. Warde labels these “errors of interpretation,” but the evidence shows mutable outcomes.
2. **The camp datapad:** fragmented entries alternate between precise predictions and paranoid revisions. Several refer to the PCs before they were born or before they began the quest. Others predict companions who are not present, deaths that did not occur, and choices the party already refused.
3. **The mercy cache:** supplies Warde gathered for visitors he claimed he intended to kill. It includes water, medical packs, and several sizes of breathing masks. He prepared to save the people he believed would execute him.

These are not locked behind rolls. Checks provide dates, authorship, and additional leverage. The central conclusion must be visible: Warde’s foresight contains truth, but his belief that one ending is compulsory is unsupported.

The gatekeeper confronts him here if his identity is known:

> “You made me to teach discernment.”
>
> Warde does not turn. “I made you before discernment became another word for delay.”
>
> “No. You made me before fear learned to speak in your voice.”

Warde shuts the holocron off with a gesture if nobody prevents him. This is not damage; it returns when the scene changes.

## Gallery of broken prophecy

The route to the central chamber passes a gallery where reflective black panels show possible futures. Use this as the last character-focused sequence before the fight. Each character sees one desired future and one feared future. Neither should be presented as objectively true.

Ask the player what detail proves the desired future would be worth its cost. Then add the cost: someone absent, a compromise normalized, an enemy erased rather than changed. Ask what detail makes the feared future believable. Then show one element already contradicted by past choice.

Warde sees the same scene every time: himself dead beneath the skylight and the party holding the crystal. He says:

> “You think alternatives make you free. I have watched alternatives collapse into this room for twenty years.”

A character can answer with evidence gathered during the campaign. Do not reduce this to a single Charm roll. Let Warde argue. He will reinterpret failed predictions, accuse the party of arriving armed, and claim that their desire for the crystal proves his vision. Strong answers name a choice that cost the party something and could not be dismissed as self-interest.

Each concrete contradiction moves Warde from **Certain** to **Provoked**, then **Doubting**. He does not redeem here, but his opening behavior in the battle changes. At Doubting, he gives the party one full exchange before attacking.

## The central skylight — identity and attack

Warde enters the central chamber and discards the cane if he still carries it. Under the cracked skylight he stands fully upright. He shows the final kyber crystal, puts it inside his robes, and reveals his lightsaber.

> “I know why you came, even if you do not. The future is fixed, but I must still play my part. If you are worthy, you will understand.”

Let the disguise fall from the characters’ perception rather than physically changing his face. They finally see Suljo Warde in the old man. He ignites the green blade.

> “You came for my foresight. You will kill me and take it. I saw your hands around the crystal before you knew my name. I cannot change that fate—but I can make you earn it. What are you waiting for, my students?”

If Warde is **Certain** or **Provoked**, roll initiative immediately. If **Doubting**, allow one character to answer or take a non-hostile action. A credible proposal—place the holocron down, step away from weapons, offer him the exit—can delay his first attack, but not end the confrontation automatically.

## Running the battle as a conversation

Warde attacks aggressively because he wants the party to fulfill the prediction. Use Move, Enhance, Foresight, Saber Throw, Parry, and Reflect. With four or more PCs, use the additional initiative slot. He changes position constantly: central dais, pillars, side rooms, shattered gallery, and balcony approaches.

He does not attack incapacitated characters. He chooses dramatic attacks over efficient executions. Every round should contain a spoken claim that players can answer:

- **Round 1:** “You drew weapons before I named you enemies.”
- **Round 2:** “Every mercy brought you closer to the power you wanted.”
- **Round 3:** “You repaired the fighter because you feared being stranded—not because wreckage can become whole.”
- **When wounded:** “There. The vision narrows. You feel how easy the ending becomes.”
- **When a PC protects him:** “Do not pretend restraint absolves the blow you intend later.”

Allow a character to spend an action making a focused appeal while defending, locked blade-to-blade, helping an ally, or placing evidence where Warde must see it. Early checks oppose his Discipline at the published formidable resistance. The argument must cite an actual event. Generic statements about hope may earn Boost from good roleplay but cannot by themselves advance redemption.

Examples of effective proof:

- The supply cache contained something Warde remembered as absent.
- The prophecy wall includes failed outcomes.
- The party spared a rival when killing was easier.
- Warde instinctively saved someone at the statues or overlook.
- The party’s composition differs from his carved vision.
- Someone rejected the exact temptation his vision assumed they would accept.
- The PCs offer a future in which Warde lives to repair damage rather than dying to escape it.

## Dark apparitions

At the start of round two, impossible shadows pour from the side halls. Give each character a tailored vision based on earlier sessions. Call for the published upgraded Hard fear check. On failure, a phantasm manifests from that fear and enters initiative.

The phantasms argue against redemption. Victims of Warde demand death. Familiar faces accuse the party of weakness. Desired futures claim they exist only if Warde is killed. These voices are not reliable spirits; they are the dark side using memory as leverage.

Let characters weaken apparitions through meaningful acts: protect an enemy, surrender an advantage, admit uncertainty, or repeat the reason they rejected an earlier temptation. Physical attacks still work according to the adversary profile, but moral actions should visibly change the room.

If an apparition takes the form of someone Warde harmed, he initially treats it as deserved judgment. A character can point out that accepting punishment from an illusion is not accountability to the living.

## The turning point

When Warde suffers a Critical Injury, reaches half wounds, or hears two strong contradictions, he claims his death is near. This is the party’s clearest opening. If they stop attacking, add the published Boost. If they continue striking, he reads it as confirmation.

Move his state through **Certain → Provoked → Doubting → Resisting → Redeemed**. A successful focused appeal advances one step when supported by evidence. Triumph can advance an additional step or cause Warde to assist the next appeal. Failure does not erase previous progress; Warde attacks harder, the palace creates a complication, or a phantasm interrupts. Despair can move the fight to the balcony or bring rivals into the chamber, but never deletes the possibility of redemption.

At **Resisting**, Warde begins arguing with the apparitions rather than the PCs. He may add his skill or characteristic to the next social check, and the resistance becomes the published Daunting difficulty without his full Discipline opposition.

If the final appeal succeeds, describe silence before surrender:

> Warde’s blade stops close enough to cast green light across your face. The phantasms continue shouting, but he looks past them. His hand opens. The lightsaber falls, extinguishes, and rolls across the red stone. For the first time since you met him, he looks not old, but tired.

He admits that he made the party his executioners because death was easier to foresee than atonement. He gives them the crystal, but does not ask forgiveness.

## If Warde is defeated instead

If the party makes no serious effort to communicate and chooses to kill him, warn about the published Conflict before the irreversible blow. Warde eventually drops Parry, Reflect, and Center of Being and forces the predicted ending. His death should feel chosen by him, not like an ordinary depleted wound threshold.

If the party defeats but stabilizes him, the future is already disproved. He wakes furious and frightened. The redemption conversation can continue without combat, although Hasar or another rival may demand judgment.

If all PCs fall or refuse to fight, Warde stabilizes survivors and leaves his lightsaber and crystal behind. His vision failed. Footprints lead into the desert and may vanish. This is not a campaign failure; it creates a living Warde whose next choice remains unresolved.

## Balcony and rival interruption

Use the balcony only if the battle needs movement or an unresolved rival needs a final choice. The cliff lies far below, lightning strikes beyond the palace, and the wind can carry unsecured objects away. Warde may retreat here to force pursuit. Hasar may arrive with a clear shot.

Give the PCs control of the triangle. Protecting Warde from Hasar is powerful evidence but does not require forgiving him. Allowing Hasar to speak may expose truths the party needs. Disarming both sides can demonstrate a genuinely new outcome.

Do not make a single failed Athletics check throw a character to death. Use the edge as escalating position: hanging, losing gear, requiring rescue, or choosing whether to save an enemy.

## Resolution — the crystal and the living consequences

After the confrontation, let the chamber become ordinary stone. The purple flames weaken. The gatekeeper returns.

If Warde is redeemed, he refuses to become the party’s master. He accepts transport or help repairing his fighter, then intends to live among people and begin repairing harms. He can return as a peer or ally, but redemption is a first step, not erased accountability.

If Warde dies, the party finds the final crystal, his lightsaber, and the prophecy datapad. Some entries are accurate; some are impossible; some refer to future hooks. Never label which are true.

When the crystal enters the holocron, describe its faces rotating and settling. The gatekeeper says:

> “You have recovered the last portion of Warde’s knowledge. Yet the choices required to reach it have taught what no crystal could preserve. You made your own path here. Continue doing so deliberately.”

The gatekeeper’s tone depends on the ending. Redemption makes it hopeful and humbled. Warde’s death makes it subdued and cautious about teaching the final Foresight abilities.

## Leaving Moraband

Do not cut directly to hyperspace. Run the return as an aftermath sequence. Resolve the rival party, recover the Delta-7B if desired, retrieve those left with the ship, and show one consequence of each session. The false oasis is gone. The canyon voices repeat a sentence differently. The repaired fighter starts. A spared enemy helps carry the wounded. A dark bargain demands recognition.

At the landing basin, the ship should bear the consequences of absence: dust accumulation, repairs completed or worsened, anxious companions, and possibly evidence that something approached but did not enter.

As the ship lifts, ask each player what their character watches until the planet disappears. End with Moraband’s red pall falling away and ordinary starlight returning—not as proof the darkness is gone, but as proof they can leave it.

## Final record

Record Warde’s fate, Hasar’s fate, ownership of the lightsaber and crystal, the gatekeeper’s attitude, each character’s unresolved fear or temptation, all Conflict consequences, the state of the Delta-7B, and which prophecies remain possible future hooks. The adventure should alter the campaign; it should not reset after XP is awarded.
