# 10 — Obsidian Stair Ruins

> **Session 3: Night of Teeth and Voices**  
> **Story function:** Use a vertical ruin to combine history, environmental danger, and a choice about forbidden knowledge.  
> **Begins when:** The expedition follows architectural evidence toward higher ground.  
> **Ends when:** They reach a survey point with a route to the Wanderer’s region.

## Opening the scene

> Each stair is worn in the center by feet that stopped climbing before the Republic existed. The higher you go, the more the wind sounds like breathing.

Pause after the first two sensory details and ask what each character is doing. Do not read the whole situation as exposition. Establish one physical danger, one ambiguous sign, and one person or companion who needs a decision.

### Entry states

- The party climbs toward higher ground before the storm.
- A shortcut deposits them midway along exposed stairs.
- Pursuers occupy the upper landing and need help more than a fight.

### Questions before dice

- Who is physically leading, and who is watching the rear or the companions?
- What is the group trying to preserve: time, secrecy, supplies, trust, or evidence?
- Which earlier fear, injury, bargain, rescued enemy, or damaged system is relevant now?
- What would make a character stop and investigate instead of pressing ahead?

## The world is already moving

- Red lightning turns black glass surfaces into mirrors.
- Each thunder interval loosens another section of stair.
- Static makes powered weapons unreliable while simple tools remain dependable.

Run one motion beat when the scene opens, another after the first meaningful delay, and a third when the group commits to an exit. These are not punishments for roleplay. They show that weather, rivals, creatures, companions, and the vergence continue to act.

## Cast, agendas, and reactions

### Suljo Warde / the Wanderer

Before his reveal, Warde observes courtesy, compassion, certainty, treatment of weakness, and willingness to revise a conclusion. He wants evidence that the group will kill him because that would make his vision safe and comprehensible. He fears being offered a future he cannot predict. If he is not physically present, the environment can still test the same questions without granting him impossible knowledge.

### The gatekeeper

The gatekeeper distinguishes memory, inference, and hope. It knows Warde’s former habits but cannot automatically recognize the person Moraband has shaped. When contradicted, it should admit limits rather than become an answer machine.

### Rivals and companions

Rivals follow their current agenda, injuries, morale, and agreements; they do not teleport into encounters. Companions react as people and animals, request help, disagree, notice sensory contradictions, and remember how the party treats them. Never use a companion merely as a hostage without first establishing their own choice and risk.

### Lines and likely questions

| Speaker | Use at the table |
|---|---|
| Rival leader | calls a temporary truce before the next strike. |
| Mirror reflection | mouths a different answer from the character. |
| Gatekeeper | recognizes the structure as a route for penitents, not royalty. |
| Companion | points out a sheltered door no map or vision mentioned. |

When players ask something outside the table, answer from the speaker’s wants and fears. A lie should protect an agenda; it should not exist only to block progress.

## Full living-world walkthrough

1. Reveal the archive niche before the safest route.
2. Let Lore identify a Sith pilgrimage hierarchy.
3. Offer a readable fragment that teaches history and temptation together.
4. Use a physical collapse only after players commit weight.
5. Failure redirects to the switchback rather than dropping characters arbitrarily.
6. Make the summit show three possible destinations.
7. Allow the gatekeeper to challenge the archive’s certainty.
8. Record whether knowledge was copied, destroyed, or left.

9. After the immediate objective is clear, ask what the characters inspect, protect, leave behind, or tell one another. Give the place time to respond.
10. Advance only the clocks justified by what happened. Record changed trust, rival position, evidence, temptation, supplies, injuries, and open promises before moving on.
11. Offer at least two exits. One can be safer and slower; one faster and more exposed. Failure opens a costly route instead of repeating the same check.
12. Close on a changed fact—a new ally, damaged route, recovered object, revealed contradiction, or altered belief—not merely “the party goes to the next map.”

## Expected player approaches and world responses

| Player approach | Living-world response |
|---|---|
| Careful survey | Reveal terrain and one clue without requiring a roll. A check determines speed, extra context, or exposure. |
| Direct confrontation | The target answers according to agenda; rivals reposition and witnesses remember tone and restraint. |
| Force use | Apply Moraband’s automatic dark-side result. Describe what the character senses before asking how they act on it. |
| Technical solution | Let tools solve physical problems. Threat can cost power, parts, noise, or time without invalidating expertise. |
| Violence | Opponents seek position, survival, goals, or escape. The environment changes and consequences continue after initiative. |
| Withdrawal | Preserve the option. Advance a clock or surrender position, but do not treat retreat as refusing the adventure. |

## Exploration, objects, and usable discoveries

- Obsidian steps sharp enough to cut climbing lines.
- Copper grounding rods built into ancient railings.
- A sealed penitent niche with water and a confession token.
- A fallen banner whose fibers remain strangely intact.

Objects are not decorative lists. A character can examine, move, preserve, trade, destroy, repair, or later cite them. If an object matters to Warde’s redemption or the return journey, add it to the consequence ledger.

## Checks and Star Wars FFG outcomes

| Check | Purpose and failure-forward result |
|---|---|
| Average Knowledge (Lore) | interpret status and route. Failure changes time, position, exposure, or cost; it never removes the route. |
| Hard Athletics | damaged stair. Failure changes time, position, exposure, or cost; it never removes the route. |
| Hard Mechanics | open niche without collapsing it. Failure changes time, position, exposure, or cost; it never removes the route. |

For every roll, state the goal and the consequence before dice. Then use symbols independently:

| Result | Spend in this scene |
|---|---|
| Success | Achieve the stated intent; do not hide required continuation behind extra rolls. |
| Failure | Reveal the necessary lead through a worse position, delay, rival control, depleted supply, or hard choice. |
| Advantage | Preserve gear, reduce strain, notice a contradiction, improve the next approach, or help another character. |
| Threat | Add strain, noise, unstable footing, temptation, lost privacy, rival progress, or a damaged but usable object. |
| Triumph | Establish a lasting shortcut, decisive evidence, a grateful witness, or a fact Warde cannot dismiss. |
| Despair | Advance an active clock, separate objectives, awaken a dormant danger, or make a moral cost immediate. Do not strand the campaign. |

## Clues and no-bottleneck logic

- The inscription treats foresight as ownership of others.
- A route marker matches Warde’s cane.
- The palace is still not visible from here.

Present each essential conclusion through at least three vectors: visible terrain or behavior, a recoverable object or record, and a person/creature/environmental reaction. Rolls control clarity, ownership, and cost—not whether the campaign can proceed.

## Optional encounters and interruptions

- A sealed lesson offers Boost to Foresee at the cost of Conflict.
- A sentinel awakens only if the archive is removed.
- A safe camp lies in the vault below.

Use one interruption when pacing needs a new pressure or when players deliberately explore. Do not stack every optional encounter merely because a map has space. An encounter should reveal character, alter a resource, change an allegiance, or produce evidence.

## Meaningful choices

- Climb during lightning intervals or shelter and lose time.
- Help stranded rivals cross, bargain for gear, or leave them.
- Open the penitent niche and accept its spoken confession ritual.

Tell players when a choice risks Conflict. Do not conceal moral stakes behind GM knowledge. A dark-side shortcut can be genuinely useful; its price should be visible enough to be a choice.

## Environment and pressure

- Collapse clock: dust, crack, shift, fall. Heavy impacts advance it.

Use three layers: physical change, doubtful perception, and personal emotional pressure. Not every manifestation attacks. Silence, misplaced familiarity, and evidence that contradicts certainty are often more effective than constant damage.

## Branches, consequences, and clocks

- Copied doctrine becomes a later temptation.
- Leaving it intact grants a quiet light-side beat.

Record changes to:

- **Expedition:** route, fatigue, water, shelter, medical state, and return path.
- **Pursuit:** rival location, injuries, morale, evidence, bargains, and witnesses.
- **Warde Trust:** courtesy, compassion, threats, proven revisions, and surrendered opportunities to strike.
- **Temptation:** accepted instruction, dark-side comfort, certainty claims, and unresolved fear tags.
- **Palace Awakening:** disturbed relics, broken oaths, violent Force use, and protective/light-side acts.

## GM-only truths

- The hidden door leads to the genuine storm vault.
- A confession spoken honestly reduces temptation; a lie awakens the niche guardian.
- The lightning is natural weather magnified by the vergence, not Warde’s attack.

Do not reveal a truth merely because it is written here. Reveal evidence, behavior, and contradictions; let the group decide what those facts mean.

## Handouts and reveal timing

- Reveal a portrait/dossier only after the subject is clearly observed or identified.
- Reveal an evidence artifact after it is physically recovered or copied; never reveal its keeper notes.
- If the scene produces a prophecy, record, route sketch, or translated inscription, state whether it is a player-held fact, an uncertain interpretation, or a GM-only connection.
- Keep exact clock thresholds, hidden identities, manifestation triggers, and rival private goals GM-only.
