| Start | The Shard Moon adventure is resolved and the party can attempt the final approach. |

## Full walkthrough

1. The coordinates are more than twenty years old. Call for **Hard Astrogation**. Success takes about forty-eight hours; failure adds twelve. A failed check with the published severe negative trigger causes 4 system strain and a Critical Hit from a near gravity-shadow collision.
2. The Horuset system contains an asteroid field, seven moons, and one planet. Ordinary navigation from the system edge to Moraband requires no further check.
3. Describe the descent as light failing rather than a normal sunset. Moraband is perpetual dusk: crags, canyons, deserts, listing statues, ruined complexes, and a red pall over everything.
4. Apply the planetary dark vergence for every Force check: whenever a character rolls Force dice on Moraband, add one automatic dark-side result. Explain its temptation in the fiction each time.
5. Let the players search from the air. Scanners cannot identify one particular tomb among planet-wide ruins, but can seed life signs, impossible readings, or the old fighter signal. Seek and similar powers can narrow direction while still encountering later trials.
6. Choose a landing zone with cover: broken walls, dunes, statues, and a clear enough pad for the current upgraded ship. Only this landing map should visibly include the ship.

## Atmosphere loop

At every transition, use one physical sign, one sensory doubt, and one personal pressure: red dust tapping the hull; a voice at the edge of hearing; a memory that feels newly justified. Avoid constant spectacle. The planet is most oppressive when the characters cannot prove what changed.

- **[CAMPAIGN]** Resume after Shard Moon with the upgraded ship and accumulated damage.
- **[ADDITION]** Structured atmosphere loop and scene handoff.

## Search procedure

Before each Moraband encounter, a different PC makes an appropriate search check. Survival, Knowledge (Lore), Perception, and Vigilance are strong choices. The base difficulty is **Average, upgraded once** by the planet's spiritual hostility. Do not let one PC repeat the same skill on consecutive legs.

| Result | Effect |
|---|---|
| Success | The PC finds a clue and the group chooses how to enter the next encounter. |
| Failure | The group stumbles into it; every PC adds 1 Setback to the first check of that encounter. |
| Threat | Strain/Conflict pressure, bad ground, or a misleading echo. |
| Triumph | Shorten the route, reveal an illusion before it closes, or locate Warde's old fighter. |
| Despair | Lead into a temptation, split the party briefly, or turn a personal memory against the searcher. |

## Search methods

### The Force [MODULE]

Seek can treat Warde as someone encountered through the holocron connection. A general attempt to “feel” the route can use Average Discipline or Perception. Failure gives no direction; severe negative results can cause a five-point choice between Conflict and strain. Foresee should be clouded by the vergence without becoming useless.

### Scanners [MODULE]

A **Hard Computers** check detects Warde's half-buried Delta-7B because metal is unusual among the stone ruins. Its hyperspace ring remains in orbit and can be found with **Hard Astrogation**. Threat or Despair produces impossible readings.

### The gatekeeper [MODULE]

It reasons that a dying Warde would seek a tomb complex. It can advise, but it does not understand the changes that brought Warde here.

### Traditional methods [MODULE]

Track recent disturbance, read architecture, study wind-preserved footprints, and reconstruct a wounded pilot's movement. Reward concrete plans with Boost dice.

## Route framework [ADDITION]

Run three to five legs: landing ruins → false refuge → night camp → Sith complex → wanderer's ground. Insert Deceptions, Beasts, and Echoes in the order that best targets different PCs. The search always eventually reaches the wanderer; checks determine condition, information, and agency, not whether the climax occurs.

- **[MODULE]** Search check structure, methods, Delta-7B, hyperspace ring, and eventual wanderer encounter.
- **[ADDITION]** Explicit leg framework.
- **[DEVIATION]** None.

## Illusion menu [MODULE]

| Form | Temptation |
|---|---|
| Mirage | A green freshwater refuge promising rest and safety. |
| The Tomb | A newer tomb that feels unquestionably like Warde's resting place. |
| Familiar Faces | Lost friends or family urging surrender, reunion, or forbidden restoration. |
| Fondest Desire | Each character's deepest wish made apparently attainable through greater power. |

## Full walkthrough

1. Begin with one verifiable detail so the illusion is not obviously false.
2. Ask each affected player what the character wants to be true before calling for dice.
3. Resist with **Hard Discipline**, or Cool when steadiness rather than spiritual resistance fits. Add Boost or Setback for personal relevance.
4. Failure means the character reacts as though the illusion were real, suffers 3 strain, and gains 1 Conflict. For each Threat, the player chooses 1 strain or 1 Conflict whether the main check succeeds or fails.
5. Do not end the scene at the roll. Let another PC intervene, accept a cost, or expose an inconsistency.
6. A defeated illusion leaves a physical ambiguity: wet dust, a footprint, a voice on a recorder. Never confirm completely whether every element was unreal.

## Tailoring rule [ADDITION]

Use prior campaign choices, especially fears and moral compromises from the Shard Moon. The illusion should challenge the reason behind an action, not punish a player for engaging with the story.

## Encounter setup

Run after nightfall, preferably at camp or amid ruins. Use two phantasmal beasts per PC, or three for a high-powered/Knight-level group. Before initiative, oppose PC Perception with the beasts' Stealth and include their Living Shadow bonuses plus darkness and cover.

## Full walkthrough

1. Start with teeth and red eyes moving inside darkness, then reveal that the group is encircled.
2. If the PCs win the opposed check, beasts begin at medium range; otherwise, short range.
3. They engage broadly, use Crippling Blow and Ensnare, then gang up on vulnerable targets. They do not flee from pain or intimidation.
4. Darkness rises around the characters' legs as the fight continues. Make the environment feel complicit without adding arbitrary damage.
5. When the last beast falls, every body vanishes. Leave claw marks or disturbed dust only if ambiguity helps the table.

## Scaling without grind [ADDITION]

For a large party, use shared minion-style groups or resolve part of the pack as environmental pressure. For a wounded or noncombat group, let focused light-side actions, Discipline, or successful survival tactics drive them off. Preserve danger without exhausting the party before Warde.

## Scene purpose

This is the strongest pre-Warde temptation and should usually be the final trial before the wanderer. Moraband Sith phantasms rise from the ground, claim the world, read the characters' moral history, and offer what each wants.

## Full walkthrough

1. Extinguish ordinary light and let robed shapes rise from the soil.
2. Each PC makes a **Hard fear check upgraded three times** for the unnatural manifestation and Sith legend.
3. Use one phantasm per PC, or up to twice that for Knight-level characters.
4. Their opening depends on the group's lowest Morality. A fallen character is welcomed “home”; characters below 50 are praised for specific compromises; a high-Morality group is taunted and attacked.
5. Accepting instruction, power, or promised guidance grants at least 5 Conflict in addition to fear consequences.
6. In combat, phantasms pass through stone and bodies, attack will rather than flesh, and can assume a target's greatest fear. Strong light-side resolve should appear to hurt them.
7. The wanderer may end the encounter if the group is overwhelmed, creating trust without proving his identity.

## Hidden truth

The hermit is Suljo Warde. Age, isolation, dark-side use, and the planet's influence make him unrecognizable even to the gatekeeper. He appears bent, gray-haired, bearded, dusty, and harmless, supported by a gnarled cane.

## Full walkthrough

1. Introduce him according to the party's condition: entering camp, meditating beside ruins, or helping after a disastrous encounter.
2. He is unsurprised by visitors and offers water. He already knows details about the PCs from clouded visions but does not explain them cleanly.
3. He calls himself a seeker of truth and says he was stranded years ago. Keep him friendly, aloof, concise, and apparently a little mad.
4. If challenged, resolve suspicion with opposed PC Discipline against Warde's Deception 3 and Cunning 4, adding two Setback to the PCs because the dark side assists him; strongly consider a Destiny upgrade. Force attempts also suffer at least two Setback and Warde resists normally.
5. He claims a wounded Jedi named Warde died in his care after predicting the PCs' arrival. He offers to guide them to the tomb for an unnamed, “trifling” price.
6. If directly accused, he deflects without panic and continues toward the alleged tomb. Early discovery can move the confrontation to the palace rather than cancel it.

## Conversation matrix

| Question | Answer in disguise |
|---|---|
| Who are you? | A seeker who found solitude more agreeable than answers. |
| Why expect us? | Time reveals secrets, including those people hide from themselves. |
| Where is Warde? | Interred in an old tomb he can reach in a few hours. |
| What is the price? | A small matter, explained at the destination. |
| What is Moraband? | A place abandoned by its builders but never empty. |

## Full walkthrough

1. Heavy arched doors stand open just enough to enter. The wanderer increases his pace on the broad steps.
2. Inside, wall torches ignite with sickly purple light. The first chamber is enormous, roofed by a high geometric ceiling, tiled in ancient symbols, lined with robed statues, and hung with a bone chandelier.
3. Empty side rooms contain ruined furniture, cracked statues, and loose stone that will matter during the battle.
4. The wanderer leads the PCs to a central chamber under a cracked skylight. The palace stands on a rise; some windows overlook a long drop.
5. He straightens, abandons the cane, reveals his lightsaber and final kyber crystal, and identifies himself through certainty rather than a theatrical confession: his vision says the PCs will kill him for his power.
6. He attacks unless the confrontation began earlier. The first round should establish movement, verticality, and his refusal to believe the future can change.

# Scene 10 — Battling and Saving Warde


## Provenance key

| Mark | Meaning |

## Combat truth

Warde fights aggressively and does not flee. With four or more PCs, give him a second initiative slot at the end of the round. Use Destiny freely. He is trying to force the death he believes inevitable.

## Round structure

1. **Round 1:** Warde demonstrates Foresight, Move, Enhance, Parry/Reflect, or Saber Throw. Use the chamber, not a stationary duel.
2. **Round 2:** Dark apparitions intensify. Each PC makes a **Hard fear check upgraded twice**. A failed check manifests that PC's fear as a Moraband Sith phantasm that rolls initiative and attacks them.
3. **Later rounds:** Repeat apparitions when momentum becomes flat or the PCs dominate. A phantasm vanishes when its linked PC is incapacitated; that PC gains 1 Conflict per vanishing phantasm.
4. Warde does not attack fallen PCs. He changes targets and keeps speaking about fixed destiny.

## Saving Warde

A PC must spend an action and give up attacking to make a focused social appeal opposed by Warde's Discipline: effectively **Daunting, upgraded by Discipline**, while the dark side resists. Useful appeals include proving the future changed, invoking Tieka or TH-313, describing repairs to Cato Neimoidia, showing the archive's lesson about defensive responsibility, or accepting risk by lowering a weapon.

After one or more successes or a Triumph, Warde begins helping resist the dark side. The difficulty becomes plain **Daunting**, and his assistance may add a Boost. Another success can redeem him. If he reaches half wounds or suffers a Critical Injury, his claim that death is near gives a peaceful appeal a Boost when the PCs stop attacking.

If the PCs make no attempt to communicate and simply kill him, warn them before assigning the published 5 Conflict each.

## If the PCs lose

Their survival proves Warde's vision false. He stabilizes survivors, leaves his lightsaber and final crystal, and disappears into the desert. His trail may end, allow mercy, or support a later return.

## Resolution branches

### Warde redeemed [MODULE]

He deactivates his weapon, recognizes that he mistook the PCs for executioners instead of teachers, and gives them the final crystal. He declines to remain their master. He may accept transport or help repairing his fighter, then leaves to atone among people he can aid.

### Warde defeated [MODULE]

When the battle has lasted long enough, Warde may intentionally stop using Parry, Reflect, and Center of Being; PCs gain a Boost to combat checks against him. If wounds exceed threshold, he suffers The End Is Nigh and dies after accepting the foreseen conclusion. The PCs recover the final crystal, modified lightsaber, supplies, and a datapad of unstable prophecies.

### PCs defeated [MODULE]

Use the mercy outcome from Scene 10. Warde's absence becomes a future thread, not a failed campaign.

## Final objects

- **Warde's lightsaber:** Damage 9, Critical 1, Engaged, Breach 1, Sunder; fully modified Dantari crystal that can recover 2 strain on the appropriate Force-result spend; no open hard points.
- **Final kyber crystal:** completes the holocron and unlocks the final Warde's Foresight teachings.
- **Prophecy datapad:** future hooks whose truth remains intentionally uncertain.

## Holocron close

When the final crystal is installed, the gatekeeper acknowledges that the trials themselves taught as much as the sealed techniques. If Warde was redeemed, it sees the PCs as capable of surpassing its creator. If Warde was killed, it teaches with visible unease. The completed holocron can advise on Jedi history, lightsaber refinement, Force study, hidden temples, and future adventures at the GM's discretion.
