# Undercity and Industry Stories

ORIGINAL FICTION - non-canon examples.

## Shift Change at the Smelter
**Guilds/themes:** Rakdos / Boros / Izzet

Rakdos workers refused to restart a furnace. The Izzet metallurgist called them superstitious. The Boros sergeant called the shutdown prudent. The foreman said, “I watched the pressure gauge climb with the main valve closed.” The engineer went silent. “That is impossible.” “Good,” said the foreman. “Explain the impossible before my people climb back in.” Nobody laughed. That was how the sergeant knew the Rakdos were truly worried.

GM lesson: each participant should pursue a recognizable institutional or personal need; Ravnica emerges when those needs collide without erasing ordinary life.

## Deadbridge Delivery
**Guilds/themes:** Golgari / Orzhov / Guildless

The corpse hauler had three bodies and four invoices. Orzhov insisted one dead man still owed interest. Golgari insisted the corpse had entered reclamation jurisdiction. The widow said, “You are both talking like he stopped being my husband when he stopped breathing.” Neither guild had a form for that sentence.

## The Wrong Crate
**Guilds/themes:** Dimir / Boros / workers

The crate was labeled lamp oil, weighed like books, and smelled like nothing. The porter told his foreman. The foreman told her Boros cousin. By the time a patrol arrived, several Dimir agents knew a suspicious crate existed, but none knew the porter had already moved it away from the night crew. That small act ruined everybody’s plan.

## Boilerpit Mercy
**Guilds/themes:** Izzet / Golgari

An Izzet apprentice fell through a grating and broke both legs. A Golgari worker found him first. “You people are not authorized in this conduit,” the apprentice gasped. The worker looked at the bone through his trousers. “Neither is that.” He carried him to the surface. A week later, new respirator filters appeared anonymously at the worker’s rot farm.

## Night in Wayport
**Guilds/themes:** Mixed

At sunset every bridge into Wayport seemed to belong to somebody different. Boros checked one. Orzhov guards watched another. Kraul traders controlled the stairs below. A Rakdos runner guided drunk merchants across a narrow span for coin. The guildless warehouse women knew the truth: the city belonged to whoever still knew which bridge was open at midnight.
