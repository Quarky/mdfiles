# Kaguya Public Terminal & Shuttle Infiltration
## Full Living-World GM Guide

**Campaign:** Shadow of the Beanstalk / Kaguya  
**Use:** GM-side session document  
**Start point:** The party exits the maintenance corridor into the shared public-terminal loop during the SXC occupation.  
**Primary locations:** maintenance exit, public terminal loop, controlled decompression zone, military checkpoints, service bypass, private-terminal corridor, inner landing field, target shuttle, landing-ring and flight-control networks.  
**Design goal:** The world keeps moving whether the PCs act, hide, argue, hack, rescue people, fight, split up, or abandon an objective.

This is the expanded living-world treatment of the shorter `Kaguya_Public_Terminal_Lockdown_Session_v1_23.md`. The shorter file is useful as an import/session index. This document is the GM play document.

# 01 — WHAT IS FIXED

These facts should remain true unless play has already changed them.

- The party begins in the **maintenance corridor** and emerges into the **shared public-terminal loop**.
- The public terminal does **not** look through windows into private boarding areas. Each private terminal is reached through its own controlled corridor and security checkpoint into the starport.
- SXC has a **heavy military presence** already in position.
- The miner team is nearby: **Riker, Mac, Sully, Fletcher, and Maeko**.
- An **SXC Breach Trooper deliberately fires** into a vulnerable pressure boundary near the maintenance-side approach.
- The resulting puncture is **small**. This is not explosive movie-style decompression.
- The affected section takes roughly **15–30 minutes** to become critical.
- Emergency bulkheads begin compartmentalizing the terminal, but **not every exit is visibly sealed** and not every route closes at once.
- SXC uses the emergency to justify a much harder lockdown.
- The inner landing area is an enormous pressurized space, roughly **3 km-scale**, with regolith visible between paved service lanes, around pads, and in utility margins.
- It contains **scores upon scores of pads**, private-terminal spurs, service equipment, cargo routes, pressure gates, and moving traffic.
- Ships are **placeable assets**, not baked into the map.
- The **Passenger Dropship** retains its established sourcebook mechanics. The Industrial Shuttle and Stealth Shuttle can be used as visual/scene assets unless full stats have been assigned elsewhere.

## One fact intentionally left for the GM

The shooter is deliberate. Whether **Commander Adrien Vale personally ordered the breach** is not fixed in the existing material.

Pick one before play or leave it unresolved:

1. **Vale ordered it.** He wanted a controlled emergency that justified detention powers and segregation of civilians.
2. **Vale approved a plan without knowing the exact method.** The shooter was following an ugly interpretation of a broader directive.
3. **A subordinate operation created the breach without Vale's knowledge.** Vale's later choices determine whether he covers it up, investigates it, or sacrifices the shooter.

Do not decide this only to make a player theory right or wrong. Decide it based on the campaign direction you want after the session.

# 02 — THE LIVING-WORLD RULE

This session is not a chain of rooms waiting for the PCs.

Every important group has something it is doing **right now**.

If the PCs spend ten minutes debating in a service closet:

- the decompression progresses;
- civilians are moved;
- SXC challenge codes change;
- the breach trooper moves farther from the scene;
- Sergeant Sol receives new orders;
- the landing ring continues operating;
- the target shuttle may be reclassified, serviced, boarded, impounded, or moved;
- the network logs are mirrored, scrubbed, or locked;
- the miner team makes decisions of its own.

Do not punish investigation. Let the world advance in visible ways.

The point is not "you waited, so you lose."

The point is: **time means something because everyone else is alive too.**

# 03 — SESSION START SNAPSHOT

## What the terminal feels like before the shot

The party has spent enough time in maintenance infrastructure that the first thing they notice is not the soldiers.

It is the air.

Maintenance corridors smell of hot insulation, lubricant, metal dust, sealant, and the faint sourness of spaces that were never designed for tourists. The public loop smells neutral by comparison: filtered air, cleaning solvent, heated food from a kiosk that is still operating because its automation has not been told to stop, and too many frightened people pretending nothing has happened yet.

The lighting is wrong for a normal terminal day.

The retail panels are bright. The advertising walls are bright. The emergency strips are also on.

That combination makes every face look slightly unreal.

SXC portable barriers divide the loop into controlled lanes. Troopers are not packed shoulder-to-shoulder. They are placed where a professional force would place them if it already knew which corridors it might need to close.

Some are watching passengers.

Some are watching doors.

A few are watching each other.

## Read-aloud: emerging from maintenance

> The maintenance hatch gives way with a pressure-seal click and the sound changes immediately.
>
> Behind you: fans, conduit vibration, pumps.
>
> Ahead: people.
>
> The public loop is open, bright, and occupied by soldiers.
>
> Temporary SXC barriers cut the floor into lanes that were never meant to be lanes. A departure kiosk is still cycling vacation advertisements above a line of civilians waiting to have their IDs checked. Red emergency strips are lit along the base of the walls even though no public alarm is sounding.
>
> You can see the miners before they see you.
>
> Mac is impossible to miss in the exosuit.
>
> Fletcher is watching the soldiers.
>
> Riker is watching the walls.
>
> Sully is talking.
>
> Maeko is looking for a way out.
>
> Nobody here looks like they believe this is temporary.

# 04 — THE FIVE GM CLOCKS

Use these as state trackers. Do not show the numbers to the players unless your table likes visible clocks.

## Clock A — Pressure Loss: 0 to 6

**Start:** 0 before the shot.

| Level | State | What people notice |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | Stable | Normal pressure. Emergency systems armed but quiet. |
| 1 | Fresh breach | Alarm, moving air, papers/debris sliding, pressure differential warning. |
| 2 | Noticeable loss | Voices sound different, air movement is constant, loose objects migrate, ears pop. |
| 3 | Dangerous sector | Cold increases, movement near the breach becomes difficult, exposed checks gain Setback where appropriate. |
| 4 | Compartment stress | One route is unsafe without protection or a local isolation action. Civilians begin showing symptoms. |
| 5 | Near critical | Unsealed exposure becomes a serious medical/environmental threat. Safety system prioritizes isolation over access. |
| 6 | Critical | The affected compartment must be sealed, evacuated, patched, or abandoned. Adjacent areas remain survivable if bulkheads did their job. |

The full progression should normally consume **15–30 minutes**, depending on the size of the affected volume and what the PCs or engineers do.

A successful Life-Safety intervention can pause or reverse this clock.

## Clock B — SXC Lockdown: 0 to 6

**Start:** 1. The force is already deployed.

| Level | State |
|---|---|
| 0 | Routine security posture |
| 1 | Pre-staged security, portable checkpoints |
| 2 | Emergency lanes, ID sweeps, civilians grouped |
| 3 | Checkpoints become detention points; challenge codes rotate |
| 4 | Service routes searched; drones and patrol pairs enter maintenance space |
| 5 | Landing-ring sectors isolate; suspicious craft are held |
| 6 | Full local manhunt / controlled sweep |

Increase it because of obvious resistance, credible reports of intruders, network alerts, violence, or time after the breach.

It may decrease locally if the PCs create a convincing stand-down, expose evidence to the right person, or redirect the emergency response.

## Clock C — Evidence Integrity: 0 to 5

**Start:** 3.

This tracks how much clean corroborating evidence remains easy to obtain.

- **5:** multiple independent sources preserved and verified.
- **4:** clean video + telemetry or order trail available.
- **3:** starting state; evidence exists in several systems but is vulnerable.
- **2:** one source has been scrubbed or corrupted.
- **1:** mostly witness testimony / partial technical fragments.
- **0:** local proof is gone; a later investigation may still reconstruct events.

Evidence Integrity drops when:
- SXC purges or mirrors logs;
- the shooter swaps equipment;
- the terminal is fully segmented;
- a network defender completes a cleanup action;
- PCs destroy infrastructure containing evidence.

It rises when:
- a verified copy is made;
- a second independent source corroborates the first;
- a physical witness is protected;
- the PCs copy the relevant audit trail and metadata.

## Clock D — Civilian Separation: 0 to 5

**Start:** 1.

SXC is trying to turn one crowd into manageable groups.

| Level | State |
|---|---|
| 0 | Crowd still mixed and mobile |
| 1 | Starting state; informal groups and queues |
| 2 | Civilians sorted by destination/status |
| 3 | miners/workers pulled aside |
| 4 | detainees moved toward controlled holding |
| 5 | witnesses and allies are physically separated from the party |

The party can slow this with social pressure, false announcements, opened service doors, actual rescue work, or evidence shown to Sol.

## Clock E — Shuttle / Departure Pressure: 8 to 0

Start this clock when the PCs commit to escaping through the landing ring or when SXC identifies a likely target craft.

At the end of meaningful rounds/scenes, count down.

- **8–6:** ordinary ground operations; the craft is accessible.
- **5:** ground crew receives a hold notice.
- **4:** local clamp status becomes restricted.
- **3:** security checks the crew manifest.
- **2:** tow/impound crew is dispatched.
- **1:** security is physically arriving at the pad.
- **0:** impound begins. The shuttle is still present, but the objective changes from "quiet departure" to "depart under active interference."

# 05 — WHAT EVERYONE WANTS

## Commander Adrien Vale

**Visible role:** Senior SXC occupation commander.

**Immediate goals:**
- prevent uncontrolled movement into the landing complex;
- control the public narrative;
- keep the military response looking disciplined;
- prevent footage of SXC causing the breach from becoming authoritative evidence;
- avoid a terminal-wide firefight if he can accomplish the same result with checkpoints and isolation.

**What he does if ignored:**
- orders compartment-by-compartment control;
- moves media/civilians away from key sightlines;
- places transport assets under hold;
- begins consolidating witnesses;
- requests technical confirmation that the breach is "contained."

**How he speaks:** calm, formal, almost reasonable.

He rarely threatens first. He describes the future as if it has already been administratively decided.

**Useful line:**

> "Nobody is being punished. We are stabilizing a pressure event. If you cooperate, you will be processed, moved to a safe compartment, and released when the situation is secure."

The problem is that "processed" gives him control of who talks to whom.

## Sergeant Kira Sol

**Visible role:** Ground-level checkpoint commander.

**Immediate goals:**
- keep civilians alive;
- maintain the cordon;
- understand why the emergency sequence does not match what she was briefed to expect;
- keep her soldiers from turning a tense crowd into a massacre.

She is not looking for an excuse to defect.

She is looking for something that makes the contradictions make sense.

**Strong evidence can create an opening.**

One clean clue makes her listen.

Two independent clues make her hesitate before enforcing a questionable order.

Three corroborating clues can make her quietly preserve evidence, misreport a search result, delay a sweep, or redirect a squad.

She still remains an SXC sergeant unless play changes that.

**Useful line:**

> "Do not tell me what you think happened. Show me something I can put in front of someone who outranks me."

## SXC Breach Trooper

**Visible role:** One rapid-insertion trooper among many.

**True role:** Deliberate trigger agent.

**Before the shot:**
- holds a position with a clear line to the vulnerable pressure boundary;
- checks the same sightline twice;
- receives a short encrypted burst or hand signal;
- shifts half a step to make the shot clean.

**After the shot:**
- does not stare at the breach;
- moves back into the formation;
- changes lane assignment at the first opportunity;
- avoids being the first soldier to "discover" the cause;
- if the unit rotates, tries to leave the immediate sector.

If pursued physically, the trooper does not sprint unless made.

A running soldier is memorable.

The trooper's best camouflage is being exactly as disciplined as everyone else.

## Riker — The Mechanic

Riker's attention goes to systems before people.

At the first pressure alarm he is already looking for:
- actuator housings;
- local engineering panels;
- emergency seals;
- whether the breach came from inside or outside;
- what the bulkhead sequence tells him about prior commands.

**If PCs do nothing:** Riker starts opening a service panel and tries to keep one evacuation route usable.

**Line:**

> "That door moved before the alarm. I'm not imagining it. The actuator was already awake."

## Mac — The Loader

Mac is a cyborg and is currently in the heavy exosuit.

He is not the person who explains what is happening.

He is the person who holds something open long enough for someone else to get through.

**If PCs do nothing:** Mac positions himself where a closing barrier, panicking crowd, or fallen cargo rack could trap people. He does not attack soldiers unless they attack first or threaten someone he is protecting.

**Line:**

> "Pick a side of the door."

That is his way of asking who the group is rescuing before it closes.

## Sully — The Fast-Talking Technician

Sully looks for:
- a maintenance credential;
- a believable uniform role;
- an unlocked terminal;
- a person he can convince that he is supposed to be here.

**If PCs do nothing:** he begins building a cover story for the group and steals the first useful access code he can get without starting a fight.

**Line:**

> "Good news: everyone is confused. Bad news: organized people are using the confusion better than we are."

## Fletcher — The Former Guard

Fletcher watches security behavior.

He notices:
- which trooper is waiting for an order;
- who has overlapping fields of fire;
- which checkpoint is real and which is theater;
- where the command person is standing;
- who moved before the alarm.

**If PCs do nothing:** Fletcher identifies the breach trooper as "wrong" before he can prove why and starts shadowing the formation.

He remains reckless, especially if he believes a security route could lead toward information about **Dr. Katashi Hara**.

**Line:**

> "That one knew where to stand before anybody else knew there was a problem."

## Maeko — The Driver

Maeko thinks in routes and vehicles.

She is tracking:
- service carts;
- tugs;
- pressure-rated vehicles;
- which lanes still lead inward;
- which traffic is being allowed through the lockdown.

**If PCs do nothing:** she finds the first plausible route toward the landing ring and marks it mentally, even if it is not currently usable.

**Line:**

> "People get stopped. Equipment gets waved through. Remember that."

# 06 — OPTIONAL LIVING-WORLD NPCS

These are scene additions. They exist to keep the terminal from feeling like it contains only PCs, named allies, and military actors.

## The Earthside family

A parent and two older children were meant to board a private terminal connection. Their bags are already beyond the checkpoint.

They are not heroic.

They are not helpless.

They are angry because the family is now physically separated from medication packed in a checked case.

If helped, they become credible civilian witnesses.

## The ground-crew pair

Two Kaguya workers in pressure-rated ground suits are stranded on the public side of a private-terminal checkpoint.

They know the landing ring.

They know which service spur connects to which pad family.

They do **not** know the military plan.

One wants to help the party.

The other wants to survive the shift and go home.

## The SXC medic

A military medic treats civilians during the decompression.

This NPC is useful because the uniform does not make the scene morally simple.

The medic will argue with any soldier who blocks access to an injured civilian.

The medic will also identify the PCs to command if they openly attack SXC personnel.

## The terminal cleaner

A cleaner saw the shooter before the shot because they were working near the pressure boundary.

They did not see a face.

They remember one strange detail: the trooper checked the exact panel line and stepped aside before firing.

They are terrified of becoming "the witness."

# 07 — OFFSCREEN TIMELINE

Use relative time so this guide still works if the preceding session's exact clock time changes.

## T+00 — Maintenance hatch opens

- Party emerges.
- Miners are nearby.
- SXC is at Lockdown 1.
- Breach trooper is already positioned.
- Vale is not necessarily on the map; he is receiving sector reports.
- Sol is managing one of the active checkpoints.
- Landing-ring operations continue under restrictions.
- No public announcement says "decompression."

## T+02 to T+05 — The setup becomes visible

If the PCs watch instead of moving:
- a checkpoint receives new code words;
- a soldier moves civilians away from one corridor but not another;
- the breach trooper gains a clear firing lane;
- Riker notices an actuator cycle;
- Fletcher notices the trooper's pre-positioning;
- Sully overhears that "sector isolation" is already in someone's orders.

This is the last easy opportunity to catch the setup before the event.

## T+05 — The shot

The breach trooper fires.

The shot is loud.

The decompression is not.

The first physical effect is air moving toward a hole that is much smaller than the volume of the terminal.

The alarm follows.

Then the bulkheads.

## T+06 to T+10 — Confusion window

This is the best time for:
- helping civilians;
- tracking the shooter;
- copying camera footage;
- using confusion to cross a controlled lane;
- reaching a local engineering panel;
- establishing a network connection before credentials rotate.

SXC Lockdown rises to 2.

Pressure Loss reaches 1–2.

Civilian Separation begins climbing.

## T+10 to T+15 — Narrative control begins

SXC announcements describe a "pressure incident."

Troopers begin telling people:
- remain where you are;
- show identification;
- move to assigned safe compartments;
- do not interfere with emergency response.

Nobody says who fired.

At least one camera feed is now inaccessible to ordinary staff.

Evidence Integrity may drop if nobody protects it.

## T+15 to T+20 — The terminal divides

Bulkheads have created new geography.

Some corridors are sealed.

Some are still open but one-way.

Some doors show safe green but no longer accept ordinary passenger credentials.

Some exits are visibly clear and tempt people into chokepoints.

SXC Lockdown reaches 3 unless interrupted.

The public terminal is no longer one social space.

It is a set of compartments.

## T+20 to T+30 — The emergency becomes political

Pressure Loss approaches 3–5 if untreated.

SXC begins moving witnesses and workers.

Service corridors receive search teams.

Landing-ring systems begin flagging unusual departures.

If the party has not moved toward the inner ring yet, one of the easier routes closes.

If they are already inside, the closing route now matters behind them.

## T+30 to T+40 — Landing-field consequences

Ground operations have adapted.

Tugs form barriers in some sectors.

Private terminal crews receive military hold notices.

Flight desks are told to verify manifests manually.

SXC believes any unauthorized movement now is deliberate.

The advantage shifts from "confusion" to "knowledge of systems and terrain."

## T+40 onward — Occupation rhythm

The emergency is no longer new.

This is worse for the PCs in some ways.

People have assignments.

Challenge codes are current.

Patrols know their sectors.

But routine also creates predictability.

A patient party can exploit that.

# 08 — SCENE 1: THE MAINTENANCE EXIT

**Foundry scene:** `Public Terminal — Militarized Lockdown`

This scene should not begin with combat.

The immediate tension is: **the players can see the trap before they know what the trap is for.**

## Physical layout at the moment they arrive

Place:
- the maintenance exit on the edge of the shared loop;
- miners within conversational distance but not necessarily clustered together;
- one portable SXC checkpoint controlling travel toward the private-terminal spokes;
- one secondary barrier controlling travel back toward public transit;
- civilians in loose queues;
- the breach trooper with an apparently ordinary line of duty near the vulnerable pressure boundary;
- Sol where she can see the central lane, not hidden behind a wall;
- one SXC medic farther back.

Do not make every door red.

Do not make every route closed.

A real lockdown is more unnerving when there are still routes that look usable.

## What a quick glance reveals

**No check:**
- SXC presence is heavy.
- civilians are being redirected.
- miners are here.
- something is wrong with the normal flow.

**Perception / Vigilance success:**
- troop positions are too orderly to have been improvised after an emergency;
- some barriers are already oriented for inward movement control;
- one trooper has a strange, unnecessary sightline toward a pressure boundary.

**Advantage:**
- identify a less watched service door;
- notice a local engineering panel;
- spot a camera covering the trooper's position;
- realize Sol is watching her own troops as much as the crowd.

**Triumph:**
- see the breach trooper receive the trigger cue;
- notice bulkhead control indicators change state before the shot;
- identify that a camera is writing to a local incident buffer.

**Threat:**
- a soldier notices the party emerging from an unauthorized-looking maintenance route;
- a miner calls out too loudly;
- the hatch remains open and draws attention.

**Despair:**
- a patrol member recognizes one of the PCs or allies from an earlier incident;
- the maintenance route is immediately marked for a search.

## Miner interaction

Let the five miners talk over one another briefly. They should feel like people with different specialties, not a quest-giving chorus.

Riker wants a panel opened.

Fletcher wants everyone to stop looking at the panel and look at the soldiers.

Maeko wants to know whether the party came through a route that can fit a vehicle.

Sully wants to know what cover identity they are using.

Mac asks the only question that matters:

> "Leaving, or staying?"

There is no correct answer.

# 09 — SCENE 2: THE DELIBERATE BREACH

**Foundry scene:** `Public Terminal — Controlled Decompression`

Do not describe the whole compartment instantly becoming a wind tunnel.

The breach is a puncture in a very large pressurized environment.

The danger grows.

That is what makes it useful to SXC.

They get time to act inside the emergency they created.

## The shot

The breach trooper fires once.

The point of aim is structural, not a person.

Describe three sounds:

1. the weapon;
2. a hard metallic crack;
3. a thin, ugly hiss almost lost beneath the alarm.

Then the floor-level emergency lighting changes.

A second later, the air starts moving.

## Read-aloud

> The shot is sharp enough to flatten every conversation in the loop.
>
> It does not hit a person.
>
> It hits the wall.
>
> For half a second nothing obvious happens.
>
> Then a strip of warning lights goes red from one end of the corridor to the other.
>
> Somewhere behind the wall, machinery wakes up.
>
> The air begins to move.
>
> Not explosively.
>
> Steadily.
>
> A paper receipt skates across the floor, catches on a barrier leg, and stays there fluttering.
>
> Then the first bulkhead starts to close.

## The first thirty seconds

Everyone reacts according to training.

- SXC soldiers move to assigned positions.
- Sol shouts for people to clear the bulkhead tracks.
- The SXC medic moves toward civilians.
- Riker moves toward the engineering access.
- Mac picks a side of the nearest closing door and begins physically moving people through.
- Sully starts shouting plausible emergency instructions before anyone has authorized him.
- Fletcher tracks the breach trooper.
- Maeko watches which service traffic SXC still permits to move.

This is the living-world baseline.

The players are not responsible for making every NPC function.

## If the PCs chase the shooter immediately

Fletcher joins unless stopped.

The breach trooper:
- falls back into a four-person formation;
- swaps physical position with another trooper;
- uses identical armor as camouflage;
- moves toward a checkpoint rotation point.

A direct chase becomes a recognition problem before it becomes a combat problem.

Ways to keep the identification:
- record the armor ID;
- mark the weapon;
- follow camera coverage;
- maintain visual contact;
- obtain squad assignment data;
- compare ammunition telemetry.

If the PCs simply fire into the formation, SXC treats it as an attack and Lockdown rises sharply.

## If the PCs prioritize civilians

Reward this with information, not just morality.

People being helped can become:
- witnesses;
- cover;
- sources of local knowledge;
- people Sol personally sees the PCs protect.

A rescue can be the reason Sol later listens.

## If the PCs try to stop decompression

Use the **Kaguya Pressure and Life-Safety Network** or local engineering actions.

A stable solution normally needs:
1. locate the leak and read pressure;
2. isolate/correct airflow;
3. open the safe evacuation route or run stabilization.

A local actuator jack can control a narrow bulkhead group even if the wider network is locked out.

Riker can assist or take one physical part of the problem.

Mac can buy time where machinery is jammed.

Sully can obtain emergency credentials.

ß
## If the PCs do nothing

At roughly T+10:
- SXC has the crowd sorted;
- the shooter is no longer in the immediate formation;
- the first relevant camera feed is restricted;
- one miner or civilian group is about to be separated;
- Riker has opened a panel and learned that the bulkhead sequence was primed suspiciously early.

The story still moves.

# 10 — THE DECOMPRESSION AS A LIVING LOCATION

The decompression is not a one-time event.

Use changing environmental details every few minutes.

## Pressure Loss 1

- hanging signs sway;
- dust and scraps migrate;
- people keep touching their ears;
- emergency announcements are still calm;
- ordinary clothing is fine.

## Pressure Loss 2

- the airflow is strong enough to be constantly felt;
- small objects roll or slide;
- speech across distance is harder;
- lightweight carts drift if brakes are not set;
- doors resist opening against the differential.

## Pressure Loss 3

- exposed work becomes physically tiring;
- temperature begins falling;
- civilians are visibly distressed;
- a child, elderly traveler, or injured person may need assistance;
- moving through the wrong doorway creates a much stronger gust.

## Pressure Loss 4

- one route should now be treated as unsafe without protection or immediate action;
- hands become clumsy in the cold;
- warning lights become more urgent;
- engineering systems prioritize closure over convenience.

## Pressure Loss 5

- unprotected civilians need to be out;
- anyone remaining is there because they are trapped, unconscious, stubborn, or trying to rescue someone;
- SXC sealed suits let soldiers move where civilians cannot.

That asymmetry should matter.

## Pressure Loss 6

The compartment is lost until repaired.

Do not destroy the whole terminal.

Kaguya's safety design exists specifically to prevent one hole from becoming total station loss.

The cost is that the station becomes a maze of sealed compartments.

# 11 — NETWORK RUN: GHOST IN THE TERMINAL

Use this when the runner goes after the public-terminal evidence and access controls.

**Primary goals:**
- copy the breach footage;
- protect or verify the incident cache;
- open a civilian/service route;
- optionally issue a temporary credential.

**Physical parallel:** checkpoint sweeps and the decompression continue while the runner is inside.

## What makes the run feel connected to the physical scene

Every network command should visibly change the room.

Examples:

**Loop a camera**
- Fletcher gets a clean movement window.
- A patrol walks past the wrong alcove.

**False gate change**
- civilians move;
- the movement can become cover;
- careless use can create a crowd surge.

**Open service door**
- a real door unlocks with real people on the other side.

**Temporary credential**
- Sully can walk through a lane once while pretending this was always authorized.

**Protect logs**
- the evidence clock stops degrading.

**Download footage**
- show the runner the frame: the trooper aiming before the alarm.

## Defender: Arun Vale

Arun is a cautious procedural sysop.

He does not want to destroy the terminal network.

He:
1. sweeps when suspicious;
2. traces when confirmed;
3. reinforces the objective ICE;
4. locks out when he has enough attribution.

Do not imply a family relationship between Arun Vale and Commander Adrien Vale unless the campaign later establishes one.

## Fail forward

If the incident cache is purged, a short-lived mirror remains in the audit path.

If the runner is locked out, the supervisor console or a local incident buffer can still provide a physical route to partial evidence.

If the footage is incomplete, pair it with weapon telemetry or eyewitness evidence.

# 12 — SCENE 3: MILITARY LOCKDOWN

Return to `Public Terminal — Militarized Lockdown`, or keep using the decompression scene with more bulkheads closed.

The key shift:

Before the breach, SXC was controlling movement.

After the breach, SXC claims emergency authority to control **people**.

## Announcement

> "Attention. A pressure-control incident has occurred in the public-terminal maintenance sector. Remain in your current compartment. Present identification when requested. Follow all SXC emergency directions. Do not approach marked bulkheads."

It is technically true.

It omits the most important fact.

## What Sol is doing

Sol is trying to prevent three problems at once:

- civilians crossing unsafe doors;
- soldiers overreacting;
- unknown actors exploiting the emergency.

If the PCs are calm, she can talk.

If they are armed and running, she has to act.

If they have visibly just saved people, give that fact weight.

## First meaningful encounter with Sol

She does not begin with "surrender."

She begins with:

> "You came out of a maintenance corridor that is not on the public evacuation route. I need names, destination, and a reason."

Good cover can work.

Truth can work if carefully framed.

Evidence can change the whole conversation.

Violence also works, but it changes the kind of session this becomes.

## Showing Sol evidence

**One clue:** she says it could be incomplete.

**Two independent clues:** she checks a private channel and finds something that does not match.

**Three corroborating clues:** she creates a narrow opening.

Possible narrow openings:
- delays reporting the party for 90 seconds;
- sends a search team to the wrong service junction;
- leaves one checkpoint challenge code unchanged;
- preserves a local copy of a log;
- allows civilians through a safe-side bulkhead without adding names to the detention list.

She does not need to say, "I believe you."

She can simply act differently.

## Vale enters the scene

Vale should appear when the players have enough leverage that a lower-ranking officer is no longer sufficient.

He may arrive physically or by secure comm.

He offers controlled bargains.

Examples:
- surrender the evidence, miners are released;
- surrender one wanted person, civilians move;
- stop interfering with life-safety, he allows medical evacuation;
- give up a target shuttle, he lets the party remain in Kaguya rather than detaining them.

His promises may be real in the narrow wording.

That makes him more useful than a liar.

# 13 — THE SHOOTER INVESTIGATION

The breach trooper can be identified through multiple paths.

Never put the truth behind one mandatory roll.

## Path A — Camera footage

Best direct visual evidence.

Weakness:
- angle may not show readable identity;
- SXC can call the footage incomplete.

## Path B — Weapon / ammunition telemetry

A military rifle discharge log, weapon diagnostic, smart-ammo record, or squad equipment audit can correlate the shot.

This is strong technical evidence.

## Path C — Bulkhead sequencing

Shows emergency isolation began before the reported breach timeline.

This does not identify the shooter alone.

It proves preparation.

## Path D — Tactical orders

The SXC occupation network may contain pre-event sector control instructions.

This proves the military expected or planned a crisis posture.

## Path E — Witnesses

The cleaner, miners, civilians, and any PC who maintained line of sight can establish behavior.

Witnesses are fragile evidence because people can be intimidated, separated, or discredited.

## Path F — The trooper

Capturing or confronting the trooper can reveal:
- equipment;
- squad assignment;
- recent comm bursts;
- altered orders;
- fear.

Do not require a confession.

People rarely confess cleanly in the middle of a military operation.

# 14 — NETWORK RUN: SILENT OCCUPATION

- clear a checkpoint;
- alter a challenge code;
- move a patrol;
- suppress a biometric alert;
- obtain signed pre-event orders;
- expose the occupation command trail.

Run the network and checkpoint in the same initiative if action becomes tight.

## Physical teamwork

A non-runner can:
- steal a current challenge code;
- create a distraction;
- cut power to a field relay;
- capture an officer token;
- escort civilians once a lane is cleared.

## What the network can do

A spoofed command can delay trained soldiers.

A valid signed command can redirect them.

One unsigned message should not permanently control a disciplined military force.

## The dangerous route

Local Command Authority is protected by dangerous ICE.

Telegraph that danger.

There should always be another path:
- officer token;
- signed-order forgery;
- physical console;
- partial objective.

The session should create hard choices, not gotchas.

# 15 — SCENE 4: SERVICE BYPASS

**Foundry scene:** `Service Bypass — Industrial Transit`

The public terminal is now too controlled for easy movement.

The service complex feels completely different.

It is louder.

The ceiling is lower.

There are no vacation advertisements.

There are pipe galleries, cargo machinery, maintenance labels, scuffed access doors, floor channels, and places where a person can understand that Kaguya is a machine pretending to be a destination.

## Read-aloud

> The service door closes behind you and the terminal disappears.
>
> No advertisements.
>
> No polished wall panels.
>
> No music.
>
> Just the mechanical pulse of Kaguya doing the work that makes the public side look effortless.
>
> Pipes run overhead in labeled bundles. Cargo tracks cut across the floor. A service cart sits abandoned with its warning light still turning.
>
> Somewhere ahead, something heavy is moving.
>
> Somewhere behind, a checkpoint alarm changes tone.

## What is happening here even without the PCs

- maintenance workers are trying to understand which systems they are still allowed to touch;
- SXC search teams are entering from both ends;
- life-safety has priority over normal work orders;
- one cargo lane is still active because landing operations cannot simply stop;
- a maintenance drone continues its route because nobody told it this is a crisis.

This area is how the party learns that **equipment can still move where people cannot**.

## Routes through the service complex

### Route 1 — Cargo lane

Fastest.

Most visible.

Useful if the party can look like authorized ground crew or hide inside/around legitimate service traffic.

### Route 2 — Pipe gallery

Slower.

Good cover.

Requires physical movement and may intersect the life-safety network.

### Route 3 — Maintenance crawl

Very concealed.

Bad for Mac's exosuit unless an alternate equipment access exists.

Use this to force a real decision rather than handwave the size issue.

### Route 4 — Utility vehicle

Maeko's preferred option.

Requires:
- access to a vehicle;
- a plausible route;
- enough authorization or confusion to survive the first checkpoint.

## Search team

A sweep team enters from one side while another report suggests a second team is approaching from the opposite side.

Their goal is capture and compartment control.

They do not spray gunfire through pressure infrastructure unless the situation has already catastrophically escalated.

## If the players hide

Let the search pass close.

Give them overheard information:
- current challenge code;
- target sector;
- mention of the landing-ring hold;
- a reference to "the shooter" being reassigned;
- a command to preserve or seize a specific evidence terminal.

Hiding should create information, not just consume time.

# 16 — LIFE-SAFETY CROSSOVER: BREATHING ROOM

If the party enters the pressure/life-safety network, use the existing **Breathing Room** run.

This is different from hacking an enemy.

The system is trying to prevent people from dying.

The engineering defender **Nadiya Chen** can become a scene partner.

## Nadiya's attitude

If the runners transmit credible casualty data and make safe requests:
- she delays trace pressure;
- she becomes more willing to allow stabilization actions.

If they:
- silence alarms;
- override safety interlocks;
- falsify lethal sensor data;
- open unsafe bulkheads;

she opposes them more aggressively.

She will not knowingly close a bulkhead on civilians.

## The moral choice

The party may discover that the safest environmental action also closes their easiest escape path.

Do not hide that.

Make them choose:
- keep the route;
- save the compartment;
- find another way.

That is better than making "success" automatically solve both.

# 17 — SCENE 5: PRIVATE TERMINAL CORRIDOR

**Foundry scene:** `Private Terminal Corridor — Security Checkpoint`

This is the architectural point that matters:

The public loop does not give passengers a glass view directly into the landing field.

A private terminal connection is a controlled transition.

The corridor narrows.

The décor becomes more corporate or carrier-specific.

Then there is a real security checkpoint before access to the starport side.

## What is at the checkpoint

- two to four SXC personnel depending on Lockdown;
- a terminal/carrier staffer or abandoned desk;
- biometric / credential scan;
- baggage or service access;
- a door that can be opened physically only if the local system agrees or someone defeats it;
- a supervisor console;
- a service-side access panel.

## Approaches

### Social cover

Sully shines here.

Possible covers:
- emergency maintenance;
- ground crew responding to a pressure fault;
- medical transfer;
- authorized service technicians;
- reassigned passengers with a private-terminal exception.

The more people in the group, the harder a thin cover becomes.

### Stolen/temporary credential

Works once if the network credential is valid.

Do not make the first soldier magically know it is fake.

Let reconciliation or later audits create consequences.

### Service access

Riker/Fletcher can identify a bypass around the public-facing scanner.

### Network route

Public Terminal operations can open the private spur door.

Landing Ring operations can control the passenger bridge side.

The party can attack the problem from either side if they can reach the correct system.

### Force

Possible.

The checkpoint is a bad place for an uncontrolled firefight:
- narrow corridor;
- pressure-rated doors;
- civilians;
- reinforcements;
- cameras.

If the PCs choose violence, make the tactical problem real rather than forbidding it.

## Sol at the checkpoint

If Sol has begun doubting the official story, this is a good place for her to create the narrowest possible opening without openly mutinying.

She can tell a subordinate:

> "Their credentials are under engineering exception. Let them through and flag it for review."

Then look directly at the party.

That means: **do not make me regret this.**

# 20 — WHO MOVES ON THE LANDING FIELD

3. detain;
4. call support.

- force the party to use cover;

# 23 — SHUTTLE INFILTRATION APPROACHES

# 24 — NETWORK RUN: REDLINE DEPARTURE

This is the full shuttle-escape run.

It uses two different systems:

1. **Kaguya Inner Landing Ring Operations**
2. **Heinlein Authority Kaguya Flight Control**

The important point is that one system cannot do everything.

## Legal departure needs four pieces

1. local pad assignment and released clamps;
2. craft identity / transponder token;
3. departure slot;
4. flight corridor + signed final clearance.

The Landing Ring can solve the local physical side.

Flight Control owns the final authority.

## Phase 1 — Make the craft physically ready

Useful commands:
- release docking clamps;
- disconnect umbilicals safely;
- extend/retract passenger bridge;
- dispatch a tug;
- clear taxi lane;
- create a local movement slot.

Every command should change the map.

A tug is now moving.

A bridge retracts.

A clamp indicator goes green.

Ground crew receive a message.

## Physical jobs while the runner works

**Pilot:** holds the craft ready and recognizes unsafe traffic instructions.

**Mechanic / Riker:** confirms clamps, power, and umbilicals; catches false sensor states.

**Face / Sully:** obtains a flight-desk credential or sells the emergency cover.

**Infiltrator / Fletcher:** reaches the pad pedestal, service trench, or gate cabinet.

**Maeko:** controls ground movement and knows when a lane is actually clear.

**Mac:** protects passengers and equipment, moves physical obstructions, becomes the person everyone notices if stealth fails.

## Phase 2 — Transfer to Flight Control

Possible entry:
- docking telemetry;
- private flight desk;
- pressure-gate actuator cabinet;
- privileged flight console.

The easiest digital route may require the hardest physical infiltration.

That is intentional.

## Phase 3 — Build clearance

### Direct strategy

Attack the final clearance node.

Fast.

Very dangerous.

### Component strategy

Collect:
- temporary transponder token;
- departure slot;
- outbound corridor;

then assemble a valid package through flight-desk credentials or a difficult hacking action.

Slower.

More moving parts.

Safer than attacking the most dangerous ICE directly.

Tell the players enough that this is a choice, not a hidden "correct answer."

# 25 — LANDING RING NETWORK IN THE WORLD

The Landing Ring network controls things the players can see.

## Pad Assignment / Clamps

If successful:
- clamp status visibly changes;
- ground crew may receive an automatic work order;
- a suspicious early release can create a hazard.

## Tug / Cargo Routing

This is one of the best "living world" tools.

A tug can:
- create moving cover;
- block a patrol vehicle;
- bring the wrong cargo to the right place;
- move an unattended shuttle component;
- make security look in another direction.

## Fuel / Power Umbilicals

Treat this with respect.

A careless command around active transfer is not just "more alert."

It can become a real fire emergency.

## Passenger Bridge

A bridge is both access and a pressure boundary.

It can move people safely.

It can also strand them.

## Emergency Departure Slot

This is only local.

It is not the same as final flight clearance.

Let players discover the institutional layering by interacting with it.

# 26 — FLIGHT CONTROL IN THE WORLD

Flight Control is security, but it is also aviation safety.

That makes it more interesting than a pure enemy system.

## If the runner creates an unsafe vector

Flight-security personnel may momentarily cooperate to prevent a collision.

Afterward they resume opposition.

That does not make them allies.

It means everyone prefers not to die.

## Gate interlocks

A giant pressure gate cannot be treated like a bedroom door.

Its safe cycle matters.

A local actuator can operate the gate when safe.

It cannot create legal flight clearance.

## Final clearance

The fastest route is also the most dangerous network route.

The safer route is bureaucratic:
- slot;
- identity;
- corridor;
- valid packaging.

The player should be able to win by understanding the system rather than always smashing through the strongest ICE.

# 27 — TRAFFIC HAZARD CLOCK

Track separately from the terminal decompression.

| Hazard | State |
|---|---|
| 0 | Normal separation |
| 1 | One delayed/conflicting craft |
| 2 | Near-miss risk; piloting in the affected zone becomes harder |
| 3 | Collision/decompression threat in three rounds |
| 4 | Disaster unless traffic is held, redirected, or physically aborted |

Raise it for:
- reckless departure commands;
- bypassing safe gate sequencing;
- moving tugs/craft blindly;
- creating contradictory flight vectors.

Lower it for:
- correct piloting;
- safe holds;
- cooperation with operators;
- sacrificing speed to fix the traffic problem.

This clock exists so "hack the traffic system" feels like controlling real vehicles, not moving icons.

# 30 — FAILURE SHOULD CHANGE THE JOB

Avoid dead ends.

- steal the local buffer physically;
- rely on witnesses + telemetry;
- move on without clean evidence.

## If they cannot stop the decompression

- isolate the zone;
- lose the route but save the adjacent compartments.

- bluff as emergency crew;

# 32 — EVIDENCE PACKAGE

Treat evidence as a package the party can build.

## Tier 1 — Suspicion

Any one:
- camera fragment;
- witness;
- early bulkhead command;
- unusual weapon discharge;
- pre-staged troop deployment.

Enough to raise questions.

Not enough to dominate the public story.

## Tier 2 — Corroborated event

Two independent sources:
- video + telemetry;
- telemetry + witness;
- bulkhead log + tactical order;
- camera + audit mirror.

Strong enough to influence Sol or a later investigator.

## Tier 3 — Authenticated package

Three or more sources with metadata preserved.

Example:
- verified camera file;
- weapon discharge log tied to the unit;
- bulkhead sequence showing pre-event command;
- signed tactical order;
- civilian/miner witness.

This is the version that survives "fabrication" accusations.

## Important

Evidence is leverage.

It is not automatic justice.

Who receives it, when, and whether they can act on it remain campaign questions.

# 33 — VALE'S BARGAIN SCENE

Use only when the party has something Vale wants or when he believes they can still be contained without a firefight.

## Setup

Vale appears on a secure wall display, checkpoint terminal, or physically with a protected command element.

He does not shout.

He names the situation as he wants it understood.

> "You are inside a restricted emergency zone. You have interfered with stabilization procedures and accessed controlled systems. I am prepared to separate those facts from whatever else happened here, if you stop escalating the situation."

## What he can realistically offer

- safe movement for injured civilians;
- release of some miners;
- a temporary delay in search;
- local surrender terms;
- treatment as detainees rather than hostile combatants;
- permission to move to a safe compartment.

## What he will not casually offer

- unrestricted access to the landing field;
- permanent immunity;
- admission that SXC caused the breach;
- control of military systems.

## If shown authenticated evidence

His reaction depends on the GM truth selected earlier.

If he ordered it:
- he focuses on chain of custody and legality;
- he wants the evidence contained.

If he approved a vague plan:
- he becomes colder;
- he needs to know who executed it this way.

If he did not know:
- he may order the shooter detained while still insisting the party surrender.

None of these automatically makes him "good."

# 36 — BOARDING THE SHUTTLE

- Is the passenger bridge usable?

# 37 — LAUNCH

A launch is not one button.

## Final checks

The craft needs:
- physical readiness;
- local movement authority or willingness to violate it;
- a safe route to the gate;
- gate cycle;
- corridor assignment or a plan to leave illegally.

## Read-aloud: clean launch

> The last clamp indicator changes from amber to green.
>
> Nothing dramatic happens.
>
> That is how you know the system believes you belong here.
>
> The shuttle eases away from the pad and joins the marked movement lane.
>
> Ground lights slide beneath the hull.
>
> Far across the landing field, a pressure gate begins to cycle.
>
> For the first time since the maintenance hatch opened, every obstacle ahead of you is moving in the same direction you are.

## Read-aloud: hot launch

> The shuttle starts moving before the field agrees that it should.
>
> Warning lights snap across the pad edge.
>
> A tug brakes hard and slews sideways.
>
> Someone on ground control says "Hold position" once, then again, louder.
>
> Ahead, the pressure gate is not fully committed to opening.
>
> Behind you, SXC vehicles are entering the pad sector.
>
> The question is no longer whether anyone noticed.
>
> The question is whether the machinery finishes what you told it to do before the people trying to stop you reach it.

Capture is a valid state, not a failed campaign.

- interrogation;
- prisoner transfer;
- miner breakout;

- Sol or another insider preserving a file;
- outside runner using the back door;
- Vale offering a second bargain.

The campaign should continue from consequences.

# 39 — END STATES

Do not collapse everything into "escaped / didn't escape."

Record the state.

## A — Clean shuttle departure

Record:
- who escaped;
- evidence quality;
- whether clearance appears legitimate;
- which network traces remain;
- whether SXC knows the craft identity.

## B — Hot shuttle departure

Add:
- Traffic Hazard;
- damage;
- active pursuit;
- whether the gate cycle was safe;
- which SXC unit saw the launch.

## C — Private-terminal survival

The party reaches a controlled terminal but does not leave Kaguya.

Good continuation for:
- social infiltration;
- hostage tension;
- later legitimate departure attempt.

## D — Mass evacuation

Miners/civilians leave with the party.

This changes:
- capacity;
- logistics;
- public narrative;
- who owes whom.

## E — Evidence victory, no escape

The party remains trapped but has preserved the truth.

This creates a different next session rather than a lesser one.

## F — Capture

Playable if meaningful state survives.

## G — Terminal stabilized, shuttle lost

The party saves lives but misses the departure opportunity.

The world should remember the rescue.

# 44 — GM QUICK FLOW

When uncertain what happens next, use this.

1. **Ask what the PCs are trying to accomplish.**
2. **Advance the relevant clock if meaningful time passed.**
3. **Move one NPC or faction according to its goal.**
4. **Show one visible consequence of the world moving.**
5. **Offer at least two real approaches.**
6. **Let success change the situation.**
7. **Let failure change the objective rather than erase play.**

Examples:

The players debate whether to chase the shooter.

Advance Pressure Loss.

Fletcher starts moving without them.

A bulkhead begins closing.

Now they can:
- follow Fletcher;
- save civilians before the door closes;
- let him go and hack the cameras.

The world created the choice.

# 46 — FINAL GM PRINCIPLE

Do not ask, "What encounter comes next?"

Ask:

**"Given what the players just did, what is everyone else doing now?"**

That is the living-world version of this session.

The breach is not a cutscene.

The soldiers are not room guards.

The miners are not followers waiting for commands.

The civilians are not scenery.

The network is not separate from the building.

The shuttle is not waiting motionless for the party to arrive.

Kaguya keeps operating while the PCs are inside it.

That is what makes the session feel alive.
