Starport Kaguya - Immersive Description

The Approach

The tube-lev car slows as it approaches the final station, the air pressure changing subtly as you descend into the main concourse. The automated voice announces your arrival in crisp, neutral American: "Starport Kaguya. Please gather all personal belongings. Mind the gap between the car and the platform."

The doors hiss open, and the first thing that hits you is the sound—a symphony of a living spaceport. The low rumble of cargo haulers being loaded in distant bays. The melodic chime of departure announcements echoing across the cavernous halls. The endless murmur of thousands of voices, layered over each other in dozens of languages. Somewhere, a child is laughing. Somewhere else, someone is arguing with a ticketing AI.

And beneath it all, the constant, almost imperceptible hum of life support systems—the breath of the Moon itself.

---

The Main Concourse

The main concourse of Starport Kaguya is a vast, cathedral-like space carved into the Lunar regolith. The ceiling arches forty meters overhead, a lattice of transplas panels and steel support beams that filter the harsh sunlight from the surface above into a soft, diffuse glow. At night, the panels polarize to reveal the stars beyond—a reminder that you are, in fact, on the Moon.

The floor is polished Lunar concrete, worn smooth by the feet of billions of travelers over the decades. It gleams under the ambient light, reflecting the endless stream of holographic advertisements that float overhead like digital ghosts.

"Welcome to Heinlein! Experience the wonders of low-G sports at the Angel Arena!"

"StarScape Shopping—duty-free luxury from across the solar system!"

"Eliza's Toolbox—all your companion needs, discreetly and professionally."

A Jinteki ad cycles through images of serene clones working in perfect harmony, their serene smiles promising a future of effortless labor. A Haas-Bioroid hologram shows a sleek bioroid greeting a smiling family—"The perfect addition to any home."

The Crowds

The concourse is packed.

Families with wide-eyed children crane their necks at the enormous screens displaying Earth from orbit. Business travelers in crisp smart-suits stride purposefully past, their PADs glowing with schedules and contracts. A group of Wylders with feline eyes and luminous tattoos laugh raucously near a food kiosk, their modified features drawing stares and whispers.

A Melange Mining recruiter stands near a booth, her voice amplified by a small drone hovering at her shoulder:

"Looking for work? The Melange Mining Corporation offers competitive contracts, housing, and a future among the stars! Henry clones preferred, but all qualified applicants welcome!"

Nearby, a cluster of tourists wearing matching "I <3 Luna" smartslicks struggle with their luggage, their expressions a mixture of awe and mild panic. Their guide, a bored-looking young woman with a monocam over one eye, gestures vaguely toward the tube-lev exits.

"And to your left, you'll see the entrance to the tube-lev to Lunacent—the beating heart of Heinlein! Please stay with the group..."

The Bazaar

Past the main ticketing area, the concourse opens into the StarScape Bazaar—a sprawling marketplace where anything can be bought, if you have enough credits.

Stalls line the walls, their vendors hawking everything from Lunar-made jewelry to low-gravity exercise equipment. The air smells of roasted Luna-beans, synthetic cinnamon, and the faint ozone tang of electronics.

An old woman with cybernetic eyes sits behind a cluttered table, selling antique Earth artifacts. She catches your eye and smiles, revealing teeth that glow faintly blue.

"Genuine artifacts from Old Earth! This one's a newspaper from before the Blackout—still got the ink on it! Fifty creds, and it's yours."

A few meters away, a young runner in a worn jacket leans against a wall, his eyes scanning the crowd. He's not looking at anyone in particular—he's watching the camdrones hovering overhead, tracking their patterns. When he notices you watching, he flashes a crooked smile and melts back into the crowd.

---

Security Checkpoint

The spaceport's security checkpoint is an imposing structure of plascrete and steel, flanked by Yellow Jackets—the Space Elevator Authority's security force. Their high-visibility yellow jumpsuits make them unmistakable, and their posture is professional, alert.

The checkpoint consists of several lanes leading to full-body backscatter scanners, their arches humming with low-energy radiation. A bored-looking officer gestures you forward.

"ID, please. PAD, credaccount, or implant. Keep your hands visible. Any weapons, cybernetic enhancements, or illegal modifications must be declared."

Behind him, a Hachi-Inu clone—a four-legged Jinteki security unit—stands motionless, its head swiveling slowly as it scans the crowd. Its handler, a tired-looking NAPD officer, pats its head absently.

A woman ahead of you argues with a security officer.

"I told you, it's a medical implant! It helps me breathe in low-G—"

"Ma'am, you'll need to provide documentation. Please step aside."

She curses under her breath and complies.

---

Departure Gates

Once past security, the departure area is quieter, more serene. The ceilings rise even higher here, and the transplas panels are fully transparent, offering an unobstructed view of Earth hanging in the black sky above.

It's breathtaking—a blue marble suspended in the void, impossibly beautiful. Some travelers stop to stare, their departure announcements forgotten. Others, veterans of the journey, barely glance up.

The seats are arranged in clusters, many occupied by travelers waiting for their beanpod calls. A bioroid in a crisp uniform stands by the gate, its voice smooth and inhumanly calm:

"Now boarding for Beanpod 7, destination Midway Station. Please have your tickets and IDs ready. Passengers with children or mobility needs may board first."

· A child crying somewhere nearby, tired and overwhelmed

The People

The Harried Businessman

A man in an expensive, slightly rumpled suit paces near the gate, his PAD pressed to his ear. His voice is strained, apologetic.

"—I understand, Director, but the beanpod schedule was delayed. Yes, I'll be there for the meeting. No, I can't make it go faster. I don't control the Beanstalk, I'm a middle manager—"

He hangs up with a sigh and rubs his temples.

The Young Couple

A young woman and man, both in their early twenties, stand near the window, arms around each other. She's crying, but she's smiling.

"I can't believe we're really doing this. We're going to Mars."

"I know. My hands are shaking. Did you pack the extra oxygen filters?"

"Yes, you asked me three times already."

The Clone

A Henry clone sits alone near the wall, her muscular frame making the plastic seat look small. She wears a Melange Mining jumpsuit, and her eyes are fixed on the floor. She's young, maybe eighteen standard years. Her hands are scarred from work.

A human man approaches her, his tone casual.

"Hey, you with Melange?"

She looks up, her expression guarded.

"...Yes. I'm on my way to the mines."

"Rough life, huh?"

She doesn't answer.

The Bioroid

A sleek, elegant bioroid stands near the gate, its synthetic skin perfect, its eyes a disconcerting shade of silver. It wears a Haas-Bioroid uniform and carries a small briefcase.

A child tugs at its sleeve.

"Are you a robot?"

The bioroid looks down, its expression neutral.

"I am a bioroid, manufactured by Haas-Bioroid. I am designed for administrative support and executive assistance. I am not a robot."

"Can you do tricks?"

The bioroid tilts its head.

"That is not within my programming. However, I can calculate the fastest route to any destination within this spaceport with 99.7% accuracy."

The Old Veteran

An old man sits alone, his face weathered, his limbs cybernetic. He wears a faded SXC jacket with patches from the War. He stares at Earth, his eyes distant.

A young woman sits down next to him.

"Beautiful view, isn't it?"

He doesn't look at her.

"Yeah. It is."

"You were in the War?"

He's silent for a moment.

"...Yeah. We came back from Mars, thinking it was over. But the War never really ends, does it?"

The Announcement

A voice echoes through the departure area:

"Attention, passengers on Beanpod 7. Boarding is now complete. Please ensure you are seated and stowed. The pod will depart in approximately five minutes."

The hum of the beanpod engines intensifies, a deep vibration that you can feel in your chest.

A man beside you, a seasoned traveler, checks his PAD.

"Five minutes, they said. Last time, it was ten."

He shrugs.

"But that's the Beanstalk for you. Always late, always worth the wait."

Final Impression

Starport Kaguya is not just a port—it's a threshold. A place where Earth and the Moon meet, where the past and the future collide, where every face has a story. It's a living, breathing place, full of noise and motion and life. It's the gateway to the stars, and it feels like it.

As you wait for your own departure, you can't help but feel the weight of it all—the history, the hope, the desperation. The Moon is not just a destination. It's a new beginning.

And the Beanstalk is waiting.
