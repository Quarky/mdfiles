He-3 Mining Facility - Immersive Description

The Approach

The transport crawler rumbles across the lunar surface, its treads kicking up plumes of fine grey regolith that hang in the airless void like frozen smoke. Through the transplas viewport, you see it rising on the horizon: the Melange Mining Facility, a sprawling complex of domes, processing plants, and skeletal loading gantries that claw at the sky like the fingers of a buried giant.

The facility is a study in contrasts. The central dome glows with warm artificial light, a bubble of life in the desolation. Around it, the processing plants are brutalist structures of plascrete and vanasteel, their surfaces pitted by micrometeorite impacts and stained by years of chemical runoff. Conveyor belts crawl like mechanical centipedes, carrying crushed regolith from the harvesters to the refineries. Pipes snake across the surface, carrying gases and liquids between buildings, their surfaces gleaming with frost in the perpetual cold.

A massive He-3 canister sits on a loading platform, its surface marked with warning symbols and the Melange Mining logo—a stylized "M" inside a circle. A crane arm hovers above it, motionless, waiting for the next shipment.

As you approach, the facility's landing pad comes into view—a flat, circular platform of plascrete surrounded by guide lights that blink in sequence, marking the approach path. A small hopper is parked to one side, its rotors still spinning down. A figure in an environsuit is walking toward the airlock, their movements slow and deliberate in the low gravity.

The airlock door cycles open with a hiss. You step inside, and the weight of the Moon lifts from your shoulders.

The Arrival Bay

The airlock opens into a receiving bay, a utilitarian space of plascrete walls and exposed conduits. The floor is scuffed and stained, marked by countless bootprints and the drag marks of heavy equipment. The air smells of recycled oxygen, ozone, and something faintly metallic—the scent of the Moon itself.

A Melange Mining logo is painted on the far wall, faded and chipped. Below it, a simple message in large, bold letters:

"SAFETY FIRST. REPORT ALL HAZARDS."

A security checkpoint blocks the path forward: a desk with a bored-looking guard, a weapons scanner, and a Hachi-Inu clone sitting motionless at the guard's feet. The guard looks up as you approach, his eyes scanning your ID with practiced disinterest.

"ID and purpose of visit. If you're here for the mines, sign the waiver. If you're here for the corporate offices, take the lift to Level 4. If you're here to cause trouble—" He pats the Hachi-Inu's head. "—Maverick here will make sure you don't go far."

Behind him, a bulletin board is covered with notices: safety reminders, shift schedules, a wanted poster for a missing miner, and a faded advertisement for a bar in Lunacent called "The Dusty Helmet."

The Main Corridor

Beyond security, the facility opens into a main corridor that runs the length of the dome. It's wide enough for two cargo haulers to pass side by side, and the ceiling arches high overhead, supported by curved vanasteel beams.

Sound fills the corridor:

· The distant rumble of heavy machinery from the processing plant
· The clank and hiss of hydraulic systems cycling
· The murmur of voices—workers heading to and from their shifts, their conversations echoing in the cavernous space
· The steady thump of booted feet on plascrete
· The drone of an automated announcement cycling in a loop

The walls are lined with pipes and conduits, their surfaces painted in color-coded bands—blue for water, yellow for gas, red for hazardous materials. Some have caution tape wrapped around them, and a few have handwritten notes taped to them:

"DO NOT TOUCH. LEAK REPAIR PENDING."

Overhead, a camdrone hovers silently, its red light blinking as it observes the flow of traffic.

The Workers

The Foreman

A burly man in a stained jumpsuit stands near a terminal, shouting into his PAD. His voice is hoarse, strained.

"—I don't care what the manifest says, we're short fifteen canisters! Someone's skimming, and I want to know who! No, don't file a report yet—I'll handle it myself!"

He hangs up and runs a hand over his face, exhausted. He doesn't notice you watching.

The Henry Clone

A Henry clone trudges past you, her muscular frame moving with the easy grace of someone who has spent her life in low gravity. Her jumpsuit is filthy with regolith dust, and her respirator mask is tucked under her arm. She looks exhausted, her eyes hollow.

She pauses to drink from a water fountain, then notices you staring. She offers a tired smile.

"First time in a mine? You get used to the dust. Eventually."

She walks on without waiting for a reply.

The Technician

A woman in a clean, well-pressed uniform leans against the wall, her PAD glowing in her hand. She's clearly not a miner—her skin is too pale, her hands too smooth. She's arguing with someone on the other end of the line.

"I told you, we can't process any more shipments this week! The refinery's at capacity, and the clones are burning out faster than we can replace them! No, I don't care that the quota's behind—"

She notices you listening and lowers her voice, turning away.

The Old Miner

An old man sits on a crate near the wall, his hands gnarled and covered in scars. He wears a faded jumpsuit with a patch that reads "25 YEARS—MELANGE MINING." He watches the flow of workers with a distant, knowing expression.

He catches your eye and grins, revealing a gap-toothed smile.

"First day? You got that look. The one that says 'I don't know what I've gotten myself into.'"

He chuckles.

"Don't worry, kid. You'll learn. Or you'll die. Either way, the mines don't care."

The Processing Plant

The corridor opens into the processing plant, a vast, cavernous space filled with the roar of machinery. Conveyor belts carry crushed regolith from the harvesters to massive grinding machines, their teeth chewing through rock with a sound like thunder. The air is thick with dust, and the ground trembles under your feet.

Workers in respirator masks move through the chaos, their movements precise and practiced. Henry clones, for the most part, their muscular frames silhouetted against the glow of the furnaces. They don't speak to each other; the noise is too loud.

A foreman stands on a raised platform, his arms crossed, watching the operation. He's a big man, and he's shouting—not at anyone in particular, just making himself heard.

"KEEP IT MOVING! WE'RE BEHIND SCHEDULE! IF THAT CONVEYOR JAMS ONE MORE TIME, I'M REPLACING THE LOT OF YOU WITH BIOROIDS!"

The clones don't react.

A massive grinding machine roars to life nearby, and a cloud of dust billows out, making you cough.

The Clone Barracks

Past the processing plant, the corridor narrows into a smaller hallway lined with doors. A sign reads:

"CLONE BARRACKS — AUTHORIZED PERSONNEL ONLY"

The doors are plain, unadorned, with small viewing ports at eye level. Through one, you can see rows of narrow bunks, each with a thin mattress and a small locker. A Henry clone sits on one of the bunks, staring at the wall with vacant eyes.

Another door opens, and a clone steps out, her jumpsuit fresh and clean. She looks at you, then quickly averts her gaze and hurries down the corridor.

A supervisor in a clean jumpsuit leans against the wall, making notes on her PAD.

"These clones... they don't complain, you know? They just do the work. But you can see it in their eyes—they're tired. Broken. Doesn't matter. Melange gets what they paid for."

The Refinery Control Room

A door marked "REFINERY CONTROL — RESTRICTED ACCESS" leads into a small room filled with terminals and holographic displays. A technician sits in the center, monitoring the flow of data.

The room is surprisingly quiet, insulated from the chaos outside. The technician, a thin man with tired eyes, notices you and gestures vaguely at the screens.

"This is where it all happens. The regolith comes in from the harvesters, gets crushed, sorted, and refined. The He-3 gets separated out and stored in canisters. The rest gets recycled or dumped back on the surface."

He points to one of the screens, which shows a diagram of the refinery.

"That's the heart of the operation. The separator. It's an old design, but it works. Mostly."

He frowns.

"We've been having issues with it lately. Pressure drops, temperature fluctuations. Maintenance says it's just wear and tear, but—" He lowers his voice. "—I think someone's been tampering with it. Sabotage, maybe. The clones don't talk, but they've been acting strange lately."

The Loading Dock

The final stop is the loading dock, where the He-3 canisters are stored before shipment. It's a vast, open space, with a high ceiling that opens onto the lunar surface through a massive airlock.

The canisters are stacked in neat rows, each one labeled with a serial number and the Melange Mining logo. A few are being loaded onto a transport crawler by a team of Henry clones, their movements synchronized and efficient.

A supervisor with a clipboard stands nearby, checking off items on a list.

"That's the last of the shipment. Good. We're ahead of schedule for once."

He looks up at you.

"Visiting? You don't look like corporate. Don't touch anything. The canisters are pressurized—if one ruptures, you'll freeze to death in seconds."

A clone walks past you, carrying a heavy canister on her shoulder. She doesn't meet your eyes.

Final Impression

The Melange Mining Facility is not a place of dreams. It's a place of work, of survival, of grinding exhaustion. The clones move through it like ghosts, their faces blank, their bodies pushed to the breaking point. The humans in charge are tired, stressed, and increasingly paranoid.

And somewhere, in the shadows, you can feel the weight of it all—the desperation, the exploitation, the simmering resentment that might one day erupt.

This is the machine that powers the solar system. It's efficient. It's relentless. And it doesn't care if you get in its way.
