Kaguya at 05:30 is a specific kind of place.

The overnight cleaning crew — twelve Haas-Bioroids in orange coveralls — are finishing their sweep of the StarScape main concourse. They move in a loose formation that has no name but is clearly a pattern, each one responsible for a strip of floor, their paths never crossing. One of them stops at a coffee cup someone left on a moving walkway handrail at 02:00 and has not been moved since. It picks it up. It holds it for a moment in a way that is not quite the way a tool gets held — more the way a person holds something when they are deciding what to feel about it. Then it deposits the cup in the nearest recycler and continues its strip.

In the Dome Alpha corporate terminal: a Jinteki representative in grey sits with her PAD, reviewing something she does not want reviewed by the person sitting two seats away. The person two seats away is a Weyland logistics manager who is also reviewing something on his PAD that he does not want reviewed. They have not spoken. They will not speak. They are waiting for a shuttle that is not delayed yet.

In the StarScape food court, open at this hour for the overnight staff and early departures: a cook — Cameroonian, late forties, has worked the Kaguya overnight shift for six years — is making a decision about whether to call his family before his shift ends. He decides yes. He steps into the walk-in refrigerator for the privacy of the ambient noise. He calls. Nobody answers. He leaves a message. He says he is fine. He says the Moon is still strange to him after six years, the way the horizon curves wrong on the surface, the way the light comes from a direction he still hasn't memorized. He says he'll call again tomorrow. He goes back to the grill.

He does not know that in about ninety minutes he is going to be in the middle of something that will be described as a terrorist attack in every official account. He has never been in the middle of anything that was described that way. He will spend years afterward knowing that what he saw was more complicated than that and having nobody who will listen.

Outside, on the surface: the crater is quiet. The stars are out in the way they are always out on the Moon — not twinkling, perfectly still, the hard unwavering light of objects in a vacuum. The harvest lights of the mag-lev rail network are dark tonight. They have been dark since Zane's people took the relay stations. Usually you can see them stretching out from the crater toward Heinlein, a faint dotted line across the grey surface.

Tonight there is nothing out there. A Globalsec surface patrol notices this at 05:22. He mentions it to his partner. His partner says the mag-lev maintenance schedule sometimes goes dark overnight. The first guard says he doesn't think that's what's happening. He is right. They keep walking their route because that is what the route says to do.

At 05:45 the signal jamming arrays go in. Four small devices in four maintenance panels. The Globalsec checkpoint comms go to static and the guards at those checkpoints look at their earpieces with the particular expression of someone who cannot decide if their equipment has failed or something worse has happened. It takes them approximately forty seconds to switch to their backup frequency. By then the main force is through.

───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────── OPERATION: FLOOD THE BUILDING Zane's Phase 1 tactical concept ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Zane is not a military commander. He has read about military commanders. He has been in three fights in his life that could be called organized violence. What he has that military commanders sometimes lack is a very clear picture of what he has and what he doesn't have.

What he has: 150 workers with concealed weapons, 35 mercenaries with real ones, the jamming arrays, the atmo override units, eight combat drones, and the facility knowledge of people who built and maintained this place.

What he doesn't have: discipline that holds under sustained military pressure, supply lines, medical evacuation, any ability to take and hold ground against an enemy with sealed suits and laser rifles once they arrive.

His plan accounts for both lists with brutal accuracy. The plan is: flood the building before anyone outside knows what's happening, hold the critical objectives long enough for Dante to finish the network runs, and then fall back through the maintenance network before the military can establish a clean compression zone.

The fall-back is the plan. It was always the plan. Zane knows they are not winning this fight today. He is trying to win what the fight is for.

PHASE 1 TACTICAL SEQUENCE:

05:45 — Jamming arrays active. 4-6 minute checkpoint blind. 05:46 — Main force moves through the outer ring in coordinated streams. Experienced fighters take three routes simultaneously: Route Alpha: StarScape Level 1 concourse → inner ring arterial Route Beta: Service corridor B-level → Dome Gamma lower access Route Gamma: Maintenance crawl network → Dome Delta service junction Workers taking the atrium occupation use the public concourse — they look like what they are. Workers arriving for a shift. Nobody stops them. Nobody has any reason to yet.

06:15 — All elements in position. No alert triggered. Globalsec has restored checkpoint comms but the main force is already past every checkpoint. The window was exact.

06:30 — Simultaneous action on three objectives: STARSCAPE ATRIUM (occupation) DOME GAMMA CARGO (lockdown) DOME DELTA TRANSIT CONTROL (network run begins)

The three objectives happen at 06:30:00, 06:30:45, and 06:31:20. The Globalsec surface coordinator receives his first alert at 06:33:14. He is in the atrium at the time. He is being politely asked to sit down.

───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────── TACTICAL ENCOUNTER 01 — THE DOME GAMMA SEIZURE 06:30, Dome Gamma cargo control room ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

THE SCENE: Dome Gamma's cargo control room is a 12-meter by 8-meter space with seven workstations, a floor-to-ceiling display showing the live cargo routing status, and at this hour two Melange logistics staff and one Globalsec guard on light duty — the overnight cargo shift is automated and the human presence is notional.

Kwame leads the breach team. Three mercenaries and two experienced fighters. The plan is: stun grenades through the access hatch, breach, secure, no casualties. The plan accounts for two Melange staff and one guard.

What the plan does not account for: the third Melange staff member who is in the back room making coffee and who comes through the door at exactly the wrong moment carrying a ceramic mug and a startled expression.

WHAT HAPPENS:

The stun grenades go in. The two Melange staff at workstations go down — disorient, no injury. The Globalsec guard draws his weapon and gets a synap pistol discharge from Kwame's secondary fighter before he can clear the holster. He drops the weapon. He is not hurt. He is very angry.

The third staff member with the coffee mug stops in the doorway. He is forty-two years old. His name is Torres. He has worked cargo logistics at Kaguya for eleven years. He is looking at Kwame with an expression that is cycling rapidly through several stages and has not settled on any of them.

KWAME: (quietly, not pointing anything at him) "Put the mug down. Sit down over there. Don't touch anything and you'll be home tomorrow."

Torres looks at the mug. He looks at Kwame. He looks at the mug again. He sets it very carefully on the nearest flat surface. He sits down where Kwame indicated. He keeps looking at the mug.

Kwame zip-ties the guard's hands behind his back — in front after the guard says he has a rotator cuff problem — and begins the manual lockdown of the cargo loading arms.

One of the mercenaries — the breach specialist — is already at the junction J-7 physical control panel. He knows what throwing it does because Zane briefed him. He throws it. The display on the wall changes color. A soft automated chime sounds.

The He-3 supply from Kaguya to the Beanstalk stops at 06:52.

Torres is still looking at his coffee mug. He says to nobody in particular: "That's eleven years of shift bonuses in that routing queue." Nobody responds. This is not because they don't hear him.

ENCOUNTER MECHANICS: If combat occurs (guard doesn't drop weapon on synap hit): Globalsec Guard: Br3 Ag3 In2 Cu2 Wi2 Pr2, Soak 5, WT 8 Armed with Cantor M2111 Pistol (Dam 6; Crit 3; Range [Med]) Will fight. ───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────── EMOTIONAL ENCOUNTER — ZANE AND THE DEFECTING PRISEC MEMBER Inner dome service corridor, Hour 1 ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Among the mercenaries Zane hired with the He-3 money: three are former prisec. Not Globalsec — a smaller firm, Argus Security Solutions, that ran contracts at three Melange outposts before Melange consolidated its security to Globalsec and terminated the contracts.

One of them — her name is Sera, Ghanaian heritage, forty-three — worked the exact security rotation at Outpost 14 that Dante ran his network against. She was not on shift the night of the Rusty Ring heist. She was reassigned two weeks before. When her contract was terminated she did not have money to get back to Earth. She took the job Zane offered because it was the job available.

But she is also here because of something else. In her last week at Outpost 14 she filed two incident reports about conditions in section F that were logged and then disappeared from the system. She knows they disappeared. She knows someone made them disappear. She has been carrying this for four months.

She finds Zane in the inner dome service corridor at Hour 1, when there is a fifteen-minute lull between the seizure and the military's arrival.

SERA: "I need to know something."

ZANE: (not looking away from the junction he's watching) "Ask."

SERA: "The Section F thing. The clone experimentation. Was that part of why we're here?"

ZANE: (a beat) "Yes."

SERA: "Because I filed reports. At Outpost 14. Two of them."

ZANE: (now looking at her) "I know."

SERA: "You know."

ZANE: "Yusuf found them. The originals, before they were deleted. They're in the evidence package Petra Vasquez has."

A very long pause.

SERA: "You could have told me that."

ZANE: "Would it have changed whether you took the job?"

SERA: (longer pause) "No." Then: "It would have changed how I felt about it."

ZANE: "I know. I should have told you."

He doesn't say anything else. Neither does she. She goes back to her position. She is a better fighter for having had this conversation. Zane is not sure why. He thinks it has something to do with the difference between someone doing a job and someone doing the thing their life has been pointing toward. He is not sure the distinction matters tactically. He is sure it matters.

───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────── VASORA'S OPERATIONS ORDER — FALLING STAR EXECUTION ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Vasora is on the ground. Metzger is at Armstrong Base on comms. They talk every four minutes. Not every minute — every four. Four minutes is the interval they agreed on because Metzger wants to give Vasora room to make decisions and Vasora wants enough oversight that her decisions are covered if they go wrong. This is the most functional aspect of their working relationship.

Vasora's operations order, as delivered to her element leaders in the forty minutes between landing and first contact:

"We are clearing a civilian facility. Our objective is containment of an armed seizure, not combat. Every person you encounter is potentially a civilian until you have direct evidence they are not. The evidence standard is: visible weapon, hostile action, or verbal threat. A person running is not evidence. A person in the wrong place is not evidence. A person who looks like they don't belong is not evidence — because we are in a place where half the population doesn't look like they belong according to someone's idea of who should be here.

The drone goes first. Every time. In every corridor. You do not move until the drone has completed its scan. I know this feels slow. It is slow. We are doing it anyway.

We do not fire laser weapons at structure if we can avoid it. We do not fire into groups where we cannot distinguish combatants from civilians. We call holds and we wait.

If you are in a situation where a hold will get someone killed — yours or theirs — you make the call and you document it and you live with it. That's the job.

Any questions?"

One of her element leaders says: "What if they use the atmo systems against us?"

VASORA: "Then our suits do what they were built to do and we have an advantage they didn't plan for. Don't break the suits."

───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────── MILITARY TACTICAL ENCOUNTER 01 — OVERWATCH AND SUPPRESSION Ring 2 outer corridor, Hour 2 ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

THE SCENE: A Ring 2 corridor, 3.5 meters wide, 140 meters long, connecting the hotel district to the inner ring arterial. This corridor is where Zane's fighters planned to hold the longest — it is the main approach route from the outer ring to the inner dome cluster and it is wide enough that the Loony fighters can use the hotel room doorways as firing positions with good lateral coverage.

What Zane planned for: Globalsec response, which would advance in a line and could be held by two bullpup carbines firing from cover with controlled bursts.

What arrives instead: Vasora's element with full military doctrine.

MILITARY TACTICAL SEQUENCE — OVERWATCH AND SUPPRESSION:

Step 1 — DRONE SCREEN: The EWS combat drone enters the corridor first. It moves to the 30-meter mark and hovers. It scans. It identifies: two Loony fighters at hotel doorways, approximately 90 meters down, weapons not yet raised. One fighter visible at the far end, approximately 130 meters, weapon at low ready. It transmits this to Vasora and to the EWS operators at Armstrong Base. It moves to the 50-meter mark and hovers again. It does not advance further — it has identified combatants at effective range. It holds and continues transmitting.

Step 2 — OVERWATCH POSITIONING: Two troopers peel left and right at the corridor entrance, finding positions in doorway recesses. They raise their NEXT L12 laser rifles and establish overwatch — covering the drone's position and the approaches behind it. They do not advance. Their job is: if the drone takes fire, they return fire on the source. If anyone tries to rush the entry while the element advances, they stop them. This takes eleven seconds. The Loony fighters are watching the drone and have not yet understood that two rifles are now covering them from behind it.

Step 3 — SUPPRESSION ADVANCE: The remaining four troopers advance in two pairs, moving on opposite sides of the corridor, using the drone's hover position as an anchor. As they move forward they identify new positions and leapfrog — one pair holds while the other moves, alternating, the classic bounding overwatch that was developed on Earth and works on the Moon because human corridors have the same geometry. They are fast. They are deliberate. They do not rush.

Step 4 — IDENTIFICATION AND CHALLENGE: At the 70-meter mark the lead pair challenges the Loony fighters verbally: "Drop your weapons. On the floor. Both of you. Now."

WHAT THE LOONY FIGHTERS DO: OPTION A — THEY HOLD THEIR POSITIONS: The overwatch troopers have them covered from behind the drone. The advancing troopers have them covered from the front. The fighter at 130 meters is in the cross-pattern. Three synap pistol discharges land before any of them can raise a bullpup carbine effectively. Nobody dies. Three fighters are detained.

OPTION B — THEY USE THE MAINTENANCE HATCH: Sera briefed them on this contingency. The hotel room at position 95 on the left has a maintenance hatch behind the bathroom panel that opens into a service crawl running parallel to the corridor. The military's drone has not scanned that room. The military's drone cannot scan through walls. If the fighters go through the hatch before the challenge — if they read the drone screen pattern correctly and move before the element is at the 50-meter mark — they are behind the military element before it realizes. This is the Facility Knowledge advantage in action. The military advances down an empty corridor. The fighters are behind them. Sera planned this. She knows these hotels. She ran security checks in them for two years.

GM NOTE: Whether Option B succeeds depends on whether the players — through Dante's network run or Kai's drone access — managed to identify the drone screen pattern early enough to brief Zane's fighters on the timing. If yes: Option B works cleanly. If no: Option A is the result. This is the payoff for the intelligence work in Phase 1.

───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────── MILITARY TACTICAL ENCOUNTER 02 — BREACH AND CLEAR Hotel district junction, Hour 2-3 ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

THE SCENE: The junction where four hotel-district corridors meet in a cross pattern. Four angles of approach. A fountain — operational, unlike the atrium one — at the center. Zane has two mercenaries and three experienced fighters holding this junction because it is the last defensible position before the inner dome cluster.

If this junction falls, the military has clean access to the inner ring.

MILITARY TACTICAL SEQUENCE — FOUR-POINT BREACH:

The military does not advance on a four-angle junction from one direction. Advancing from one direction on a four-angle junction means three of your four flanks are exposed. Vasora knows this.

Step 1 — SIMULTANEOUS APPROACH: Vasora splits her element into four sub-teams of two and positions each at one of the four corridor mouths approaching the junction. Each sub-team is out of sightline from the junction itself — they are positioned at the 90-meter mark on each corridor, around corners, invisible. This takes six minutes to coordinate via comms. During those six minutes the Loony fighters at the junction can hear movement in all four corridors and cannot determine where it is coming from or where it is going. This is deliberate. The ambiguity is a weapon.

Step 2 — EWS DRONE SATURATION: Four EWS drones — one per corridor — advance simultaneously into the junction from all four directions. Four drones. From four angles. At the same time. The fighters in the junction have nowhere to point that isn't pointing at something. The drones are not firing. They are watching. From four angles simultaneously. The effect on the fighters: reported later by a survivor as "like being inside a scan. Like being read."

Step 3 — CHALLENGE AND WAIT: Vasora, over the junction's PA system (which the military now has access to via EWS network control): "You are surrounded. Drop your weapons and lie face down. You will not be harmed." She means this. Her troopers know she means it. The fighters in the junction know she might mean it. They do not know how to tell for certain. This uncertainty is the gap Zane built into the fall-back plan.

Step 4 — THE FALL-BACK WINDOW: The fighters have one option that Vasora's four-point approach does not cover: down. The maintenance crawl access hatch in the junction floor — a hatch that appears on no tactical document the military has because it was added during a plumbing retrofit three years ago and the facility map was never updated — opens into a crawl that goes directly to the SC-7 corridor where Dante is. Zane told these specific fighters about this hatch because these are the fighters who need to reach Dante's position to escort him to the launch core.

They go through the hatch during the three-second gap between Vasora's challenge and the moment her troopers would begin advancing. They close the hatch behind them.

The four drone screens converge on an empty junction. Vasora's teams advance into a space with a still-running fountain and no one in it. One of the troopers says, quietly: "Where did they go?" The answer is: into the floor. The military spends eleven minutes finding the hatch.

───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────── MILITARY TACTICAL ENCOUNTER 03 — EWS NETWORK VERSUS DANTE SC-7 service corridor, Hour 2-3 ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

THE SCENE: This encounter is in the network, not in the corridor. But it has physical consequences that land in the corridor.

At minute 41 of Dante's run, the EWS goes live. The sensation is described above — the building changing timbre, the teeth knowing before the ears do.

Inside the network: the ICE that Dante has been navigating begins behaving differently. Not stronger — more alive. There is a quality of active attention that wasn't present before. The gaps he found and used were gaps because the system wasn't watching them. Now the system — the EWS operators at Armstrong Base — is watching everything simultaneously. Not just his run. Everything. Every packet, every handshake, every protocol conversation the network is having with itself. And where the network has conversations, they are closing them.

THIS IS WHAT EWS INTERFERENCE LOOKS LIKE TO A RUNNER:

The ICE strength doesn't change on paper. The behavior changes. ENIGMA, which was responding to probes in a predictable two-beat pattern that Dante learned in the first thirty seconds, is now responding in four beats, the fourth of which is new and arrives from an unexpected angle. It feels like talking to someone who learned the same lines you have but added new lines between yours.

DATAHOUND, which was cycling its patrol pattern every fourteen seconds, is now cycling every nine. Not randomly — deliberately. Someone on the other end adjusted the interval. Someone who knew that fourteen seconds was long enough for a runner with Dante's access speed to slip through cleanly. They know this because they have simulated it. Their simulations did not include Dante specifically but they included people like Dante.

RAIDER appears. This is described in the network document. In the physical world: the EWS drone comes through the maintenance hatch.

THE EWS OPERATOR: His name does not appear in any document Dante ever sees. He is twenty-nine years old. He is in a room at Armstrong Base that smells of recycled air and the specific ozone of server banks. He has been running EWS in simulation for three years. This is the first live deployment.

He is excellent at his job. He is also slightly too fast — he moves to close gaps before they are fully exploited and this means he sometimes closes a gap that wasn't a threat while leaving open a gap he hasn't noticed yet. His supervisor, watching over his shoulder, sees this pattern. The supervisor does not intervene because the operator is making correct decisions at the time he makes them. The gap he leaves unnoticed is the one Dante is already through.

At 07:23 the EWS operator says, to his supervisor: "There's a runner in the Delta system. Good one. They're not using any technique I have a file for." His supervisor says: "RAIDER." The operator says: "Already deployed." His supervisor says: "Then we wait."

───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────── MILITARY TACTICAL ENCOUNTER 04 — THE COMPRESSION AND THE CHOICE Inner ring connecting corridors, Hour 3-4 ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

THE SPECIFIC SITUATION:

A group of seven people in the connecting corridor between Dome Beta and Ring 1 StarScape. TROOPER ELEMENT LEADER (T1): He is twenty-six. He has been on the Moon for three days. He sees: seven people, three of whom he can assess as armed (holstered, not raised), four of whom he cannot assess, one of whom is being actively helped to walk.

───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────── MILITARY TACTICAL ENCOUNTER 05 — ZANE'S FINAL JUNCTION Dome Delta access corridor, Hour 4 ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

THE SCENE: The corridor between Dome Delta and the Beanstalk terminal. 40 meters long. 2.5 meters wide. No doorways. No hatches. The military element approaches from the Dome Delta end. Vasora is with this element personally.

THE MILITARY APPROACH — CLOSE QUARTERS BREACH:

In a 2.5-meter corridor with no cover, the drone screen cannot establish overwatch positions. The drone goes in. It identifies four combatants at the far end. Two with weapons raised. Two with weapons holstered. One — Zane — with a blade.

Vasora looks at the drone feed. She looks at the corridor. She makes a call that is not in the doctrine she was issued:

VASORA: "Element, hold."

She steps in front of her troopers. She walks to the edge of the corridor mouth — visible to Zane's position. She does not draw her weapon.

VASORA: (at speaking volume, not shouting) "My name is Lieutenant Vasora. I'm going to walk to the middle of this corridor alone. I'd like to talk."

Silence from Zane's end.

VASORA: "I'm not authorized to negotiate on behalf of the United States government or Melange Mining Corporation. I am authorized to make decisions about how we do this in the next thirty seconds. I would prefer to make a decision that doesn't involve anyone in this corridor getting hurt. I think you would also prefer that."

She walks to the middle of the corridor. Alone. Her element is behind the corner. Her drone is above her. She is forty meters from four armed people including one with a blade who she has no tactical information about except what the drone told her, which is that none of them have fired at her yet.

ZANE: (from the far end, not moving closer) "There's nothing to talk about. You already won."

VASORA: "Then make it official and walk out."

ZANE: "And go where?"

VASORA: "Detention, processing, legal counsel if you're eligible. I don't know what happens after that. I'm not the person who decides what happens after that."

ZANE: "Neither am I."

A silence. Not comfortable. Real.

VASORA: "What did you want?"

ZANE: "That's a long answer."

VASORA: "I have thirty seconds before my supervisor asks me for a status update. Give me the short version."

ZANE: (a beat) "We wanted them to know it doesn't run without us. All of it. The He-3, the Beanstalk, the whole thing. It doesn't run without the people who dig it out of the rock. We wanted them to know we know that. And we know they know it. That was the short version."

VASORA: "Did it work?"

ZANE: "Ask me in twenty years."

Another silence. Then Zane sets his monoblade on the floor. His fighters look at each other. They set down what they have. They walk to the middle of the corridor. Vasora walks back to her element. She gets on comms. "Detention team to Delta access corridor. Four individuals, cooperative, no medical required."

She does not report the conversation. This is a decision she makes in the fifteen seconds between putting them in the detention team's custody and filing her status update. She is not sure why she makes it. She makes it anyway.

It will be the decision she thinks about most from today. Not the tactical ones. This one.
