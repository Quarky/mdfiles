The heist at Outpost 14 happened last night. Nobody knows what it means yet. Melange knows a shipment is missing. Globalsec knows something happened on their watch. The Loonies know they pulled it off. Dante knows about Section F.

Everyone else just knows the air feels different.

───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────── LUNACENT — THE CORPORATE CORE ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Lunacent is Heinlein's gleaming center — transplas domes, Weyland plascrete towers, the Columbiad Arcology needle visible from anywhere in the district. The streets are pressurized walkways connecting sealed commercial blocks. The air here is the cleanest on the Moon. The NBN feeds are the most curated.

The morning after the heist, Lunacent looks exactly like it always looks. That is the point. That is what it is for.

WHAT DANTE SEES: · NBN morning broadcast on every public screen. Serena Nightshade announcing Melange Mining's record He-3 output projections for the quarter. Cheerful. The numbers are real. The quarter is not over. · Columbiad Arcology lobby: corporate workers filing through biometric checkpoints. A Haas-Bioroid rep in a tailored suit talking quietly into a PAD. Two Jinteki employees in matching grey — one human, one clearly a clone in a well-pressed version of the same grey — waiting for an elevator. The clone stares at the middle distance. · A Melange Mining regional office on the third level of a commercial block. The lights are on earlier than usual. Through the transplas window: four people around a table, one of them using a lot of hand gestures. · The Columbiad's lower commission tenements: families eating in units the size of closets visible through identical small windows stacked forty stories up. Above them, the penthouse levels are obscured by privacy glass. Same building. Different gravity. · Tube-lev station: long queues of workers heading outbound — toward the Docklands, toward the processing districts, toward the outposts. None of them look like they slept well. This is not new.

AMBIENT SOUND: The low hum of atmospheric processors audible everywhere. The city breathes. You pay for the breath. Everyone knows this.

───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────── THE DOCKLANDS — THE GREY ZONE ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Beyond Lunacent's major domes, the Docklands sprawl in a wide ring — half-constructed habitats, faded factory hubs, skeletal landing ramps reaching from the cold grey surface like bad architecture left unfinished. This is where Heinlein's law stops meaning what it says.

Starshield and Argus prisec patrol the corporate-owned sections. The NAPD has limited reach here and everyone knows it. The Katala Brothers, 14K, and dozens of smaller outfits run the rest. The people who lost their homes after the Insurrection ended up here. Their children grew up here. Their children's children are growing up here now.

WHAT DANTE SEES: · Landing pads with cargo being transferred between unmarked hoppers and sealed containers. Nobody is checking manifests. This is normal. · A Stormriders courier crew — distinctive worn flight jackets with a lightning-bolt patch — moving fast through the main arterial tunnel, carrying something in a sealed case that they're not discussing. Stormriders move anything, no questions, but they've been busier lately. · A Starshield patrol — two ex-military types in matching grey hardshell — standing at the boundary of a corporate-owned warehouse cluster. One of them is watching the crowd with the particular attention of someone who has been told to watch for something specific but not told what. He doesn't know what he's looking for yet. · A mural on the outer wall of a decommissioned processing hub: a stylized moon face with its eyes open. Underneath: WE REMEMBER. The paint is maybe three weeks old. Someone has tried to sandblast the bottom half. They ran out of time or motivation before finishing. · A small queue outside a Jinteki maintenance depot — Henry-line clones waiting for their quarterly neural recalibration. They stand perfectly still in line. One of them has a small scratch on its wrist that nobody has repaired yet. It stands in line anyway.

AMBIENT SOUND: Industrial. Distant. The rattle of cargo equipment, the hiss of airlocks, the murmur of generators running on whatever fuel is cheapest this month.

───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────── BEYOND HEINLEIN — THE OUTPOSTS ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

The tube-lev connects Heinlein to the closer outposts. Beyond that: hoppers, mag-lev freight runs, and the long silences between. The outpost settlements are not cities. They are facilities with people in them.

WHAT DANTE SEES (if he passes through an outpost or transit hub): · A transit hub waiting room with 20 workers in it. They are not talking. They are looking at their PADs or at the floor. The PADs are old — scratched, second or third-hand, the kind that don't get SYNC's full Network coverage out here. One of them is showing a text feed, not a video feed. Text loads faster on a weak signal. · A Melange company notice board — physical, printed, mounted near the airlock exit. Normal safety notices. A new one at the bottom, added recently: OUTPOST 14 MAINTENANCE SHUTDOWN — OPERATIONS PAUSED PENDING INSPECTION. REASSIGNMENT NOTICES TO FOLLOW. Fourteen workers read this board this morning. None of them said anything out loud. They didn't have to. · A Globalsec investigator — young, clearly Earth-born, still adjusting to the gravity — asking questions at a transit hub checkpoint. She has a list of names. She is not finding the people on it because the people on it were warned an hour before she arrived. · The mag-lev rail visible through a scratched transplas porthole as the tube-lev passes an elevated section. Below, on the surface: a Melange security hopper moving fast in the direction of Outpost 14. Moving faster than a routine inspection would require.

AMBIENT SOUND: The silence of the lunar surface. Felt more than heard. The outposts don't have the city's constant hum. In the silence you can hear the ventilation. You can hear every conversation two rooms away. Everyone knows everyone can hear them. People have learned to say important things in the open and unimportant things in quiet rooms. The inverse of Lunacent.

These conversations can be overheard, participated in, or reported to Dante by his contacts. They represent the political temperature across all layers of Heinlein society in the days immediately following the heist.

───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────── CONVERSATION 01 — TWO MELANGE WORKERS, TUBE-LEV OUTBOUND (Overheard. They don't know Dante.) ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

WORKER 1 (PRIYA, 40s, Southeast Asian, tired): "They're saying it was an equipment fault. Conveyor junction failure, canisters got misdirected, the reconciliation AI flagged it too late."

WORKER 2 (TOMÁS, 30s, speaks with a Brazilian accent, skeptical): "Conveyor junction failure. Right. With a Globalsec investigation team already at the site."

PRIYA: "Maybe they're just—"

TOMÁS: "Priya. I've been running inventory on that line for six years. A junction fault doesn't bring out an investigation team overnight. A junction fault brings out a maintenance clone and a work order."

(Pause. The tube-lev rattles.)

PRIYA: "My reassignment notice said two weeks. That's not maintenance downtime. That's... that's a full operational review."

TOMÁS: "Two weeks. Yeah."

(Neither of them says anything else for a while.)

TOMÁS: (quietly, to his PAD, not looking at Priya) "I heard the night shift foreman isn't answering his comms."

───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────── CONVERSATION 02 — GLOBALSEC GUARDS, DOCKLANDS BOUNDARY POST (Dante can observe or be near this. Guards don't know he's relevant.) ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

GUARD 1 (CHEN, veteran, Globalsec badge worn smooth): "Briefing said elevated posture. Didn't say why."

GUARD 2 (REYES, newer, Earth-born, still learning the gravity): "Elevated from what? It's always elevated."

CHEN: "That's what I said. My shift supervisor said this is different. Came from the Melange liaison directly. Not through the normal chain."

REYES: "Melange liaisons don't usually..."

CHEN: "No. They don't. That's what I said."

(Pause. A Stormriders courier passes. Chen watches him go.)

CHEN: "You know what the last time was that a Melange liaison called us directly?"

REYES: "No."

CHEN: "Me neither. That's how often it happens."

REYES: "What are we watching for?"

CHEN: (shrug) "The briefing said 'persons of interest in connection with outpost security irregularities.' Which means nothing and everything."

REYES: "Do we have descriptions?"

CHEN: "We have a physical type. Not a face. Someone who knows their way around a network splice. Which on this Moon is..."

REYES: "Everyone in the Docklands."

CHEN: "Yeah."

───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────── CONVERSATION 03 — LOONIES, DOCKLANDS BACK ROOM (Dante is likely present for this one or debriefed immediately after.) ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

The room: a sealed chamber behind a recycling depot, low ceiling, three people around a scratched table. Two Dante knows. One he doesn't — yet.

CONTACT (YUSUF, 50s, Somali-Kenyan heritage, the one who brought Dante in. Deliberate. Measures his words. Perpetually smells of recycler chemicals.): "The canisters moved. They're secured. The physical team got out clean."

FACTION REP (MIREILLE, 40s, Franco-Vietnamese, tight-cropped hair, clearly hasn't slept. The politics person.): "Melange has flagged the outpost for inspection. We have maybe 72 hours before they put the timeline together."

UNKNOWN (SIGG — more on this character below): "The timeline doesn't matter if we move before they finish building it."

MIREILLE: "We are not ready to move."

SIGG: "We were never going to be ready. That's not how this works."

YUSUF: (to Dante) "There's something else. From the run last night. Tell them what you found."

(Beat. This is the Section F moment. What Dante says here shapes how these three people respond to him for the rest of the arc.)

───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────── CONVERSATION 04 — NBN CORRESPONDENT, LUNACENT PRESSROOM (Dante can observe this from the public gallery or via a feed.) ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

A Melange PR spokesperson at a small pressroom podium. Three journalists. One NBN crew with a camera. The others with PADs.

SPOKESPERSON: "Outpost 14 is currently offline for scheduled maintenance. We anticipate resuming full operations within the standard review period. There is no safety incident to report and no impact on quarterly projections."

NBN JOURNALIST (professionally cheerful, not pushing): "Can you speak to the Globalsec presence at the outpost overnight?"

SPOKESPERSON: "Globalsec provides security services at all Melange facilities. Their presence is standard protocol and unrelated to the maintenance shutdown."

SECOND JOURNALIST (independent, older, less cheerful): "We're hearing worker reassignment notices went out this morning to all Outpost 14 personnel. That's unusual for a maintenance shutdown."

SPOKESPERSON: "Worker scheduling decisions are made by operations management and are not within the scope of today's briefing. We appreciate—"

SECOND JOURNALIST: "Is it true that the night shift foreman has not responded to company contact attempts since—"

SPOKESPERSON: "We appreciate your time. Melange Mining is committed to transparency and worker safety. Further updates will be provided through official channels. Thank you."

(The camera stays on the empty podium for a moment after she leaves. The NBN journalist smiles at the camera. The independent journalist is already writing something on her PAD.)

───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────── CONVERSATION 05 — A CLONE AND A HUMAN WORKER, OUTPOST TRANSIT HUB (Overheard. Brief. The most important conversation on this list.) ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

HUMAN WORKER (OKONKWO, 60s, Nigerian heritage, been on the Moon 20 years): "You hear about the 14?"

HENRY-LINE CLONE (no name given — clones don't get names officially. This one has a small scratch on its wrist. The same one from the Docklands.): "Yes."

OKONKWO: "They're saying maintenance shutdown."

CLONE: "Yes."

(Beat.)

OKONKWO: "You believe that?"

CLONE: (a pause that is slightly too long for a clone's standard response time) "No."

(Okonkwo looks at it. The clone looks back. This is the kind of look that means something has been understood between two people who have been in the same place long enough to know what the silences contain.)

OKONKWO: "You be careful."

CLONE: "Yes."

Session 2 is not an action session. It is a politics session. The heist succeeded. Now Dante has to navigate what that success means inside the rebellion's own structure — which is fractured, ideologically inconsistent, and increasingly urgent in ways that don't all point in the same direction.

───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────── THE FACTIONS WITHIN THE REBELLION ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

THE PRAGMATISTS (Mireille's faction) Position: The rebellion needs resources, alliances, and timing. Not yet. Not like this. The Section F revelation is a weapon but only if used correctly — leaked strategically, timed to maximum political damage, coordinated with sympathetic journalists and off-Moon contacts who can amplify it before NBN buries it.

What they want from Dante: The suppressed incident file. Intact. Verifiable. And his silence until they decide to use it.

What they're afraid of: That the hardliners will force action before the file can be weaponized properly. That they'll get one shot at this and spend it badly.

Mireille's tell: She refers to clone workers as "the workforce" in political arguments and "people" in private. She knows the difference. She's made a choice about which word is useful in which context. It costs her something.

THE HARDLINERS (Sigg's faction) Position: The corps have been preparing for this. Every day that passes is another day Melange hardens its defenses, another day the SXC at Armstrong Base gets a better picture of the Loonies' network. The moment to move is now, while the outpost seizure has them reactive. Strike Kaguya. Strike the mag-lev infrastructure. Make the He-3 trade impossible to run without negotiating.

What they want from Dante: His skills for the Kaguya operation. His knowledge of Outpost 14's network architecture as a template for what's at Kaguya. They don't need the Section F file — they already know enough to be angry.

What they're afraid of: Nothing, visibly. This is more frightening than the alternative.

Sigg's tell: He talks about the clones differently from how he talks about the human miners. The human miners are "our people." The clones are "them" or "the workforce" or not mentioned at all. He hasn't resolved this. When pushed on it his jaw tightens.

THE NETWORK (Yusuf's position) Position: Yusuf is not a faction leader. He is the connective tissue. He built the cell structure, brought in the runners, maintained the safe houses, sourced the equipment. He is the reason the heist worked. He has no political position because he has decided that taking a political position would compromise his ability to work with both sides. This is a position. He knows it.

What he wants from Dante: For Dante to understand that both Mireille and Sigg are right about some things and that someone needs to be able to move between them. He is offering Dante that role without saying he's offering it. Dante has to see it.

Yusuf's tell: He always knows where the exits are. Not just physically. He knows which conversations have exits and which ones are commitment points. He's been directing Dante toward the commitment points without telling him that's what they are.

THE CLONES (not a faction — a question) The rebellion has not resolved what it is fighting for. The human Loonies are fighting for rights, wages, self-determination. The clone workers have been exploited differently, more completely, and with the full sanction of the law. Section F is not an aberration. It is an acceleration. The rebellion's leadership has not agreed on whether the clones are people they are fighting alongside or people they are fighting for or people who are outside the scope of what they are fighting about.

This is the fault line. Session 2 is where Dante finds it.

───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────── KEY SESSION 2 SCENES ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

SCENE A — THE DEBRIEF (the Section F decision) The back room. Yusuf, Mireille, Sigg, Dante. Dante presents what he found in the suppressed incident file. What happens next depends on what he says and how he says it.

If Dante presents it as a political weapon: Mireille takes control of the room. Sigg defers — for now. She wants to know everything: the Jinteki serial number, the supervisor ID, the exact wording of the suppression order. She is already building the leak in her head.

If Dante presents it as a moral fact: The room goes quiet in a different way. Sigg says "This is why we don't wait." Mireille says "This is why we have to be careful." Yusuf says nothing and watches Dante.

If Dante asks what happens to the clones: Nobody has a good answer. Sigg says the corps will have to negotiate. Mireille says that's a post-victory conversation. Yusuf looks at the table. The silence is the answer.

SCENE B — THE ARGUMENT (hardliners vs pragmatists) A larger meeting. More people. The wider cell leadership. Sigg makes the case for Kaguya. Now. The Outpost 14 action has already committed them — Melange is looking, Globalsec is asking questions, the window before they start finding names is closing.

Mireille makes the case for patience. Six weeks. Coordinate with the sympathetic journalist (name: PETRA VASQUEZ, independent, currently on Earth, the one from the pressroom). Get the Section F file to her. Let NBN try to bury a story that's already on the wire. Hit Kaguya after the corps are already on the defensive.

The vote is close. Closer than Mireille expected. The outcome is not decided this session. Dante's position in the argument matters for which way it goes.

SCENE C — YUSUF'S OFFER (private, after the meeting) Yusuf finds Dante alone.

"You noticed that nobody answered your question about the clones."

He doesn't frame it as an observation. He frames it as information. He tells Dante that there is a Henry-line clone in the Docklands who has been in contact with the Loony network for eight month s. Not as a worker. As a person who has things to say. The clone's name — the name it gave itself, not a designation— is KAI.

Yusuf says: "I don't know what Kai is. I don't know if the law cares. What I know is that it knows things about what Jinteki does to the workforce that we can verify against what you found last night. If Mireille wants to use Section F, Kai makes it real. If Sigg wants to move, Kai is a reason to move faster. But Kai asked me not to bring it to the leadership yet. Kai asked me to find someone who would listen first."

This is Yusuf offering Dante the connector role. Whether Dante meets Kai is the session's ending decision.

SCENE D — THE HENRY-LINE CLONE (optional, end of session) If Dante agrees to meet Kai: A small room in the Docklands, beneath a recycling operation. Kai is physically identical to every other Henry-line clone Dante has seen on the Moon. Same height, same features, same work-worn hands. The scratch on its wrist has been cleaned up — it has medical supplies, which means it has resources, which means it has been operating autonomously for some time.

Kai speaks carefully. Not because it is afraid, but because it has learned that humans hear it differently depending on how it phrases things. It has been learning which phrasings work.

What Kai knows: · Section F was not unique. Three other Melange outposts have similar locked sections. Kai has the location designations. Not the evidence. The locations. · The Jinteki neural experimentation was not rogue. It was directed from a Jinteki research directive Kai saw a portion of before the section was sealed. The directive references "workforce optimization through direct neural parameter adjustment." · There are Henry-line clones at other outposts who have noticed things. They do not have words for what they noticed. Kai has been trying to give them words. Quietly. Through the only channel available: the maintenance network handshake frequency that drone units use.

Kai's ask: If the rebellion moves, it needs to make a decision about the clones before it moves. Not after. Because after, whoever wins will make that decision for them, and winners have historically not made it well.

These events happen in the days and weeks after Session 2. They are the world tightening. Each one is visible to Dante if he is paying attention — and impossible to miss if he isn't.

───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────── ESCALATION EVENT 01 — MELANGE CLOSES THE OUTPOSTS (Days 3-5) ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Melange announces "temporary operational consolidation" of 6 smaller He-3 outposts. Workers are reassigned to larger facilities or given "voluntary separation packages." The real reason: Melange is hardening its remaining infrastructure and replacing human workers with additional clone units at the consolidated sites.

WHAT DANTE SEES: Hundreds of workers arriving in Heinlein's transit hubs with separation packages and nowhere to go. The Docklands absorbs some of them. Others queue at the Beanstalk terminal trying to get back to Earth on credits that won't cover the fare. The tube-lev runs are crowded in one direction only.

NBN FRAMING: "Melange Mining Streamlines Operations for Efficiency and Safety." Serena Nightshade: "Great news for productivity on the Moon today—"

LOONY RESPONSE: This is what Sigg has been saying. Mireille's six-week timeline just got shorter. The workers arriving in the Docklands are angry, organized by circumstance if not by intention, and some of them already know Yusuf's name.

───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────── ESCALATION EVENT 02 — GLOBALSEC SWEEP (Days 5-8) ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

A coordinated Globalsec sweep of the Docklands — more organized than usual, with Starshield and Argus prisec unusually cooperative with NAPD requests. The sweep is framed as "gang activity suppression." It pulls in 40+ people. Most are released within 24 hours. Three are not.

WHAT DANTE SEES: The Docklands feels different for 48 hours after — quieter, more watchful. Safe houses relocate. Some of Yusuf's network goes dark for 72 hours as a precaution. One of the three people not released is a Stormriders courier who ran packages for the Loony network. He doesn't know names. He knows pickup points. The pickup points are now burned.

LOONY RESPONSE: Mireille says this is proof that six weeks is the right call — they need to rebuild the logistics network before they can move. Sigg says this is proof they're already out of time. Yusuf says nothing and moves two safe houses and burns a contact list.

───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────── ESCALATION EVENT 03 — PETRA VASQUEZ PUBLISHES (Days 10-14) ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

The independent journalist from the pressroom — Petra Vasquez — publishes a story. Not the Section F story. She doesn't have that yet. She publishes what she does have: the discrepancy between Melange's "maintenance shutdown" statement and the evidence of a Globalsec investigation, worker reassignment notices, and a night shift foreman who hasn't been seen since the shutdown.

The story runs on an independent network wire. It is not picked up by NBN. It is shared via PAD-to-PAD on the outpost transit networks where SYNC's coverage is thin enough that NBN can't suppress it completely.

WHAT DANTE SEES: The story is being read in every break room and transit hub on the Moon. Not on screens — on personal PADs, passed hand to hand. Melange issues a statement calling it "irresponsible speculation." The independent network's offices in Lunacent are visited by a Melange legal team the same afternoon.

NBN FRAMING: The story is not covered. A different story runs in the same slot: "Helium-3 Output Records Broken Three Months Running." True figures. Different story.

LOONY RESPONSE: Mireille says: "She needs the file. Now." Sigg says: "She just painted a target on herself. How long before NBN buries her and Melange lawyers shut the wire down?" Both of them are right.

───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────── ESCALATION EVENT 04 — THE PROSPERITY ANNIVERSARY (Days 15-20) ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

The anniversary of the Prosperity Mining Depot disaster — the event that triggered the original Insurrection — is approaching. Every Loony knows the date. The corps know the Loonies know the date.

Gatherings are not illegal. Gatherings of more than 50 people in the Docklands without a permit are technically illegal but rarely enforced. This year District Manager Hubbard announces a "permit review process" for all gatherings over 20 people in the Docklands during the anniversary week.

WHAT DANTE SEES: The Docklands becomes a negotiation between the thing that is technically legal and the thing that is actually happening. Smaller gatherings. More of them. In more places simultaneously. The WE REMEMBER mural has been replicated on three other walls. Someone sandblasts one. Two more appear. Armstrong Base runs a visible patrol hopper over Heinlein at twice its normal frequency during the week. Colonel Metzger says nothing publicly.

LOONY RESPONSE: The anniversary gatherings are not organized by the rebellion. They are organized by grief and habit and the specific weight of a date that everyone carries. The rebellion's leadership attends. They are not in charge of what happens. This is both an opportunity and a warning.

───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────── ESCALATION EVENT 05 — KAI DISAPPEARS (Days 18-22) ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

If Dante met Kai: Kai stops responding to the maintenance handshake channel. Yusuf tells Dante quietly, without alarm in his voice, which means he is very alarmed. A Henry-line clone that stops responding is technically a Jinteki asset that has malfunctioned. Jinteki can request Globalsec assistance in recovering malfunctioning assets. This is legal. It happens regularly. It is not usually noticed.

WHAT DANTE SEES: Nothing visible. That's the problem. Somewhere in the Docklands or the outpost network a clone unit is being "recovered." It will be returned to a Jinteki maintenance facility. What happens to neural tissue that has developed unauthorized autonomy during standard Jinteki maintenance procedure is not covered in any public document.

Dante has the locations Kai gave him. Three other outposts. Kai is gone. The locations remain.

───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────── ESCALATION EVENT 06 — THE VOTE (Days 20-25) ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

The rebellion's leadership votes on Sigg's Kaguya proposal. The parameters: seize Starport Kaguya's network, disrupt He-3 shipment infrastructure, demonstrate that the corps cannot run the lunar economy without negotiating with the people who live here. The Battle of Kaguya — the physical confrontation that ends the Insurrection — is the consequence of this vote going wrong. That comes in Act III. The vote itself is Act II's end point.

HOW DANTE'S ACTIONS SHAPE THE VOTE:

If Dante gave Mireille the Section F file and she got it to Petra Vasquez: The story is on the wire. NBN is suppressing it but not fast enough. Mireille argues they are winning politically. The vote is close. If Dante backs Mireille in the room: the vote fails narrowly. Six more weeks. Petra publishes. The window closes a different way.

If Dante supported Sigg's timeline: The vote passes. Kaguya goes on the planning board. Dante's network knowledge from Outpost 14 is immediately requested as a template for the Kaguya infiltration.

If Dante met Kai and raised the clone question in the leadership meeting: The room fractures in a new direction. The vote is postponed while the leadership argues about something they have never argued about before. This is Mireille's worst outcome and Sigg's second worst. Yusuf watches Dante with something that might be satisfaction.

If Dante does nothing: The vote passes. Sigg wins. Dante has made a choice by not making one. Yusuf tells him this, gently, afterward.

───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────── ESCALATION EVENT 07 — OPERATION FALLING STAR BEGINS (Days 28-35) ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

The corps have been watching. They have been building a picture. The picture is not complete — the cell structure held, Yusuf's network discipline bought time — but it is complete enough.

The first sign is not military. It is financial. Three Docklands businesses that were providing safe house cover have their corp licenses revoked simultaneously on a Tuesday morning. No explanation. They have 48 hours to vacate.

The second sign is the SXC patrol hopper frequency doubling again. Not over Heinlein. Over the outpost transit corridors.

The third sign is the Melange announcement of a "security partnership" with Globalsec and Starshield — consolidated command structure, shared intelligence, coordinated response. District Manager Hubbard signs off. The NAPD is notably not included.

WHAT DANTE SEES: Yusuf burns the last safe house list. He does this without ceremony or explanation, on a Tuesday afternoon, in a recycling depot that smells of ammonia. He watches the list dissolve in the recycler and then he looks at Dante and says:

"Whatever you're going to do, decide now."

This is Act III's beginning. Kaguya is on the board. Operation Falling Star is already in motion. The only question left is which side of it Dante is on when the broadcast runs.
