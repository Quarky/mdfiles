# THE BUILDING — OVERVIEW

The NBN Heinlein Broadcast Facility occupies a corner position in Lunacent's commercial block seven. Six stories of transplas facade over plascrete framing, the warm amber of the NBN logo dominating the main face. Clean. Expensive. The kind of building whose architecture announces that the people inside it do not worry about the same things the people outside it worry about.

Today the building is in crisis broadcast mode. Every screen on the exterior face is running Kaguya coverage. Serena Nightshade's voice comes through the external speakers at a volume calibrated to be heard without being intrusive — the background noise of today, ambient and inescapable.

The main entrance is locked. Emergency protocol. A notice reads: FACILITY IN CRISIS BROADCAST MODE — AUTHORIZED PERSONNEL ONLY.

The south face has a service entrance with a biometric panel. Adjacent to it, on the roofline above, a pressure equalization hatch — hatch EV-7 — sits very slightly ajar. It should not be ajar. The stun grenade from Prycek's fighters outside triggered the building's emergency atmo protocol forty minutes ago and nobody has reset it because the facilities manager is watching the Kaguya feed on Level 3.

This hatch is how Dante gets in.

# LEVEL 1 — THE LOBBY

## Physical Description

The freight elevator from the south service corridor deposits Dante into a lobby that smells of the Moon's institutional recycled air mixed with something faintly floral — an artificial scent piped into the filtration system specifically for this floor, chosen by someone whose job is to make spaces feel welcoming. Today it sits wrong against the Kaguya footage on every screen.

The lobby floor is polished plascrete, the specific grey-blue of Heinlein's better commercial spaces, kept clean by automated systems that do not know today is different from any other day. The ceiling is three meters up. The lighting is warm amber — NBN's corporate color spectrum — calibrated to match the exterior signage and produce a sense of continuity between outside and inside.

Six screens of varying sizes cover the main wall, south face. All showing Kaguya. The orbital camera footage of the crater. The LIVE indicator in the upper right corner of each screen. Serena's voice — she is being piped back in through the lobby speakers from the studio two floors above. The building is broadcasting itself to its own lobby.

Three NBN security staff occupy the floor. Two are near the main entrance, watching the locked door and the crowd of three people visible through the transplas — a journalist with a press badge arguing with the intercom, two others simply watching. The third security staff member — OLU — is at the security station on the east wall, watching the screens, checking his phone between screen-checks.

## THE PHOTOGRAPH WALL

Twelve frames. Floor to ceiling. A corporate history.

The photographs are professionally lit, warm-toned, the colors of an institution that has decided how it wants to be remembered. They tell a story. The story has two deliberate gaps.

Frame 1 — "Vertex Corporation — New Angeles HQ." Dated 43 years ago. A glass and steel tower, now demolished. Keith Randolph Kane Sr. stands at the front of thirty people. He is not yet forty. He stands the way a man stands when he has decided that the front of the photograph is where he belongs.

Frames 2-7 — The Vertex expansion years. Growth. Acquisitions. Government contract signings. In each successive photograph the group around Kane Sr. is different — the corporation cycles through people. Kane Sr. does not cycle. In the last of these, dated to approximately when partition legislation passed, Kane Sr. is looking slightly to the left of the camera lens. Not distraction. Something else.

Frames 8-9 — EMPTY. The frames are there. The caption plates beneath them are there. The photographs are gone. The wall behind each frame is a slightly different shade — it did not fade the same way as the surrounding plascrete because it was covered for years. The photographs were there for a long time. Then someone took them down. The caption plates read: "Year of Transition" and "The Recovery Period." Two years. The Blackout years. Someone decided these years should not be visible in the lobby. Empty frames are more informative than photographs would have been.

Frame 10 — "NBN — New Beginnings." Twenty people. Much smaller group. Kane Sr. at the front. Same posture. Same decision about where to stand. Dated two years after the Blackout. The people around him are different from the Vertex photographs — younger, fewer. As if the Vertex era required many people and this era requires only specific ones.

Frame 11 — "SYNC Lunar Relay Network — Activation Ceremony." Engineers in pressure suits on the lunar surface. A relay antenna structure behind them. Earth on the horizon. The date on the caption plate reads three years before Heinlein's first permanent habitation dome was operational. SYNC was transmitting on the Moon before anyone lived here. Network infrastructure built for a population that didn't exist yet.

Frame 12 — "NBN Heinlein — Opening Day." The lobby they are standing in, twenty years ago. Kane Jr. — the son, current president — at the center. Same posture as his father. Same position in the frame.

PERCEPTION (Average): The gap between frames 7 and 10. Two years missing. This was a choice.

PERCEPTION (Hard): The date on frame 11. SYNC predated the population. Someone built the network before the people arrived. Someone knew the people were coming.

The production studio, Serena's private dressing room and preparation space, the green room for guests and analysts, the wardrobe and cosmetics suite. # LEVEL 3 — THE EDITING FLOOR

## Physical Description

Level 3 is the operational core of the broadcast — the floor where the live feed is assembled, managed, and shaped in real time. If Level 2 is where the performance happens, Level 3 is where the performance is constructed.

The floor is divided into three distinct zones separated by low partitions rather than walls. This is a working environment, not a presentational one — the aesthetic here is functional. Exposed ceiling ducts. Bright, even, slightly cold white lighting. The smell of electronics under load and the synthetic coffee that has been brewing in the corner since 05:30 and has not been replaced because nobody has had time.

The noise level is moderate — dozens of conversations happening simultaneously at low volume, the constant audio bleed from monitoring speakers running the broadcast feed, the soft percussion of workstations being operated by people who have been at them for hours and are running on the specific adrenaline of professional crisis.

Twenty-two people are on this floor. Twelve in the main editing pit. Four in the graphics suite. Three managing the feed routing station. Two in the sound design booth. One floor manager moving between all of them, coordinating through an earpiece with Level 2 below.

### ROOMS ON THIS LEVEL

Main Editing Pit — The center of the floor. Twelve workstations in three rows. The live feed editing operation. Where footage is selected, sequenced, captioned, and pushed to broadcast in real time.

Graphics Suite — A separate zone with specialized workstations. Maps, lower-third captions, the Kaguya orbital overlay graphic. Three people, all currently working at high intensity.

Feed Routing Station — The technical backbone. Controls which signals go to which channels. Three technical operators. This station is adjacent to a hardwired panel that connects to the building's uplink infrastructure.

Sound Design Booth — A small glass-walled room. Two audio engineers managing the broadcast sound mix, Serena's earpiece feed, ambient sound levels.

Archive Access Terminal — A single workstation in the northeast corner, slightly separated from the main pit. Labeled EDITORIAL ARCHIVE — RESTRICTED ACCESS. This is where Mira works. Today it is unattended.

Server Access Corridor — A narrow corridor connecting Level 3 to the freight elevator shaft and the building's technical infrastructure. The Level 3 environmental crawl access panel (EV-ACCESS 3-C) opens from here.

# LEVEL 4 — ADMINISTRATION

## Physical Description

The administrative level is quieter than the floors below and emptier. The crisis has drawn everyone useful down to Level 3 or Level 2. The people who remain are the ones who cannot be useful in a crisis broadcast — accounting, HR, legal affairs — and who have been told to shelter in place, which they have interpreted as permission to congregate in the break room and watch the same feed they could have watched at their desks.

The air on Level 4 is the most recycled in the building. The filtration on this floor has been running for twelve years and it shows in a thin chemical undertone beneath the institutional neutral. The ceiling is standard height. The lighting is fluorescent and honest — not the warm amber of the lobby, not the production floor's performance lighting. Adequate light for people who are supposed to be working.

The corridor from the stairwell runs east-west the full length of the floor. Seven office doors on the south side. The common area is northwest. The legal affairs suite is southwest. The archive access room — where the physical building records are kept — is directly opposite the stairwell entrance.
