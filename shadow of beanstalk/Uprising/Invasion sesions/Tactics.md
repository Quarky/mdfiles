## THE MILITARY MINDSET

The SXC troopers at Kaguya are not sadists. They are professionals with a job to do. They are doing it in an environment they trained for but have never fought in. They are following orders. They are not thinking about politics. They are thinking about the next corridor.

### Vasora's Command Philosophy

Vasora is the commander on the ground. She has been in command for 12 hours. She has not slept. She has not eaten. She has been moving through the facility, seeing the situation with her own eyes, adjusting tactics as she goes.

**Her Motives:**
1. **Secure the Beanstalk.** This is the primary objective. Everything else is secondary.
2. **Minimize military casualties.** Her troopers are professionals. She will not waste them.
3. **Minimize civilian casualties.** She is not a monster. But civilians are not the primary objective.
4. **Finish the job.** The longer this goes on, the more complex it becomes. She wants to end it.

**Her Decision-Making:**
- She will not sacrifice her troopers to save civilians.
- She will not sacrifice the Beanstalk to save civilians.
- She will not stop the advance for civilians.
- But she will not deliberately target civilians.
- She will detain Loonies when possible. She will kill Loonies when necessary.

**The Human Element:**
Vasora knows that every decision she makes will be scrutinized. She knows that there will be investigations. She knows that she will be blamed for everything that goes wrong. She is doing the job anyway. She believes in the mission.

### The Trooper Mindset

The average SXC trooper at Kaguya is 25-30 years old. They have trained for lunar combat. They have never used it. They are afraid. They are professional. They are following orders.

**What They Believe:**
- They are the good guys.
- They are protecting the Beanstalk.
- The Loonies are terrorists.
- Civilians are collateral.
- They want to go home.

**What They Do in Combat:**
- They follow doctrine.
- They cover each other.
- They advance methodically.
- They do not hesitate.
- They do not think about politics.

**What They Are Not:**
- They are not sadists.
- They are not murderers.
- They are not heroes.
- They are soldiers doing their job.

## ADVANCED TACTICAL PATTERNS

### Pattern 1 — The Dynamic Drone Screen (Advanced)

| Element | Description |
|---------|-------------|
| **Purpose** | The drone screen is the foundation of SXC tactical doctrine. It provides advanced intelligence before committing troops. |
| **How It Works** | The drone moves ahead of the squad, scanning for threats. The squad follows at a distance, using the drone's data to form a plan. |
| **Adaptive Variation** | When the enemy starts moving after the scan, the drone adapts. It doesn't just scan once—it loops back, re-scans, and updates the squad. |

**Advanced Tactic — Drone Baiting:**
The drone enters the corridor and scans. The squad waits. The enemy moves to a new position. The drone re-enters—different angle this time. The enemy is caught in the open. The squad advances and clears the corridor.

**Counter-Tactic:**
The Loonies know the drone's pattern. They have learned that the drone re-scans after 90 seconds. They move exactly at the 90-second mark—after the scan, before the re-scan. The drone sees nothing. The squad advances into a corridor that is already empty, while the Loonies are behind them through a maintenance hatch.

**Tactical Challenge (For Dante):**
If Dante is in the corridor when the drone enters, he has 90 seconds to move before the re-scan. If he moves too early, the drone sees the movement. If he moves too late, the squad arrives.

### Pattern 2 — Controlled Pair Advance with Overwatch

| Element | Description |
|---------|-------------|
| **Purpose** | The controlled pair advance creates a moving wall of firepower. |
| **How It Works** | Team A advances 10 meters. Team B provides overwatch. Team A takes cover, Team B advances past them. Repeat. |
| **Adaptive Variation** | In narrow corridors, the pairs advance side-by-side—one covering the left, one covering the right. |

**Advanced Tactic — The Bait and Switch:**
The squad advances slowly, telegraphing their position. The enemy focuses on the advancing squad. A second squad flanks through a maintenance hatch. The enemy is caught between the advancing squad and the flanking squad.

**Counter-Tactic:**
The Loonies know the facility's maintenance hatches. They know which ones are sealed and which are open. When the military uses a hatch, they are using a route the Loonies know about. The Loonies simply seal the hatch behind them. The flanking squad is trapped in the maintenance tunnel with no exit.

**Tactical Challenge (For Dante):**
If Dante is in the corridor when the controlled pair advance begins, he needs to either move with the advance (blend in) or find a way out. The pair advance is too methodical to evade. It's easier to confuse it.

### Pattern 3 — The Compression Zone

| Element | Description |
|---------|-------------|
| **Purpose** | The compression zone is a two-front assault that forces the enemy into a killing field. |
| **How It Works** | The military pushes from the rim. Globalsec pushes from the inner domes. The enemy is compressed into the middle. |
| **Adaptive Variation** | When civilians are present, the military slows the advance slightly to allow them to exit. But they don't stop. |

**Advanced Tactic — The False Retreat:**
The military pushes into the compression zone, then retreats—seeming to withdraw. The Loonies push forward, thinking they've gained ground. The military turns around and re-engages, catching the Loonies in the open.

**Counter-Tactic:**
The Loonies know the military's patterns. They know that the military doesn't retreat unless it's a trap. So they don't take the bait. They hold their position. The military's false retreat becomes a real retreat.

**Tactical Challenge (For Dante):**
If Dante is in the compression zone, he is in the worst possible position. He needs to find a way out—through the maintenance network, through the civilian flow, or through the chaos.

### Pattern 4 — The Sealed Suit Advantage

| Element | Description |
|---------|-------------|
| **Purpose** | The military uses sealed suits to move through decompressed areas that the Loonies cannot survive. |
| **How It Works** | The military creates a decompression event—either by intentionally breaching a section or by advancing through one that has already been breached. |
| **Adaptive Variation** | The military doesn't need to decompress the entire area. They just need to make it unpleasant enough that the Loonies can't hold the position. |

**Advanced Tactic — The Slow Depressurization:**
The military doesn't rush into decompressed areas. They wait. The decompression event is slow—80% oxygen, then 60%, then 40%. The Loonies don't notice it at first. They think it's just the pressure differential. By the time they notice, they're already disoriented. The military advances into a position that the Loonies are barely able to defend.

**Counter-Tactic:**
The Loonies know the atmospheric systems. They monitor the oxygen levels. When they notice a drop, they move to a different section. They don't wait to see if it gets worse.

**Tactical Challenge (For Dante):**
If Dante is in a decompressing corridor, he has minutes to get out. The military is advancing into that corridor. He needs to get out before they arrive.

### Pattern 5 — The EWS Siege

| Element | Description |
|---------|-------------|
| **Purpose** | The EWS is the military's invisible weapon. It degrades the Loonies' network advantage. |
| **How It Works** | The EWS operators are not at Kaguya. They are at Armstrong Base, coordinating remotely. They are attacking the network from the outside. |
| **Adaptive Variation** | The EWS focuses on the most critical systems first—the departure freeze, the cargo AI, the internal comms. They don't need to take down everything. They just need to take down the things that matter. |

**Advanced Tactic — The Coordinated Breach:**
The EWS breaches the cargo AI. The departure freeze is still active. But the cargo system is compromised. The military knows where the Loonies are moving cargo. They can intercept.

**Counter-Tactic:**
The Loonies know that the EWS is breaching the system. They move cargo before the EWS can track it. They use the maintenance network to bypass the cargo system entirely.

**Tactical Challenge (For Dante):**
If Dante is in the network, he needs to either defend the network or move quickly. The EWS is tracking him. He has to stay ahead of them.

### Pattern 6 — The Information Loophole

| Element | Description |
|---------|-------------|
| **Purpose** | The military uses information to anticipate the Loonies' moves. |
| **How It Works** | The military is listening to everything. They have intercepted PAD-to-PAD communications. They have civilians who are talking. They are building a picture of the Loonies' network. |
| **Adaptive Variation** | The military doesn't need perfect information. They just need enough to make the Loonies predictable. |

**Advanced Tactic — The False Information:**
The military leaks false information—a location that the Loonies will move to, a time that they will move. The Loonies take the bait. The military is waiting for them.

**Counter-Tactic:**
The Loonies know that the military is listening. They use code words. They only share information on need-to-know basis. They don't tell anyone more than they need to know.

**Tactical Challenge (For Dante):**
If Dante is the one giving or receiving information, he needs to be careful. The military is listening. He needs to use code words or find another way.

### Pattern 7 — The Extraction Point Trap

| Element | Description |
|---------|-------------|
| **Purpose** | The military knows that the Loonies will try to leave. The extraction points are the most obvious target. |
| **How It Works** | The military positions fire teams at all known extraction points—the launch core, the tube-lev, the private pads, the emergency hatches. |
| **Adaptive Variation** | The military waits. They don't rush to intercept. They just stay in position, watching. The Loonies will eventually try to leave. The military is ready. |

**Advanced Tactic — The Delayed Intercept:**
The military lets the Loonies leave Kaguya. They follow them—through the tube-lev, across the surface, into the outposts. They wait until the Loonies feel safe, then they strike.

**Counter-Tactic:**
The Loonies know the military is watching. They don't leave through obvious extraction points. They use the maintenance network. They use unlisted routes. They take their chances.

**Tactical Challenge (For Dante):**
If Dante is trying to leave, he needs to find a way out that the military isn't watching. He needs to be unpredictable.

## ENCOUNTER TYPE 1 — THE MAINTENANCE CORRIDOR CHASE

### Location Context
This encounter can be placed in any maintenance corridor network. In Kaguya, it's the service tunnel beneath the inner dome cluster.

### Setup
The squad is moving through the maintenance tunnels. The military has breached the network—a fire team is behind them, and another fire team is ahead of them. The squad is trapped in a narrow corridor with no exits.

### The Challenge
| Element | Description |
|---------|-------------|
| **Environment** | Narrow tunnel (2 meters wide), low ceiling, emergency lighting only, multiple side hatches (some sealed, some open) |
| **Enemy 1** | Military Fire Team A (behind). 4 troopers. Controlled pair advance. 90 seconds away. |
| **Enemy 2** | Military Fire Team B (ahead). 4 troopers. Blocking the exit. 120 seconds away. |
| **Objective** | Escape the corridor before being trapped between the two teams. |

### Tactical Options

| Option | Description | Difficulty | Outcome |
|--------|-------------|------------|---------|
| **Fight Through** | Engage one of the fire teams directly, break through their line | Hard | High chance of casualties, but could break the trap |
| **Use a Side Hatch** | Find an open side hatch and escape into a different section of the maintenance network | Average | Requires finding a hatch and a route through an unknown section |
| **Create a Distraction** | Trigger a decompression event in the corridor, forcing both teams to retreat | Daunting | Dangerous, but creates confusion and time |
| **Negotiate** | Use Fletcher's old uniform or social skills to convince the military that the squad is not the target | Hard | Requires specific conditions (Fletcher's uniform, believable story) |
| **Wait** | Wait for the military to make a mistake | Very Hard | The military doesn't make mistakes |

### NPC Actions

| NPC | Action | Effect |
|-----|--------|--------|
| **Riker** | Bypass a side hatch door | Opens an alternative route |
| **Junior** | Identify a side hatch that isn't on the schematics | Finds a route the military doesn't know about |
| **Fletcher** | Use his old uniform to bluff the fire team | Delays the fire team by 30 seconds |
| **Sully** | Cut through a sealed panel | Creates a new exit |
| **Mac** | Hold the corridor while the team escapes | Provides 30 seconds of cover |

### Outcome
If the squad escapes: They move into an unknown section of the maintenance network. The military loses track of them—temporarily.
If the squad fights: The fire team engages. The squad takes casualties. The military reinforces.
If the squad splits: Some escape, some are captured.

### Setup
The squad needs to pass through an airlock to reach the next dome. The military is holding the far side of the airlock—a fire team has set up a defensive position. The squad is trapped on the near side. The airlock is sealed.

|---------|-------------|

| **Enemy** | Military Fire Team (far side). 4 troopers. Defensive position. Bipod mounted weapon. |
| **Objective** | Get through the airlock to the next dome. |

### Tactical Options

| Option | Description | Difficulty | Outcome |

| **Use the Ventilation System** | Go through the ventilation system above the airlock | Hard | Tight space, but avoids the fire team |
