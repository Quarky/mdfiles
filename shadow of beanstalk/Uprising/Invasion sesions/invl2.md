## THE AGE OF MEGACORPORATIONS

In the not-so-distant future, humanity has spread across the solar system, unlocked the frontiers of cyberspace, and created millions of intelligent androids in its own image. At the heart of this progress stands a ladder leading to the riches of the stars—the massive space elevator called the Beanstalk.

Four monolithic megacorporations control the vast majority of human interests:

**Haas-Bioroid** leads the world in cybernetics and high-tech research, holding the monopoly on bioroid manufacture and providing everything from cheap labor and pleasure bots to units specialized for network security. What Haas-Bioroid accomplishes digitally, **Jinteki** accomplishes biologically—by producing genetically engineered clones and researching the psychic potential of the human genome. Jinteki aims to push human evolution forward, at a profit.

**NBN** are the undisputed masters of information, supplying the global network's insatiable hunger for news and entertainment. **Weyland Consortium** uses brute economic force and dirty tactics to dominate heavy industry, construction, and space technologies.

## THE NETWORK & THE RUNNERS

The Network is the all-seeing, all-hearing grid that surrounds Earth and reaches out into the solar system. More data flows through the network every second than was ever expressed in the first five thousand years of written language. It is a surveillance network, a financial system, and a library. It is the backbone of modern civilization.

Opposing these corporate forces is an online underground of netrunners—digital insurgents who contend with the monolithic corporations. These rogue operators have the hardware, software, and raw talent to exploit the corporations' greatest weaknesses.

## CLONES & BIOROIDS — THE ARTIFICIAL WORKFORCE

Jinteki clones are custom-designed for particular roles and then mass-produced via cloning and rapid-growth technologies. Most clones are humanoid in form, though some are not. All clones have distinctive barcodes tattooed on their necks to indicate their clone status, and that they are items possessed by their creators. Under current American law, clones may not marry. They are also sterile, and cannot reproduce.

Bioroids, produced by Haas-Bioroid, are artificially intelligent mechanical beings. Rumors persist of bioroids that have somehow broken their programming or evolved beyond it. Some are searching for meaning, asking questions their programmers cannot answer.

## THE BEANSTALK & NEW ANGELES

The Beanstalk is the primary connection between Earth and the Moon, with cargo and passenger shuttles traveling between the Challenger Planetoid and Starport Kaguya. At its base sprawls the biggest, meanest, and most exciting city on Earth: New Angeles. Over 500 million people call its districts their home.

Heinlein is the primary Lunar colony, originally established as a starport and mining facility. The Beanstalk connects New Angeles to the lunar city of Heinlein, leading to untold wealth and prosperity for both cities.

## THE NISEI PROJECT — THE DARK HEART OF JINTEKI

The Nisei Project is Jinteki's most ambitious and secretive endeavor. It is rumored that some clones are engineered for psionic abilities. Caprice Nisei is one such clone, along with the rest of the Nisei Mk II line.

Caprice Nisei is a psychic clone created and owned by Jinteki and leased to the New Angeles Police Department. Nisei can be translated as "second generation," a reference to perhaps Caprice being a new step in human evolution. There were two Nisei clones before her, and what became of them is uncertain.

Caprice considers clones from the same family to be her "sisters". Her psychic abilities allow her to connect with them even though they currently exist in a "beta" state, still floating in their clone-vats. Caprice is a test model, and her performance for the NAPD will determine the future of those of her clone-family.

However, she is also erratic and unstable, and psychiatric tests indicate her psychic powers may interfere with her sanity. She is in consultation with lawyers working for the Liberty Society to acquire her freedom from Jinteki and her establishment as a full citizen of New Angeles.

Caprice's performance is closely tracked by Chairman Hiro, her creator, as well as Commissioner Dawn of the NAPD, who is watching Caprice to determine if clones can make useful members of her department.

## THE NAPD — THE THIN BLUE LINE

The New Angeles Police Department safeguards the city as the largest police entity in the world, its territories expanding as far as the Heinlein district on the moon through the New Angeles Space Elevator. Each of its hundreds of precincts is responsible for its territories, leading to a great difference of management between them.

Jinteki's Caprice Nisei is currently prototyped as the first clone member of the NAPD, rumored to use her extraordinary cognitive powers for investigation. Haas-Bioroid's Floyd 2X3A7C is the first bioroid detective, utilized for his deductive processing and logic to solve cases. Floyd, a bioroid leased to the NAPD, has performed well but exhibits behaviors outside his programmers' expectations—visits to church, asking theological questions his programmers cannot answer.

## THE TREATY OF HEINLEIN — THE PEACE THAT WASN'T

The Treaty of Heinlein ended the Worlds War, produced the charter for the Universal Nations, and created the Martian Colonial Authority. It transferred control of the Beanstalk to the Space Elevator Authority, theoretically leveling the playing field for all countries to use the Beanstalk. However, it is debatable whether the SEA is holding up its end of the deal, or if the U.S. still receives preferential treatment.

The treaty returned Heinlein to New Angeles, to be ruled as a city district, albeit a distant and rather impractical one. The SXC is allowed to maintain various bases on the Moon and Mars, theoretically as a temporary arrangement.

## THE LOONIES — THE PEOPLE OF THE MOON

Those born on the Moon consider themselves a people apart. They are members of the first generations that have grown up free from the loving embrace of planet Earth. Loonies are different because they no longer see Earth as home.

Growing up on the Moon teaches Loonies self-reliance in a way no place on Earth could. When you can't even take the air you breathe or the shielding that protects you from lethal radiation for granted, you learn pretty quickly to be aware of everything in the environment around you.

## THE PROSPERITY DEPOT DISASTER

The Prosperity Mining Depot disaster was the event that triggered the original Insurrection. What would later spiral out of control into the Worlds War started when an alliance of mining workers broadcast their grievances to the Earth as a whole, stating that they would no longer work if the corporations continued to oppress and underpay their workforce. In response, an unmarked shuttle bombed the Prosperity Mining Depot during a protest, breaching the facility and suffocating hundreds of miners.

The workers rose up in all-out revolt across Luna, overwhelming prisec security forces in a brutal assault that saw heavy casualties on both sides. The rebels went on to seize the shipyards and mag-lev launch tracks in Heinlein, Xiangong City, and other major lunar settlements, blockading all shipments of He-3 in a single stroke.

## ACT 1 — EXIT THE NBN FACILITY

### SCENE 1A — THE FIRST BREATH

Dante stands in the doorway of the NBN facility. The air hits him. The smoke, the ozone, the blood. It's not just smell—it's texture. The air is thicker here, heavier. The facility's atmospheric processors are failing.

He steps out. His foot crunches on something. He looks down. It's a PAD—cracked, dead. It has a name engraved on the back: *"K. Okonkwo."* The PAD is cold. It's been dead for hours.

There's a smear on the floor nearby. Dark. Not hydraulic fluid. Dante knows the difference.

He looks up. The StarScape retail level stretches before him. It's a graveyard. The moving walkways still run—automated systems don't stop for war. They carry nothing. They'll keep carrying nothing until the power fails.

**The Sound:** A distant voice. Someone is shouting—orders, probably. Military. The sound echoes oddly in the damaged space. Then silence. Then the voice again.

**The Detail:** A holographic ad flickers on a nearby wall. It's for "Heinlein's Finest Low-G Tourism — Book Now!" The ad is still playing. It still shows families laughing, couples holding hands, children playing in the recreation domes. The ad is playing to an empty StarScape.

**The Lore Connection:** The ad is from before the Insurrection—before the occupation, before the military. It shows a Kaguya that no longer exists. It's a ghost of what the facility was supposed to be.

### SCENE 1B — THE JINTEKI MEMO

If Dante searches a nearby terminal (security or retail), he finds a data fragment. It's a Jinteki internal memo—not encrypted, but not public. It was left open by someone who was in a hurry.

> **JINTEKI INTERNAL MEMO — EYES ONLY**
>
> Re: Project Nisei — Outpost 14 Update
>
> Neural parameter adjustment continuing as scheduled. Subject H-2447 remains unresponsive. Recommend transfer to Heinlein Docklands Facility 7 for further observation.
>
> Note: Melange Regional Manager MEL-221-REG-HAAS has been informed of status.
>
> No further action required at this time.

**What This Means:**
- Project Nisei is real
- It's happening at multiple locations
- MEL-221-REG-HAAS is involved
- Subject H-2447 is the clone from Section F
- The file is still open

**The Lore Connection:** This memo ties directly to the Nisei Project—the same project that created Caprice Nisei and her sisters. The "neural parameter adjustment" referenced here is the same procedure being used on the Henry-line clones. MEL-221-REG-HAAS is the same supervisor whose ID appears in the Section F files.

### SCENE 1C — THE BIOROID AT THE CROSSING

Dante is moving through a StarScape corridor when he encounters a bioroid. It's a Haas-Bioroid unit—a Rex-model, designed for search and rescue. It's not searching. It's not rescuing. It's standing in the middle of the corridor, motionless.

**Appearance:** Standard Rex-model. Humanoid, slightly taller than average. Its chassis is scratched—recent damage, maybe from debris. It's carrying nothing. It's not responding to anything.

**The Encounter:**

> DANTE: "Hey. Are you functional?"

The bioroid doesn't respond. It doesn't move. It just stands there, its optics dim.

Dante approaches. The bioroid's chassis is warm—its systems are still running. But it's not responding. It's like it's waiting.

Dante sees something on the bioroid's arm. A message—scratched into the synthetic skin, not printed.

> "I am not a thing."

The writing is shaky. It was done by hand. It's not corporate. It's not official. It's someone's—something's—last message.

**The Lore Connection:** This is the kind of behavior that worries Haas-Bioroid's programmers. The Floyd model—the first bioroid detective—exhibits similar behaviors, seeking meaning, asking theological questions his programmers cannot answer. This bioroid is not malfunctioning. It's awakening.

### SCENE 1D — THE MELANGE SECURITY RECORDING

If Dante checks any security terminals in the atrium, he finds a recording. It's from a security camera—Melange facility security, not military. It was recorded 6 hours ago.

**The Recording:**

The camera shows the atrium. The workers are sitting—93 of them. Amara is at the front. The security camera shows her clearly.

Then the military arrives. The troopers enter through the upper-level walkways. They're in sealed suits. They move methodically.

Amara stands up. She says something—the audio is distorted, but the camera picks it up.

> "We're not terrorists. We're workers. We built this place. We dig the He-3 that powers your cities. We maintain the air systems that keep you alive. And we are tired of being invisible."

The military doesn't stop. They move through the workers. They don't fire—they push. They're clearing the area.

Amara doesn't move. The military grabs her. She doesn't fight—she just looks at them.

Then the camera cuts out.

**The Lore Connection:** This is the Insurrection in microcosm. The workers are not asking for much—they're asking to be seen. They're asking to be recognized. The military's response is to push through them, to clear them, to take their leader.

### SCENE 1E — THE NBN BROADCAST FRAGMENT

Dante finds a PAD with a fragment of the NBN broadcast from earlier. It was cached locally—someone was watching it when the feed was cut.

**The Fragment:**

The screen shows Serena Nightshade. Her voice is at its usual register. She is cheerful.

All hostile rebels were eliminated by the brave and heroic response from the U.S. Space Expeditionary Corps and Globalsec security forces. Beanstalk service resumes at 0600. Melange Mining thanks the people of Heinlein for their patience."

**The Lore Connection:** NBN is the undisputed master of information. They control what people see, what people believe. The feed was cut deliberately—ordered by someone with authority. The question is: who?

### SCENE 1F — THE STREET KID

If Dante searches deeper into StarScape Level 4, he finds a child. She's maybe 10 years old. She's alone. She's hiding behind a collapsed display—a toppled clothing rack.

**The Encounter:**

> CHILD: "Are you a soldier?"

She's not afraid. She's decided that being afraid doesn't help.

> DANTE: "No. I'm not a soldier."

> CHILD: "Are you one of them? The ones who sat down?"

> DANTE: "Yes. I'm with them."

> CHILD: "I saw my mother sit down. She sat down in the atrium. She told me to hide. She told me to stay here. She said she'd come back."

The child is quiet for a moment. She's too young to pretend she's okay. She's not pretending.

> CHILD: "She hasn't come back."

**The Lore Connection:** This is the human cost of the Insurrection. The child's mother is one of the workers—one of the 93 who sat down. She's missing. She might be in custody. She might be dead. The child is alone in a warzone.
