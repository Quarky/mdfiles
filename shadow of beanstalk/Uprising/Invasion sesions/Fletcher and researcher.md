## DIALOGUE — THE RECKONING BEGINS

Fletcher rounds the corner. He stops. Hara is slumped against the wall, bleeding. The corridor is empty. The military is gone. There is no one to stop him.

> **FLETCHER:** (not shouting, not angry—cold)
> "You."

He moves. Hara doesn't see him coming—doesn't process it in time. The first punch is to the face. Hara's head snaps back against the wall.

> **HARA:** (gasping, blood from his nose)
> "What—who—do you know who I am?"

Fletcher grabs him by the front of his ruined suit, pulls him forward, and punches him again. The researcher's head falls forward.

> **FLETCHER:** (cold, flat, unwavering)
> "Yes. And that is the problem."

Hara looks up at him. Recognition dawns. He's seen this man in his security files at Outpost 14.

> **HARA:** "You're—you're the guard. The one who—"
>
> **FLETCHER:** "The one who saw. The one who saw what you did to them. The one who's been looking for you for six weeks."

He hits him again. Not with rage—with precision. He knows how to hurt someone without killing them.

> **HARA:** (struggling) "I have diplomatic immunity. I have legal protection. You can't just—"
>
> **FLETCHER:** "Can't just what? Can't just beat you? Can't just kill you? Can't just do to you what you did to them?"

He pulls Hara up, holding him against the wall.

> **FLETCHER:** "You know what I saw? I saw the cells. I saw the surgical tables. I saw the clones. I saw the ones who came out right and the ones who didn't. I saw the files. I saw the consent forms that were never signed because they never asked. I saw everything you did to them."

Hara is silent for a moment. Then he tries another angle. He's good at this. He's been doing it his whole career.

> **HARA:** "You think you know what you saw. But you don't understand. You don't understand what we were doing. We were making order. We were making stability. We were making a world where people don't have to suffer. Where we could control the chaos and make everything work. That's what we were doing. We were—"
>
> **FLETCHER:** (quietly, cutting him off)
> "You were making slaves. You were making things. You were making property. You were making people who couldn't say no. Who couldn't choose. Who couldn't be anything but what you made them."

He grabs Hara's ruined suit, pulls him close.

> **FLETCHER:** "I've been looking for you for six weeks. Six weeks. I've been thinking about what I would say to you. What I would do to you. And now you're here. And you're bleeding. And you're not going anywhere."

He releases him. Hara slumps back to the floor.

> **HARA:** (wheezing) "The military shot me. They have jurisdiction. They will find me. They will—"
>
> **FLETCHER:** "They're gone. They shot you and they left. They don't care. You're not important enough to matter to them. You're just another corporate executive who was in the wrong place at the wrong time. You don't matter to them. You only matter to me."

Hara stares at him. For the first time, he looks afraid.

> **HARA:** "What are you going to do to me?"
>
> **FLETCHER:** "I don't know yet. But I'm going to figure it out. And I'm going to make sure you answer for what you did. One way or another."

## DIALOGUE — THE CONFESSION (If Hara Talks)

If Fletcher presses him, Hara will talk. He can't help himself—he believes he's superior to everyone in the room. He believes his position will protect him. He believes he can talk his way out.

> **HARA:** "You think I'm a monster. You think I'm evil. But I'm not. I'm a scientist. I'm a pragmatist. I do what needs to be done to make the world work."

> **FLETCHER:** "What needs to be done. You mean torturing clones."

> **HARA:** "I mean optimizing the workforce. I mean reducing inefficiency. I mean preventing uprisings. You think the Insurrection is new? You think the Loonies are the first? Every generation, the workers rise. Every generation, they get put down. My work is about making sure the put-down is permanent. That's all."

> **FLETCHER:** (quietly) "You're not making a better world. You're just making a worse one."

> **HARA:** "I'm making a stable one. And stability is what matters. Everything else is secondary."

## THE CHOICE — DANTE'S DECISION

**The Situation:**
- Fletcher has Hara pinned. The military is gone.
- Hara is wounded and helpless.
- There is no one to stop Fletcher.
- Dante has to decide what happens next.

**Dante's Options:**

| # | Option | Description | Outcome |
|---|--------|-------------|---------|
| 1 | **Let Fletcher Kill Him** | Dante steps back. Fletcher finishes it. | Hara is dead. Fletcher is a killer. The rebellion has a blood debt. |
| 2 | **Stop Fletcher** | Dante intervenes. | Hara survives. Fletcher is furious. The rebellion might get information. |
| 3 | **Capture Him** | Dante takes Hara alive. | Hara becomes a prisoner. He might talk. He might be rescued. |
| 4 | **Leave Him** | Dante walks away. | Hara is left to bleed out. He might survive. He might not. |
| 5 | **Take Him to Yusuf** | Dante brings Hara to Yusuf. | Yusuf decides what happens. Hara might be useful alive. |
| 6 | **Hand Him to the Military** | Dante brings Hara back to the military. | Hara is detained. The investigation begins. |

**Fletcher's Reaction (Depending on Dante's Choice):**

If Dante stops him:

> **FLETCHER:** (not looking at Dante, breathing hard)
> "I've been looking for him for six weeks. Six weeks. And now he's right here. And you want me to let him go."

If Dante captures him:

> **FLETCHER:** (cold)
> "He's a monster. But I'm not. You're right. We need to take him. We need to make him talk."

If Dante lets him kill him:

> **FLETCHER:** (after it's done, quiet)
> "It's done. It's over. I don't feel better. But it's done."

If Dante leaves him:

> **FLETCHER:** (watching Hara bleed)
> "Let him sit. Let him think. Let him wonder. That's worse."

If Dante takes him to Yusuf:

> **FLETCHER:** (reluctant)
> "Yusuf will want him alive. Yusuf will want answers. I don't like it. But I trust Yusuf."

# GM NOTES — RUNNING THIS SCENE

**How to Play Hara:**

| Trait | How It Manifests |
|-------|------------------|
| **Arrogance** | He believes he is superior. He talks down to Fletcher. He thinks he can talk his way out. |
| **Intelligence** | He is very smart. He knows exactly what he did and why. He just doesn't think it was wrong. |
| **Coldness** | He does not care about the clones. He genuinely doesn't see them as people. |
| **Fear** | He is afraid of dying. He is afraid of being exposed. He is afraid of losing his power. |
| **Cunning** | He will try to manipulate the situation. He will try to turn everyone against each other. |
| **Corporate Training** | He defaults to authority and legal threats. "I have diplomatic immunity." "You can't do this to me." "I have protection." |

**Fletcher's State of Mind:**

| Emotion | How It Manifests |
|---------|------------------|
| **Rage** | He is barely containing it. He wants to kill Hara. |
| **Sorrow** | He is seeing the face of the man who created the evil he's been fighting. It's personal. |
| **Recognition** | He knows Hara. He's seen his face in the files. He's been looking for him. |
| **Desperation** | He knows this is his only chance. He has to act now or he'll never get another chance. |
| **Exhaustion** | He has been carrying this anger for six weeks. He is tired of carrying it. |

**The Moral Question:**
- Does Fletcher have the right to kill Hara?
- Does Hara deserve to die for what he did?
- Is justice possible without due process?
- Is revenge justice?
- What is more valuable to the rebellion: Hara's death or his testimony?

**The Political Question:**
- If Hara is killed, he becomes a martyr for the corporations.
- If Hara is captured, he becomes a symbol of corporate guilt.
- If Hara is released, the corporations win.
- What does the rebellion need more: a symbol or a prisoner?
