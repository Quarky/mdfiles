# SESSION ATMOSPHERE — THE KAGUYA THAT EXISTS NOW
# ═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

The Kaguya of 12 hours ago—the tourist paradise, the duty-free shopping, the families taking photos with Earth in the background—no longer exists. What exists is a facility under siege, transformed.

## WHAT DANTE SEES (Exiting NBN Facility)

### The Outer Ring
- **Smoke.** Multiple fires burning in StarScape retail levels. The automated suppression systems have failed in 60% of the sectors. The air is thick with particulates.
- **Damage.** Transplas panels cracked, some breached. Emergency seals glowing amber on 30% of visible corridors. Emergency lighting in most sectors.
- **People.** Not many. Those who remain move with purpose—carrying supplies, weapons, wounded. Small groups. No crowds. The tourists are gone. The civilians who didn't get out are in shelters.
- **Military presence.** Visible on the crater rim. Dropships cycling between orbit and surface. Drones scanning every approach. Exosuits at the choke points. They haven't pushed into the interior yet—they're waiting for something.
- **Globalsec.** Fractured. Some units have integrated with military command. Others are holding their own positions, not sure which orders to follow. Some have abandoned their posts entirely.

### The Soundscape
- The constant hum of the Beanpole is gone. It hasn't been running for 8 hours.
- What remains: distant gunfire, echoing through the dome structure. The particular sound of laser weapons—sharp, clean, final.
- The sound of emergency systems running. Backup generators. Ventilation fans.
- Voices. Shouted orders. Sometimes screaming.
- And beneath it all: the sound of the facility's structure groaning—damage to the dome integrity, pressure differentials, structural stress.

### The Smell
- Smoke, first and always.
- Ozone—the signature of weapons fire in a sealed environment.
- Hydraulic fluid from damaged systems.
- Something else—the faint sharp smell of fear. It's not a physical smell. But it's present.

# MILITARY TACTICS — THE ADVANCED REFERENCE
# ═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

The SXC is using doctrine designed for lunar combat. It was developed for this environment but never tested live. The gap between doctrine and reality is where the Loonies can exploit.

## TACTICAL PATTERN: CONTROLLED PAIR ADVANCE

**How It Works:**
- Two fire teams (4 troopers total) advance in staggered formation
- Team A moves 10 meters, covers while Team B moves
- Each team watches the other's blind spots
- Never more than 2 troopers visible from any angle

**What It Looks Like to the Loonies:**
- They can't engage the entire team at once
- If they fire on Team A, Team B has clear line of sight
- The advance is slow but methodical
- Every corridor takes 8-10 minutes to clear

**The Gap:**
- It was designed for vacuum and zero-G
- Kaguya is pressurized with corridors
- The spacing is too wide for the narrow maintenance tunnels
- The Loony fighters know this and are using it

## TACTICAL PATTERN: DRONE SCREEN

**How It Works:**
- EWS drone enters the corridor first
- Full sensor scan (heat, motion, acoustic, visual)
- Transmits to the team before they advance
- Team moves based on what the drone sees

**What It Looks Like to the Loonies:**
- The drone enters with a sound like a large insect
- Amber light pulsing
- It hovers at head height and looks at everything
- Then the troopers come around the corner in perfect formation
- They already know exactly where everyone is standing

**The Gap:**
- The drone scans for heat, motion, and acoustic signatures
- It doesn't recognize the sound of a maintenance hatch being opened
- It doesn't know which corridors are sealed and which are not
- The Loony fighters know where the hatches are and use them

**Counter-Tactic:**
- When the drone enters, do nothing
- Let it scan you
- Then move before the team comes around the corner
- The drone's data is 40 seconds old by the time the troopers arrive
- The position it reported is empty
- The fighters are behind them through a maintenance hatch

## TACTICAL PATTERN: BOUNDING OVERWATCH

**How It Works:**
- One fire team provides covering fire
- The second team moves to a new position
- Then the first team moves while the second covers
- Always one team in cover, one team moving

**What It Looks Like to the Loonies:**
- There are always troopers firing
- There are always troopers moving
- It's impossible to engage them all at once

**The Gap:**
- Requires significant open space
- Kaguya's corridors are narrow
- The covering fire team can't get clean shots if civilians are in the way
- The military doesn't stop for civilians—but they do have to identify them

## TACTICAL PATTERN: DECOMPRESSION PROCEDURE

**How It Works:**
- If a decompression event occurs, the military advances through it
- Their suits are sealed
- They have 4 hours of oxygen
- The decompression doesn't affect them

**What It Looks Like to the Loonies:**
- The military doesn't stop when the air runs out
- They don't slow down
- They just advance through it

**The Gap:**
- Loonies know which seals are degraded
- They know how to trigger a decompression event without losing their own people
- If they can get their people into hardsuits, they can fight in the decompressed section

# LOONY COUNTER-TACTICS — THE ADVANCED REFERENCE
# ═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

The Loonies built this place. They know where every maintenance hatch goes. They know which corridor sections have degraded atmo seals. They know which checkpoints Globalsec never properly staffed. They know these things the way you know your own house in the dark.

## TACTICAL PATTERN: THE MAINTENANCE NETWORK

**How It Works:**
- The service corridor network connects every dome
- Maintenance hatches every 50 meters
- Unlisted routes that don't appear on any tactical document
- Some routes are partially sealed, requiring cutting or crawling

**What It Means for the Loonies:**
- They can move anywhere the military can't
- They can flank any position
- They can appear behind the military's advance
- They can withdraw when the military is closing in

**The Military's Response:**
- They have thermal imaging
- They can see heat signatures through walls
- They know where the Loonies are in the maintenance tunnels
- They can't shoot through the walls—but they can position fire teams at every exit

**Counter-Tactic:**
- Move in small groups
- Use heat-shielding materials
- Don't stay in one place
- The military knows where they are—they don't know where they're going

## TACTICAL PATTERN: THE DEGRADED SEAL TRAP

**How It Works:**
- The Loonies know which corridor sections have degraded atmo seals
- They know how to create a controlled decompression event
- They can depressurize a corridor between the military's position and the Loonies'
- The military advances through it—but it buys time

**What It Means for the Loonies:**
- They can slow the military's advance
- They can create choke points
- They can force the military into positions where civilians are in the way

**The Military's Response:**
- They advance through it anyway
- Their suits are sealed
- The decompression doesn't affect them

**Counter-Tactic:**
- Don't use it to stop them. Use it to slow them.
- Every minute they spend in the decompressed corridor is a minute the Loonies have to move.
- The decompression triggers emergency seals—which also slow the military's advance.

## TACTICAL PATTERN: THE CIVILIAN SHIELD

**How It Works:**
- Not a tactic the Loonies want to use
- But civilians are present in every corridor
- The military doesn't stop for civilians—but they do have to identify them
- In the compression zone, civilians and fighters are mixed

**What It Means for the Loonies:**
- If they can keep civilians between them and the military, the military has to identify them before firing
- This costs time
- Time is the only thing they have

**The Military's Response:**
- They are not restricted from firing in civilian areas
- But they don't want to create a massacre that will be broadcast
- So they identify targets before firing
- This costs time

**Counter-Tactic:**
- Move civilians through the maintenance network
- Separate them from the fighters
- Let the civilians leave the combat zone
- This gives the military clean targets—but it also removes the civilians from danger
