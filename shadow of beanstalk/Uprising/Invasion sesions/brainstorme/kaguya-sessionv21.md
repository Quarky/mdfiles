STARPORT KAGUYA — PHYSICAL LAYOUT
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

Kaguya sits in the Hypatia-C Crater — 15 kilometers in diameter, the rim
a natural outer wall that gives the spaceport its defining silhouette.
Arrivals and departures equal the busiest Earth airports. Bioroid work crews
guided by suited engineers cycle endlessly through mechanical checks between
the relentless docking and launch schedules. The upper launch levels thrum
constantly with activity.

The Tourism Board has spent years making this place look like a welcome mat.
It does not look like a battleground. It is about to become one.

ZONES IN DETAIL

LAUNCH CORE
  The Beanstalk's lunar terminus. Beanpods — elevator cars that travel
  the length of the Beanstalk to GEO and down to New Angeles — dock and
  depart on a continuous schedule. The core is sealed, pressurized,
  maximum security. Heinlein Authority runs departure clearance.
  Globalsec runs physical security. Two layers of biometric verification
  before boarding. A third layer of identity check at the pod itself.
  This is System 03 from the transit network map — the Departure Clearance
  Hub, the golden objective that adds passenger slots and suppresses
  biometric flagging.

  Getting anyone on a beanpod without authorization during an active
  security incident requires either the golden objective or something
  the golden objective can't provide: an authorized person inside
  Heinlein Authority who looks the other way. Deliberately.

INNER DOME CLUSTER — LANDING DOMES
  Five major landing domes arranged in a rough pentagon around the launch core.
  Each dome handles different traffic:
  · DOME ALPHA: Corporate charter arrivals/departures. Melange. Haas-Bioroid.
    Jinteki. Weyland. Each corp has a private terminal section.
  · DOME BETA: Commercial shuttle traffic. Earth-Kaguya tourist runs.
    The Challenger Memorial Ferry docks here.
  · DOME GAMMA: Cargo. He-3 canister transfer from lunar mag-lev to
    orbital freight pods. The mag-lev terminus for Heinlein's He-3 export
    infrastructure terminates in this dome. This is the strategic chokepoint
    Sigg's faction is targeting.
  · DOME DELTA: Heinlein Authority operations hub. Traffic control, departure
    scheduling, security coordination, the server infrastructure that runs
    the departure clearance system. System 03 from the transit network lives here.
  · DOME EPSILON: Emergency services, medical, and the Heinlein Authority
    security garrison. The garrison is larger than it looks from public areas —
    two full Globalsec response companies and a Heinlein Authority rapid
    response team are based here.

INNER DOME CLUSTER — CONNECTIONS
  All five domes connect via sealed pressurized transit corridors at two levels:
  · UPPER LEVEL: Public passenger transit. Moving walkways.
    Bioroid work crews. Under normal operations, constant foot traffic.
  · LOWER LEVEL: Cargo transit. Maintenance. Service infrastructure.
    The He-3 canister conveyor from Dome Gamma runs through here.
    Less monitored. Narrower. The approach route the Loonies use.

OUTER DOME RING
  Dozens of hexagonal domes spreading from the inner cluster to the crater rim.
  Organized in concentric rings outward:
  · RING 1: StarScape Shopping. Duty-free retail paradise.
    Hundreds of levels. Constant civilian foot traffic.
    The most crowded space in Kaguya. Terrible for controlled movement.
    Excellent for disappearing.
  · RING 2: Hotels. The extravagant ones face inward toward the launch core.
    The reasonably priced ones face outward toward the crater rim.
    Viewing decks. Recreation parks. Half the month you can see Earth.
  · RING 3: Low-G sports facilities. The Angel Arena equivalent.
    G-ball courts. Aero hockey. Low-ceiling areas with unusual geometry.
    Emergency atmo systems in here are separate from the main facility —
    a tactical consideration if someone needs to create a localized event.
  · RING 4 (outermost): The crater rim structures. Emergency surface access.
    The Treaty of Heinlein is displayed here in its transplas tomb just
    outside the main shuttle terminus. The Tourism Board has a display
    about it. It diplomatically omits the Battle of Kaguya.
    The private landing pads maintained by corps and wealthy individuals
    are out here — separate from the main station, their own airlocks.

TUBE-LEV TERMINUS
  The tube-lev from Heinlein terminates at the outer dome ring's eastern edge.
  This is how most workers and Docklands residents arrive at Kaguya.
  It is also how Sigg's main force arrives — in plain clothes, in groups
  of 3-4, spread across six consecutive tube-lev cars over two hours.
  Metzger re-activated the tube-lev specifically to enable predictable
  civilian movement. It also enabled this.

THE LOONY PLAN — SIGG'S OBJECTIVES
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

Sigg's plan is not to hold Kaguya. He knows they can't hold Kaguya.
The plan is to demonstrate that they can shut it down — that the He-3
trade, the Beanstalk traffic, the entire economic lifeline of Heinlein
can be interrupted by people who have had enough. Force negotiation
or force the world to watch the corps refuse to negotiate.

OBJECTIVE 1 — DOME GAMMA NETWORK SEIZURE
  Lock the He-3 cargo routing system. Shut down the mag-lev terminus
  and the orbital freight pod loading. No He-3 leaves the Moon
  until someone talks.
  DANTE'S ROLE: The network run. This is what Sigg needs him for.

OBJECTIVE 2 — DOME DELTA COMMS SEIZURE
  Get into Heinlein Authority's departure clearance system.
  Freeze all departures. Nothing leaves via the Beanstalk.
  Corp executives who came to Kaguya to evacuate before things
  got worse find themselves unable to leave.
  DANTE'S ROLE: Also this. The two systems are adjacent.
  Running both in sequence is the session's core network challenge.

OBJECTIVE 3 — STARSCAPE OCCUPATION
  A hundred Loony workers occupy StarScape Shopping's main atrium.
  Peaceful. Visible. On every camera in the facility.
  This is the image Sigg wants on the NBN feed — workers sitting
  in the tourist paradise they built and were never allowed to visit.
  DANTE'S ROLE: This is happening in the background. He can't be
  in two places but what happens here affects what he's doing elsewhere.

OBJECTIVE 4 — THE BROADCAST
  Sigg has a prepared statement. Video. The Section F evidence
  from Petra's story embedded in it. The human cost laid out
  plainly, without rhetoric. He intends to push it through
  Kaguya's internal broadcast system and simultaneously
  inject it into the outpost PAD-to-PAD network.
  If Dante seized Dome Delta's comms in Objective 2 he can
  also push this through the Authority's uplink.
  DANTE'S ROLE: Optional. High risk. Getting this onto the
  Authority uplink means it goes to SYNC's Network before
  NBN can suppress it. For about four minutes.

OPERATION FALLING STAR — METZGER'S RESPONSE
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

Metzger's 24-hour planning window produced a layered response.
It activates in stages. Dante can see each stage coming if he's
watching the right systems — and has minutes to react before each one.

FALLING STAR STAGE 1 — CORDON (Hour 0, simultaneous with Loony arrival)
  Armstrong Base orbital platforms reposition over Kaguya.
  Their weapons can hit surface targets. They are targeting
  the crater rim — specifically the private landing pads
  in Ring 4. Nothing leaves Kaguya via the rim.
  Li Wei's hopper, if it was staged there, is now a problem.

  Globalsec seals all tube-lev connections from Heinlein.
  Nobody new enters. Nobody leaves.
  The workers already in Kaguya — Sigg's people, civilians,
  tourists, corp staff — are now all inside together.

  DANTE SEES THIS AS: The tube-lev departure board going blank
  simultaneously. Every slot. At once. Not a malfunction.

FALLING STAR STAGE 2 — CONTAINMENT (Hour 1-3)
  SXC rapid insertion teams arrive via orbital dropship.
  Not through the Beanstalk — from orbit directly.
  They come in on the crater's far side, away from civilian areas.
  Fully equipped. EWS support active — military ICE goes live
  on every Kaguya network simultaneously. This is what RAIDER
  and BLACKOUT were warming up for.

  The EWS doesn't hit Dante's run directly at first — they're
  focused on locking down Kaguya's own broadcast systems
  to prevent Sigg's statement from going wider. But they
  are in the network. Dante can feel them the way you feel
  someone else in a dark room — the ICE behaves differently,
  the response times shift, things that should be open
  are suddenly occupied by something that doesn't
  announce itself.

  Globalsec + Starshield begin clearing the outer dome ring
  inward. StarScape first. The occupation there doesn't last.
  It lasts long enough to be photographed.

FALLING STAR STAGE 3 — COMPRESSION (Hour 3-6)
  SXC teams push inward from the rim. Globalsec pushes outward
  from the inner dome cluster. The Loonies in the middle are
  being compressed into the space between.

  This is where it goes wrong in ways Sigg didn't plan for.
  The Loony workers in the compression zone are not all Sigg's
  fighters. Some of them are just workers who came to Kaguya
  on the tube-lev that morning because they had business here.
  The cordon doesn't distinguish. The compression doesn't distinguish.
  Several airlocks in the connecting corridors between inner and
  outer domes are sealed by automated security responses — some
  by Globalsec, some triggered by the EWS network interference
  disrupting the atmo management system.

  In one of the connecting corridors between Dome Beta and
  Ring 1 StarScape, a section decompresses partially.
  Not catastrophically — emergency seals activate.
  But the people in that corridor have eleven seconds
  of bad air before the seals close.
  Some of them are Sigg's people.
  Some of them are tourists.

  This is the moment the battle stops being a demonstration
  and becomes the thing history will call the Battle of Kaguya.

FALLING STAR STAGE 4 — THE BROADCAST (Hour 4-5)
  Regardless of whether Dante pushed Sigg's statement
  through the Authority uplink, something goes out.
  NBN has been broadcasting from Kaguya since Stage 1.
  Their footage is controlled — tourist areas, the occupation
  in StarScape looking chaotic and frightening, SXC teams
  looking disciplined and protective.

  Then: a cut. Four minutes where the signal does something
  it wasn't supposed to do. What goes out in those four minutes
  depends on what Dante did with Dome Delta's comms system.

  DANTE SEIZED DELTA AND PUSHED SIGG'S STATEMENT:
    The Section F footage. The Jinteki serial numbers.
    Sigg talking plainly about what the workers are asking for.
    The worker death toll including the clone workers.
    Four minutes on SYNC's Network before NBN reasserts control.
    In four minutes it reaches every PAD on the Moon.
    In four minutes it is cached in a thousand places
    NBN cannot reach. It will matter. Later.

  DANTE DIDN'T REACH DELTA IN TIME:
    The four-minute cut is dead air. A technical failure.
    NBN attributes it to the network interference from the battle.
    Sigg's statement goes out on the PAD-to-PAD network only.
    Slower. Patchier. Still there. Still spreading.
    But NBN controls the first draft of what happened today.

FALLING STAR STAGE 5 — RESOLUTION (Hour 5-8)
  The SXC does not announce victory. It announces
  "the successful neutralization of hostile elements
  threatening the security of Starport Kaguya and
  the Beanstalk infrastructure."

  The Loony fighters who can be found are detained.
  The ones who can't be found are the ones who had
  a way out that wasn't through the crater rim.

  The broadcast comes. Serena Nightshade is back.
  Her voice is at its usual register. She is cheerful.

  "Starport Kaguya is fully operational following
   the successful suppression of a terrorist attack
   by anti-colonial extremists. All hostile rebels
   were eliminated by the brave and heroic response
   from the U.S. Space Expeditionary Corps and
   Globalsec security forces. Beanstalk service
   resumes at 0600. Melange Mining thanks the
   people of Heinlein for their patience."

  The screen shows the crater. It looks like a spaceport.

PHASE 1 — ARRIVAL & SETUP (pre-Hour 0)
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Dante arrives at Kaguya ahead of Sigg's main force — he needs to
be in position before Stage 1 locks the tube-lev. He comes in as
a legitimate traveler. His cover: a runner contracted for a routine
corporate network audit. He has forged credentials. If Act II gear
included Ghost Protocol this is where it buys him the entry scan.

His first task is finding a splice point in the lower service
corridor network — the maintenance level below the inner dome
cluster's transit corridors. This is where he can access both
Dome Gamma (He-3 cargo) and Dome Delta (Authority comms) from
a single physical location.

WHAT KAGUYA LOOKS LIKE BEFORE IT STARTS:
  Normal. Aggressively normal. Tourist families in the outer ring.
  StarScape is busy. A school group from Earth is doing a low-G
  tour of the sports facilities. Two Jinteki reps in grey are
  at Dome Alpha's corporate terminal. A bioroid crew is doing
  maintenance checks on a shuttle docking collar.
  The Treaty of Heinlein is in its transplas tomb outside
  the main terminus. Someone has left flowers at its base.
  Real flowers. Grown on the Moon. Very expensive.
  Nobody is looking at it.

SPLICE POINT LOCATION:
  Service corridor junction SC-7 — lower level, between
  Dome Gamma and Dome Delta, accessible from the maintenance
  crawl network that parallels the cargo conveyor.
  The crawl has one Globalsec camera, facing the wrong direction.
  It has been facing the wrong direction for at least six months.
  This is in the maintenance log that nobody reads.

PHASE 3 — WHILE DANTE RUNS: THE PHYSICAL BATTLE
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

While Dante is in the network, this is happening around him.
He cannot control it. He can be aware of it. It creates time pressure
and physical complications for his splice point.

HOUR 0-1: Sigg's people occupy StarScape atrium. The image Sigg wanted —
  workers sitting in the tourist paradise — is real for 47 minutes.
  Tourists evacuate. Some of them stop to watch. One of them is recording
  on a PAD. This recording will matter more than the 47 minutes did.
  The tube-lev goes blank.

HOUR 1-2: Globalsec begins clearing StarScape from the outer ring inward.
  Sigg's fighters fall back toward the inner dome cluster.
  The lower service corridor where Dante is spliced becomes
  a transit route for Loony fighters moving between positions.
  Three of them pass SC-7 while Dante is running System A.
  One of them stops and looks at him. Dante looks back.
  She moves on. This is the closest combat gets to him — this session.

HOUR 2-3: SXC dropships come in on the far crater side.
  The sound reaches Dante through the structure — a deep resonance
  in the corridor walls that doesn't match any mechanical system.
  This is what Falling Star Stage 2 feels like from the inside.
  It feels like the building making a noise it shouldn't make.

HOUR 3: The connecting corridor between Dome Beta and Ring 1 decompresses
  partially. Emergency seals activate. Dante's network run is not
  directly affected — but SC-7 is two corridors from that junction.
  The atmo management system in the lower level shifts into emergency mode.
  His splice point's power supply flickers for 4 seconds.
  Everything he's done in System A holds. System B's ICE reacts to the
  power fluctuation by temporarily downgrading one tier — an unintended
  gap that lasts approximately 90 seconds.
  Whether Dante catches this window determines how System B ends.

HOUR 3-4: The compression zone forms. The middle ring of Kaguya — between
  Globalsec coming inward and SXC coming outward — fills with a mixture
  of Loony fighters, Loony workers who aren't fighters, and civilians
  who couldn't evacuate in time. Nobody in the compression zone is
  distinguishable from the outside. The SXC doesn't try very hard
  to distinguish. Globalsec tries. Somewhat.

PHASE 4 — THE EXIT (Hour 4+)
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

The exit from SC-7 — once Dante is done running — is not the way he came in.
The lower service corridors are now active military space.
His options depend on what he accomplished and what he carried in from Act II.

THE OPTIONS:

OPTION A — THE BEANSTALK (if golden objective reached in System B)
  Dante assigned himself and up to 3 others to a departure slot.
  Biometric suppression active. He walks to the launch core
  through the maintenance level, bypassing the public corridors
  where the SXC is operating.
  The launch core is the one place in Kaguya that Metzger
  is keeping clear — she needs the Beanstalk operational.
  A person with a valid slot and clean biometrics
  walks onto a beanpod and ascends.
  The Moon shrinks to a grey disk below.
  The broadcast is running before the pod clears the crater.

OPTION B — CREW MANIFEST (if Departure Slot Control reached)
  Dante is on the crew manifest for a service shuttle —
  not a Beanstalk pod. A surface-to-orbit hop to a
  commercial station that isn't under Metzger's direct
  authority. From there: a freight run, a corp transport,
  anything that moves. Less clean. It works.

OPTION C — LI WEI'S HOPPER (if contacted in Session 3)
  Li Wei said 36 hours. The hopper is staged in a private
  pad on the crater rim — Ring 4. The orbital platforms
  can see it. They are watching it. But Metzger's rules of
  engagement don't cover a Chinese government-registered
  hopper sitting legally in a private pad until it fires its
  engines. When it fires, it has approximately 90 seconds
  before the orbital platforms reclassify it.
  Li Wei says 90 seconds is enough.
  Li Wei has done this before.

OPTION D — NO EXIT
  If none of the above were prepared: Dante is inside a
  military cordon at a spaceport. He has a runner's skills
  and no authorized departure. He can move through the
  lower service corridors indefinitely — the EWS is focused
  on the network, not the maintenance crawls. But eventually
  the cordon tightens. Eventually every maintenance hatch
  is checked. This is how the session ends on a dark note
  that's darker than planned. Or it's how Dante decides
  to stay and help — moving people through the maintenance
  network to departure points while the broadcast runs.
  Choosing to help others out before himself costs him his
  own departure window. Whether the arithmetic works out
  is luck, which is exactly how it should be.
