500 million credits is not a black market shopping trip. It is a geopolitical event that happens to look like a purchase order.

At this scale the Loonies are not buying weapons from a Docklands fixer. They are placing orders through shell companies, paying through layered intermediaries, and sourcing from suppliers who ask no questions because they have been paid not to have questions. The logistics took six weeks. Yusuf ran it. He is very good at this.

After the transaction costs — the shell companies, the intermediaries, the safe storage, the transport from Heinlein to Kaguya hidden in legitimate cargo manifests — the operational budget for the Kaguya action is approximately 380 million credits. The remainder funds the broader insurrection infrastructure that continues after Kaguya regardless of outcome.

500 million credits draws serious professional attention. The political filter removes almost all of it.

WHO SAYS NO: Every established prisec firm — Globalsec, Starshield, Argus, NetSec. They are either directly contracted to the corps or dependent on corp contracts for their next job. Taking the Loony side ends their business. Any mercenary with a public profile. Being identified as the person who fought the SXC at Kaguya is not a career enhancement. Anyone who believes the SXC will win quickly and cleanly — because they will, and being on the losing side of a 24-hour operation is worse than not being paid.

WHO SAYS YES: A very specific type. The book describes them precisely: veterans of the Lunar Insurrection and Martian Colony Wars, augmented beyond their natural prime, paying off their g-mods and cybernetics. People for whom the political calculation is either personal or irrelevant. Specifically:

TYPE 1 — THE LUNAR VETERAN WHO NEVER LEFT Fought in the original Insurrection. Was on the wrong side then or the right side and lost either way. Has been on the Moon since. Takes jobs from anyone with credits. Has no illusions about outcome. Has enough illusions about principle to still be here.

TYPE 2 — XIANGONG REMNANTS Chinese veterans of the War, or descendants. Operating through Li Wei's network without Li Wei formally acknowledging it. Their political motivation is not the Loonies' politics — it is the specific anti-U.S. anti-corp alignment that makes an SXC embarrassment feel like a victory regardless of outcome.

TYPE 3 — THE MERCENARY WITH NOTHING LEFT TO LOSE Usually means debts. Sometimes means something else. The g-mod payments, the cybernetic maintenance costs — some of these people are not choosing this job. They are choosing it over defaulting on loans held by people who are significantly worse than the SXC.

TYPE 4 — ORGCRIME FIGHTERS (KATALA BROTHERS AND ADJACENT) The Katala Brothers on Luna watch the insurrection the same way orgcrime always watches political upheaval: as a business environment. They do not formally join. But individual fighters within their network take private contracts. At this rate of pay some of them are very privately contracted. They have no politics. They have rates. The Loonies are paying the rate.

HOW MANY: Approximately 35 mercenaries take the full Kaguya contract. Another 20 take partial contracts — specific objectives, specific exits, specific rates for specific hours. This is not an army. It is a professional force multiplier inside an operation that is primarily run by workers. The workers and the mercenaries do not entirely trust each other. Sigg manages this tension by keeping them in separate roles.

At 380 million operational budget the constraint is not money. The constraint is what can be physically moved into Kaguya without triggering automated manifest scanning, what can be operated inside a pressurized facility without catastrophic structural risk, and what the fighters can actually use effectively.

Yusuf's procurement principle: buy what the people you have can use. A micro-missile launcher in the hands of someone who has never fired one is not an asset. It is a liability with a blast radius.
