
# THE NBN HACK — DURING THE BATTLE OF KAGUYA

**The miners are fighting outside. Dante goes in.**

---

## THE SITUATION OUTSIDE

The NBN Heinlein Broadcast Facility is in Lunacent — seven blocks from the Columbiad Arcology. Kaguya is the Beanstalk terminus across the crater. The tube-lev that connects them has been running all morning moving Zane's people into position.

Right now, at this moment, the miners are fighting Globalsec and the U.S. military at Kaguya. The battle is happening. The He-3 cargo is locked. The Beanstalk is frozen. Zane is holding a junction with whatever he has left.

And in Lunacent, seven blocks away, the NBN broadcast facility is in full operational crisis mode.

Because the battle is their story. And they are covering it live.

---

## WHAT THE BUILDING LOOKS LIKE RIGHT NOW

The NBN facility is not calm. It is the opposite of calm. It is calm the way a machine running at 200% capacity is calm — everything moving fast, everything purposeful, the appearance of order masking the specific controlled panic of people doing the most important broadcast of their careers.

Every screen in the lobby is live Kaguya footage. The building's exterior screens — the ones that normally cycle through cheerful Heinlein tourism content — are showing the crater from the orbital camera that NBN pre-positioned two days ago. Someone planned for this. Someone at NBN knew the crater was going to be a news event.

The lobby is empty of visitors. The tour scheduled for today was cancelled this morning. Security reasons, the automated message said. No further explanation.

Three NBN security staff are in the lobby. They are watching the screens. One of them has been watching the screens for forty minutes without looking away. He is from Heinlein. He grew up in the lower habitation sections. He recognizes some of what he's seeing from where he grew up. He does not know what to do with this.

The front door is locked from inside. A standard emergency protocol — activated when the building goes to crisis broadcast mode. Nobody comes in. Nobody goes out without authorization.

---

## THE MINERS OUTSIDE — PRYCEK'S FORCE

Prycek is not Zane. He is one of the secondary faction leaders — a Docklands organizer who came up through the 14K network before breaking with them over politics that didn't align with what he found in the He-3 fields. He has twenty-three fighters with him. They are not trying to get into the NBN building.

They are trying to hold the Lunacent arterial corridor against the Globalsec response force that was diverted from the inner Heinlein security network when the Kaguya operation began. Globalsec pulled units from Lunacent to reinforce Kaguya. Prycek's job: make sure they can't pull more. Make sure the corridor between Lunacent and the tube-lev terminal stays contested enough that Globalsec is watching it instead of watching anything else.

His secondary objective — which he was given by Yusuf three days ago and which he has not shared with his fighters — is to make enough noise in front of the NBN building that nobody inside it is thinking about what might be happening on Level 5.

He doesn't know why. He trusts Yusuf. He has his twenty-three fighters and a plan and a lot of the equipment the He-3 money bought. He is making noise.

## THE SOUND OF THE BATTLE — WHAT DANTE HEARS FROM INSIDE

Heinlein's tunnels carry sound in specific ways. The pressurized corridors act like acoustic pipes — a weapons discharge three blocks away arrives as a low-frequency pressure event, felt in the chest, a fraction of a second after the sound itself.

From inside the NBN building Dante hears the battle outside as a series of these pressure events. Irregular. Unpredictable. Each one arrives during his network run and requires a specific kind of discipline — the discipline of knowing that the sound means people are fighting and dying and that stopping the run to find out which people helps no one and costs everything.

The building shakes slightly at minute 12 of the run. Not a weapon — a stun grenade detonating against a corridor wall three blocks away. The acoustic wave travels through the structure. A server rack in Level 5 rattles. A cooling fan changes pitch. Dante's splice connection flickers for 2 seconds.

The building shakes again at minute 34. This one is different — heavier, more resonant. A synap pistol bank discharge across a Lunacent checkpoint. The NBN security staff on Level 1 are now all watching the screens and the door simultaneously.

At minute 51: silence. The specific silence that follows a sustained engagement when both sides have pulled back to regroup. In Heinlein's tunnels, silence travels the same way sound does. Dante feels it as a change in the building's ambient pressure. Something stopped outside. He doesn't know what stopped. He can't know. He keeps running.

## HOW DANTE GETS IN — THE BATTLE CREATES THE DOOR

The emergency protocol that locked the front door also activated the building's emergency ventilation procedure — a standard Heinlein safety measure that opens secondary atmospheric equalization hatches throughout the structure when a pressure event is detected in adjacent corridors.

The stun grenade at minute 12 qualified as a pressure event.

Hatch EV-7 — on the building's south face, at sublevel 1, designed for atmospheric equalization during corridor pressure events — opened automatically at the detection trigger and has not closed because the automated reset requires a facilities manager to physically confirm the all-clear from inside the building.

The facilities manager is on Level 3 watching the Kaguya feed on every screen and has not thought about hatch EV-7.

The hatch is 0.8 meters wide. It opens onto a maintenance crawl that connects to the building's environmental system network. The environmental system network connects to every level. Including Level 5.

This is not a coincidence. Yusuf planned this. Yusuf planned it by knowing that Heinlein's standard safety protocols would open exactly this hatch in exactly this situation, because Yusuf has been on this Moon for twelve years and knows how it breathes.

## INSIDE THE BUILDING — THE LIVING WORLD

### Level 3 — The Production Floor

The production floor is not making entertainment right now. It is making history. Everyone in the room knows this and the knowledge has changed how they move — faster, more purposeful, the specific electricity of people doing the most important version of their job.

Three cameras are running simultaneously. One is feeding live orbital footage of Kaguya crater. One is on Serena Nightshade at the anchor desk. One is on a studio analyst — a former Globalsec consultant named Haines who was booked for this segment three days ago — which means Haines was booked before the battle started.

Serena is reading the Sigma-7 script. She knows it is Sigma-7. She recognized it when they handed it to her this morning. She has read it four times. She is reading it on air with the particular professional grace of someone who has made a decision and is executing it cleanly.

She says "anti-colonial extremists" and her face does not change. She says "brave and heroic response" and her face does not change. She says "all hostile rebels were eliminated" and her face does not change.

Between segments, when the camera cuts to the analyst, she looks at her hands for exactly three seconds. Then she looks up and the face is back.

The director is in the production booth. He is twenty-nine. He has been at NBN for four years. He has never directed live crisis coverage before. He is doing it correctly by following the crisis coverage protocol he was trained on. The protocol is Sigma-7. He doesn't know it has a name. He knows it as "the crisis runsheet."

He is a good director. He is directing a lie with the competence of someone directing the truth.

### NPC Encounter — Serena Nightshade

**Setting:** Level 3, during a camera cut

If Dante moves through Level 3 via the environmental crawl and emerges near the production area during a segment break:

Serena is sitting at the anchor desk. The cameras are on the analyst. She has forty seconds before her next segment. She is looking at her hands.

She looks up and sees Dante in HVAC maintenance overalls emerging from a panel behind the set. They look at each other.

She does not call security. Not immediately. She looks at him for a moment that is slightly too long for someone who is about to call security.

> **Serena:** *(very quietly, not into a mic)* "You're not here to fix anything, are you."

It is not a question.

*If Dante says nothing:*

She nods once. She looks back at the camera that is about to come back to her.

> "Level 5 server room is behind the air handling units. The panel you want is on the north wall."

She pauses.

> "I don't know why I'm telling you that."

She picks up her earpiece. She looks at the camera. The red light comes on. She is Serena Nightshade again.

> "—and we're getting reports now from the outer ring that the situation at Starport Kaguya remains under control thanks to the decisive response from—"

*If Dante asks why:*

> "Because I've read the runsheet and I know what's in it and I know what's not in it and I'm sitting here saying it anyway."

A beat.

> "Level 5. North wall panel."

The red light comes on. She performs the broadcast.

*If Dante asks what she knows:*

> "Not enough. Whatever's in there is more than I know. It has to be, or they wouldn't work this hard to keep it there."

The red light comes on.

> "I've been doing this for eleven years. Ask me again in another eleven and maybe I'll have a different answer. Maybe. Level 5. North wall."

### Level 4 — Administration

Administration at crisis broadcast capacity is a specific kind of chaos. Everyone has somewhere to be and is moving there quickly. A person in maintenance overalls moving with purpose in a building full of people moving with purpose is almost invisible. Almost.

The managing editor — the one who signed the Kaguya coverage decision, whose name is in the editorial archive — is on Level 4. His name is Brennan. He is fifty-three. He has been at NBN for twenty years. He signed Sigma-7 three days ago because he was told to sign it and because he has been told things and signed them for twenty years and the weight of what he has signed lives in him in a place he has learned to not visit.

Today it is harder to not visit.

He is standing in the Level 4 corridor outside his office watching the Kaguya feed on a wall screen. He has been standing here for eleven minutes. He has not gone back into his office. He is holding a coffee cup that has gone cold.

If Dante passes through Level 4 and Brennan sees him:

> **Brennan:** *(not to Dante specifically, to the hallway in general)* "There are forty-three people in that atrium right now. The script says we don't name them. We don't show their faces. We call them extremists and we cut to the military response."

He looks at his cold coffee.

> "I've been doing this job for twenty years."

He goes back into his office and closes the door.

He does not call security. He will think about this for a long time. He does not know what Dante is doing in the building. He has decided not to find out.

### Level 5 — SYNC Infrastructure / Server Room

Level 5 is the quietest floor in the building. It is always the quietest floor. Servers don't make drama. They hum at a constant frequency that Dante recognizes from the Rusty Ring — the specific pitch of systems that have been running so long they no longer sound like machines. They sound like infrastructure. Like air. Like something that was always here and always will be.

The north wall panel is where Serena said it was. Behind the air handling unit for the server room's cooling system. The panel has a warning label that says AUTHORIZED ACCESS ONLY in four languages. The label is eleven years old. The adhesive on one corner has failed and it curls slightly.

Dante pulls it back and jacks in.

The network feels immediately different from anything he has run before. Not the Rusty Ring's cheap corporate ICE. Not Kaguya's confident Authority architecture. This is something older. Something that was built by people who had been doing this since before there was a name for it. The ICE here has the specific quality of systems designed by the people who designed the first systems — not imitating a template but working from principles that the template was derived from.

SYNC built the Network. This is what that feels like from inside.

The sound of Prycek's fighters outside reaches Level 5 as a subsonic vibration in the server rack nearest the south wall. The rack hums at its constant frequency. The vibration is a different frequency. They interfere with each other and create a third frequency that isn't quite either.

Dante runs his systems inside that sound.

## THE SECURITY STAFF — WHO'S WATCHING

The NBN building normally has eight security staff on shift. Today it has twelve — four extras called in when the crisis broadcast protocol activated. All twelve are Lunacent-based NBN employees, not Globalsec. They carry stun weapons. They are not soldiers.

- **Four** are on Level 1 watching the door and the screens.
- **Two** are on Level 3 with the production crew.
- **Two** are on Level 4 patrolling administration.
- **Two** are on Level 5 and 6 combined — they rotate between floors.
- **Two** are monitoring the security network from a Level 2 desk.

The Level 5/6 rotation means one guard is on Level 5 approximately every 12 minutes. The rotation is not clockwork — it varies by 2-3 minutes depending on what they find on each level. Dante learns the rotation pattern from the first pass. He has approximately 9-14 minutes between Level 5 guard appearances.

The guard who does the Level 5 rotation is named **Olu**. He is twenty-seven. He has worked NBN security for two years. His entire attention today is on the Kaguya situation because his younger brother works a maintenance shift at Kaguya. He has not been able to reach his brother for three hours. His phone is in his pocket. He checks it every four minutes.

When he passes through Level 5 during Dante's run:

He is looking at his phone. He checks it. No signal. He puts it back in his pocket. He looks at the server room. He does not look at the north wall panel because the north wall panel is behind the air handling unit and the air handling unit is running at crisis-level capacity which it does during major Heinlein events when the building's atmospheric load increases due to occupancy. It is making more noise than usual. The noise covers the sound of Dante's work.

Olu goes back to the elevator. He checks his phone again.

## THE NETWORK SYSTEMS — SAME AS PLANNED, NEW CONTEXT

The five systems remain as designed. But running them during an active battle creates specific differences:

**SYSTEM 1 IS LIVE AND RUNNING AT FULL CAPACITY.**
The broadcast network is active. More active than it has ever been. AIRWAVE is fully staffed — the monitoring that normally runs on a skeleton overnight crew is now running with four operators. Every access attempt is flagged faster than normal. But: the broadcast is also generating enormous amounts of traffic that the monitoring systems are processing simultaneously. The noise-to-signal ratio is the highest it has ever been. A careful runner looks like traffic.

**THE SIGMA-7 PROTOCOL IS ACTIVELY RUNNING.**
The content suppression system is live. The crisis protocol engine is making real-time decisions. These are not archived — they are happening now. Dante can see the editorial decisions as they are made. In real time. As Serena reads them.

**SYSTEM 2'S PSYCHOGRAPHIC DATA IS BEING UPDATED IN REAL TIME.**
Heinlein's population is watching the Kaguya broadcast. Their reactions are being fed into the psychographic model as they happen. The model is learning from the crisis while the crisis is ongoing. The Loonies' psychographic anomaly data — the 14-month record of people showing signs of organizing — is being updated with new data from today. Right now. The model knows who watched the Kaguya coverage and had an anomalous reaction. It has their PAD IDs. If Dante can reach the suppression target registry: he can delete those PAD IDs before they are flagged. Before NBN knows who to watch next.

**SYSTEM 4 SECURITY IS ALERT BUT FOCUSED OUTWARD.**
All twelve security staff are watching the building's physical perimeter and the Kaguya situation. The network security monitoring is running on automated protocols because the two people who staff it normally are both watching the Kaguya feed on their secondary screens. Automated protocols are good. They are not as good as two humans who are paying attention. This is the largest gap the battle creates inside the building.

**SYSTEM 5 — LEVEL 6 — IS UNCHANGED.**
DEAD MAN does not care about the battle. The Kane archive does not care about the battle. What is in there has been waiting since before the Blackout. It can wait through a morning's fighting.

## TACTICAL COMPLICATIONS — THE BATTLE AFFECTS THE RUN

### Complication 01 — Power Fluctuation (Minute 12)

The stun grenade outside creates a pressure event that triggers the emergency ventilation protocol. The same protocol that opened hatch EV-7 and let Dante in. As a side effect: the building's power management system briefly prioritizes the broadcast infrastructure over everything else — including the server room cooling. For 90 seconds, the server room cooling runs at reduced capacity. The ICE response times slow slightly. The gaps get marginally wider. A runner who recognizes this window has 90 seconds where the network is fractionally more vulnerable. This window will not repeat.

### Complication 02 — NBN Security Repositions (Minute 28)

A Globalsec unit working with the building's security coordinator requests two of the Level 1 security staff move to the corridor junction adjacent to the building to hold a position against Prycek's fighters. NBN security does not do this. Their supervisor refuses. The refusal generates a heated comms exchange that occupies both the security coordinator and the Globalsec liaison for approximately six minutes. During those six minutes nobody is actively monitoring the Level 5 security rotation. Olu's check-in is missed. Nobody notices for seven minutes. Dante has a longer window than expected.

### Complication 03 — Prycek's Fighters Breach the Perimeter (Minute 45)

Prycek makes a tactical decision: he pushes two fighters into the building's service corridor adjacent to the south face — not into the building itself, but close enough that the motion sensors on Level 1 trigger. Every security staff member in the building goes to alert. Every screen in the security monitoring room shows the sensors. Nobody is watching the network anomaly that VIEWHOUND has been quietly accumulating for 45 minutes. VIEWHOUND's alert is in the queue. The queue is not being processed.

This is also not a coincidence. Prycek doesn't know why Yusuf told him to push two fighters to the south face service corridor at minute 45. He did it because Yusuf told him to. Yusuf knew VIEWHOUND's review window. Yusuf planned this run more carefully than Dante knew.

### Complication 04 — The Broadcast Cuts (Minute 58)

If Dante has reached System 1's Master Broadcast Override: The moment he uses it — pushes the Kane archive, the Section F evidence, the Sigma-7 memo, any of it — every screen in the building changes simultaneously. The production floor sees it. Serena sees it. The director sees it. Brennan sees it from his office. Every security staff member sees it on every monitor.

For approximately eight seconds nothing happens. Everyone is watching the screens.

Then the director says into his comms: *"We have a breach. Building-wide. All staff."*

Those eight seconds are real. They are the window between the truth going out and the building responding to it.

## THE EMOTIONAL WEIGHT — RUNNING THE HACK DURING THE BATTLE

At some point during the run — the GM chooses when, based on the session's pacing — the following happens:

The Kaguya broadcast is running on the wall screen that Dante can see from his position at the Level 5 panel. The screen is small. The image is clear.

Serena is saying: *"All hostile rebels were eliminated by the brave and heroic response—"*

And Dante is inside the system that is producing that sentence. He can see the editorial decision that wrote it. He can see it was written three days ago. He can see the psychographic modeling that determined which words would land cleanest with which demographic segments. *"Brave and heroic."* Tested. Calibrated. Chosen.

He can also see, because System 2 is updating in real time, that in the worker habitation sections of Heinlein's lower levels the psychographic anomaly rate for this broadcast is significantly elevated. People are watching it the wrong way. They are watching it and their brain responses don't match the responses the model predicted for *"brave and heroic response."*

The model is flagging them. The model is logging their PAD IDs. The model is building a list of people who watched the Kaguya broadcast and felt the wrong thing.

Dante is the only person in the world right now who can see that list being built and delete it before it becomes the next suppression target registry.

He is also the only person in the world right now who knows what is in the Level 6 archive. He cannot do both at the same time. He may not have time to do both at all.

This is the session's core decision point. The Kane archive — the truth about the Blackout, the thing that started all of this — or the list of names of the people who watched a broadcast and felt the wrong thing and will be watched for it for the rest of their lives.

**History or the people standing in it right now.**

## THE EXIT

The same hatch EV-7 that let Dante in. Prycek's fighters are still in the service corridor adjacent. They don't know Dante is in the building. They are covering their assigned position.

When Dante comes out of EV-7 into the service corridor: one of Prycek's fighters is three meters away. She looks at him. He looks at her. She says: *"Yusuf's?"* Dante says whatever he says. She nods and goes back to watching the corridor.

Prycek is at the far end. When Dante reaches him:

> **Prycek:** *(not looking away from the junction he's watching)* "Whatever you needed — did you get it?"

*If yes:*

He nods.

> "Then we fall back in four minutes. Yusuf's channel is burned but the secondary still works. Tell him it held."

*If no:*

He nods.

> "Then we fall back in four minutes anyway. Some things don't work out."

He glances at Dante.

> "Were you at Kaguya?"

Dante answers or doesn't.

Prycek goes back to watching his junction. Four minutes later his fighters fall back through the corridor. Clean. Nobody left behind.

The building behind Dante is still broadcasting. Either it is broadcasting what it was designed to broadcast, or it is broadcasting something else. Either way it is broadcasting.

The battle is still happening at Kaguya. Dante is walking away from both of them carrying whatever he found.
