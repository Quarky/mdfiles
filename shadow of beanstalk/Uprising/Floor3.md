## ROOM 1: THE MAIN EDITING PIT

### Physical Description

The main editing pit is a sunken area — 1.2 meters below the main floor — with twelve workstations arranged in three rows of four. Each workstation is a command center: three large monitors (left for raw feeds, center for the edit output, right for system status), a mixing board for audio level control, a physical keyboard, and a BMI interface for direct neural editing (though most editors rely on the keyboard today). The pit is surrounded by low partitions that dampen sound but don't prevent sightlines — anyone in the pit can see anyone else.

The air in the pit is warmer than the rest of the floor — the heat from the workstations accumulates and the ventilation hasn't caught up. It smells of warm electronics and the specific chemistry of sustained focus. The editors are running on the adrenaline of a crisis broadcast and the caffeine of coffee that stopped being good hours ago.
