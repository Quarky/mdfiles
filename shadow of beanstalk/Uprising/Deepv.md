
# THE NBN HACK — DURING THE BATTLE OF KAGUYA

**The miners are fighting outside. Dante goes in.**

**Timeline Note:** This session takes place **simultaneously** with the Battle of Kaguya. While Zane's fighters are holding the junctions at Starport Kaguya, while the SXC is dropping from orbit, while the He-3 cargo is being locked — Dante is seven blocks away in Lunacent, inside the NBN Broadcast Facility, running the most important hack of his life.

The battle outside is the reason he can get in. The battle outside is also the reason he can't stop to think about what he's doing.

## THE SITUATION OUTSIDE

The NBN Heinlein Broadcast Facility sits in Lunacent — seven blocks from the Columbiad Arcology needle, three blocks from the tube-lev terminus that connects Lunacent to Kaguya's outer ring. The tube-lev has been running all morning, moving Zane's people into position. Now it's running the other way — Globalsec reinforcements moving toward Kaguya, medical teams moving back, the endless churn of a crisis in motion.

Right now, at this moment, the miners are fighting Globalsec and the U.S. military at Kaguya. The battle is happening. The He-3 cargo is locked. The Beanstalk is frozen. Zane is holding a junction with whatever he has left. Amara is sitting in the atrium with her people. The SXC is advancing through the corridors.

And in Lunacent, seven blocks away, the NBN broadcast facility is in full operational crisis mode.

Because the battle is their story. And they are covering it live.

When Yusuf approached him, he said yes. Not because he trusted Yusuf — he trusted nobody — but because Yusuf's network was the only one that didn't ask for loyalty oaths. It asked for results. Prycek could work with that.

## HOW DANTE GETS IN — THE BATTLE CREATES THE DOOR

The emergency protocol that locked the front door also activated the building's emergency ventilation procedure — a standard Heinlein safety measure that opens secondary atmospheric equalization hatches throughout the structure when a pressure event is detected in adjacent corridors.

The stun grenade at minute 12 qualified as a pressure event.

Hatch EV-7 — on the building's south face, at sublevel 1, designed for atmospheric equalization during corridor pressure events — opened automatically at the detection trigger and has not closed because the automated reset requires a facilities manager to physically confirm the all-clear from inside the building.

The facilities manager is on Level 3 watching the Kaguya feed on every screen and has not thought about hatch EV-7.

The hatch is 0.8 meters wide. It opens onto a maintenance crawl that connects to the building's environmental system network. The environmental system network connects to every level. Including Level 5.

This is not a coincidence. Yusuf planned this. Yusuf planned it by knowing that Heinlein's standard safety protocols would open exactly this hatch in exactly this situation, because Yusuf has been on this Moon for twelve years and knows how it breathes.

### SYSTEM 1 — PUBLIC BROADCAST NETWORK

**The face. What NBN wants the world to see and how it controls what they see.**

**Splice Point:** Level 3 maintenance panel (Option A/C) OR external relay (Option B)

**System Type:** **PUZZLE + TRAP**

**The Puzzle: The Editorial Archive is a Narrative Maze**

The editorial decision archive isn't a simple database. It's structured as a **narrative tree** — every editorial decision branches into possible alternatives, and the archive records not just what was chosen, but what *wasn't* chosen. The Sigma-7 protocol is buried in this tree, but finding it requires understanding the branching logic.

**How the Puzzle Works:**

The archive is organized like a decision tree. Each node represents an editorial choice point:
- **Node:** "Kaguya Coverage — Primary Frame"
  - **Branch A:** "Terrorist Attack" (chosen)
  - **Branch B:** "Worker Uprising" (rejected)
  - **Branch C:** "Infrastructure Incident" (rejected)

To find Sigma-7, Dante must trace the rejected branches. The protocol itself is attached to **Branch B**, which was rejected but not deleted. The archive preserves rejected branches as "alternative coverage scenarios" — a standard editorial practice that NBN uses to maintain historical records of what *could* have been said.

**The Clue:** A note at the bottom of the archive's main index reads: *"Sigma-7 referenced in supplementary materials — see Branch B, Node 14."*

**Skill Check:** Average (●●●) Knowledge (The Net) or Computers (Hacking) to understand the archive's structure and navigate to the rejected branches.

**Failure:** Dante finds the wrong node and triggers a VIEWHOUND alert. The alert is queued but not processed immediately due to the battle's noise-to-signal ratio. He has 10 minutes before it escalates.

**Success:** Dante finds Sigma-7 — the full protocol, written three days before the battle.

**The Trap: The Content Suppression System is a Honeypot**

The content suppression system — the one that flags keywords and topics — appears to be a simple keyword filter. It is not. It is a **honeypot**. **How the Trap Works:**

- After 3-5 related queries, the system generates a **behavioral alert** that is sent to the security desk.
- The security desk is currently distracted, but the alert remains in the queue.

**Fail Forward:** If Dante triggers the honeypot, the system creates a profile of him as a high-priority threat. This means:
- Future access attempts are upgraded in difficulty.
- The profile is sent to the security desk after the crisis is over.
- But the profile is also **interactive** — if Dante can find the profile and delete it (in System 4), he can erase the evidence of his queries.

### SYSTEM 3 — SYNC LUNAR INFRASTRUCTURE NETWORK

**The backbone. What keeps Heinlein connected and what keeps NBN in control of that connection.**

**Splice Point:** Level 5 server room hardwired terminal (via HVAC shaft)

**System Type:** **PUZZLE + FALLBACK**

**The Puzzle: The StarReach Archive is a Locked Box**

The StarReach archive — the historical network modeling data, the pre-Blackout records — is not accessible through a simple password. It requires a **specific handshake protocol** that changes based on the time of day and the state of the network.

**How the Puzzle Works:**

The archive is protected by a **frequency-based handshake**. To access it, Dante must send a series of network pings in the correct order, at the correct intervals, matching the frequency pattern of SYNC's own maintenance protocols. The pattern is embedded in the network's traffic — but it's not obvious. It requires analysis.
