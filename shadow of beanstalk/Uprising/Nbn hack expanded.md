## PROLOGUE: THE WEIGHT OF WHAT DANTE ALREADY KNOWS

*This session takes place **after** the Battle of Kaguya. The Insurrection has happened. The War is beginning. NBN's coverage of Kaguya is what Dante is trying to understand — and what leads him to the deeper truth.*

Dante has already seen what NBN does with its power. He watched Serena Nightshade deliver the Kaguya coverage script — the one that called the Insurrection a "terrorist attack" and never mentioned clone casualties. He knows the Sigma-7 protocol was written before the battle even started. He knows someone told NBN what was coming.

But that's just the surface. The real question Dante is chasing is older. Much older.

### The Conspiracy That Started It All

Before NBN was NBN, there was **Vertex** — a media and technology conglomerate that dominated global entertainment, news, and infrastructure. Vertex was so powerful that governments feared it. Legislation was introduced to break it up. Vertex was going to lose everything.

Then the **Blackout** happened.

The Blackout was a global cyber catastrophe. A virus — one with a rudimentary AI — triggered cascading failures across regional networks. Air traffic was grounded. Trillions of dollars evaporated into corrupted bits. Hospitals went dark. The old Internet died, and with it, the infrastructure that the world had come to depend on.

When the dust settled, one company had the solution: **SYNC**, a subsidiary of what had been Network Broadcast News. SYNC's protocol was immune to the Blackout virus. Desperate to get back online, governments signed over control of their networks. NBN rebuilt itself from the ashes of Vertex — larger, more powerful, and with governments dependent on it rather than regulating it.

The question Dante is asking: **was the Blackout an accident?**

The Kane Archive suggests the answer is no.

## THE PHYSICAL LOCATION — EXPANDED

### NBN Heinlein Broadcast Facility — Historical Context

The NBN Heinlein Broadcast Facility was built during the post-War reconstruction boom, when Melange Mining's clone workforce and Weyland's construction firms were reshaping the Moon. It was designed to serve two purposes: broadcast NBN's programming to the growing lunar population, and house the infrastructure for the **StarReach Pathway** project — NBN's ambitious plan to create a deep-space broadcast satellite network.

The facility's architecture reflects NBN's dual nature. The lower levels are open, welcoming, designed to reassure visitors that NBN is a friendly media company. The upper levels are sealed, isolated, designed to protect secrets that would destroy the company if they were ever exposed.

### Level-by-Level Breakdown

**Levels 1-3: Broadcast Production**
These levels are what the public sees. The production floor is a hive of controlled activity — directors, camera operators, bioroid support staff, and anchors like Serena Nightshade going through their carefully scripted routines. The lighting is precise. The sets are designed to feel warm and trustworthy. Everything is calculated to make you believe what you're seeing.

**Level 4: Administration**
The administrative level is where NBN's corporate bureaucracy lives. Offices, meeting rooms, HR departments, legal teams. This is where suppression orders are drafted, where contracts with Heinlein Authority are negotiated, where decisions about what the public sees and doesn't see are made.

**Level 5: SYNC Infrastructure**
This is the heart of the facility. Server racks line the walls, their indicator lights blinking in endless patterns. The StarReach project's working data lives here. The historical network modeling data — including the pre-Blackout records — is stored here. This level is air-gapped, isolated from the public Network, accessible only through hardwired terminals.

**Level 6: The Founder Archive (Does Not Exist)**
The sixth level isn't on any public floor plan. It appears on the building schematics filed with Heinlein's planning authority as "environmental systems — restricted access." It is not environmental systems. This is where the Kane family kept their secrets. Project Static. The Vertex files. The proof that the Blackout was planned.

## NPC ENCOUNTERS — EXPANDED

### Adaeze Okafor — Deeper Background

Adaeze has been on the Moon for twenty-two years. She came during the reconstruction, when Weyland was rebuilding Heinlein and hiring anyone who could hold a tool. She started as a junior technician, worked her way up, and eventually bought the HVAC firm from its retiring owner.

She has seen the Moon change. She remembers when the Docklands were just construction sites. She remembers when the Insurrection started. She remembers what the corps did to put it down.

She doesn't talk about any of this. Not because she doesn't care, but because she has learned that caring loudly is a good way to lose everything. She has a business. She has employees who depend on her. She has a life she built from nothing.

When Yusuf asked her for help, she said yes because she owes him — he helped her daughter get into a decent school when the system said no. But she also said yes because she has been watching the news. She has seen what NBN says about the Insurrection. She has seen what they don't say.

She doesn't know what Dante is looking for. She doesn't want to know. But she knows it's important. That's why she's helping.

### Callum, the Facilities Manager — Deeper Background

Callum has been on the Moon for three years. He came from Earth — a mid-level facilities management job in BosWash that he traded for a promotion and a relocation package. He thought he was moving up. He didn't realize that Heinlein's NBN facility was a dead-end assignment, a place where promising executives go to have their careers quietly ended.

He knows something is wrong with his career trajectory. He doesn't know why. He suspects it has something to do with a report he filed three years ago — a report about irregularities in the Level 5 server room maintenance logs. He didn't think much of it at the time. But after he filed it, his promotion track stalled. His requests for transfer were denied. He was told he was "too valuable" in his current position.

He doesn't know what he found. He doesn't know that the irregularities he reported were related to the StarReach archive. He doesn't know that NBN flagged him as a potential security risk and buried him in a position where he could do no harm.

He is a good man. He is also a man who has been systematically ground down by a system that doesn't care about him. When he asks Dante about the missing subcontractor authorization, it's not because he suspects anything — it's because he is a bureaucrat who has learned that missing paperwork is the only thing he can still control.

### Min, the Security Officer — Deeper Background

Min has worked NBN security for nine years. She was born on the Moon — one of the first generation of Lunar-born. Her parents were miners. They lost their jobs when Melange switched to clone labor. They ended up in the Docklands.

Min joined NBN security because it was a steady job with benefits. She didn't think about what she was protecting. She just did her job. She walked her patrols, checked her PAD, went home.

But she has been watching the news. She has seen what NBN says about the Insurrection. She has seen what they don't say. She has friends who were at Kaguya. Some of them didn't come back.

She doesn't know what Dante is doing in the server room. But when she hears the sound — the specific harmonic of a maintenance terminal being accessed outside of a scheduled window — she knows something is wrong. She has a choice to make: follow procedure, or listen to what Dante has to say.

She has lived on the Moon for forty-one years. She knows what the Insurrection was. She knows someone called it a terrorist attack. She was watching the same screens as everyone else.

## THE LIVING WORLD — EXPANDED

### The Production Floor — What Dante Sees

The production floor at 14:00 is a carefully controlled chaos. Camera operators move in precise patterns. Directors speak in clipped commands. Bioroid support staff execute tasks with mechanical efficiency.

**Serena Nightshade** is at the anchor desk. She is delivering a segment about Heinlein's increasing tourism numbers. Bright. Cheerful. Numbers going up. She delivers it three times because the director wants a slightly different cadence on the second line. On the fourth take she nails it. She smiles the smile and holds it.

Between takes she sits in silence with her eyes closed. She has been doing this for eleven years. She is very good at it.

What she doesn't know: three floors above her head, there is a file with her private notes in it. Notes that describe her discomfort with the Kaguya coverage script. Notes that reveal she knows more than she broadcasts.

What she does know: she felt that discomfort. She made a decision about it and delivered the script anyway. She makes that decision every day. Some days it costs more than others.

*If Dante passes through Level 3 on Option A:*

He walks behind the set between takes. Serena Nightshade is twelve feet away. She looks up from her silence and looks at him. He is wearing HVAC maintenance overalls. She looks back down. That is the whole encounter. It is enough.

### The HVAC Shaft — Sensory Description

The air handling shaft is narrow — barely wide enough for a person in maintenance overalls. The walls are lined with conduits and cables, their surfaces warm to the touch. The air is recycled and faintly metallic, carrying the smell of ozone and the particular stale quality of air that has been filtered too many times.

The shaft connects Level 3 to Level 5 via a vertical run of approximately fifteen meters. There are service ladders bolted to the wall, their rungs worn smooth by years of use. The lighting is dim — emergency strips every few meters, casting long shadows.

At the Level 5 junction, there is a maintenance hatch. It is not locked. It has never been locked. The facilities manager and the security manager have been disputing whose budget covers the repair for four months. The camera that used to watch this hatch has been in "maintenance pending" status for the same amount of time. It is not working.

### The Server Room — Sensory Description

The Level 5 server room is cold. The temperature is kept at a precise 18 degrees Celsius to prevent the servers from overheating. The air is dry and smells of electronics — the particular sharp scent of heated silicon and cooling fans.

Rows of servers line the walls, their indicator lights blinking in patterns that are almost hypnotic. The hum of cooling fans is a constant presence, a low drone that you stop noticing after a few minutes.

In the corner of the room, partially obscured by a server rack, is a hardwired maintenance terminal. It is not on any network map. It is not supposed to exist. But it is here, connected to the building's infrastructure via cables that run through the walls. This is Dante's target.

## THE EMOTIONAL WEIGHT — EXPANDED

### What Dante Finds, in the Order It Lands

**Sigma-7 (System 1 Editorial Archive):**

The document is dated three days before the Battle of Kaguya. From: NBN Director of Operations. To: Heinlein Editorial Team.

> *"In the event of an organized worker action at Kaguya, coverage protocol Sigma-7 applies. Sigma-7: lead with infrastructure security, frame as terrorism, military response as protective. Do not humanize occupation participants. Do not broadcast occupation demands. Do not mention clone casualties."*

Someone knew. Someone told NBN what was coming. The coverage was written before the battle even started.

**"Subject 14" (System 4 Counter-Runner Profiles):**

Yusuf has been watched for eight months. The profile is detailed: his contacts, his movements, his patterns. Everything Dante and Yusuf did together — every meeting, every transaction, every safe house — was potentially visible. The network may have been compromised before it began.

**Petra's Suppression Order (System 4 Legal Archive):**

The document is dated before Petra published anything. Before the Battle of Kaguya.

> *"Subject J is persistent, connected to anti-corporate elements, and in possession of material that presents significant reputational risk to NBN operations."*

Someone told NBN she was going to be a problem. The same someone who told NBN about Kaguya.

**"Pre-Event Behavioral Corpus" (System 3 StarReach Archive):**

The dataset label reads: *"Vertex Archive — Pre-Event Behavioral Corpus. Classification: Founder Only."*

"Pre-Event." Not "Pre-Blackout." Someone called it an event before it was called anything. The data was preserved on purpose. The SYNC protocol was ready before the Blackout created the need for it.

**Project Static (System 5):**

> *"The window will open in 8 months. Static is ready. SYNC protocol is in place. When the window closes, we will have built what they spent thirty years trying to take from us."*

Keith Randolph Kane Sr., eight months before the Blackout.

**"Collateral modeled at acceptable parameters."**

That line.

## WHAT DANTE CAN DO — EXPANDED WITH CONSEQUENCES

### Option 1 — Give It to Petra

Petra Vasquez is a journalist who has been chasing the truth for years. She has the sources, the context, and the courage to publish. She is currently alive and writing somewhere.

If Dante gives her the Kane Archive, she will publish it. She will name names. She will expose Project Static. She will force NBN to spend the next decade in legal combat.

She will also be in danger. NBN has already tried to suppress her. With the full proof in her hands, they will try harder. She knows this. She would publish anyway.

### Option 2 — Broadcast It Directly

Push it to every NBN channel simultaneously. Project Static. The Kane Archive. All of it. To every screen in Heinlein. To every PAD.

NBN will take it back in minutes. But in those minutes, the files will be cached on a thousand PADs in the outpost transit hubs where SYNC can't reach. The second draft of history, written in real time.

### Option 3 — Send It to the Architects' Rivals

Three of the eleven architects are still alive. Their rivals would pay a great deal for this information. Not to expose it. To use it.

This is the most cynical option. It is also the most likely to produce lasting structural change. It creates leverage that doesn't go away when an article is buried.

### Option 4 — Destroy It and Walk Away

If Dante deletes the Kane Archive cleanly — and deletes the logs of the deletion — the evidence is gone. NBN loses the record they kept of their own crime.

That's not justice. It's not nothing either.

### Option 5 — Keep It and Wait

The information exists on whatever storage medium Dante carries it on. It is leverage. Against NBN. Against the surviving architects. Against anyone who would benefit from it staying buried.

This is how the Kane family operated. Dante now knows that.
