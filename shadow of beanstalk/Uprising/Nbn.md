## LUNACENT — THE WORLD OUTSIDE

Lunacent is the beating heart of Heinlein — the largest dome, the oldest corridors, the place every resident passes through at least a few times a week whether they want to or not. Today it smells of recycled air and anxiety. The Kaguya battle is happening seven kilometers away through vacuum and tunnels and everyone in Lunacent knows it and nobody is saying so directly.

The corridor Dante walks to reach the NBN building is one of the main arteries — wide enough for four people across, ceiling three meters up, lit by warm amber strips that have been providing light for twelve years and will provide it for twelve more. The walls are plascrete, smooth and grey, the kind of surface that absorbs footsteps and gives back nothing. Every tenth panel has a small seccam mounted at the corner junction — they have always been there and everyone has always known they are there and nobody thinks about them anymore.

Today the seccams feel different. Dante notices this before he can say why. Then he realizes: the status lights that normally blink green are blinking amber. All of them. The whole corridor.

Amber means elevated monitoring. Amber means someone told the system to pay more attention than usual.

The people around him have not noticed the amber lights. They are looking at their PADs or at the floor or at the middle distance of people who are thinking about something that is happening elsewhere and cannot be reached.

A man in a Melange logistics uniform is crying quietly at a junction corner. Not loudly. The specific controlled crying of someone who has gotten bad news and is not going to stop moving because stopping would mean something. Nobody around him acknowledges it. On the Moon, you learn not to. Space is too small for everyone else's grief to be your problem.

The NBN building is visible two blocks ahead. The amber on its exterior screens — the familiar warm glow of the NBN logo — looks different against the corridor's amber seccams. Usually it signals welcome. Today it signals something Dante does not have a word for.

### THE ENTRY PUZZLE — GETTING IN AND GETTING PAST

The south face service entrance has a biometric panel. But biometric panels on emergency lockdown require secondary authorization that routes through the building's admin system — and the admin system is running on crisis broadcast protocols that are deprioritizing non-broadcast-critical access requests.

In plain language: the biometric panel is technically functional but practically unresponsive. It will eventually process the request. "Eventually" means somewhere between four and forty minutes. This is not Dante's window.

#### Option A — The EV-7 Hatch (discovered by observation)

The roof hatch connects to the environmental crawl. The crawl is tight, dark, and smells of the specific chemical cocktail of a building's climate control system — not unpleasant exactly, but institutional in a way that communicates you are inside something's lungs.

The crawl connects to every floor's air handling systems. The Level 3 junction is accessible through a maintenance panel behind the production set. The panel is labeled EV-ACCESS 3-C. It is not locked because maintenance panels in environmental crawls are never locked because no one imagines anyone wants to be in there.

#### Option B — The Journalist (social)

The journalist at the main entrance is named **RAFAEL SANTOS**. He is thirty-one, Heinlein-born, works for the independent wire service that ran Petra's first story. He is trying to get press access because he believes something is happening inside this building that is connected to what is happening at Kaguya and he is not wrong.

> **Rafael:** *(not looking away from the intercom)* "They cut the press access list this morning. Four journalists had building access. Three of us got messages at 06:00 saying our credentials were suspended pending security review."

He looks at Dante.

> "You're not press."

He waits.

*If Dante tells him something useful:*
Rafael files it. He also tells Dante something useful in return:

> "There's a maintenance subcontractor who goes in and out through the south service entrance. She's been making trips every thirty minutes. Something is going on with Level 5. I've been watching the access log on the external panel — Level 5 has had three access requests in the last two hours. Level 5 is supposed to be infrastructure. Nobody accesses infrastructure during a crisis broadcast."

This is a gift. Rafael doesn't know what it means. He is a journalist. He is collecting things that might mean something later. He gives it to Dante because Dante gave him something first. He is not naive about what Dante might be doing. He decides not to ask.

#### Option C — The Subcontractor (practical)

The maintenance subcontractor Rafael mentioned is **KEIKO MURA**. She is forty-four, handles atmospheric system maintenance contracts for three Lunacent commercial buildings including this one. She is making trips because her monitoring equipment flagged an EV hatch as open and she is trying to reach the facilities manager to authorize a reset and the facilities manager is not answering.

She is on her third trip when Dante catches her at the south entrance. She is carrying a diagnostic tablet and the specific expression of someone who is very good at their job being prevented from doing it by someone who is very busy being bad at theirs.

> **Keiko:** *(swiping her access card at the panel, not looking at Dante)* "If you're from the facilities desk you can tell Callum that the EV-7 hatch has been open for forty minutes and if he doesn't authorize the manual reset in the next twenty minutes I'm logging it as a safety incident and that goes to the district office."

*If Dante says he's from the facilities desk (deception):*

She looks at him.

> "You're not from the facilities desk. Callum is forty-seven, balding, and he always smells like the synthetic coffee they sell in the Level 2 break room."

She considers him.

> "But you want to get in. And I need someone to hold this panel open while I reset the hatch from inside because I cannot do both simultaneously with one set of hands."

She holds up her access card.

> "You hold the panel. I reset the hatch. You go wherever you were going to go. We both pretend this conversation didn't happen."

She swipes her card. The panel opens. She does not look at him again.

**This is the cleanest entry.** It requires Dante to be useful, not clever. Keiko is not naive. She has decided something. What she decided is between her and whatever she believes.

## LEVEL 2 — THE BREAK ROOM AND THE MAINTENANCE CORRIDOR

Level 2 is the building's intermediate floor — accounting, HR, storage, and the break room that serves both Level 2 and Level 3 staff. Today the break room has more people in it than usual. Six people sitting around a table with the specific silence of colleagues who have discovered they have nothing to say to each other during a crisis.

They are watching a wall screen. Serena Nightshade is saying something about "coordinated efforts to protect vital infrastructure." One of the six people at the table is crying, quietly, in the same controlled way as the man Dante passed in the corridor outside. On the Moon you learn to cry in a way that doesn't require anyone else to respond.

The maintenance corridor runs parallel to the break room on its north side. Standard Heinlein construction: a service passage 1.2 meters wide, pipes and cable runs along the ceiling, floor panels that can be lifted for subfloor access. The passage connects to the environmental crawl junction for Level 3 and to the freight elevator that serves Levels 1-5.

### CLUE 02 — THE MAINTENANCE LOG

**Location:** Mounted on the maintenance corridor wall. Nobody reads it. Dante should.

Every building in Heinlein keeps a physical maintenance log as a backup to the digital records — a requirement from the district office after a power failure in an older building caused three days of untracked maintenance work. The log for the NBN building is a physical binder in a sealed perspex case mounted on the maintenance corridor wall at Level 2.

The binder has 847 pages. Most of them are routine. But this is an investigation and Dante has a question: **when was the VX-ARCHIVE-7 rack on Level 5 installed?**

A runner who pages through the installation records finds:

- Standard installation records going back twenty years.
- And then: a record dated eight years ago, filed under "infrastructure transfer."

> "Received: 1 server rack unit, manufacturer Vertex Systems International. Classification: VX-ARCHIVE-7. Installation: Level 5, northwest corner. Authorization: FOUNDER CREDENTIALS. Special handling: isolated power circuit, secondary cable routing to Level 6 (see supplemental permit SA-6-89). Note: Unit is not to be included in standard maintenance schedules. Inspection by authorized personnel only."

Supplemental permit SA-6-89. There is a reference to a permit filed with the Heinlein district office. A public record. In theory, accessible. In practice: it is the kind of public record that requires someone to know it exists before they can ask for it. Dante knows it exists.

## LEVEL 3 — THE PRODUCTION FLOOR

The production floor is where the building's purpose becomes physical. Three broadcast sets arranged around a central production pit, camera rigs on motorized mounts, the specific quality of light that exists only in television studios — too even, too warm, designed not to illuminate but to perform illumination.

Today every set is running. The main anchor desk where Serena sits. A supplementary analysis set where a former Globalsec consultant named Haines is explaining, with the confidence of someone who has never been wrong in a way that mattered, why the military response at Kaguya was not only justified but necessary. A third set running B-roll footage from the crater's orbital camera.

The production pit is the center of the floor's activity. Six people at workstations, a director in a glass-walled booth above, assistant producers moving between stations with the specific controlled urgency of people who are very good at managing a crisis they did not create.

Dante moves through this floor via the environmental crawl access behind the main set. The panel is where Serena told him it would be. EV-ACCESS 3-C. He comes through it during a camera cut. He is twelve feet from Serena Nightshade.

## LEVEL 4 — ADMINISTRATION

The administrative level is quieter than the floors below. Crisis broadcast mode has emptied most of the offices — everyone who can be useful in the production workflow has gone down to Level 3, and the people who cannot be useful have been told to shelter in place and have interpreted this, largely, as permission to watch the screens in the common area and feel bad about what they are watching.

The common area has four people in it. They are watching the broadcast their own building is producing. They look at the screen and see Serena and they know she is three floors below them and they still watch the screen because the screen is where you watch things happen.

One of the four is a young assistant editor named **MIRA** who has been at NBN for eight months. She is watching the screen with her knees drawn up to her chest and her arms wrapped around them, which is the posture of someone who has made their body smaller. She is not crying. She is past the crying point. She reached that point about forty minutes ago and came out the other side into a kind of clarity that is very uncomfortable to inhabit.

## LEVEL 5 — THE SERVER ROOM

The freight elevator opens onto Level 5 with a pressure equalization hiss that is slightly louder than usual — the Level 5 atmospheric systems are running at elevated capacity because of the crisis broadcast load above. The floor is colder than the floors below. By four degrees. The human body notices four degrees immediately.

The server room is through a set of double doors marked AUTHORIZED ACCESS — ENVIRONMENTAL AND INFRASTRUCTURE SYSTEMS ONLY. The doors are not biometrically locked today because the biometric authorization system on Level 5 is running through the same admin backend that is deprioritizing non-broadcast-critical access requests. The doors can be opened with a simple handle press. This is a gift that the crisis inadvertently created.

## THE FIVE NETWORK SYSTEMS — DISCOVERY SEQUENCE

### SYSTEM 1 — THE BROADCAST NETWORK — "THE FACE"

**What Dante finds: The machinery of the lie, running in real time**

He is inside the system that is producing what Serena is saying. He can see the editorial decisions as they are made — not recorded, not archived, live. The Sigma-7 protocol generating the exact language that is being spoken three floors below while he reads it.

**The first thing he finds:** Serena's private notes on the Kaguya coverage. Filed in the content schedule manager under her personal access credential. The notes are in the voice of someone who is writing to themselves:

Further on:

> "The clone casualty note. Do not mention. This is the one that will follow me."

**The second thing:** the Sigma-7 template history.

| Version | Event |
|---------|-------|
| **Version 1** (5 years ago) | Workers' rights protest in Melange outpost sector |
| **Version 2** | Labor organizing attempt near the Melange board. Buried. |

The pattern: every time workers organized, every time the human cost of the He-3 trade became visible, Sigma was activated. The system was built to cover this specific thing. It has been used six times before.

**THE HONEYPOT MOMENT:**

The editorial archive is accessible. It is designed to be accessible. It is real and damning and it is also bait. If Dante copies it, the system knows someone took it. The real information is elsewhere. The archive points to it if you read it carefully: Sigma versions 4 and 5 are FOUNDER-ONLY. That classification exists in one place: Level 5's secondary circuit. The place Dante is running from right now.

### SYSTEM 2 — PSYCHOGRAPHIC DATA — "THE MACHINE THAT KNOWS YOU"

**What Dante finds: His own profile. And what was done with it.**

The psychographic database opens and the first thing Dante sees is his own name. Not his real name. A designation: SUBJECT-DANTE-RH. The profile has been building since he got his first PAD. It knows his movement patterns, his purchase history, his reaction times to different content types, his stress indicators from biometric data collected through the PAD's haptic sensors.

It knows, with 73% confidence, what he is going to do next in any given situation. The remaining 27% is labeled "anomalous." The anomalous percentage has been growing. In the last three months it has grown significantly. NBN's model is having more trouble predicting him. This is noted as a flag:

> "Subject exhibiting increasingly non-standard behavioral patterns. Recommend enhanced monitoring."

**The second thing:** the suppression target registry. Dante's name is on it. Petra's name is on it. Yusuf's name is on it. And building in real time, right now, as people watch the Kaguya broadcast and feel things the psychographic model didn't predict they would feel: a new list. Hundreds of PAD IDs. The people whose emotional responses to the Kaguya coverage don't match the predicted profile. People who watched "brave and heroic response" and felt something other than reassurance. People who are being flagged for that feeling. Right now. While Dante watches.

**THE CORE DECISION:**

He can delete this list. Right now. Before it becomes a permanent suppression target registry. Hundreds of people who watched the wrong broadcast the wrong way and are about to be watched for it for the rest of their lives — he can make them not-watched.

Or he can keep going. The Kane archive is deeper. The proof of the Blackout is deeper. He cannot guarantee there will be time for both.

**This moment should not be rushed. The GM should let it sit. Let Dante decide what kind of story this is.**

### SYSTEM 3 — SYNC INFRASTRUCTURE — "THE SKELETON"

**What Dante finds: Six rehearsals for today**

The relay network's historical logs hold the infrastructure record of what the broadcast system calls Sigma events. Here they are called Project Clarity events. Different name. Same pattern.

Six times in five years, the SYNC relay nodes covering specific areas of Heinlein and the outpost transit corridors were deliberately adjusted — signal strength reduced in areas where specific events were occurring, expanded in areas where the NBN narrative needed to reach. Six times the coverage map was used as a suppression tool.

And the Blackout modeling records — archived here for "StarReach modeling" — contain the technical architecture of how the old Internet was destroyed and this system was built to replace it.

The records are the most heavily trapped files in the building. Three tracking signatures. Extracting them without the metadata strip utility means carrying a beacon that pings location every four hours. The utility is buried in the Relay Master Controller. Dante has to find it before he extracts anything.

**THE BEAUTIFUL FAILURE:**

If Dante fails FREQUENCY LOCK, the relay network goes into maintenance lockdown. The SYNC relay nodes in the outpost transit corridors go dark for two hours. PAD-to-PAD networks expand automatically into the gaps. Zane's broadcast — whatever Dante pushed, if he pushed it — spreads further than it would have. The failure does the thing the success would have done. In a different way. With different consequences. But it does it.

### SYSTEM 4 — NBN SECURITY — "THE WATCHER WATCHING"

**What Dante finds: The leak. And Yusuf.**

The counter-runner profiles. The previous hack attempt two years ago — a runner with the callsign STATIC who got to System 3 and no further. Profile built on the runner: small, precise, organized, long-game orientation. Not the profile of someone who runs for money. The profile of someone with a specific goal.

And then: Yusuf. "Subject 14." Eight months of monitoring. The reason he was first flagged: a search query, two years ago, from a public terminal in the Docklands. The search terms: "Project Static" + "Blackout" + "Vertex predecessor." He already knew the name. He searched it because he wanted confirmation. He was looking for this building. He has been looking for this building for two years. He built the network that built the Insurrection that created the conditions that opened the EV-7 hatch. He built a chain of events that put Dante in this server room. He knew what was here.

The legal archive, once decrypted with the first government's name:

### LEVEL 6 — THE FOUNDER ARCHIVE

**What Dante finds: Everything. And the man who built it.**

The stairs are behind the environmental panel on Level 5's north wall. The panel is labeled ENVIRONMENTAL SYSTEMS — RESTRICTED. The panel opens onto a concrete staircase that goes down one floor. The staircase smells like a room that is opened rarely — the specific stillness of air that has not moved in a while.

Level 6 is one room. Smaller than Level 5. Lower ceiling. Server equipment on two walls — older hardware, the same vintage as the VX-ARCHIVE-7 rack upstairs, as if this room and that rack were built together for a purpose and separated only when the building was constructed around what was already here.

A desk in the center. A chair. The chair has been sat in recently. The cushion still holds an impression. The impression is not large. Someone slim. Someone who sat here for four hours and seventeen minutes, six days ago, reading something or checking something was still here.

**On the desk:**

**A PHOTOGRAPH, UNFRAMED:**

Twelve people standing in front of a server room. Old hardware behind them. A room that no longer exists in this form. Kane Sr. is in the photograph — young, not yet forty, the same age as the first lobby photograph. Eleven others around him. None of them are identified on the front. On the back, in handwriting that is careful and small:

> "The Architects. Pre-Static. Year one."

Eleven people. This is what they looked like before they changed the world. Some of them are smiling. Kane Sr. is not smiling. He is looking at the camera with the expression of someone who has already made the decision and is now simply waiting for the moment to act on it.

**A SMALL PERSONAL DRIVE, LABELED:**

One word, in the same handwriting: **"AFTER."**

This is what remains after everything was decided and done. What Kane Sr. felt was worth keeping.

*If Dante plays it:*

Eleven minutes of Kane Sr., elderly, recorded within the last three years, speaking to a camera in a room Dante cannot identify. Alone. No audience. He speaks about Vertex. About the legislation. About what he decided when the legislation passed. He does not apologize. He explains. He speaks with the precision of someone who has organized these thoughts for decades and is now simply stating them.

At minute seven he says:

> "The model we build predicts behavior. It does not predict conscience. We have found, over time, that conscience is rarer than the model assumed. This has made the work easier than it might otherwise have been."

At minute ten he says:

> "Phase 2 will be more difficult. Mars is further from Earth. The delay in oversight is longer. The people who will build it will need to believe they are building infrastructure. They are. They will also be building something else. As we did. As everyone does, when they build anything large enough."

The last thirty seconds:

He looks directly at the camera.

> "I don't know who will find this. I kept it because the network remembers everything, and I intend to remember everything too. If you found it, you were looking for it. That means you already know enough to look. That means you already know most of what I could tell you."

A pause.

> "I'm sorry about the people who died in the Blackout. I am not sorry about the Blackout."

Three seconds looking at the camera. Then the recording ends.

**THE FULL CONSPIRACY, ASSEMBLED:**

- Vertex was the world's media monopoly. Legislation broke it apart.
- Keith Randolph Kane Sr. assembled eleven people (the Architects) and planned the Blackout.
- A targeted cyber attack that destroyed the old Internet while leaving SYNC — developed in parallel, deliberately — as the only viable successor.
- **Phase 1. It worked.**
- NBN reconstituted Vertex's empire under a new structure where governments were dependent on it rather than regulating it.
- "Collateral modeled at acceptable parameters" — the people who died when hospital systems, financial systems, and communication infrastructure collapsed simultaneously. They were parameters in a model. They were acceptable.
- Sigma-7 is not the conspiracy. Sigma-7 is the maintenance of the conspiracy. Covering the Kaguya story. Covering what the Insurrection was about. Covering the workers and the clones and what happened in section F. Six times before. Seven times now. The suppression system was built from the beginning to cover exactly this.
- **Phase 2: The Martian Network.** Fifteen years from Static completion. StarReach is the infrastructure. The Moon's workers are already inside it. Mars is next. The same plan. The same architecture. Different planet.
- Yusuf knew. Two years ago he was looking for this. He built the events that opened the door. What he intends to do with what is behind it is the question Dante carries out of the building.

Dante comes back up through the Level 5 server room. The VX-ARCHIVE-7 rack is as he left it, or almost. He pushes it back to its position. The stripped bolt doesn't engage. He leaves it. Someone will notice. Eventually. Not tonight.

> "The district office has a public records terminal. Supplemental permits are filed there. SA-6-89. Filed eight years ago. Covers Level 6 of this building. If someone was looking for a paper trail in a public place, that would be where to look."

Outside, Lunacent is different from when Dante entered. Prycek's fighters are gone from the street outside the building. The street looks exactly like it did before they were there. The Moon doesn't keep the marks of small fights. Only the large ones.

Dante walks into Lunacent carrying whatever he found. The building behind him continues broadcasting. The amber lights continue pulsing. The man who was crying at the junction corner is gone. Maybe he got news. Maybe he kept walking. On the Moon you learn not to assume which.
