# Network Run — Breathing Room

**Network:** [Kaguya Pressure and Life-Safety](../03_EXAMPLE_NETWORKS/04_KAGUYA_LIFE_SAFETY.md)  
**Length:** 45–75 minutes  
**Objective:** Stabilize atmosphere and open an evacuation route  
**Opposition:** Safety automation, damaged hardware, and a cautious engineering sysop  
**Core tension:** The defender is not evil; unsafe intrusion can make the emergency worse.

## Crisis

A pressure fault has trapped civilians between two emergency bulkheads. Smoke is moving against the expected ventilation flow, and the automatic controller refuses remote commands because sensor inputs disagree.

Begin at **Hazard 1**, **Evacuation 6**, **Alert 0**, and **Trace 0**. Reduce Evacuation by 1 each round. At 0, conditions become unsurvivable without immediate local rescue.

## Access

This network is wired only. Reaching an access point is part of the encounter.

- Engineering panel: closest, locked, Hard system access.
- Actuator jack: Average access and direct Local Bulkhead Controller, but does not reach atmosphere control without moving through the Gateway.
- Fiber trunk: difficult physical crawl; bypasses Authenticator.
- Plant console: Easy digital route inside a hazardous machinery room.

## Engineering sysop — Nadiya Chen

Use the Corporate Sysop profile. Nadiya begins unaware of hostile intent but sees sensor anomalies.

- If runners identify themselves and transmit credible casualty data, she delays Trace and grants 1 Boost to the Heimdall interaction.
- If they silence alarms, override interlocks, or issue unsafe commands, Alert rises and she Reinforces the affected ICE.
- She Sweeps before Tracing unless the intrusion damages something.
- She will not Burn the system or close a bulkhead on known civilians.

## Required solution chain

A stable rescue usually needs three commands:

1. Read pressure/leak location.
2. Isolate or correct atmosphere flow.
3. Open the safe-side bulkhead or run Stabilization Override.

Quick Commands can compress this. Players may skip diagnosis, but the first blind command advances Hazard on two Threat or Despair.

## Physical parallel scene

- Mechanics patches a local actuator and reduces one network command’s complexity.
- Athletics moves an unconscious civilian before the bulkhead cycles.
- Medicine triages smoke exposure, preventing the first Hazard-2 injury.
- Coordination reaches the fiber trunk through a maintenance crawl.
- Leadership prevents panic; success pauses Evacuation for one round.

## Heimdall as a scene partner

Heimdall is a barrier, not a sapient person, but its interactive routines can evaluate evidence. The runner may spend a maneuver to lower its strength by 1. Describe this as presenting sensor correlations, casualty counts, and a coherent stabilization proposal.

Grant an additional temporary reduction for exceptional truthful evidence or a verified engineering credential. False data may work, but Threat advances Hazard.

## Symbol spends

- Advantage can reveal a safe compartment, add Boost to a physical rescue, or pause Hazard.
- Triumph can isolate the damaged controller, preserve a complete telemetry copy, or open a safe route without depressurizing another zone.
- Threat can spread smoke, jam an actuator, or consume emergency oxygen.
- Despair should create a second endangered zone, not simply kill the trapped group.

## Outcomes

- **Stabilized rescue:** Hazard returns to 0 and civilians escape.
- **Evacuated but unstable:** Civilians live; the zone is lost and later operations lose a route.
- **System saved, people delayed:** Telemetry and infrastructure survive, but survivors suffer injuries and resentment.
- **Lockout:** Local controller remains accessible from the actuator jack. A physical/manual solution can open one bulkhead but cannot restore the whole zone.
- **Hazard 4:** The team must evacuate immediately. A final stabilization command can still save adjacent sectors at the cost of abandoning this one.

## Carry-forward state

Record civilian count, atmosphere status, telemetry evidence, Nadiya’s attitude, damaged controllers, and whether the team falsified safety data.
