## The everyday Network

Most people experience the Network as an always-present utility. PADs mediate communication, shopping, navigation, work, entertainment, credentials, and domestic devices. Augmented overlays make digital information part of ordinary physical space. This matters in play because a run rarely affects an abstract computer alone: it changes doors, queues, cameras, payment, transit, reputation, or the behavior of people who trust the display in front of them.

## SYNC and NBN

SYNC maintains the protocol and infrastructure that joins personal devices and large systems across Earth, Luna, orbit, and beyond. NBN sits at the center of the setting’s information economy: the organization that carries and analyzes communication also produces news and entertainment. For a GM, this makes metadata valuable. The content of a stolen file may be less dangerous than proof of who accessed it, where they were, which identity followed them through the city, and which audience can be shown a tailored version afterward.

## Why subnetworks exist

Businesses, arcologies, stations, and governments operate subnetworks with their own sysops and policies. These are often less consistently defended than the core infrastructure, making them practical adventure locations and stepping stones. A compromised small network should not automatically grant control of the global Network; proprietary verification and segmented authority keep the scope local unless the story establishes a bridge.

## Shadow networks

Unlicensed networks piggyback on legal infrastructure, hide behind forgotten hardware, or connect devices outside normal indexing. They serve runners, communities, criminals, activists, and people avoiding corporate surveillance. A shadow network may offer safety from ordinary tracing while creating dependence on the person who maintains it.

## BMI perspective

Full immersion is learned, not intuitive. A runner’s mind translates processes into spatial, sensory, or mythic experiences. Describe the same node differently for different users while keeping its mechanical identity stable. A sentry can appear as a hunting bird, an audit clerk, or a pressure behind the eyes; it remains the same sentry with the same strength and consequence.

The danger is physical as well as digital. Conventional programs can damage software and rigs; hostile systems and advanced ICE can hurt immersed users. Describe risks clearly enough that a player can make an informed choice.

## “God ICE,” ghosts, and uncertainty

Runner culture contains stories of self-aware code, surviving fragments of the Blackout, and metadata echoes of dead users. Treat these as setting beliefs until a campaign deliberately confirms one. They are excellent clues and unreliable witnesses: a familiar voice may be a person, an AI, a training artifact, a malicious imitation, or the runner’s own interface imposing narrative on raw data.

## Kaguya and Heinlein network texture

Kaguya’s systems exist in a place where software decisions move pressure gates, passenger bridges, ground vehicles, fuel lines, traffic corridors, and life support. Authority is deliberately split:

- public-terminal systems manage people and local access;
- occupation systems manage force and identity;
- landing-ring systems manage physical ground operations;
- isolated life-safety systems prioritize deterministic emergency control;
- Heinlein flight control issues final traffic authority.

That division prevents one successful check from controlling the whole port and gives physical specialists meaningful ways to obtain better access.

## Table principles

- Every digital command should have a visible human or physical consequence.
- Every high-security network should have a reason it cannot simply be switched off.
- Every runner should benefit when teammates reach a better jack, steal a credential, protect their body, or create time.
- Every failure should change route, cost, exposure, or safety—not erase the scenario.
- Evidence is not the same as public belief; credentials, provenance, and who controls the channel still matter.
