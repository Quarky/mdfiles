# THE APPROACH TO ELISTREE
## Living World Sequence — The Forest Notices You

### GM Intent

Do not describe this as a guarded border.

Do not tell the players that they have entered protected territory.

Do not mention patrols.

For several minutes, nothing openly confronts them.

The forest should simply begin behaving in ways that are difficult to dismiss.

Not supernatural.

Not obviously threatening.

Just enough that experienced travelers eventually start wondering:

**How many people are out there?**

The Wood Elves have already seen the party.

The High Elves farther ahead already know someone is coming.

The party does not know either of those things yet.

Let the realization grow naturally.

---

# THE FIRST TEN MINUTES

## Read Aloud

> The trail continues beneath the trees.
>
> At first, there is nothing particularly remarkable about it.
>
> Old pines crowd close to the path in places, their roots lifting the earth into uneven ridges. Ferns and low flowering plants gather wherever sunlight reaches through the canopy.
>
> Somewhere to your left, water moves over stone.
>
> A bird calls once from high in the branches.
>
> Farther ahead, another answers.
>
> You keep walking.
>
> The trees grow older.
>
> The path bends.
>
> And after a while, without being able to point to exactly when it happened, the road behind you begins to feel very far away.

after only a few minutes beneath the trees, a strange feeling begins to settle over you.
>
> It is difficult to explain.
>
> Nothing moves toward you.
>
> Nothing threatens you.
>
> Nothing even seems particularly unusual.
>
> But it feels as though the forest itself is watching you.
>
---

# SMALL THINGS

Nothing happens all at once.

Drop these into ordinary descriptions as the party travels.

Do not present them as a sequence of clues.

Some players may notice.

Some may not.

That is fine.

---

## A Branch That Wasn't Like That Before

A low branch forces someone near the rear of the group to move around it.

Several minutes later, if anyone happens to look back along the path, the branch is no longer hanging over the trail.

It has been pulled gently aside and hooked behind another limb.

No rope.

No cut.

Nothing dramatic.

Just moved.

If nobody looks back, do not mention it.

---

## The Bird

A short call comes from somewhere ahead.

Two notes.

Nothing unusual.

Several minutes later the same two notes sound far off to the party's right.

Then much later, from behind them.

Not an exact imitation.

Close enough that someone paying attention might begin listening for it.

If asked whether it sounds like a bird:

Yes.

Probably.

---

## The Animal That Doesn't Run

A small deer stands between the trees perhaps sixty feet from the trail.

It watches the party pass.

Then its ears turn toward something deeper in the forest.

The deer looks in that direction for several seconds.

Only then does it leave.

It does not flee from the party.

It moves away from whatever it heard.

The party hears nothing.

---

## The Path

At one fork, the trail is ambiguous.

Both directions look traveled.

If the party hesitates, give them a moment.

Then somewhere in the brush ahead:

**tap.**

Wood against wood.

Nothing else.

The branch on the correct path shifts slightly.

Wind?

Maybe.

If they take the wrong path anyway, let them travel perhaps a hundred yards.

Eventually they find a thin sapling lying neatly across the trail.

Not fallen.

Placed.

Beyond it, the undergrowth becomes inconveniently dense.

No threat.

No warning.

Simply:

**not this way.**

---

# THE FOREST NEVER QUITE GOES QUIET

Do not use the cliché where every bird suddenly stops.

That announces danger too clearly.

Instead, keep the forest alive.

Birds still call.

Water still runs.

Insects still move.

Wind still passes through the canopy.

But every so often something happens between those sounds.

A rustle that stops when a character turns toward it.

The faint scrape of leather against bark.

A single stone shifting somewhere uphill.

Leaves moving several seconds after the wind has passed.

Nothing that can be pointed at.

Nothing that proves anything.

---

# SOMETHING ON THE RIDGE

Eventually give one character — preferably whoever naturally watches their surroundings — a glimpse.

Not a full figure.

> For just a moment, through two trunks on the ridge above the trail, you see something pale.
>
> Vertical.
>
> Too narrow to be a tree.
>
> Then the path turns beneath another stand of pines and it is gone.

If they stop and search:

There is nothing there.

If they climb toward it:

They find disturbed moss on a stone.

One shallow indentation.

Nothing else.

Someone stood there.

Recently.

---

# THE SECOND GLIMPSE

Wait.

Do not immediately follow the first.

Let five or ten real minutes of play pass if conversation allows.

Then someone else notices movement.

This time lower.

Green cloth perhaps.

Brown leather.

A shape moving behind a tree.

The important thing:

It is somewhere completely different from the first sighting.

If the players assume one person is following them, this should begin changing that assumption.

---

# THE SOUND BEHIND THEM

Eventually:

> Somewhere behind you, there is the soft sound of a foot touching loose gravel.
>
> Just once.

If someone spins around:

Nothing.

The path is empty.

A butterfly moves between the trees.

Water continues running somewhere downhill.

---

# IF THE PARTY STARTS TALKING ABOUT BEING WATCHED

Do not confirm it.

Simply keep describing the forest normally.

This makes it stronger.

If someone shouts:

> "We know you're there!"

Nothing answers.

A bird calls somewhere far ahead.

Then the forest continues being a forest.

---

# IF THEY ACTIVELY SEARCH

Successful Perception or Survival checks should not simply reveal "three elves hiding there."

Instead they reveal pieces.

### Modest Success

A boot impression on damp earth beside a tree.

Only one.

No second footprint nearby.

Whoever made it stepped onto stone immediately afterward.

### Good Success

A fern has been compressed where someone crouched.

The leaves are still slowly lifting back into place.

### Strong Success

Someone catches the smallest movement high above.

A shape passes from one branch to another.

Not jumping.

Moving deliberately.

By the time they focus on it, foliage blocks the view.

### Exceptional Success

For perhaps one second:

An elf.

Green-brown clothing.

A bow.

Watching them.

The scout sees that they have been seen.

There is no panic.

No attack.

They simply take one step backward.

And somehow vanish into an arrangement of branches that should not have concealed anyone.

---

# A LIVING FOREST AROUND THEM

The watchers are not standing still waiting for the adventurers to reach an encounter trigger.

They are working.

Occasionally allow the party to perceive evidence of activity that has nothing to do with them.

A distant whistle.

Another answer.

Something large moving quickly several hundred feet away.

A Wood Elf briefly appearing on a ridge and then moving *away* from the party rather than following them.

The party might assume the scout has lost interest.

In reality, that scout is moving ahead with information.

---

# THE REALIZATION

Do not read this aloud unless the players genuinely put it together.

But this is what should slowly become apparent:

The party is not being stalked by one person.

They are passing through a network.

Someone sees them.

Moves.

Passes information.

Someone else takes over.

There may have been eyes on them for miles.

And they have no idea when it started.

---

# SOMETHING CHANGES

After enough time has passed, change the pattern.

This is the first genuine warning that they are approaching something important.

The recurring bird calls stop.

Not the birds.

Just **that particular call**.

Then:

One call ahead.

A pause.

One answer from the left.

Another farther behind.

And after that:

nothing.

The watchers are no longer moving around them.

They have taken positions.

---

# THE LAST HUNDRED YARDS

## Read Aloud

> The path climbs gradually between two enormous pines.
>
> Water can still be heard somewhere below you.
>
> Sunlight falls through the branches in long broken shafts.
>
> There is no gate ahead.
>
> No wall.
>
> Nothing to suggest that you have reached a border.
>
> But the feeling of movement in the woods has stopped.
>
> Completely.
>
> Not the animals.
>
> Not the wind.
>
> The other movement.
>
> The small sounds you have been hearing just behind everything else.
>
> Those are gone.
>
> For the first time in perhaps half an hour,
>
> whoever has been moving through the forest around you
>
> is standing still.

Pause.

Let them respond.

Then let them continue.

---

# THE HIGH ELF

Do not have a guard jump dramatically from concealment.

The party rounds another bend.

He is simply there.

## Read Aloud

> The trail curves around the base of an old pine.
>
> On the other side, a man stands beside the path.
>
> Tall.
>
> Pale-haired.
>
> Silver armor beneath a dark blue cloak.
>
> He is not blocking the road.
>
> He is simply waiting beside it.
>
> One hand rests loosely near his sword.
>
> Not gripping it.
>
> Waiting.
>
> You have no idea how long he has been there.

Give the players a second to look around.

Only if someone specifically scans the forest:

> Now that you know where to look, you notice another figure farther back among the trees.
>
> Then perhaps the curve of a bow high on the opposite ridge.
>
> And suddenly it becomes difficult not to wonder how many others you still haven't seen.

The High Elf studies the group.

Then:

> "You are approaching protected ground."
>
> His voice is calm.
>
> "The people beyond this forest are under our friendship and our defense."
>
> His eyes pass slowly across the party.
>
> "State your names and your business with Elistree."

---

# GM NOTE — DO NOT RUSH THIS CONVERSATION

This is now the guard encounter.

The Itich is **not here yet**.

Let this conversation become its own scene.

The party should have time to:

- explain themselves;
- ask about Elistree;
- realize the High Elves and Wood Elves are working together;
- decide whether they like Captain Aelthir;
- encounter Lyraen's suspicion;
- engage Selis's curiosity;
- perhaps start earning trust;
- notice that scouts continue quietly operating around the conversation.

This should feel like the conclusion of the forest approach.

For several minutes, the tension can actually begin dropping.

That is important.

The players have finally discovered who was watching them.

They have a name.

A face.

People they can negotiate with.

Let them begin believing they understand the situation.

**Then the patrol signal changes.**

And only then does the well-dressed Itich begin walking toward the clearing.
