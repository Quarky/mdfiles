# Elistree Session — The Forest Watches

## Session Purpose

This session introduces Elistree as a living settlement rather than simply another destination.

The party should experience three distinct changes in tone:

1. **The approach through the watched forest** — quiet tension, uncertainty, and the growing sense that the forest itself is observing them.
2. **The elven interception** — controlled social tension as High Elves openly confront the party while unseen Wood Elves continue operating throughout the forest.
3. **The sanctuary claimant** — after the party has had time to establish a relationship with the guards, a strangely articulate, heavily dressed Itich arrives and asks Elistree for sanctuary while warning the elves not to trust the party.

Only after this should the party enter Elistree itself.

The settlement should then feel like a sharp contrast to the approach: open, beautiful, populated, alive, moon-centered, and without defensive walls.

The defenses were the forest.

---

# GM Master Briefing

## Core Facts

- Elistree is **not walled**.
- The surrounding forest is protected by allied **High Elves and Wood Elves**.
- The outer forest patrols are separate from the settlement proper.
- The Wood Elves specialize in unseen observation, tracking, path control, and communication through the woods.
- The High Elves handle formal interception, diplomacy, and visible defense.
- Elistree itself is built around flowing water, gardens, pale stone, blue-violet roofs, moon symbols, shrines, bridges, markets, and woodland integrated directly into the settlement.
- Elistree should strongly reflect its **moon-goddess religious identity**.
- It is simultaneously a sanctuary, functioning settlement, religious center, refuge, and place where ordinary people live.

## The Itich

Ordinary Itich are primitive, highly animalistic, survival-focused insectoid creatures.

Their appearance is closer to a **horned ant humanoid** than a reptilian creature:

- insectoid exoskeleton;
- visible mandibles;
- short horns;
- no hair;
- simple hides and primitive materials;
- wood, bone, hide, scavenged stone;
- no sophisticated metalworking;
- capable of using scavenged metal tools and weapons.

The well-dressed Itich is immediately abnormal because of its intelligence, speech, self-control, clothing, and behavior.

The crystals infecting it are **hidden beneath its garments**.

The elves do not know this.

The detailed crystal system is essentially known only to the party at this stage.

Do not let the elves suddenly understand the infection.

# Scene 1 — The Forest Watches

## Tone

Do not begin with danger.

Begin with beauty.

Then add awareness.

The characters should feel watched before they understand who is watching them.

Do not make every sign obviously supernatural or obviously caused by scouts.

The important feeling is:

**It is as though the forest itself is watching them.**

---

## Opening Read-Aloud

> The trail narrows beneath older trees.
>
> Pines rise high overhead, their trunks dark and straight, with pale shafts of light slipping between the branches.
>
> Somewhere below the slope, unseen water moves over stone.
>
> Birds call occasionally from deep in the canopy.
>
> At first, there is nothing particularly unusual about the forest.
>
> But after several minutes beneath the trees, a feeling begins to settle over you.
>
> Nothing approaches.
>
> Nothing threatens you.
>
> You cannot point to any single thing that is wrong.
>
> And yet it feels as though the forest itself is watching you.
>
> Every rustle among the leaves seems just slightly too deliberate.
>
> Every distant birdcall sounds, for an instant, as though it might have been meant for someone else.
>
> When you look between the trees, there is never anyone there.
>
> The farther you walk, the stronger that sensation becomes.

Do not immediately call for checks.

Allow the players to react first.

---

# Living World — The First Signs

Use these intermittently.

Do not present them like numbered clues.

Let ordinary travel happen between them.

---

## The Birdcall

Two notes from somewhere ahead.

Later:

Two similar notes from the right.

Much later:

Again from somewhere behind them.

It could be a bird.

Probably.

---

## The Deer

A deer stands among the trees.

It notices the party.

But rather than running from them, its ears turn toward something deeper in the forest.

The animal stares into empty trees for several seconds.

Then quietly leaves in the opposite direction.

The party heard nothing.

---

## The Moved Branch

A branch hangs low across the path.

Someone ducks beneath it.

If anyone later looks back, the branch has been carefully hooked behind another limb.

No one heard anyone approach it.

---

## The Footprint

Someone spots a fresh boot impression beside the path.

Only one.

There should be another.

Instead the trail ends on a flat stone.

Whoever made it understood exactly where tracks would disappear.

---

## Movement Above

A character catches something moving high between the trees.

Not enough to identify.

A darker shape crossing a branch.

When they look directly at it:

nothing.

---

# If the Party Searches

Do not reveal a conveniently stationary scout.

Success reveals evidence rather than answers.

### Basic Success

A recently disturbed fern.

### Better Success

A depression where someone had been kneeling.

### Strong Success

Movement between two elevated positions.

### Exceptional Success

For perhaps a second:

A Wood Elf.

Green-brown clothing.

Bow in hand.

Watching them.

The scout realizes they have been spotted.

They do not attack.

They step backward—

and disappear into the forest.

---

# The Forest Is Working

The party is not merely being followed.

They are passing through a defensive network.

Different scouts rotate positions.

Messages move ahead of them.

Others inspect the trail behind them.

Some are determining whether the party itself is dangerous.

Others are determining whether **something is following the party**.

This distinction matters later.

---

# Scene 2 — The Pattern Changes

Eventually the repeated signals stop.

Not the forest.

Birds continue calling.

Wind continues moving.

Water continues running.

But the subtle movements that have accompanied the party disappear.

---

## Read-Aloud

> The path climbs between two enormous pines.
>
> Water still murmurs somewhere below.
>
> A bird calls far overhead.
>
> Wind passes through the canopy.
>
> But something has changed.
>
> The smaller sounds beneath those sounds—
>
> the occasional scrape,
>
> the almost-footstep,
>
> the movement that was never there when you turned toward it—
>
> are gone.
>
> Whoever has been moving through the forest around you
>
> has stopped moving.

Let them continue.

# Scene 3 — The High Elf Interception

There is no gate.

No barricade.

No wall.

They simply round another bend.

---

## Read-Aloud

> The trail curves around the trunk of an ancient pine.
>
> A man is standing beside the path.
>
> Tall.
>
> Pale-haired.
>
> Silver armor beneath a dark blue cloak.
>
> He is not blocking your way.
>
> He is simply waiting.
>
> One hand rests loosely near his sword.
>
> You have no idea how long he has been there.

If the party looks around:

> Now that you know where to look, you notice another figure farther back among the trees.
>
> Then, much higher, perhaps the curve of a bow against the branches.
>
> Once you see those two, it becomes very difficult not to wonder how many more you still haven't noticed.

---

# Captain Aelthir Vael

**High Elf Patrol Leader**

Aelthir is professional rather than hostile.

His purpose is to determine whether these strangers should be permitted closer to people under his protection.

He should not behave arrogantly merely because he has tactical advantage.

He knows he has tactical advantage.

That is why he does not need to demonstrate it.

---

## Aelthir's Opening

> "You are approaching protected ground."
>
> His gaze moves across the group.
>
> "The people beyond this forest are under our friendship and our defense."
>
> "State your names and your business with Elistree."

---

# Other Patrol NPCs

## Sentinel Lyraen

High Elf archer.

More suspicious than Aelthir.

She pays particular attention to:

- weapons;
- conflicting explanations;
- concealed equipment;
- anyone watching the trees more than the conversation.

Possible line:

> "I am less interested in whether you believe yourselves harmless than whether everyone behind you would agree."

---

## Lore-Warden Selis

High Elf scholar-observer.

Selis is especially interested when the party discusses:

- anomaly magic;
- strange creatures;
- drow activity;
- unusual enchantment;
- remote mental influence;
- crystal infection.

She does **not** already know the answers.

She asks precise questions.

> "You used the word infection. Infection of what?"

Or:

> "When you say they are connected, do you mean sympathetic magic, telepathy, or something else?"

---

## Wood Elf Scouts

Usually unseen.

Possible scouts include:

- Pellas
- Nym
- additional unnamed path-keepers

They continue working while the High Elves talk.

One might cross behind the party.

Another checks the road they came from.

A third moves farther ahead.

The conversation is important.

The rest of the forest does not stop because the party arrived.

# Scene 4 — Let the Conversation Breathe

Do not introduce the Itich immediately.

This conversation needs enough time to become real.

Ideally the party begins forming opinions about the patrol before anything interrupts them.

Topics may include:

- why they seek Elistree;
- where they came from;
- what danger follows them;
- the Council of Nine;
- the drow;
- the anomaly;
- the crystal threat;
- the transformed world;
- whether the party is asking for shelter, information, passage, or assistance.

---

# Sample Guard Conversation

## If the Party Is Cooperative

Aelthir:

> "Then speak plainly. Secrets are understandable. Surprises are less so."

If they explain unusual dangers:

> "I do not require that I understand everything you have seen."
>
> "I require enough understanding to decide whether bringing you closer endangers those under our protection."

---

## If the Party Mentions the Crystals

Aelthir:

> "You believe these crystals are dangerous?"

Let the party explain.

Then:

> "Can they spread?"

If they explain that the bacteria infect metal:

> "Metal specifically?"

Selis immediately notes this.

> "And the crystal remains dangerous after the organism that creates it is dead?"

That is a useful distinction the party can teach her.

Do not give Selis knowledge she has not learned from them.

---

## If They Discuss the Itich

The elves understand Itich broadly.

Primitive.

Insectoid.

Dangerous in sufficient numbers.

They do **not** understand what the party will soon encounter.

Aelthir may say:

> "There have been signs of Itich movement elsewhere."
>
> "Nothing I would call organized."

That sentence becomes more important shortly.

---

# Living-World Background During the Conversation

Use these between stretches of dialogue.

---

## Scout Rotation

A Wood Elf appears briefly behind Aelthir.

They exchange three quiet words.

The scout departs.

Another takes their place without the party noticing exactly when.

---

## Trail Check

A scout returns from the direction the party came from.

> "Nothing following them closely."

Aelthir:

> "Closely?"

The scout:

> "Not closely."

Then they leave again.

Do not explain that.

---

## Civilian Rerouting

Farther down another woodland path, a civilian carrying baskets begins approaching.

A Wood Elf emerges.

They exchange a few words.

The civilian turns around without complaint.

The patrol quietly redirects normal traffic around the encounter.

---

# Aelthir Begins Reaching a Decision

Eventually move toward:

> "Very well."
>
> "I think—"

Stop.

A signal comes from the woods.

# Scene 5 — The Signal Changes

One birdcall.

Pause.

Two rapid notes.

Every elf reacts.

Not dramatically.

Just enough.

Lyraen's eyes immediately move toward the trees.

Aelthir goes silent.

For the first time, one of the concealed Wood Elves openly enters the path.

---

## Scout

> "One approaching."

Aelthir:

> "Alone?"

> "Apparently."

Aelthir:

> "Armed?"

The scout hesitates.

> "Not in a way I understand."

Allow that sentence to sit.

---

# Scene 6 — The Well-Dressed Itich

The newcomer should be visually unmistakable as Itich.

Even beneath clothing:

- insectoid head;
- short horns;
- mandibles;
- chitin visible around the face and hands.

But almost everything else is covered.

Layered robes.

Wrapped limbs.

Gloves or bindings.

High collar or scarf.

The hidden crystal infection is beneath those clothes.

There should be **no visible crystal giveaway**.

---

## Read-Aloud

> Something moves between the trees.
>
> Slowly.
>
> Deliberately.
>
> A figure steps onto the trail.
>
> At first the clothing makes its shape difficult to understand.
>
> Layers of cloth cover almost the entire body.
>
> Arms wrapped.
>
> Torso concealed.
>
> Legs hidden beneath a long outer garment.
>
> Then you see the head.
>
> Short horns curve from a hard insectoid skull.
>
> Mandibles shift beneath the face.
>
> An Itich.
>
> It stops well short of the gathering.
>
> Not crouched.
>
> Not snarling.
>
> Not moving with the restless, animal urgency you would expect.
>
> It adjusts one sleeve.
>
> Looks toward the elves.
>
> And bows.

Pause.

Let players react.

Then:

> "I humbly request the sanctuary of Elistree."

Another pause.

It turns its head toward the party.

> "And I would advise you not to trust these travelers."

---

# Important GM Information

This should immediately feel wrong to the party.

Ordinary Itich intelligence is much more primitive and survival-driven.

The elves know that this behavior is strange.

They do not know why.

The party knows much more about the crystal threat than anyone else here.

Mage Sight does not simply reveal the entire answer.

The infection is physically concealed beneath the clothing.

Do not shortcut the scene.

---

# Immediate Reaction

Aelthir does not attack.

He raises one hand.

> "Remain where you are."

To the party:

> "You as well."

Then:

> "You speak."

The Itich:

> "I do."

Aelthir studies it.

> "That was not a question."

---

# The Sanctuary Claim

The word **sanctuary** matters.

Elistree's identity makes the claim difficult to dismiss casually.

Lyraen immediately objects.

> "Whatever else it is, it does not enter the settlement."

Selis:

> "It requested sanctuary."

Lyraen:

> "It is an Itich."

Selis:

> "Those are not mutually exclusive statements."

Aelthir:

> "Enough."

---

# Questioning the Itich

Aelthir:

> "Why do you require sanctuary?"

Possible answer:

> "Because there are those who would kill me."

Aelthir:

> "Who?"

The Itich slowly looks toward the party.

> "Perhaps them."

---

# If the Party Accuses It

Allow them to explain.

Possible claims:

- Itich should not be speaking like this.
- Something has altered it.
- It could be controlled.
- It is hiding something beneath the clothes.
- It may carry the crystal infection.
- It could be linked to the enemy.

The elves should take the claims seriously.

They should not automatically accept them as fact.

---

# Aelthir's Challenge

If the party demands immediate violence:

> "You arrived here asking strangers to judge you by your intentions."
>
> He looks toward the Itich.
>
> "It has now done the same."
>
> "Give me evidence sufficient to make those requests different."

This is not Aelthir defending the Itich.

It is Aelthir being consistent.

---

# The Itich Defends Itself

Keep it calm.

That is what makes it disturbing.

> "They describe dangers only they understand."

> "They ask you to fear what only they claim to recognize."

> "I have harmed no one here."

Then:

> "I am asking only to live."

---

# If Asked to Reveal What Is Beneath Its Clothing

The Itich resists politely.

> "Must a petitioner surrender every dignity before sanctuary is considered?"

This immediately makes the situation harder.

If the elves insist:

The Itich may begin bargaining.

Do not necessarily expose the crystals immediately unless useful for the session pacing.

---

# Scene 7 — The Forest Mobilizes

While everyone argues, the elves react intelligently.

Wood Elf scouts begin moving.

One backtracks the Itich's route.

Another checks for additional Itich.

Another watches the party.

Another returns toward Elistree.

The entire defense network begins responding.

---

# Scout Report

Eventually:

> "No others."

Aelthir:

> "You are certain?"

Scout:

> "No."

Aelthir gives the scout a look.

Scout:

> "I am certain I found no others."

That distinction matters.

# Possible Sanctuary Outcomes

## Outcome A — Outer Sanctuary

The strongest default.

Neither the Itich nor the party gets unrestricted movement immediately.

The Itich is taken to an outer sheltered location under guard.

The party is escorted separately into Elistree.

Investigation continues.

This preserves the Itich for later.

---

## Outcome B — Party Earns Trust

If the party presents convincing information and behaves responsibly:

The Itich is isolated.

The party is escorted into Elistree with considerably greater trust.

---

## Outcome C — The Itich Gains Sympathy

If the party becomes aggressive or inconsistent:

The elves become more cautious about the party.

The Itich receives provisional sanctuary under supervision.

This creates an excellent later complication.

---

## Outcome D — Representative Comes Out

If you want to prolong the woodland scene:

Elistree sends a priest, mediator, or senior representative to the patrol location rather than allowing anyone farther inward.

# The Feeling of Elistree

Elistree is not silent.

It is lived in.

Use background activity constantly.

- laundry hanging beside homes;
- gardeners tending medicinal plants;
- refugees repairing temporary accommodation;
- traders arguing over inventory;
- acolytes carrying offerings;
- children feeding fish from bridges;
- musicians practicing;
- people carrying water;
- patrols passing without disrupting ordinary life;
- carpenters repairing a home;
- boats carrying goods through the waterways;
- evening lanterns being prepared before sunset.

The settlement should not feel like a theme park waiting for adventurers.

# Elistree at Night

Elistree changes character after sunset.

The moon identity becomes much stronger.

Lanterns illuminate paths and bridges.

Moon symbols catch reflected light.

Shrines become active.

Water reflects dozens of small lights.

Music carries farther.

Patrols become less visible rather than more visible.

The Wood Elves become almost impossible to see.

# The Evening Moon Rite

This is an optional major social scene.

Residents gather in the sanctuary.

It is not necessarily a formal church service.

Some pray.

Some watch.

Some use the gathering socially.

Some refugees attend because they are grateful for sanctuary despite not sharing the faith.

Use it to show Elistree's culture rather than lecture about theology.

---

# Ongoing Itich Thread

The well-dressed Itich should remain present in the background even if physically separated from the party.

Rumors spread.

Different people interpret the event differently.

Examples:

> "They say it spoke better Elvish than I do."

> "My cousin says there were crystals under its clothes."

This should be wrong unless somebody actually saw them.

> "No. I heard the travelers brought it with them."

Also wrong.

> "The patrol captain wouldn't let it inside."

Possibly true.

> "Someone says it asked for sanctuary."

True.

Rumors should mutate rapidly.

---

# What Makes the Itich Disturbing

Do not rely on monstrous appearance.

Everyone already knows Itich look alien.

The wrongness comes from behavior.

It sits still.

It waits patiently.

It asks careful questions.

It notices social cues.

It speaks diplomatically.

It may apologize.

Those are the things an Itich should not normally be doing.

---

# Hidden Truth — GM Only

Beneath the clothing:

the crystal infection is present.

The crystals remain concealed.

The bacteria are responsible for creating additional crystal material.

Killing the bacteria prevents further growth but does not neutralize crystals already present.

Existing crystals remain potential remote-access points.

Destroying or dispelling individual crystal structures is necessary to completely sever those connections.

The party understands this far better than Elistree does.

That knowledge gap should drive the tension.

---

# Optional Investigation — Examining the Itich

If the elves eventually allow examination, make it controlled.

Not:

"Roll Arcana; you understand everything."

Different methods reveal different information.

### Medicine

Something is physically abnormal beneath the garments.

### Arcana

Multiple effects appear layered rather than a single curse.

### Psychic Examination

Something connected to the creature may notice the examination.

### Attina

Attina possesses the most practical experience dealing with the infection and its semi-astral bacterial component.

Her knowledge should matter.

# GM Conversation Reference — Selis

Selis tends to reduce dramatic statements into useful questions.

Party:

> "These crystals let something control you from miles away."

Selis:

> "Control continuously, or establish access?"

Party explains.

Selis:

> "Good."
>
> "That distinction matters."

# GM Conversation Reference — The Well-Dressed Itich

Keep responses brief.

Too much eloquence makes it theatrical.

The disturbing quality is precision.

## Sanctuary

> "I ask protection."

## Why Elistree?

> "Because I believed you might listen before killing me."

## About the Party

> "They are afraid."

## Are They Wrong to Be Afraid?

Pause.

> "No."

That is potentially one of its strongest answers.

## Are You Dangerous?

> "Yes."

If everyone reacts:

> "You asked whether I was dangerous."
>
> "Not whether I intended violence."

## What Are You?

> "Itich."

If pushed:

> "That answer was once sufficient."

# Session Pacing

## Act I — Watched

20–30 minutes.

Let the forest breathe.

Do not hurry.

## Act II — Intercepted

20–40 minutes.

Allow meaningful conversation.

## Act III — Sanctuary Claim

30–60 minutes.

The social centerpiece.

## Act IV — Entering Elistree

10–20 minutes.

A tonal release.

## Act V — Exploration

Open-ended.

Allow the party to choose which Elistree scene to visit.
