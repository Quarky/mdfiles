# The Stranger at the Moonlit Path
## Living-World Approach to Elistree

### GM Purpose

This scene should make the approach to Elistree feel like entering a place that is already aware of the party long before the party reaches it.

The forest is not magical in the simplistic sense that every tree is literally watching them.

It is watched.

Constantly.

The Wood Elves know these trails intimately. High Elf patrols control the final approaches. Signals pass through the woods without the party initially understanding what they mean.

The party should gradually realize:

**They did not discover the guards.**

**The guards decided when to reveal themselves.**

Do not rush to the Itich.

The first half of this scene belongs to the forest and the elves.

Let the party speak with the guards for several minutes. Let suspicion rise or ease. Let them begin forming opinions about one another.

Then introduce the Itich.

---

# Scene 1 — The Forest Begins Watching

## First Impression

The road toward Elistree gradually stops feeling like a road.

There are no walls.

No border markers declaring that the settlement begins here.

No fortified gate.

Instead, cultivated land gives way to older forest.

The canopy becomes thicker.

The path becomes narrower.

Yet it is clearly maintained.

Branches that should obstruct the road have been cut back.

Loose stones have been moved from the center of the trail.

Several fallen logs have small portions carved away to make walking easier.

Someone maintains this route.

But there is no one here.

At first.

---

## Read Aloud — The Change

> The forest does not change all at once.
>
> There is no boundary stone.
>
> No gate.
>
> No wall.
>
> Just a gradual narrowing of the path beneath older and older trees.
>
> The sounds of the world behind you begin to fade.
>
> Wind moves through the canopy.
>
> Water can be heard somewhere deeper among the trees.
>
> Birds call occasionally.
>
> Nothing seems wrong.
>
> And yet, after several minutes, you begin to get the uncomfortable impression that you are no longer traveling through an empty forest.
>
> You are traveling through an occupied one.

---

# Signs of Observation

Do not reveal every sign immediately.

Scatter them naturally through travel.

The goal is not to scream "ambush."

The goal is to make the players slowly notice that intelligent people are moving around them.

---

## Sign 1 — The Branch

A low branch hangs partly across the trail.

One character may need to duck beneath it.

A few minutes later, if anyone looks behind them:

The branch is no longer hanging over the trail.

It has been tied back against the trunk.

No one heard anyone move it.

---

## Sign 2 — The Birdcall

A short birdcall comes from somewhere ahead.

Three notes.

A few moments later the same call answers from somewhere behind the party.

Then another answers from their right.

It could simply be birds.

A character familiar with forests may notice something slightly wrong.

The timing is too clean.

The calls are too consistent.

They are signals.

---

## Sign 3 — The Tracks

A perceptive character spots a print beside the path.

Light boot.

Narrow sole.

Fresh.

It disappears after two steps onto moss.

There should be another print.

There isn't.

The person who made it knew exactly where not to step.

---

## Sign 4 — Movement

Someone sees a figure between two trees.

Not clearly.

Just pale cloth.

Or the edge of a cloak.

Perhaps silver reflecting sunlight.

When they look directly—

nothing.

Later, another character catches darker movement high among the branches.

This time green and brown.

Two different groups are watching them.

---

# What Is Actually Happening

Wood Elf scouts detected the party well before this scene began.

They have been shadowing them from concealed positions.

Once the party's direction toward Elistree became obvious, word was quietly passed ahead.

A High Elf patrol has moved into position farther along the route.

The Wood Elves remain in the trees.

The High Elves will conduct the actual challenge.

The scouts are not preparing an attack unless the party forces one.

They are doing three things:

- identifying the travelers;
- determining whether anyone is following them;
- giving the High Elves time to prepare.

---

# The Forest Responds to the Party

Make the forest patrol feel reactive.

## If the Party Stops

Nothing attacks.

Nothing confronts them.

The watchers simply stop moving as well.

After a long silence, somewhere far ahead:

**three birdcalls.**

A moment later:

**two from behind.**

The patrol is communicating.

---

## If the Party Searches the Trees

They may find signs.

They should almost never simply catch a scout standing behind a tree.

Successful checks reveal:

- a branch disturbed recently;
- a boot mark on a stone;
- an arrow scratch against bark;
- grass compressed where someone briefly knelt;
- a scrap of dark green cloth caught on thorn.

Exceptional success may allow a character to actually glimpse a Wood Elf moving to another position.

The scout does not attack.

They simply disappear deeper into the forest.

---

## If the Party Calls Out

There is initially no answer.

Let several seconds pass.

Then:

> "Keep walking."

The voice comes from somewhere off the road.

It is impossible to immediately tell where.

If asked who is speaking:

> "Someone ensuring you reach the next conversation alive."

Then silence again.

---

## If the Party Becomes Aggressive

The forest changes instantly.

Birdsong stops.

The party still does not necessarily see anyone.

But every experienced warrior should recognize the feeling:

Weapons have just been readied.

Do not start combat automatically.

Give them the opportunity to reconsider.

---

# Scene 2 — The High Elf Guard

Eventually the path opens into a small natural clearing.

Nothing about it looks fortified.

That is important.

No palisade.

No gate.

No wall.

There does not need to be one.

This clearing is merely the place the patrol has chosen to speak.

---

## Read Aloud — First Contact

> The trail opens beneath a broad old tree.
>
> Someone is standing in the center of the path.
>
> You are certain he was not there a moment ago.
>
> A High Elf.
>
> Silver armor beneath a dark blue cloak.
>
> One hand rests near the hilt of his weapon, but does not draw it.
>
> Another High Elf stands farther back beside the trees.
>
> Then you notice a third.
>
> And only after seeing the third do you realize something else.
>
> These are simply the elves who have chosen to be visible.
>
> Somewhere in the forest behind and around you, a bird gives two quiet notes.
>
> The elf in the road waits until the sound stops.
>
> Then he speaks.
>
> "You approach lands under our protection."
>
> His eyes move across each of you before returning to the group.
>
> "State your names, your purpose, and your business with Elistree."

---

# Captain Aelthir Vael

Aelthir should not behave like a hostile border guard.

He is controlled.

Professional.

Cautious.

His job is to determine whether these strangers should be allowed anywhere near the people under his protection.

He does not need to dominate the conversation.

Let the PCs talk.

Let him listen.

When someone else talks, he watches the others.

---

## Opening Questions

Aelthir begins simply.

> "Your names."

Then:

> "Why are you traveling to Elistree?"

Then:

> "Who told you where to find us?"

This question matters.

Elistree is not necessarily secret, but its protected approaches are not casually advertised.

---

## Follow-Up Questions

Use only those appropriate to what the players say.

Do not run this as an interrogation checklist.

Possible questions:

> "Who knows you came here?"

> "Is anyone following you?"

> "What do you intend to ask of Elistree?"

> "How long do you intend to remain?"

> "Are you carrying anything dangerous that our people should know about?"

> "Have you had dealings with the drow?"

> "With Lolth's servants?"

> "With anything you would describe as an anomaly?"

If the PCs mention the crystals, infection, Elder Brains, or other major threats, Aelthir becomes much more attentive.

He does not suddenly know what the crystals are.

He asks questions.

> "You said infection. Infection of what?"

> "Does it spread between people?"

> "Can it be detected?"

> "Can it be removed?"

> "Have any of you been exposed?"

---

# The Wood Elves During the Conversation

The Wood Elves do not vanish from the scene just because the High Elves are talking.

They continue working.

This makes the encounter feel alive.

During the conversation:

- one scout silently crosses behind the party;
- another relocates from one tree to another;
- a faint birdcall receives an answer farther away;
- someone checks the trail behind the party;
- another scout watches the canopy rather than the PCs;
- one scout examines the party's tracks.

The elves are also checking whether something followed them.

A character who notices this may realize:

**The patrol is not only worried about the party.**

They are worried about what the party might have brought with them.

---

# Sentinel Lyraen

Lyraen is considerably less trusting than Aelthir.

She speaks rarely.

When she does, her questions are direct.

If the party gives an elaborate explanation:

> "That was a great many words."
>
> She looks toward the speaker.
>
> "Which part should concern me?"

If someone claims they mean no harm:

> "Everyone says that before being allowed closer."

If someone becomes irritated:

> "Being annoyed is permitted."
>
> "Proceeding is not."

---

# Lore-Warden Selis

Selis is interested in the strange details.

She carries writing materials and quietly records unusual statements.

She is especially interested in descriptions of:

- anomaly magic;
- the crystal infection;
- unfamiliar creatures;
- strange magical behavior;
- anything involving remote mental influence.

She does not act as though she understands these subjects already.

Often she asks the smallest, most useful question.

> "You said it changed afterward."
>
> "What changed first?"

Or:

> "When you say connected, do you mean magically or mentally?"

---

# The Conversation Should Have Time to Breathe

Do not introduce the Itich immediately.

This is important.

The players should have enough time to:

- explain themselves;
- form an opinion of Aelthir;
- notice the hidden Wood Elves;
- decide whether they trust the patrol;
- potentially begin discussing Elistree;
- possibly relax.

Ideally the conversation reaches the point where Aelthir is beginning to make a decision.

Perhaps:

> "I believe we can escort you—"

Then he stops.

Not because he has noticed the Itich.

Because someone else has.

---

# The Signal

A bird calls from deeper in the woods.

One note.

A pause.

Two quick notes.

Every elf in the clearing reacts.

Very slightly.

Lyraen turns first.

Aelthir does not draw his sword.

One of the concealed Wood Elves appears on the edge of the clearing for the first time.

That alone should tell the party something unusual is happening.

The scout speaks quietly.

> "One approaching."

Aelthir:

> "Alone?"

The scout pauses.

> "Apparently."

Aelthir:

> "Armed?"

Another pause.

> "Not in any way I understand."

That should be the first warning.

---

# Scene 3 — The Well-Dressed Itich

The party knows what an Itich normally looks and behaves like.

Primitive.

Animalistic.

Tribal.

Driven primarily by basic survival instincts.

What comes walking down the trail does not behave that way.

---

## Read Aloud — The Stranger

> Movement appears between the trees.
>
> Slow.
>
> Deliberate.
>
> A figure steps onto the path.
>
> For several seconds the clothing makes it difficult to understand what you are looking at.
>
> Layered cloth.
>
> A long outer garment.
>
> Gloves.
>
> Wrappings around the throat.
>
> Almost every part of the body covered.
>
> Then the shape of the head becomes clear.
>
> The posture.
>
> The hands.
>
> An Itich.
>
> But it is walking calmly.
>
> Upright.
>
> Carefully.
>
> It stops well outside weapon range.
>
> Looks first at the elves.
>
> Then at you.
>
> And bows.

Pause here.

Let the players react before it speaks.

Then:

> "I humbly request the sanctuary of Elistree."

Pause again.

Its eyes move toward the party.

> "And I advise you not to trust these travelers."

Do not immediately explain anything.

End the beat there.

---

# What the Elves Know

Almost nothing.

This is crucial.

They know what Itich are in the broadest possible sense.

They know the normal creatures are primitive and dangerous.

They do **not** know the detailed crystal system.

They do not know what the party knows.

The Itich's crystals are concealed beneath its garments.

Nothing obvious visually reveals the infection.

This means the elves cannot simply look at it and agree with the party.

---

# Aelthir's Immediate Reaction

Aelthir does not attack it.

He also does not allow it closer.

He raises one hand.

> "Remain where you are."

To the party:

> "You will do the same."

Then to the Itich:

> "You speak our language."

The Itich:

> "I do."

Aelthir:

> "That was an observation, not a question."

This is the first moment Aelthir visibly becomes disturbed.

---

# The Sanctuary Problem

The Itich has chosen its words deliberately.

**Sanctuary.**

That matters here.

If Elistree claims to shelter refugees and endangered people, the elves cannot simply dismiss a request because the petitioner is strange or frightening.

Selis quietly says:

> "It invoked sanctuary."

Lyraen:

> "It is an Itich."

Selis:

> "Those statements do not contradict one another."

Aelthir, sharply:

> "Enough."

He looks back to the stranger.

> "Why do you require sanctuary?"

---

# Possible Itich Responses

Do not make it rant.

The disturbing thing is how calm it is.

> "Because there are those who would kill me."

If asked who:

It looks toward the party.

> "Perhaps them."

If asked why:

> "They believe I am something that should not exist."

If asked whether this is true:

A slight pause.

> "That is a philosophical question."

That answer should bother everyone.

---

# Let the Party Argue

This is where the scene becomes interesting.

Do not resolve it for them.

The party possesses information the elves do not.

They may say:

- Itich do not speak like this.
- Something is controlling it.
- It may be infected.
- Its clothing is concealing something.
- It cannot be trusted.
- It is connected to the enemy.

The elves should listen.

But they should also require evidence.

---

# Aelthir to the Party

If they demand the Itich be killed:

> "You arrived asking us to trust your intentions."
>
> He gestures toward the stranger.
>
> "Now it asks the same."
>
> "Give me a reason to treat one request differently from the other."

If they explain that ordinary Itich cannot behave this way:

> "I had already noticed."

If they mention hidden crystals:

Aelthir looks toward the Itich.

> "Is there something beneath those garments?"

The Itich:

> "There is a body."

Aelthir:

> "That was not my question."

---

# The Itich Turns the Argument Back

The Itich should not win automatically.

But it should be intelligent enough to create doubt.

> "They arrived armed."

> "They describe enemies only they understand."

> "They ask you to fear what only they claim to recognize."

Then:

> "I ask only to be allowed to live."

This creates the actual problem.

Not whether the Itich is suspicious.

It obviously is.

The question is whether suspicion is enough to deny sanctuary.

---

# Lyraen's Position

Lyraen distrusts it immediately.

> "Whatever else we decide, it does not enter the settlement."

She looks at Aelthir.

> "Not yet."

This is a reasonable compromise and prevents the elves from behaving foolishly.

---

# Selis's Position

Selis wants to understand it.

> "If what the travelers claim is true, killing it teaches us nothing."

That may make the players uncomfortable.

Good.

---

# Aelthir's Position

Aelthir makes the practical decision.

> "No one proceeds farther until I understand what has walked into my forest."

Then he starts issuing orders.

This is where the living world becomes visible.

---

# The Forest Mobilizes

The forest suddenly becomes much busier than the party realized.

Wood Elves begin appearing.

Not dozens.

Enough.

One moves back down the trail the Itich used.

Another takes a position overlooking the clearing.

Two more quietly redirect anyone traveling toward the area.

A runner leaves toward Elistree.

Another patrol begins checking the woods behind the Itich.

The elves are now investigating:

- where it came from;
- whether it came alone;
- whether anyone followed it;
- whether its trail is normal;
- whether there are other Itich nearby.

The entire defensive network reacts to one strange arrival.

---

# Background Living-World Events

Use these during pauses in conversation.

Do not announce them as encounters.

They make the forest feel occupied.

## 1. The Returning Scout

A Wood Elf appears beside Aelthir without warning.

> "No second set of tracks."

Aelthir:

> "That doesn't mean it was alone."

The scout:

> "No."

Then disappears back into the trees.

---

## 2. The Civilian Traveler

A distant elf appears on the trail carrying a basket.

A scout intercepts them before they reach the clearing.

A short whispered conversation follows.

The civilian turns around and leaves.

The defensive system is quietly rerouting traffic.

---

## 3. The Animals

A deer approaches a nearby stream.

It notices the gathering.

It freezes.

One Wood Elf gives a soft click of the tongue.

The deer changes direction and disappears into the woods.

It is a tiny thing.

But it shows familiarity between the forest patrol and the environment.

---

## 4. The Changing Watch

A Wood Elf who has been covering one angle quietly leaves.

Another replaces them.

The party may realize that the elves are maintaining a watch rotation even during this conversation.

Life did not stop because the PCs arrived.

---

## 5. Message From Elistree

Eventually a messenger returns.

The response should depend on how the scene develops.

Possible message:

> "Elistree will hear the sanctuary claim."
>
> "Neither group enters freely."
>
> "Bring them to the outer sanctuary."

Or:

> "The claimant is to remain outside the settlement."
>
> "The travelers may proceed under escort."

Or:

> "A representative is coming here."

This allows the GM to move the story wherever needed.

---

# GM Guidance — Maintaining Tension

The tension should come from intelligence and uncertainty.

Not immediate violence.

The elves are competent.

The party has information the elves lack.

The Itich knows enough to exploit that gap.

Nobody needs to behave foolishly.

The elves should neither:

- immediately believe the Itich;
- nor immediately believe the party.

They investigate.

They contain.

They listen.

That makes the situation more dangerous because the Itich cannot simply be solved by shouting:

**"Kill it."**

---

# Key Timing

Use roughly this progression:

### Beat 1 — 5–10 minutes
The forest watches the party.

### Beat 2 — 5–15 minutes
The High Elf patrol challenges them.

### Beat 3 — as long as useful
Actual conversation with the guards.

Let relationships begin forming here.

### Beat 4
The unusual scout signal interrupts the discussion.

### Beat 5
The Itich appears.

### Beat 6
The sanctuary claim changes the entire nature of the encounter.

Do not have the Itich waiting beside the guards when the PCs arrive.

Its arrival should feel like a new problem intruding upon an existing scene.

---

# Transition Toward Elistree

Regardless of the immediate outcome, the party should leave this scene understanding something important about Elistree:

It is not protected primarily by walls.

Its protection begins miles before the settlement.

The road is watched.

The woods communicate.

Travelers are noticed.

People are redirected without ever seeing the patrol that redirected them.

And by the time someone reaches Elistree itself, the settlement often already knows they are coming.

As the party eventually resumes travel:

> The path continues through the trees.
>
> For the first time, you can occasionally see pale stone through the canopy ahead.
>
> A bridge.
>
> A silver-blue roof.
>
> The glimmer of moving water.
>
> Somewhere farther into the woods, another bird gives three short notes.
>
> This time you know exactly what it means.
>
> You have been seen.
>
> And word of your arrival is traveling ahead of you.
