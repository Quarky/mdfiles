# Official Public Fiction — Reading Map for Table Tone

This index is for **how people behave**, not for copying prose.

## The Gorgon and the Guildpact
Official Magic Story.

What to study:
- how guild representatives bring grievances;
- how Jace's authority functions socially;
- how Lavinia translates impossible constitutional responsibility into actual administration;
- how personal impatience and institutional formality coexist.

## Project Lightning Bug
Official Magic Story.

What to study:
- undercity atmosphere;
- Ral Zarek's voice;
- how planeswalker secrecy intersects guild politics;
- how Ravnica can feel urban even in sunken or ruined spaces.

## Catching Up
Official Magic Story.

What to study:
- the burden of constant delegations and unresolved guild demands;
- the smell, noise, and interpersonal irritation of political work;
- how the Living Guildpact role creates an endless queue of conflicts rather than a solved city.

## War of the Spark: Ravnica stories
Use only as a later-era overlay.

What to study:
- guild cooperation under existential crisis;
- how long-standing distrust persists even when everyone faces the same enemy;
- how guild leaders and ordinary people react when civic assumptions collapse.

Do not import War-era political facts into the V2 baseline without labeling the time jump.

## The Secretist material
Official Return to Ravnica-era fiction provides useful examples of streets, guild representatives, investigation, and the feel of institutions.

Use for tone and behavior while checking era-specific leadership against the chosen campaign date.

## Reading practice

For every story, extract four things:

1. **physical city detail**;
2. **how a guild member speaks when not explaining lore**;
3. **what ordinary work happens around the plot**;
4. **what institutional pressure exists even when nobody is fighting**.

Those four categories improve table play more than plot summary.
