# Selesnya Conclave — Deep Dossier

## The Selesnyan self-image

Selesnya offers something genuinely powerful in Ravnica: a community where food, housing, spiritual meaning, protection, companionship, and purpose are shared.

Its central promise is **you do not have to be alone**.

Its danger is the price of harmony when individual desire conflicts with collective need.

## Vernadi

The Conclave is organized into local communes called **vernadi**, each centered on a great tree and guided by a dryad called a **voda**.

This gives the guild a profoundly local scale. Members can know their spiritual leader personally. Children grow up around the same courtyards, teachers, training grounds, healers, kitchens, and gardens.

Vernadi should feel lived-in, not merely sacred.

## Mat'Selesnya and Trostani

The Worldsoul, Mat'Selesnya, grounds the Conclave's belief that life is interconnected. Trostani embodies guild leadership in intimate connection with that ideal.

This produces a culture where social belonging and spiritual truth reinforce one another. Leaving a vernadi can therefore feel like more than changing affiliation—it can feel like severing connection to family, community, and the world itself.

## Military capacity

Selesnya is peaceful in aspiration, not helpless.

Ordinary members train. The Conclave fields militia, archers, riders, elementals, Ledev guardians, and other defenders. Its strength is numbers, organization, local knowledge, and willingness to mobilize whole communities when threatened.

## Sensory palette

Leaf shade over stone. warm food. wood smoke. wet soil. flowers. wool. animal fur. incense made from herbs rather than heavy resins.

Hooves on paving. communal singing. training calls. children. birds. tools in gardens. many quiet conversations happening at once.

## Why people join

Not because they were "brainwashed," but because the offer is attractive.

A lonely worker gains a home. A poor family gains food security. A frightened citizen gains people who will stand beside them. A spiritual seeker gains a worldview where their life matters as part of something larger.

The pressure becomes visible later, when someone wants to choose something the community believes harms the whole.

## Interaction habits

**Golgari:** shared belief in life's interconnectedness, deep conflict over death and decay.  
**Gruul:** both resist sterile urban domination; Gruul sees Selesnya as domestication.  
**Simic:** cooperation on ecology and health; distrust when adaptation becomes engineering.  
**Boros:** natural defensive allies, different command cultures.  
**Orzhov:** rival models of obligation—communal duty versus debt hierarchy.  
**Rakdos:** mass community provides both audience and ideological target.

## How to play a Selesnyan NPC

Make warmth real.

Good dialogue:
- "Eat first. Then tell me why you came."
- "You keep saying 'my choice' as though choices stop affecting people when they leave your mouth."
- "We can make room for you. The question is whether you can make room for us."

## Scene pressure

A vernadi shelters families displaced by an industrial accident. City officials want the courtyard reopened as an emergency transit route. The voda refuses to move people until permanent housing is guaranteed.

Thousands need the route. Dozens need the shelter. Both are true.
