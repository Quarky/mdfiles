This is a **new parallel version**. The existing `Ravnica/` tree is intentionally frozen and is not edited by this project.

V2 exists because a usable Ravnica guide cannot stop at guild slogans and one-line location summaries. The GM and players need enough cultural, sensory, political, economic, architectural, historical, and interpersonal context to inhabit the plane rather than merely recognize its faction symbols.

## Source-first rule

Use published Ravnica material before generating new lore.

Priority sources in this project include:

- *Guildmasters' Guide to Ravnica*;
- official Planeswalker's Guides;
- official Living Guildpact stories including *The Gorgon and the Guildpact*, *Project Lightning Bug*, *Catching Up*, and *Jace's Origin: Absent Minds*;
- official original-trilogy retrospective material;
- official *War of the Spark: Ravnica* stories;
- campaign material such as *The Glass Run* only where campaign canon is intended.

V2 labels the boundary between **canon**, **canon-supported inference**, **table synthesis**, **campaign canon**, and **original fiction**. Generated content fills gaps; it does not silently overwrite published lore.

## V2 operating standard

Every major setting note should answer five questions:

1. **What does this place or institution do on an ordinary day?**
2. **What does it look, sound, smell, and feel like from street level?**
3. **Who depends on it, who resents it, and who profits from it?**
4. **How do multiple guilds touch the same problem differently?**
5. **What changes if the PCs linger, interfere, make a promise, break a rule, or attract attention?**

## Default era

The baseline is the period in which **Jace Beleren is the Living Guildpact** and the guilds operate in a precarious peace. Jace's absence matters because the city has not lost the idea of the Guildpact; it has lost reliable access to the person who embodies it.

Earlier and later political states are overlays, not blended into a timeless present:

- Original Ravnica / Guildpact / Dissension

## The shortest correct mental model

Ravnica is not "ten factions sharing a city." It is a city whose **law, food, plumbing, debt, medicine, military force, waste, entertainment, research, religion, parks, labor, information, transit, and social identity are historically entangled with ten rival guilds**.

Most residents do not wake up thinking about color philosophy. They wake up wondering whether the lift works, whether the landlord raised the rent, whether a Boros cordon means danger, whether an Azorius notice invalidated a permit, whether the Golgari delivery arrived, whether an Orzhov collector is waiting downstairs, and whether tonight's Rakdos show is worth the risk.

The guilds become real when they are felt through those ordinary pressures.

## Canon before invention

Before inventing a location, guild job, faction, or political relationship, check the source atlas and source map. V2 should use official names, roles, institutions, and story behavior wherever they exist. Synthesis is for the enormous amount of daily urban life that official sources necessarily leave undescribed.

## Source Note: 00-GM/Immersion-Standard.md

# GM Immersion Standard

A scene is not finished when it has a location name and an encounter. A Ravnica scene should feel as though it existed before the PCs arrived and will keep moving after they leave.

## The five-layer description

When the party enters somewhere new, describe at least three of these layers immediately and let the remaining layers emerge through play.

### 1. Skyline and enclosure

Ravnica is vertically crowded. A street may have five visible eras of construction: an old arch swallowed by an apartment block, a bridge carrying another road above, cables and pipes hung underneath that bridge, roof gardens above that, and a tower vanishing into haze.

Ask: **what blocks the horizon? what hangs overhead? what older thing is the current city built on?**

### 2. Movement

Crowds should move with purpose. Couriers squeeze through. Pack beasts pull carts. Lift cages rattle up facades. Centaurs take wider lanes. Goblins use maintenance shortcuts. Boros patrols create temporary currents in foot traffic. An Azorius inspection can dam an entire avenue into angry queues.

### 3. Sound

Use overlapping sound rather than one signature noise: iron-rimmed wheels, shouted prices, distant bells, steam hiss, roc cries, argument, choral Selesnyan song from a courtyard, Izzet crackle from a conduit, a Rakdos drumline three blocks away, a judge's amplified proclamation, rain in gutters, a troll bellowing from a freight lane.

### 4. Smell and physical sensation

Stone after rain. Coal smoke. hot metal. yeast and coffee. fungal broth. wet greenery. lamp oil. incense. ozone. animal musk. perfume over funeral wax. sewer damp rising from a grate. The city is tactile: vibration from machinery, pressure waves from magic, warm pavement over heating lines, cold drafts from undercity shafts.

### 5. Social pressure

Every public space has rules, even when the rule is that nobody can enforce rules there. Who is watching? Who is entitled to stand where? Who can make a queue move? Who has a badge, tabard, writ, guild insignia, or reputation?

## The three-citizen rule

For any street scene, know three ordinary people:

- someone trying to finish work;
- someone trying to avoid trouble;
- someone trying to turn the current situation into opportunity.

They should not all belong to the guild that "owns" the scene. A Simic clinic might employ guildless cleaners, buy Izzet refrigeration service, owe Orzhov financing, and be under Azorius inspection.

## The consequence ladder

PC action should alter more than hit points.

1. **Immediate:** a door closes, a crowd scatters, a worker helps, a patrol arrives.
2. **Institutional:** a guild marks the PCs as useful, reckless, indebted, dangerous, or deniable.
3. **Neighborhood:** prices, rumors, access, and local trust shift.
4. **Political:** another guild exploits the outcome.
5. **Memory:** Ravnica remembers through records, gossip, debts, favors, scars, and public spectacle.

## Do not flatten the guilds

A Boros soldier can be tired, funny, frightened, kind, authoritarian, or corrupt without ceasing to be Boros. An Orzhov priest can sincerely believe debt creates social order. A Golgari rot farmer can be proud of feeding people. A Rakdos stagehand may care more about safe rigging than murder. A Simic technician may hate uncontrolled mutation.

The guild identity is a **pressure and vocabulary**, not a personality replacement.

## Read-aloud test

Before using descriptive prose, ask: could this paragraph describe Waterdeep, Sharn, or any generic fantasy metropolis if the guild names were removed? If yes, add Ravnican structure: vertical city layers, guild jurisdiction, civic magic, mixed species, overlapping infrastructure, visible old ruins, and social friction.

## Source Note: 00-GM/Scene-Hydration-Checklist.md

# Scene Hydration Checklist

Before a scene is table-ready, fill these fields. This is intentionally stricter than the older guide.

## Physical place

- What is above the PCs?
- What is below them?
- What older layer of the city is visible?
- What piece of infrastructure is functioning in the background?
- What does the street/building do when no adventurers are present?

## Sensory layer

Provide at least:

- 3 visible details;
- 2 simultaneous sounds;
- 1 smell;
- 1 tactile or temperature detail;
- 1 sign of crowd movement.

Avoid decorative description that does not affect play. A lift bell matters because somebody is arriving. Warm pavement matters because the heating main runs beneath it.

## Ordinary people

Name:

- a worker;
- a resident/customer/patient/petitioner;
- someone with informal local influence.

Each needs a current practical goal unrelated to the main quest.

## Guild overlap

At least two guilds should touch most civic scenes indirectly even if only one is physically present.

Example: the Simic clinic is financed by Orzhov credit, inspected by Azorius, connected to Izzet water, and receives low-cost Golgari reagents.

## Stakes

Write all three:

- **personal:** who is hurt?
- **material:** what service/property/resource is at risk?
- **institutional:** which guild gains or loses authority?

## PC consequence

For each likely PC approach, decide what changes socially.

- comply;
- threaten;
- bribe;
- expose;
- fight;
- help;
- walk away.

Winning initiative is not a complete consequence model.

## Five-minute continuity

If PCs leave and return five minutes later, what changed?

Someone called a supervisor. The crowd grew. A worker fixed half the problem. A rumor started. A door was sealed. The patient worsened.

## One-day continuity

If PCs return tomorrow, who remembers them and how?

That answer is the living world.

# Calendar, Festivals, and Civic Time

Ravnica uses a 365-day year divided into twelve months. The year begins on **1 Seleszeni**, corresponding to March 1, and the annual Guildpact celebration begins on **28 Zuun** and carries into the new year.

The exact calendar is useful not because players need to memorize month names, but because **city behavior changes around shared dates**.

## The months

1. Seleszeni — March  
2. Dhazo — April  
3. Prahz — May  
4. Mokosh — June  
5. Paujal — July  
6. Cizarm — August  
7. Tevnember — September  
8. Golgar — October  
9. Quaegar — November  
10. Xivaskir — December  
11. Griev — January  
12. Zuun — February

## Guildpact celebration

This is part civic holiday, part political theater, part neighborhood festival, part security nightmare.

Every guild wants visibility without appearing to threaten the others. Procession order, banners, who speaks, who stands beside whom, and which streets close become political signals.

Rakdos performers lampoon the ceremony. Azorius overplans it. Boros worries about crowd safety. Orzhov sponsors expensive events. Selesnya turns communal meals into recruitment. Dimir uses the volume of movement to hide quieter exchanges.

## Ordinary civic time

Ravnica also runs on smaller clocks:

- shift changes;
- court filing deadlines;
- market opening;
- lift schedules;
- patrol rotations;
- lab observation windows;
- Selesnyan communal meals;
- debt payment dates;
- performance nights;
- sanitation collection;
- freight windows on major roads.

A city scene feels real when characters can be **late** for something.

## Seasonal texture

Even in a plane-wide city, weather and light affect life. Rain changes traffic. Heat makes industrial quarters harsher. Cold reveals which buildings have reliable Izzet heating.

Seasonal change is experienced through infrastructure more than agriculture in many neighborhoods.

## GM time pressure

Use civic deadlines instead of arbitrary countdowns.

- The courthouse closes in forty minutes.
- The night freight window begins at dusk.
- The Selesnyan meeting starts when the evening meal is served.
- The Orzhov payment comes due tomorrow.
- The Izzet test can only run during a low-demand power period.
- The Rakdos show begins once the crowd is large enough.

This makes urgency feel like city life rather than adventure scripting.

## Source Note: 01-Setting/Childhood-Education-and-Family.md

# Childhood, Education, and Family

A city feels real when the GM knows what happens before adulthood.

## Family structures

Ravnican families can be guild-homogeneous, mixed-guild, guildless, adoptive, communal, or institutionally raised.

A household with a Boros parent and Azorius parent can argue over law at dinner without becoming a political metaphor. A Selesnyan child may grow up in a vernadi where dozens of adults share responsibility. A guildless family may have relatives in four guilds and call all of them irritating.

## Education

Education is uneven and institutionally shaped.

Azorius values legal literacy and formal study. Izzet and Simic train technical specialists. Selesnya teaches spiritual and communal practice alongside military readiness. Boros trains discipline and service. Orzhov teaches hierarchy, finance, ritual, and legal leverage to those inside its system.

Guildless schools, tutors, apprenticeships, family trades, and neighborhood institutions fill gaps.

## Apprenticeship

Many professions are learned through work. A teenage courier learns city geography by doing it. A market child learns prices and social reading. An Izzet apprentice learns which hum means "normal."

This creates NPCs who are competent young without feeling like miniature adults in every domain.

## Recruitment

Guild recruitment can offer real advantages.

- housing;
- food;
- education;
- career;
- community;
- status;
- protection;
- access to magic.

The ethical tension is strongest when the offer is genuinely life-changing.

## Mixed-guild families

Use them often.

A family can love one another and still interpret the world differently.

Example: a Boros daughter thinks her Orzhov uncle is predatory. He paid for her mother's surgery. Both facts shape every holiday.

## Children in scenes

Children should know the city differently.

They know shortcuts, rooftops, which guards tolerate play, which maintenance crews give them scrap, which Rakdos posters are too dangerous to touch, and where food can be found cheaply.

They also repeat guild stereotypes with brutal simplicity.

## Scene seed

A school trip is trapped behind an emergency cordon. The teacher is guildless, the escort is Azorius, the nearest safe shelter is a Selesnyan vernadi, and one child's parent has explicitly forbidden Selesnyan religious influence.

The emergency is simple. The social decision is not.

## Source Note: 01-Setting/City-World-Deep-Lore.md

# City-World Deep Lore

## Ravnica is urban geology

Ravnica covers the known world in construction layered over construction. The important idea is not simply "a very large city." It is that **the city has become terrain**. Streets can be rooftops of older neighborhoods. Cellars open into forgotten plazas. A sewer line can intersect a shrine, a collapsed viaduct, and a Golgari fungus field before reaching a modern pumping station.

People navigate by districts, precincts, trade ways, towers, guildhalls, bridges, and neighborhood names because a flat map cannot express the city well. "Across town" can mean climbing two hundred feet, crossing three bridges, descending through a market hall, and taking a public lift to a lower street that technically lies beneath the block you started in.

## The ten-guild settlement is in everything

The Guildpact historically assigned civic functions to the guilds, but millennia of ambition, interpretation, and institutional drift turned those functions into entire subcultures.

That means guild conflict is rarely cleanly separated from public necessity. If the Izzet stop maintaining a conduit because an Azorius order freezes access, apartments lose heat. If the Boros seal a street after a Gruul attack, merchants lose a day's income. If the Golgari stop processing waste or moving food upward, the poor feel it first. If Orzhov credit vanishes, some businesses close while others are bought cheaply. If Selesnyan growers refuse a redevelopment, a neighborhood may lose both a garden and a militia post.

The city survives because rivals keep performing work they resent one another for controlling.

## The core and the wider plane

The central city is divided into ten enormous districts, all crossed by all ten guilds. The Tenth District is especially politically dense because the guilds maintain important headquarters there and because many famous civic locations cluster nearby.

The Transguild Promenade links the core districts and functions as trade artery, parade route, meeting ground, and symbolic neutral space. Its neutrality is not absolute peace; it is a shared recognition that everyone loses if the arterial route becomes a permanent battlefield.

Beyond the core lie more districts and old urban centers absorbed by expansion. Ravnica has neighborhoods whose identities predate modern administrative boundaries. A resident may identify more strongly with Deadbridge, a smelting quarter, a moss haven, or a local vernadi than with a formal district number.

## The Living Guildpact problem

The current baseline is precarious rather than post-apocalyptic. Jace can render judgments with extraordinary authority, but he is often absent. This creates a strange constitutional culture:

- everyone agrees that a supreme mechanism exists;
- almost nobody can reliably access it;
- every guild therefore keeps its own lawyers, precedents, leverage, investigators, and contingency plans;
- local officials must decide whether to act now or wait for impossible certainty.

The result is a city where procedure and improvisation coexist. A neighborhood survives because people develop customs faster than central authority can settle disputes.

## A city of competence

Ravnica should not feel like a collapsed setting where nobody knows how anything works. It is ancient, crowded, dangerous, and politically dysfunctional, but it also supports central heating, plumbing, lifts, mass markets, guild hospitals, public law, freight networks, universities, military garrisons, organized waste processing, restaurants, theaters, parks, and powerful magical engineering.

Its danger is often the danger of **complex systems under rival ownership** rather than simple absence of civilization.

## What outsiders notice first

An off-world traveler usually notices three things:

1. the skyline seems to continue forever;
2. magic is embedded in ordinary civic systems rather than confined to wizards;
3. everyone appears to understand institutional signals the outsider cannot read—uniform cuts, insignia placement, document colors, queue etiquette, which alley belongs to whom, which bell means inspection, which bell means fire, and which bell means somebody important is arriving.

A good first session lets the outsider learn those signals through consequences.

## Source Note: 01-Setting/Civic-Life-Economy-Housing-and-Transit.md

# Civic Life, Economy, Housing and Transit

## Money is only one layer of obligation

Ravnicans use **zibs and zinos**, but purchasing power also comes from guild access, housing rights, credit, work assignments, sponsorship, permits, favors, ration entitlement, and debt.

A character with coin can still be unable to buy laboratory time, legal access, a military escort, or a safe introduction. Conversely, a broke guild member may eat, sleep, travel, and obtain equipment through institutional support while becoming increasingly obligated to superiors.

## Food

Ravnica eats constantly because Ravnica works constantly.

Street food should be everywhere: breads, filled pastries, skewers, stews, roasted vegetables, fried dough, strong coffee, tea, cheap beer, preserved fruit, and foods specific to species or neighborhood.

The Golgari provide a survival floor for people who cannot afford better. Their contribution is easy for comfortable citizens to joke about until supply is interrupted. Selesnyan cultivation, guildless farms and gardens, trade networks, and specialist growers provide variety beyond subsistence.

A food shortage in Ravnica is therefore political immediately. The question is rarely only "is there food?" but **which route failed, which guild controls the warehouse, who is hoarding, whose permits were suspended, and which neighborhood is considered expendable?**

## Housing

Housing stacks vertically and socially.

- upper floors can mean light, air, status, and exposure to wind;
- lower floors can mean convenience, damp, noise, and proximity to freight;
- basement levels may be older than the building above them;
- wealthy blocks have lifts, attendants, better heat, and private courtyards;
- poor tenements may still have solid masonry and public utilities but suffer crowding, maintenance delays, predatory landlords, and disputed access.

A family can have lived in the same "building" for generations while the street level outside moved upward through reconstruction.

## Work

Guild identity often overlaps with profession but never perfectly. Guildless people do enormous amounts of essential labor. The city contains clerks, cooks, porters, cleaners, teachers, builders, scribes, mechanics, teamsters, nurses, performers, undertakers, messengers, guards, repair crews, lawyers, bookkeepers, printers, brewers, cobblers, animal handlers, lift operators, landlords, students, academics, street vendors, and people doing three jobs badly to keep a room.

When improvising an NPC, give them a **work problem before a faction problem**.

## Transit

Walking remains fundamental, but vertical transit changes the city. Public and private lifts, ramps, broad freight lanes, stairs, bridges, elevated roads, tunnels, and major trade ways produce different traffic cultures.

Centaurs and large-bodied species shape street width and queue design. Flying creatures and mounted aerial patrols create a second traffic layer. Freight beasts need turning space. A single broken lift can force hundreds of residents onto stair routes never designed for that volume.

The Transguild Promenade acts as an artery through the core districts. On major days it becomes procession space; on ordinary days it is commerce, freight, gossip, and neutral encounter territory.

## Leisure

Ravnicans have cafes, restaurants, literature, opera, symphonies, arenas, clubs, illegal fights, religious gatherings, parks, street theater, and Rakdos spectacles. "Urban fantasy" here includes the ability to finish a shift, complain about your supervisor over coffee, attend a performance, and argue about politics on the way home.

## The practical GM rule

For every neighborhood, decide:

- what people eat cheaply;
- where they wash;
- how they get to work;
- who owns most housing;
- what service fails first;
- what people do for fun;
- what residents complain about even when no adventure is happening.

# Crime, Black Markets, and Informal Power

Crime in Ravnica is not one guild's department.

## Why black markets exist

The city has regulation, scarcity, guild monopolies, debt, dangerous magic, restricted research, confiscated goods, and people who need things quickly.

That guarantees unofficial markets.

## Common illicit trades

- forged permits;
- stolen lab access;
- restricted magical components;
- information;
- counterfeit insignia;
- smuggled creatures;
- debt records;
- unlicensed medicine;
- weapons;
- hidden transit;
- property deeds;
- body modifications;
- performance materials banned for public safety.

## Guild involvement

**Dimir:** information, routes, identity, secrecy.  
**Orzhov:** financing, laundering, protection, coercion.  
**Rakdos:** venues, muscle, crowd cover, illicit entertainment.  
**Golgari:** undercity routes, reclamation, hidden spaces.  
**Izzet:** stolen prototypes and parts.  
**Simic:** illegal modifications or escaped specimens.  
**Azorius/Boros:** corruption is possible, but more often their records and enforcement create the pressure black markets route around.

## Gangs and neighborhood power

A local gang may be more important to one block than any guild.

Their power comes from:

- knowing residents;
- controlling stairwells;
- managing illegal utilities;
- collecting protection;
- settling disputes;
- organizing smuggling;
- maintaining a relationship with one guild contact.

## Crime as service

The most interesting criminals solve real problems badly.

A smuggler delivers medicine around an Azorius quarantine. A gang keeps a violent landlord away. A Dimir broker finds missing people. An Orzhov collector enforces contracts no court can process quickly.

If the players remove the criminal, ask what service disappears with them.

## Scene seed

A neighborhood pays protection to a gang. Boros finally has enough evidence to arrest the leaders.

Residents quietly ask the PCs to delay the raid until they can arrange another night watch because the gang is also the only reason a predatory Rakdos crew stopped targeting the street.

Lawful success can create a security vacuum.

# Food, Markets, and Hospitality

Food is one of the fastest ways to make Ravnica feel inhabited.

## Markets as cross-guild spaces

Markets are where institutional systems become personal.

A Golgari supplier delivers mushrooms. A Selesnyan grower sells herbs. An Orzhov lender financed the stall. An Azorius inspector checks weights. Boros keeps a lane open. Izzet heat plates cook food. Dimir couriers eat while waiting for deliveries. Rakdos performers pull a crowd and block customers.

No guild owns "the market." Every guild touches it.

## Food by class

Poor food should not mean rotten garbage. Cheap staples can be hot, filling, repetitive, and culturally familiar.

Wealth buys variety, freshness, imported delicacies within the plane, better wine, private dining, and time.

A laborer may have a favorite inexpensive stew shop with strong community reputation. A rich official may eat in a polished restaurant where reservations themselves signal status.

## Golgari food

Golgari sustenance is politically charged because it makes dependency visible. The upper city can mock fungal gruel while relying on it as a hunger safety net.

Some Golgari cuisine should be genuinely good. A culture centered on fungus, reclamation, and underground agriculture would develop skill, not only desperation food.

## Selesnyan hospitality

Meals reinforce belonging. Guests are fed because feeding people is part of community.

But repeated acceptance may carry a social expectation: help with work, attend a meeting, listen to a teaching, become part of the circle.

## Orzhov dining

Business hospitality creates controlled obligation. Fine food, privacy, and courtesy make the guest feel respected while also reminding them who paid for the room.

## Rakdos food and drink

Street food around performances should be loud, spicy, messy, cheap, and designed for crowds. Vendors know which shows produce good business and which end in property damage.

## GM table

For any food scene choose:

- **temperature:** hot stew / chilled fruit / baked bread / fried snack
- **texture:** crisp / chewy / creamy / fungal / smoky
- **social meaning:** charity / date / business / ritual / shift meal / celebration
- **hidden pressure:** debt / recruitment / gossip / status / permit / shortage

A meal can carry more setting than a lore speech.

# History, Political Memory, and What People Remember

Ravnica's history is not a list of ancient dates. It is **present in institutions, ruins, family stories, neighborhood grudges, public holidays, and arguments over what the Guildpact was supposed to mean**.

## The original Guildpact

More than ten thousand years of relative guild order created an expectation that the ten-guild system is not merely political. It is the shape civilization takes.

Even citizens who dislike guilds may think in guild categories because the institutions have existed longer than almost any family, district boundary, or neighborhood government.

The original pact did more than prevent war. It assigned civic functions. Over millennia, guilds grew away from those original roles while still using them as justification.

## The Decamillennial rupture

The breaking of the old Guildpact is recent enough to be political memory, not myth.

Older adults may remember a time when the magical order felt more fixed. Younger adults grew up in the uncertainty after its collapse.

That generational difference matters. An older Azorius official can sincerely say, "we used to know what the limits were." A younger official may regard constant institutional improvisation as normal.

## The Implicit Maze and the Living Guildpact

Jace's rise as Living Guildpact restored a binding mechanism in a radically personal form.

This should feel strange even years later. A plane-wide constitutional principle is now embodied by a man who can leave the plane.

Ravnicans debate this constantly:

- Is Jace's absence negligence or unavoidable reality?
- Should guilds wait for his judgment?
- Can precedent substitute for his presence?
- Does his authority weaken local institutions or save them from deadlock?
- What happens if a guild decides absence equals opportunity?

## History in the built environment

A wall can be older than the office using it. A shrine can predate the street around it. A buried plaza can preserve inscriptions from earlier guild arrangements.

When players investigate a place, ask what political era physically shaped it.

## Historical memory by guild

**Azorius:** precedent and the fear of returning to chaos.  
**Boros:** remembered sacrifices, battles, and institutions that failed to protect people.  
**Dimir:** history is information; what is forgotten can be more valuable than what is remembered.  
**Golgari:** all structures decay; history proves their philosophy.  
**Gruul:** history is dispossession—evidence that the city kept expanding into what was once theirs.  
**Izzet:** history is a record of solved and unsolved technical problems.  
**Orzhov:** history is debt, inheritance, ownership, and continuity of power.  
**Rakdos:** history is material for satire; yesterday's sacred institution becomes tonight's punch line.  
**Selesnya:** continuity of community and Worldsoul outlasts political cycles.  
**Simic:** history reveals changing environments and the cost of failing to adapt.

## GM use

Whenever a conflict feels flat, add an older version of the same conflict.

Today's housing dispute stands on yesterday's burned guildhall. Today's laboratory occupies an old court. Today's road crosses land a clan remembers differently.

Ravnica becomes deep when present politics has archaeological layers.

# Language, Signage, and Etiquette

Ravnica is cosmopolitan enough that communication problems usually come from **institutional language, class, jargon, and etiquette** rather than basic inability to speak.

## Signage

A street can contain:

- official Azorius notices;
- commercial signs;
- guild insignia;
- transit markers;
- handwritten repair warnings;
- chalk route marks;
- Selesnyan community announcements;
- Rakdos posters;
- Orzhov legal notices;
- coded Dimir marks;
- Gruul paint deliberately defacing formal signs.

The visual density should feel urban.

## Guild jargon

**Azorius:** statutes, jurisdiction, filing, injunction, deposition, variance.  
**Boros:** squad, sector, civilian, threat, secure, hold, evacuate.  
**Izzet:** load, field, alignment, pressure, calibration, containment.  
**Simic:** adaptation, viability, host, clade, response, baseline.  
**Orzhov:** consideration, security, obligation, principal, penalty, guarantor.  
**Golgari:** substrate, cycle, reclaim, rot, harvest, upper waste.  
**Selesnya:** vernadi, community, harmony, communion, shared need.  
**Rakdos:** billing, act, audience, mark, rig, finale.  
**Dimir:** delivery, source, contact, route, verification, exposure.  
**Gruul:** clan, ground, wall, hunt, raid, scar, old place.

Jargon becomes roleplay when NPCs assume outsiders understand it.

## Queue etiquette

Ravnicans are used to bureaucracy and density. They know how queues work and become furious when someone uses status to skip one—unless status is exactly what the culture expects to permit skipping.

## Insignia etiquette

Visible insignia can function like badge, uniform, credential, warning, or invitation.

House Dimir complicates this because overt affiliation and covert membership do not align cleanly.

## Hospitality

Accepting food, entering a vernadi, drinking in a Rakdos club, taking a private Orzhov meeting, or accepting laboratory protective gear all signal different degrees of trust.

The GM should let players learn these meanings by watching locals.

## Social mistake examples

- addressing an Azorius clerk as though they can overrule a judge;
- insulting Golgari food while eating subsidized rations;
- treating a Boros sergeant as a generic city guard;
- assuming a Selesnyan communal meal is free of obligation;
- asking a Simic hybrid whether their body is "natural";
- calling a Gruul camp abandoned because it lacks permanent construction;
- accepting an Orzhov "favor" without asking what is recorded.

## Source Note: 01-Setting/Law-Justice-Debt-and-Authority.md

# Law, Justice, Debt and Authority

Ravnica has several overlapping systems that outsiders often mistake for one "law guild."

## Azorius: legitimacy through procedure

The Azorius Senate creates, interprets, records, and enforces formal law through a vast bureaucratic apparatus. Their power is strongest when everyone agrees that a process must be followed.

At street level, Azorius pressure looks like notices, injunctions, permits, inspections, hearings, sealed doors, controlled queues, arresters, records, witness statements, and an official who refuses to skip step seven because step seven exists for a reason.

Their failure mode is not merely cruelty. It is **procedure becoming more important than the human problem the procedure was invented to solve**.

## Boros: legitimacy through protection and justice

The Boros Legion is a standing army and a force of public protection. A Boros officer is more likely to ask who is in danger right now, who started the violence, and what action ends it.

That makes Boros faster and emotionally legible, but also more willing to use force and to decide that moral urgency overrides technical process.

A classic Ravnican dispute is not "law versus crime." It is an Azorius official saying, "You cannot enter until this order is reviewed," while a Boros officer says, "People are dying inside."

## Orzhov: legitimacy through contract, property, and obligation

The Orzhov create a parallel architecture of power through debt, loans, leases, patronage, religious authority, ownership, and enforceable promises. A person can be legally free and still economically trapped.

The frightening part is not that every Orzhov agreement is fraudulent. Many are valid, useful, and voluntarily entered. The horror comes when dependency makes refusal theoretical.

## The Living Guildpact

Jace's authority sits above normal interguild conflict, but his absence makes ordinary officials reluctant to rely on intervention. Everyone knows a supreme arbiter exists; everyone also knows the dispute in front of them may be settled, exploited, or forgotten long before he returns.

## Jurisdiction scene template

When the PCs cause a public incident, decide:

1. **Who arrives first?** Boros patrol, Azorius arrester, private Orzhov guard, local guild security, neighborhood watch.
2. **Who claims authority?**
3. **What evidence matters to them?**
4. **What outcome do they need before shift change?**
5. **Who benefits if the case is delayed?**

## Documents as drama

Use documents as objects with social weight:

- transit permit;
- guild work order;
- search writ;
- lease;
- debt instrument;
- lab safety variance;
- military requisition;
- medical waiver;
- witness affidavit;
- sealed Guildpact petition.

A document can open a door, but it can also create a paper trail. Players should learn that showing credentials solves the immediate problem while telling institutions exactly where they are.

## Street-level consequence examples

- Bribing a clerk succeeds, but the forged timestamp is later noticed.
- Helping Boros stop a riot earns gratitude and an Azorius inquiry into unauthorized force.
- Paying an Orzhov debt saves a family and transfers the obligation to whoever paid.
- Destroying an illegal lab prevents harm but leaves the surrounding block without a service the lab was secretly providing.
- Refusing to testify protects an ally and makes the PCs unreliable in formal proceedings.

Ravnican law is most interesting when **every solution changes who has leverage next**.

## Source Note: 01-Setting/Living-Guildpact-Day-to-Day-from-Official-Fiction.md

# Living Guildpact Ravnica — Day-to-Day Life from Official Fiction

This note starts from **published Ravnica fiction and setting material** rather than inventing a city from scratch. It extracts the lived implications of official scenes and only then adds clearly marked table synthesis.

## Source base

Primary anchors:

- *Guildmasters' Guide to Ravnica*.
- *The Gorgon and the Guildpact* by Doug Beyer.
- *Project Lightning Bug* by Doug Beyer.
- *Catching Up* by Kelly Digges.
- *Jace's Origin: Absent Minds*.
- *Planeswalker's Guide to Return to Ravnica*, Parts 1–3.
- *Planeswalker's Guide to Gatecrash*, Parts 1–2.
- *Ravnica, Then and Now*.

The prose below is paraphrase and setting analysis, not copied fiction.

# 1. The Chamber of the Guildpact is an administrative pressure valve

Official fiction shows the Chamber less as a throne room and more as a place where interguild conflicts pile up faster than one person can resolve them.

Delegations arrive with boundary disputes, levies, security concerns, contracts, accusations, murders, petitions, and demands for a ruling. Some are loud and theatrical. Some are technical. Some are genuinely urgent. Others are framed as urgent because a guild wants priority.

Jace possesses extraordinary authority, but somebody still has to:

- receive representatives;
- establish what dispute is actually being presented;
- separate questions of fact from questions of law;
- stop angry guild members from turning the waiting room into a fight;
- collect documents;
- identify who has standing;
- decide whether the matter truly requires the Living Guildpact;
- implement whatever ruling is eventually made.

That is why **Lavinia matters**.

## Table implication

The Chamber should always feel occupied.

Use:

- clerks carrying annotated petitions;
- messengers arriving before the previous delegation has left;
- rooms for mediation;
- guards trying to avoid a diplomatic incident;
- copied maps covered in boundary notes;
- exhausted petitioners who have waited too long;
- legal aides trying to downgrade a "plane-threatening emergency" into a normal property case;
- guild representatives furious that another guild was heard first.

The Living Guildpact did not remove bureaucracy. His existence created another layer of it.

# 2. Lavinia is the model for how Ravnican institutions actually function

Official fiction repeatedly contrasts Jace's immense authority with Lavinia's practical competence.

She knows procedure. She knows who has filed what. She knows which complaints are routine, which murders are politically explosive, which officers have already contaminated a scene, and which guild representatives can be sent home without causing a war.

This is a general rule for the setting:

**legendary leaders define direction; mid-level professionals keep the city alive.**

A deputy, sergeant, syndic, voda, swarm-boss, project leader, ringmaster, laboratory director, or Speaker may understand the immediate operational problem better than the guildmaster does.

When improvising institutional NPCs, give them:

- a scope of authority;
- a backlog;
- a superior;
- subordinates;
- a procedure they trust;
- a procedure they privately think is absurd;
- a local workaround;
- one recurring problem nobody above them understands.

# 3. Guild conflict is often mundane before it becomes violent

One official story opens with Boros representatives complaining that Orzhov actors are violating a boundary arrangement and collecting levies where the Legion believes they should not.

That dispute is simultaneously:

- territorial;
- contractual;
- economic;
- political;
- potentially violent;
- ordinary enough to be considered routine by the Chamber.

This is the ideal Ravnican scale.

Before inventing a secret superweapon, try a dispute over:

- sewer access;
- a condemned building;
- bridge repair liability;
- whether an emergency cordon was lawful;
- whether a mobile Rakdos stage requires a permit in every precinct it crosses;
- whether a Simic organism counts as property or patient;
- whether a Golgari salvage claim predates a surface deed;
- whether a Selesnyan vernadi is a residence, a temple, a public park, or a military asset;
- whether an Izzet test altered property beyond the authorized boundary;
- whether debt survives a change in guild status.

Ravnican tension is strongest when **more than one institution has a plausible claim**.

# 4. Political scenes remain physically urban

Ravnica does not become quiet just because the subject is important.

Official fiction surrounds politics with:

- hoofbeats;
- messengers;
- doors opening and closing;
- crowded halls;
- streetlamps;
- restaurants;
- patrols;
- carts;
- rain;
- undercity passages;
- public squares;
- guild insignia.

A political scene should carry the city's sound bed with it.

## Chamber sound bed

- minotaur hooves striking stone;
- paper sorted and restacked;
- guards shifting position when voices rise;
- a clerk whispering a correction;
- a messenger arriving breathless;
- street traffic outside;
- magical communication in the background.

## Street sound bed

- wheels;
- vendors;
- hooves;
- roc wingbeats;
- pressure vents;
- bells;
- repair work;
- argument;
- distant music;
- a public notice being read aloud.

# 5. Ravnicans explain the strange through guild categories

*Jace's Origin* is especially useful because Jace arrives as an outsider into a city whose residents already share assumptions.

A person appearing suddenly in a street does not create philosophical wonder. Bystanders immediately imagine a guild-related explanation.

That is a core cultural fact.

Ravnicans use the guilds as explanatory shorthand:

- strange magical failure: probably Izzet;
- formal closure: Azorius;
- emergency force: Boros;
- ugly debt issue: Orzhov;
- secretive information problem: Dimir;
- lower-city corpse or fungus issue: Golgari;
- public spectacle: Rakdos;
- impossible biology: Simic;
- communal green intervention: Selesnya;
- smashed new construction: possibly Gruul.

These assumptions are often unfair. They are still culturally real.

# 6. The undercity remains a city

*Project Lightning Bug* presents a sunken street in the undercity, open to weather and recognizably urban.

That corrects a common GM mistake.

The undercity is not just sewer tunnels. It contains:

- streets;
- façades;
- plazas;
- older infrastructure;
- buried civic spaces;
- ruins that still preserve neighborhood geometry;
- passages that used to be normal routes before newer construction rose over them.

## Run buried urbanism

A descent can reveal:

- a street sign below current street level;
- an old storefront now used as storage;
- a courthouse wall incorporated into a fungus farm;
- a balcony opening into a tunnel because the air outside became a later foundation;
- rain falling through a shaft five stories above;
- modern utility pipes driven through older masonry;
- a sealed stair that once led to a vanished public square.

The city did not erase itself. It accumulated.

# 7. Weather exposes infrastructure

Official Ravnica fiction frequently uses cloud, drizzle, wet streets, and lamps.

Because the plane is vertical, weather becomes civic mechanics.

Rain:

- overloads drains;
- exposes leaks;
- turns lower streets slick;
- washes refuse downward;
- steams off hot Izzet structures;
- feeds mold and fungus;
- drips from upper roads onto lower neighborhoods.

Fog:

- hides upper bridges;
- turns lamps into halos;
- conceals sky patrols until wingbeats are heard;
- makes distance feel uncertain.

Heat:

- makes industrial quarters oppressive;
- changes crowd behavior;
- raises public-health risks;
- pushes people into shade, underground routes, and water-rich spaces.

A weather description should change what people are doing.

# 8. Jace's authority produces strategic delay

The guilds know a supreme arbiter exists. They also know he can be absent.

That makes delay politically meaningful.

A guild expecting to lose before Jace may:

- settle privately;
- create facts on the ground;
- pressure witnesses;
- move assets;
- rewrite a contract;
- provoke a rival into an unlawful response;
- insist the matter is beneath Guildpact review.

A guild expecting to win may do the opposite:

- preserve evidence;
- resist retaliation;
- keep the dispute formally alive;
- demand a written record;
- wait.

The Living Guildpact does not freeze politics. He changes the incentives.

# 9. Restaurants, coffee, and private apartments belong beside epic politics

*Catching Up* is useful precisely because legendary Planeswalkers still need food, coffee, rest, privacy, and ordinary meeting places.

Ravnica contains:

- cafés;
- restaurants;
- maître d's;
- private dining;
- social etiquette;
- appointments;
- staff who know regular customers;
- service workers who watch powerful people come and go.

Put major scenes in these places.

An Orzhov lawyer having lunch.
A Boros officer eating after shift.
An Izzet researcher arguing over coffee.
A Dimir courier reading at the next table.
A Selesnyan family sharing food after market.

Political life becomes believable when it occupies the same city as lunch.

# 10. Reputation travels through density

Ravnica does not need magical surveillance for news to spread.

A public incident produces:

- witnesses;
- rumors;
- official reports;
- street jokes;
- altered business decisions;
- guild interest;
- neighborhood memory.

A vendor saw the fight.
A courier recognized the tabard.
A child repeats the funniest line.
A Rakdos performer turns it into satire.
An Azorius clerk gets the report.
An Orzhov creditor hears the shop was damaged.
A Dimir operative notices what every version leaves out.

The city remembers through both institutions and gossip.

# 11. Guild characters have jobs before they have philosophies

Official fiction gives guild characters personal irritation, humor, impatience, grief, ambition, and professional habits.

Do not introduce an NPC with a lecture on white-blue ideology.

Start with:

- what they are doing;
- what deadline they are under;
- what person they report to;
- what part of the job they hate;
- what they need from the PCs.

Only then let guild philosophy shape their judgment.

## Example

An Azorius clerk does not begin with "Order is essential."

They begin with:

"Your form names the building by its pre-renovation address. I cannot attach an emergency access order to a property that, according to this filing, does not exist."

The ideology is inside the problem.

# 12. Ravnica lives on overlapping time scales

Official sources constantly juxtapose ten-thousand-year institutions with today's errands.

The city contains:

- ancient guild founders still remembered by name;
- current guildmasters who are immortal or extremely long-lived;
- post-Guildpact buildings;
- ruins older than modern boundaries;
- workers worried about this afternoon's shift.

Use the **three-age description**:

1. one ancient thing;
2. one recent thing;
3. one immediate problem.

Example:

Ancient arch with obsolete heraldry.
Recent Boros barricade built after last month's riot.
Immediate delivery cart that cannot fit through the new gap.

That single frame feels more Ravnican than three paragraphs of generic Gothic architecture.

# 13. What remains synthesis

Official fiction is selective. It does not define every rent practice, school day, neighborhood custom, or household routine.

V2 fills gaps by asking:

- what must exist for the published system to function?
- what would a city this dense require?
- which guilds would touch the need?
- what social tension follows?

Those additions remain **table synthesis** and should yield to later canon.

# Magic Infrastructure and Urban Hazards

Magic in Ravnica is not only adventuring magic. It is civic engineering, medicine, law, transport, communication, agriculture, entertainment, and industrial process.

## Izzet infrastructure

The Izzet League builds and maintains major systems while also treating the city as an irresistible research environment. Heating, plumbing, pressure systems, magical power, transport experiments, metallurgy, weather control, and spatial research can all sit uncomfortably close to residential life.

The important tone is **competent volatility**. Izzet systems usually work. The city depends on them. The same culture that keeps them running also tolerates experimentation that would terrify a normal municipal authority.

### What an Izzet utility site feels like

Copper and blue-white light. Mizzium housings. Warning glyphs layered over handwritten modifications. Technicians arguing while repairing something live. Heat through walls. The smell of hot dust and ozone. Pipes vibrating at a frequency residents recognize as either normal or extremely not normal.

## Simic civic biology

The Simic began from public-health concerns and now combine medicine, adaptation, biomancy, environmental engineering, and biological research. Their work can solve real urban problems: disease, pollution, hostile environments, injuries, food resilience.

A Simic answer often asks, "why make the environment safe for the body when the body can be made safe for the environment?" That question can be humane, visionary, unsettling, or all three.

## Selesnyan living infrastructure

Gardens, vernadi, trees, communal spaces, healing, food production, and green corridors are not decorative. They cool streets, provide gathering space, organize neighborhoods, and can become militia positions quickly.

## Golgari metabolic infrastructure

The Golgari treat decay as a resource flow. Waste becomes substrate, food, fungus, soil, labor, and new life. Upper-city residents often disdain the undercity while depending on it materially.

## Common urban magical hazards

- unstable elemental pressure in a conduit;
- misaligned lift enchantment;
- animated legal seal preventing access;
- escaped weird;
- biomantic runoff changing vermin;
- old ward interacting with new construction;
- spirit bound to property terms nobody currently understands;
- Selesnyan roots breaking into older masonry;
- undercity gas ignited by careless magic;
- spatial distortion near an orientation experiment;
- overlapping guild enchantments producing contradictory effects.

## Repair culture

Ravnicans are not amazed when magic breaks. They are irritated. They know which guild to blame, which office to call, which shortcut a technician will take, and how long a permit will delay repairs.

That is a useful tonal difference from high fantasy where magical failure automatically feels mystical. Here, a floating streetlamp flickering may be the equivalent of a bad transformer—until the Izzet apprentice says the current is flowing backward.

## GM escalation ladder

Start with inconvenience, not explosion.

1. hum changes;
2. lights flicker;
3. residents complain;
4. a technician arrives;
5. two guilds disagree about responsibility;
6. system fails dangerously;
7. evidence suggests sabotage, negligence, or something older under the street.

The city becomes believable when catastrophe grows out of ordinary maintenance.

# Medicine, Health, Disability, and the Body

Ravnica's magical and biological sophistication changes health without eliminating suffering.

## Multiple medical cultures

**Simic:** diagnosis, adaptation, biomancy, biological intervention.  
**Selesnya:** healing embedded in community, spirituality, herbs, and restorative magic.  
**Boros:** trauma care, battlefield triage, rehabilitation.  
**Golgari:** practical knowledge of fungus, toxins, decay, disease, and what bodies do after death.  
**Orzhov:** access to expensive care through debt and patronage.  
**Izzet:** devices, prosthetic mechanisms, environmental support, hazardous exposure expertise.

Guildless healers and clinics combine methods as needed.

## Disability in an adapted city

A city with centaurs, loxodons, hybrids, flying beings, undead, and magically altered bodies cannot have one default body.

Accessibility is still unequal, but Ravnicans are used to designing around bodily difference.

A lift too small for a centaur is a civic failure. A Simic hybrid with gills may need humidity. A veteran may use a magical prosthesis. A deaf citizen may rely on visual guild signals.

## Healing is not consequence erasure

Magic does not need to make injury socially meaningless.

Treatment costs money, time, favors, or access. Trauma remains. Some injuries cannot be fully restored. Some people refuse available modification. Others choose transformation enthusiastically.

## Simic ethics

Consent matters most when the treatment can change identity or appearance permanently.

A researcher can be correct that adaptation will save a life and still be wrong to treat consent as inconvenience.

## Orzhov medical debt

A lifesaving loan is still lifesaving. That is what makes it powerful.

The patient may feel gratitude and resentment at the same time.

## Public health

Dense urban living makes sanitation, clean water, waste removal, ventilation, disease tracking, and food safety politically crucial.

Public-health disputes naturally involve Simic, Golgari, Izzet, and Azorius, with Boros appearing when quarantine or crowd control becomes coercive.

## Scene seed

A new disease is spreading through one housing block.

Simic wants temporary biological screening. Azorius wants a formal quarantine. Boros worries people will riot if exits close. Golgari workers say the symptom appeared below the street weeks earlier and nobody listened.

The outbreak is not a monster. It is a systems test.

# News, Rumor, and Information

Ravnica is too large for anyone to know what is happening everywhere. Information travels through **couriers, guild reports, market gossip, official notices, performers, sermons, taverns, neighborhood networks, and deliberate misinformation**.

## Speed of news

Public spectacle travels fast.

A fight on the Transguild Promenade can become district gossip within hours because thousands saw it, vendors repeat it, couriers carry versions outward, and Rakdos performers may parody it by night.

Private institutional news travels differently. A legal filing may reach three offices before the person named in it hears anything.

## Dimir information ecology

House Dimir thrives because the city already needs information specialists.

A useful Dimir plot does not invent a separate spy world. It hides inside normal information flow:

- courier routes;
- archive requests;
- print shops;
- messenger services;
- private investigators;
- damaged records;
- memory magic;
- paid witnesses;
- rumor brokers.

## Official notices

Azorius notices create public truth even when everyone knows the underlying reality is more complicated.

A building is "condemned." A street is "closed." A demonstration is "unlawful." Those words change what institutions are allowed to do next.

## Rakdos as media

Rakdos performance can become political media. A successful satire turns a complicated scandal into one humiliating image everyone remembers.

That makes Rakdos dangerous to institutions even when the show reveals nothing technically secret.

## Selesnyan networks

Vernadi spread news through dense trust. The information may move more slowly than courier systems but arrive with social interpretation already attached: who needs help, who can be trusted, what the community should do.

## Rumor table

When news spreads, distort one element:

1. casualty count doubles  
2. wrong guild gets blamed  
3. location shifts one precinct  
4. motive becomes political  
5. one eyewitness is elevated to hero  
6. one official becomes villain  
7. magical detail is exaggerated  
8. true secret is dismissed as absurd  
9. unrelated old scandal gets attached  
10. rumor becomes mostly correct

## PC reputation

Track not only "fame" but **which version of the story each network heard**.

Boros may know the PCs saved people. Azorius may know they violated an order. Rakdos may know they looked ridiculous doing it. Orzhov may know who paid for the damage.

All are true.

# People, Identity, Class and Belonging

## Species is visible; citizenship is social

Ravnica is cosmopolitan. Humans, elves, goblins, loxodons, minotaurs, vedalken, centaurs, hybrids, and many other beings share streets built over millennia.

Do not treat species as neighborhood decoration. Architecture and etiquette adapt around bodies. A loxodon may need stronger floors and wider seating. A centaur changes how a tavern, stair, alley, or lift works. Goblins know maintenance spaces larger folk ignore. Flying creatures create rooftop access patterns. A Simic hybrid may need a humid room or unusual food. These are ordinary design problems in a city that has accommodated strange bodies for a very long time.

## Guild identity

Guild membership can be occupation, family tradition, religion, political identity, social network, housing system, or all of them. Leaving a guild may therefore resemble changing careers, converting religion, leaving a military, breaking with family, and losing insurance at the same time.

Guildless does not mean cultureless. Many guildless citizens have strong neighborhood identities, professions, family networks, unions, criminal ties, clubs, schools, and political opinions.

## Class

Ravnica has wealth stratification despite advanced civic amenities.

A poor resident can live in sturdy construction with heat and plumbing and still be one missed payment from eviction. A wealthy resident can live above the crowd with private lifts, servants, magical climate control, legal retainers, and enough institutional relationships to make problems disappear.

The useful contrast is not medieval squalor versus magic luxury. It is **dense urban inequality inside a sophisticated city**.

## Reputation

People read insignia, uniform quality, posture, vocabulary, documents, companions, and confidence. A Boros breastplate polished by an armorer signals something different from a dented local garrison kit. An Orzhov functionary carrying no visible guards may be more dangerous than one surrounded by them.

## Belonging is local

Ask an NPC where they are from and they may answer with a neighborhood before a district. Ask who they trust and the answer may be a building superintendent, vernadi healer, street vendor, former squadmate, guild clerk, or cousin—not a guildmaster.

This matters because large institutions are experienced through small relationships.

## Conversation cues by social position

**Clerk:** precise about process; knows which rule is routinely bent.  
**Laborer:** knows what systems actually fail and which supervisors lie.  
**Merchant:** tracks patrols, permits, customer mood, and credit.  
**Student:** overconfident about theory, underexposed to consequences.  
**Veteran:** compares current crisis to three older crises.  
**Guildless tenant:** understands overlapping jurisdictions because they live with them.  
**Wealthy patron:** treats access as a thing that can be purchased indirectly.  
**Courier:** knows routes, timing, door staff, and where nobody asks questions.

## The Ravnican social test

Before an NPC answers the PCs, decide:

- what institution can hurt them;
- what institution can help them;
- what local person they cannot afford to alienate;
- what stereotype they hold about the PCs' apparent guild;
- what practical thing they need today.

That produces setting-specific behavior faster than assigning alignment.

## Source Note: 01-Setting/Religion-Death-and-Meaning.md

# Religion, Death, and Meaning

Ravnica does not have one simple religious structure. Guild philosophy, spirits, community ritual, ancestral practice, personal belief, and public institutions overlap.

## Orzhov faith

The Orzhov turn religion into hierarchy, debt, ritual, and institutional continuity.

Their services can be emotionally real. A mourner can receive genuine comfort in a basilica and still leave owing money.

The Deathless demonstrate that wealth and authority can persist beyond death, which gives Orzhov theology a terrifying material proof: the powerful literally refuse to leave.

## Selesnyan spirituality

Selesnya offers communion with Mat'Selesnya and a lived sense of belonging to a greater whole.

Ritual is communal rather than transactional. Meals, gardens, training, singing, and daily work can all become spiritual practice.

The tension appears when the collective's understanding of harmony conflicts with individual choice.

## Golgari relationship to death

The Golgari refuse to treat death as opposite to life. Decay, necromancy, fungi, insects, and new growth belong to the same cycle.

This does not erase grief. It changes what grief means.

A Golgari funeral may focus less on preserving a body and more on ensuring that what remains re-enters the cycle properly.

## Rakdos and mortality

Rakdos culture looks at the certainty of death and concludes that solemnity is often hypocrisy.

That can produce courage, hedonism, satire, nihilism, art, cruelty, or a refusal to waste the present.

## Boros moral certainty

Boros justice can function like faith even when not formal religion. Angels make transcendent ideals physically visible. Soldiers can believe sacrifice matters because justice is larger than personal survival.

## Secular citizens

Many Ravnicans are practical rather than doctrinal. They attend an Orzhov funeral because family expects it, ask a Selesnyan healer for help, buy Golgari food, and curse every guild equally.

## Spirits and the dead

Because ghosts, undead, necromancy, and magical afterlife effects are real, death creates legal as well as spiritual problems.

Who owns a dead person's property if the person still manifests as a spirit? Does a debt survive? Who controls remains? Can testimony from the dead be admitted? Which guild has jurisdiction?

These are ordinary Ravnican questions with extraordinary mechanics.

## Source Note: 01-Setting/Sensory-Palette-and-Daily-Rhythm.md

# Sensory Palette and Daily Rhythm

## Morning

Morning is not quiet. Freight has often been moving all night. Bakers open shutters into streets already occupied by delivery carts, Golgari food sellers, sanitation crews, sleepy Rakdos performers, clerks, students, and soldiers finishing night watch.

**See:** mist between towers, damp cobbles, banners hanging heavy with moisture, lift cages beginning their first full runs, winged patrols crossing pale sky.

**Hear:** shutters thrown open, wheels on stone, temple bells, vendors calling breakfasts, courier whistles, boots changing shift.

**Smell:** yeast, coffee, wet stone, horse and beast sweat, hot pipes, fungal broth, cheap soap.

**Social tone:** purposeful, impatient, practical. A delay feels personal because thousands of people are trying to reach work through the same bottleneck.

## Midday

The city becomes bright, crowded, and transactional. Bureaucracies are fully awake. Courts, labs, workshops, schools, markets, and guild offices operate at maximum pressure.

**See:** document runners, public notices pasted over older notices, street repairs, uniformed escorts, packed cafes, queues under awnings.

**Hear:** argument, construction, official proclamations, street music, machinery, classroom recitation, distant magical detonations that nobody nearby finds surprising.

**Smell:** hot metal, cooking oil, fruit, incense, ozone, dust, warm masonry.

**Social tone:** visible hierarchy. Who gets waved through? Who waits? Who is served first? Who has an appointment? Who can interrupt the line?

## Evening

Shift changes make streets briefly chaotic. Office workers, laborers, students, soldiers, and performers all move at once. Restaurants and taverns fill. Selesnyan courtyards become communal spaces; Orzhov establishments become negotiation spaces; Rakdos crews start assembling mobile stages.

**See:** lanterns and magical lights appearing floor by floor; reflections in rainwater; guild colors becoming more dramatic after dark.

**Hear:** music, bells, drinking songs, sermon, machinery winding down in one block and starting up in another.

**Smell:** grilled meat, mushroom stew, perfume, lamp oil, smoke, wet vegetation, sewage from stressed drains.

## Night

Ravnica does not sleep uniformly. It changes ownership.

Couriers still run. Watch patrols change routes. Dimir activity becomes harder to distinguish from ordinary delivery work. Golgari workers emerge from lower passages. Rakdos stages can transform a square in minutes. Izzet researchers keep impossible hours. Orzhov collectors prefer private visits. Hospitals, barracks, freight depots, and major markets remain lit.

Night should feel **less crowded, not empty**.

## Weather

Rain is useful because it reveals verticality: water runs off roofs, through gargoyles, across bridges, down chained gutters, into grates, and eventually into systems maintained or contested below. Fog hides upper bridges before it hides street level. Wind behaves strangely among towers. Heat leaks from infrastructure in winter and makes some alleys steam.

## Layered sound rule

Never use only one ambient sound. Combine:
1. **near:** shoes, argument, utensil, machine;
2. **middle:** cart, crowd, patrol, workshop;
3. **far:** bells, horns, roc cry, industrial pulse, performance music.

Example: "A clerk's pen scratches beside you; outside, freight wheels hammer the avenue; somewhere above the roofs a Boros horn answers another across the district."

## Smell as geography

Smell can tell players they changed neighborhoods before narration does.

- perfume/incense before Orzhov frontage;
- damp fungus before an undercity access;
- ozone before Izzet works;
- hot iron before Smelting Quarter;
- brine before Zonot Seven;
- wet greenery before Selesnyan space;
- smoke and hide near rubblebelt camps.

## Crowd reaction vocabulary

When tension starts, crowds do more than flee.

- people stop but pretend not to watch;
- vendors pull fragile goods inward;
- parents move children behind them;
- couriers calculate alternate routes;
- regulars identify exits;
- off-duty guild members reveal themselves by instinct;
- opportunists move closer;
- workers become angry if the incident blocks their job;
- someone starts telling a version of the story before it is over.

That last reaction is especially Ravnican: the event is becoming politics while the PCs are still inside it.

# Canonical Named Locations — Deep Atlas

This atlas prioritizes **officially named locations** from *Guildmasters' Guide to Ravnica* and the official Planeswalker's Guides. Any extension beyond explicit canon is marked **table synthesis**.

# City-spanning geography

## Transguild Promenade

**Canon:** a major avenue running through the core districts, lined with markets, parks, and colonnades, used for freight and public processions. It has special neutral importance.

**At the table:** use it as a cross-section of the plane. One block can hold guildless vendors, Orzhov offices, Boros traffic control, Azorius notices, Izzet access panels, Selesnyan plantings, Dimir couriers, and Rakdos advertising.

**Table synthesis:** sheltering colonnades collect food stalls and copyists; freight pull-offs create constant conflict between carts and pedestrians; political posters accumulate over old legal notices.

## Tenth District

**Canon:** one of the city's core districts and a dense center of guild activity with six precincts and major guild headquarters.

**At the table:** never treat it as one guild's territory. Its identity is **collision**.

# Azorius and civic sites

## New Prahv

**Canon:** rebuilt Azorius guildhall after the destruction of old Prahv, monumental in scale, supported by magical construction.

**Functions:** lawmaking, courts, administration, enforcement coordination, archives, diplomacy.

**Table synthesis:** the district around it supports legal copyists, food vendors, temporary lodging, advocates, messengers, evidence couriers, and people selling help to citizens who do not understand procedure.

## Augustin Station

**Canon:** Tenth District station near an Izzet Laboratory of Gravitational Inversion workshop.

**Campaign use:** central to *The Glass Run*.

**Table synthesis:** a place where commuters, freight, and experimental movement infrastructure meet. Magical failures here inconvenience ordinary people immediately.

## Prism University

**Canon:** the Izzet Laboratory of Arcane Geometry has a small presence here.

**Use:** lectures, academic politics, copied research, visiting experts, student rivalries, permit disputes.

# Boros sites

## Sunhome

**Canon:** Boros guildhall, fortress, barracks, and symbolic religious/military center.

**Living feel:** drill courts, armor maintenance, mess halls, command rooms, memorial spaces, aerial access, supply movement, healers, messengers.

## Parhelion II

**Canon:** immense flying Boros garrison.

**Use:** strategic mobility and civic spectacle.

**Table synthesis:** its passage changes street light; residents look up; children point; criminals recalculate.

## Kamen Fortress

**Canon:** major Boros command site, associated in Return-era material with anti-Rakdos operations.

**Use:** hard military atmosphere, intelligence, staging, detention, rapid-response planning.

# Dimir sites

## Duskmantle

**Canon:** restored guildhall hidden in the undercity behind memory wards.

**Play principle:** its security is epistemic. Visitors can leave knowing they were somewhere without being able to reconstruct how they arrived.

## Ismeri Library

**Canon:** officially guildless public library operated by Dimir personnel and used as a communications node.

**Why it matters:** it is genuinely useful. Citizens research history, property, magic, literature, and law while covert messages pass through the same information system.

## Dinrova Heights

**Canon:** rooftop meeting place for high-ranking Dimir mages, protected against observation.

**Use:** espionage with the entire city visible below.

## Bane Alley

**Canon:** point of access to Dimir extortion, intimidation, murder, and clandestine problem-solving.

**Play principle:** knowing how to reach it is itself part of the price.

## Nightveil

**Canon:** area of Dimir elites, caverns, tunnels, crypts, walled mansions, and spectral security.

**Use:** affluent secrecy and evidence that Dimir power is not confined to poor alleys.

# Golgari sites

## Korozda, Maze of Decay

**Canon:** Golgari guildhall, an arched cathedral enclosed by an immense circular maze of overgrown ruins.

**Use:** governance embedded in reclamation. The approach should communicate that decomposition here is managed.

## Stonefare

**Canon:** old brick tunnel network in Golgari territory with slime, fungi, webs, and petrified remains incorporated into the environment.

**Use:** transport, neighborhood boundary, intimidation, ancient city infrastructure.

## Deadbridge

**Canon:** major Golgari area built around a vast sinkhole and the bridge/platform spanning it.

**Use:** catastrophe converted into neighborhood identity and resource economy.

## Zanikev, the Great Rot Farm

**Canon:** enormous reclamation zone created from ruined neighborhoods.

**Use:** the Golgari as urban ecologists and resource processors.

## Corpsefloat Valves

**Canon:** fungal membranes regulating waterways and corpse traffic.

**Implication:** Golgari territory includes schedules, maintenance workers, transport knowledge, and practical infrastructure—not only monsters.

# Gruul sites

## Rubblebelt

**Canon:** discontinuous wasteland of gutted buildings, shantytowns, ruins, and roaming elementals near more developed areas.

**Core interpretation:** this is contested post-urban land, not untouched wilderness.

**Canon:** ruined palace used as a cross-clan gathering ground, marked by a great fire and no guaranteed truce.

**Canon:** Slizt territory characterized by tall ancient structures and excellent ambush positions.

- instrument repair;
- attendants;

**Canon:** major Precinct Six feature tied to Deadbridge and undercity descent.

# Canon-first generation rule

Named canon sites are anchors, not limits.

When creating an unnamed café, school, alley, apartment block, clinic, workshop, or local shrine:

1. anchor it near a published place;
2. determine what real civic need it serves;
3. connect at least two guilds to that need;
4. give it one older physical layer;
5. label the invented proper name as table synthesis.

Example:

Nivix canonically needs workers, freight, records, food, waste removal, housing, medicine, and transport. Therefore an invented all-night café for Izzet attendants is plausible urban infill. It is not a published named location and should not be presented as one.

# Precinct One — Named Places, Street Texture, and Scene Use

Precinct One is the **Guildpact Precinct**, the district's center of wealth, formal diplomacy, public display, finance, and interguild politics. Its defining contrast is that immaculate order depends on an enormous invisible labor force and on political arrangements that are far messier than the avenues look.

## Tenth District Plaza

### Canon anchor
The plaza is a major meeting, recreation, and shopping space maintained with magical cleanliness and heavily watched by Azorius arresters. It functions as neutral ground for guilds and factions.

### What the PCs see
The first impression is **space**. In most of Ravnica the skyline presses inward; here the city deliberately opens. Broad paving creates enough distance to see whole facades. Fountains, trimmed gardens, pale stone, monuments, shaded benches, and carefully placed trees make the plaza feel designed rather than accumulated.

Neutrality has visible infrastructure. Guard posts are positioned for clear sightlines. Carriage drop-offs do not block pedestrian routes. Public notices are mounted in standardized frames. Street vendors are fewer and better dressed than elsewhere because permits and rents select for wealth.

### Sound
Conversation carries because there is less enclosing street noise. Carriage wheels sound crisp on clean paving. Arresters' boots are distinct. Diplomats speak in low voices because they know other diplomats are listening. A distant fountain can be heard between conversations.

### Smell
Clean stone after rain, clipped vegetation, perfume, cafe coffee, carriage animals kept unusually well groomed, faint incense from nearby Orzhov properties.

### What ordinary people do here
- tourists take in architecture;
- government clerks cross between offices;
- diplomatic aides wait for meetings;
- wealthy clients meet advocates;
- couriers hand off sealed packets;
- gardeners and cleaners work constantly but are socially ignored;
- prospective employees wait outside institutions they hope will change their lives.

### Social rule
Drawing a weapon is not merely dangerous; it announces that the person cannot function under shared civic norms. Even rivals who would happily sabotage one another elsewhere have reasons to preserve the plaza.

### Scene pressure
A Gruul representative arrives under negotiated safe passage. A wealthy spectator deliberately provokes them, assuming arresters will intervene if violence starts. The representative knows that retaliating would confirm every stereotype used to justify excluding Gruul from civic space.

The PCs can stop the provocation, defend the representative's dignity, expose the spectator, or watch a diplomatic incident form in real time.

---

## Chamber of the Guildpact

### Canon anchor
A monumental meeting hall reached from the plaza. The Living Guildpact has an office here but is rarely present; functionaries attempt to manage interguild disputes in his absence.

### What the building means
The Chamber is Ravnica's constitutional contradiction in stone: a place built to serve authority that is theoretically supreme and practically unavailable.

Every waiting room therefore contains two conversations:
1. the dispute people officially came to settle;
2. the question of whether anybody present can actually settle it.

### Street-to-hall transition
Outside: banners, carriage traffic, petitioners, guards.  
Grand stair: conversations become quieter; people sort themselves into delegations.  
Entry hall: insignia, clerks, schedules, waiting benches, messengers.  
Upper levels: less public movement, more aides and functionaries carrying unresolved problems from office to office.

### Sound
Pages turning. Shoes on immense floors. Clerks calling names. Delegates rehearsing language. Echoes from a chamber too large for most meetings held inside it.

### Human texture
A junior functionary may know more about daily interguild disputes than a guildmaster because they see the paperwork from all ten factions.

A cleaner knows which delegations throw documents after meetings.

A cafe worker nearby knows which diplomats stay after formal sessions to negotiate privately.

### Scene pressure
Three guilds petition over the same infrastructure failure. The office cannot issue Living Guildpact judgment. It can only broker a temporary agreement. Every party wants the "temporary" language written so it later becomes precedent.

## Plaza East

### Canon anchor
Government officials live here alongside temporary diplomatic housing; stately residences mix with more modest apartments, and carriage transit to the central plaza is common.

### Street texture
Morning is busiest. Carriages arrive in waves. Servants move luggage. Diplomatic guards learn unfamiliar routes. Clerks leave home carrying document cases and return with more than they started.

The neighborhood looks affluent without being purely luxurious. Apartment buildings are well maintained. Door staff recognize uniforms. Corner cafes specialize in fast breakfasts for people who cannot be late to formal appointments.

### Scene pressure
A visiting delegation loses a sealed case between its residence and carriage. The case is politically sensitive but not dangerous. The local carriage driver remembers a child running into the road, a clerk changing seats, and an Orzhov servant offering to help.

Nobody saw a theft.

---

## Plaza West

### Canon anchor
Prestigious nightclubs and restaurants cluster here, many run by the Orzhov; permanent residents are exceptionally wealthy.

### Day versus night
By day, Plaza West can feel almost sleepy: deliveries, cleaning crews, cooks, florists, servants, and discreet security preparing for evening.

After dark it becomes a choreography of status. Carriages queue. Door staff recognize regulars. Musicians arrive through service entrances. Guests are judged before they reach the threshold.

### Sound
Soft music through walls, carriage doors, laughter, restaurant kitchens, private arguments that become silent when staff enter.

### Smell
Wine, grilled food, perfume, wax, flowers, polished wood, rain on carriage roofs.

### Social pressure
Admission is a political fact. Being seen entering the wrong club can matter. Being denied entry can matter more.

### Scene pressure
The PCs need to meet someone who refuses a public audience. Their target is dining in an Orzhov-owned restaurant where every staff member is professionally discreet and socially observant.

Getting in is easy with money. Getting in without announcing themselves is harder.

---

## Plaza South — “Downside”

### Canon anchor
A commercial district along the Transguild Promenade, with specialty shops serving tourists and wealthy customers. It has more permanent residents than the other plaza neighborhoods.

### Why it matters
This is where Precinct One becomes economically comprehensible. Artisans, merchants, service workers, and residents turn elite demand into actual goods.

The wealthy may call it Downside because it is less prestigious. Residents may hear that name as evidence that people who depend on their labor still consider them socially lower.

### Scene pressure
A specialty crafter refuses an Orzhov buyout that would let them retire comfortably because the shop employs two generations of neighborhood families. Their children think the refusal is selfish.

No villain is required.

---

## Orzhova — Church of Deals

### Canon anchor
The Orzhov guildhall stands on the western edge of Tenth District Plaza. Its cathedral-like architecture houses elite gatherings and the Obzedat; opulent mausoleum spaces lie below.

### What it feels like
The exterior announces that finance, religion, and eternity are one institution.

Inside, visitors should feel **courteously evaluated**. Floors gleam. Clerks speak softly. Knights stand still enough to become part of the architecture. Servants anticipate needs before guests ask.

The mausoleum below changes the emotional temperature. Wealth does not disappear underground; it intensifies. The dead remain socially present.

### Sound
Footsteps softened by rugs, low prayer, coin and pen at distant desks, occasional ghostly speech with no visible speaker.

### Smell
Incense, wax, old stone, perfume, paper, preserved flowers.

### Scene pressure
A poor debtor and a wealthy oligarch wait in the same antechamber. Both are treated politely. Only one can afford the consequences of waiting.

---

## Vizkopa Bank

### Canon anchor
A monumental Orzhov bank and mint guarded by gargoyles and indebted spirits.

### Read it as infrastructure
Vizkopa is not merely a dungeon with money. Businesses, landlords, guild projects, inheritances, and private fortunes depend on what happens inside.

A rumor that Vizkopa will freeze credit can change markets before any official announcement.

### Scene pressure
The PCs need to stop a fraudulent withdrawal. The fastest way is to freeze an entire account network, which would also block payroll for hundreds of unrelated workers until review is complete.

The bank official is willing to do it—if the PCs sign responsibility for losses.

## Precinct One GM memory line

**Clean stone, expensive quiet, visible order, invisible labor, and every polite interaction carrying institutional weight.**

# Precinct Two — Named Places, Neighborhood Life, and Pressure

Precinct Two is one of the best places to show that Ravnica can be orderly, prosperous, domestic, and corrupt at the same time. Professionals, officials, retired soldiers, working families, small businesses, and criminal influence all coexist beneath the visual authority of New Prahv.

## New Prahv

### Canon anchor
The Azorius guildhall is the tallest structure in the Tenth District and is formed around three enormous towers corresponding to the Senate's three Columns.

### What the skyline does
New Prahv is orientation as much as architecture. Residents know where they are by which side of the towers they can see. Shadows move across neighborhoods as the day changes.

From far away, the three-column form makes Azorius government look unified.

Inside, the three branches argue constantly.

### Street approach
The closer the PCs get, the more everyday business becomes procedural. Messenger lanes appear. Notice boards multiply. Uniforms become common. Food sellers specialize in quick meals for officials. Copyists, advocates, witnesses, petitioners, and people carrying bundles of evidence crowd approaches.

### Sound
Bells, carriage wheels, clerks reading names, pages running messages, doors closing, legal argument escaping through open windows.

### Smell
Paper, ink, rain, stone, coffee, dust from old files, leather document cases.

### Scene pressure
A witness needs to reach a hearing before the filing window closes. A local protection crew blocks the quickest street but will not cause violence within sight of New Prahv.

The conflict is about time, intimidation, and who is willing to make the threat public.

---

## Whitestone

### Canon anchor
An orderly northern neighborhood of pale houses developed to house people working in New Prahv. Earlier residents were displaced during its construction, many toward denser Griffin Heights.

### Surface impression
Whitestone appears to prove that planning works: aligned facades, maintained walks, tidy drains, predictable services.

The deeper story is displacement. Older residents remember what stood here before. Newer residents may not know.

### Sights
Alabaster rows, trimmed stoops, identical drainage channels, small family gardens, official workers walking the same morning routes.

### Social texture
Neighbors care about reputation. A resident can resent the Senate and still call an arrester when someone parks a freight cart illegally.

### Scene pressure
A century-old compensation claim resurfaces because a newly discovered property marker suggests the original relocation survey was wrong.

The present homeowners did nothing wrong. The displaced family's descendants are also right.

---

## Griffin Heights

### Canon anchor
A southern neighborhood with modest houses, corruption, and strong Orzhov influence. Residents may pay protection to Orzhov knights and develop genuine loyalty to them.

### Do not play it as “the bad neighborhood”
People live here because it is home.

There are bakeries, children, retired soldiers, mechanics, clerks, small gardens, neighborhood arguments, and people who know which Orzhov collector is cruel and which one quietly gives extensions.

Corruption works because it is relational.

### Sound
Doors open onto conversation. Children in streets. shop bells. neighborhood gossip. distant station horns.

### Scene pressure
Boros plans a raid on an Orzhov protection crew. Residents oppose the crew's extortion but fear what happens when the people who currently deter burglary vanish overnight.

The PCs can help build a replacement before the raid or dismiss the concern and learn why residents were afraid.

---

## Augustin Station

### Canon anchor
The Tenth District's major airship station sits on the western end of Griffin Heights. Numerous platforms handle balloons, gondolas, and enormous Simic-bred flying creatures; traffic continues around the clock.

### First impression
This is **Ravnica in motion above the streets**.

Passengers drag luggage. Freight crews shout weights. Handlers soothe giant creatures. Porters compete for work. Announcements overlap. Families embrace at arrivals while business travelers push around them.

### Visual field
Platforms project into open air. Rigging lines sway. Balloon envelopes loom overhead. Creatures large enough to carry compartments shift their weight against restraints. Below, the city drops away in layers.

### Sound
Wind, ropes, bells, animal calls, platform numbers, arguments over baggage, warning horns, distant machinery.

### Smell
Oil, animal musk, rain, hot food, rope fiber, ozone from nearby Izzet work.

### Ordinary workers
- platform dispatcher;
- beast handler;
- baggage porter;
- ticket clerk;
- maintenance technician;
- street vendor who knows every delayed flight;
- unofficial guide who recognizes lost visitors instantly.

### Why it is politically useful
Stations create plausible contact between every guild, outsiders, smugglers, couriers, officials, and ordinary travelers.

Security must stay functional without making travel impossible.

### Scene pressure
An incoming passenger is wanted by Azorius. Boros has been told they are dangerous. The station master refuses to halt all traffic without a written order because a shutdown will strand hundreds of passengers and live cargo.

Meanwhile the person may already be on a connecting platform.

---

## Statue of Agrus Kos and neighborhood memory

A public statue or memorial is not just scenery. Boros veterans leave small tokens. Students sit nearby. Tourists ask who the figure was. Someone uses the base as a meeting point because everyone knows it.

When historical figures remain in public space, they shape how current institutions justify themselves.

## Precinct Two GM memory line

**Serviceable homes, civic pride, long commutes, old favors, protection money, and the shadow of law literally falling across the neighborhood.**

# Precinct Three — Named Places, Green Urbanism, and Community

Precinct Three is the Greenbelt, but it should never be described as countryside dropped into the city. It is **urban nature shaped by centuries of cohabitation**: parks, stables, gardens, old stone, wooden structures strengthened by magic, elevated green roadways, animal traffic, multigenerational homes, and Selesnyan communal institutions.

## Vitu-Ghazi

### Canon anchor
Vitu-Ghazi is the Selesnya guildhall, a monumental city-tree and cathedral to Mat'Selesnya. It stands as the center of Selesnyan culture, law, pilgrimage, and identity.

### Approach
The tree becomes visible long before the entrance is obvious.

The skyline changes from towers dominating trees to one living structure dominating towers. Roots have shaped paving and foundations. Streets widen where crowds of pilgrims routinely gather.

### Sound
Leaves high above ordinary rooftops, birds, prayer, conversation, animal harness, communal songs, children, market noise from approaches.

### Smell
Bark, soil, flowers, cooking, rain caught in vegetation, animals, herbal incense.

### Social experience
Visitors without hostile intent can enter public areas, so Vitu-Ghazi should contain both devotion and tourism.

Some people come seeking spiritual communion. Some come because the markets are good. Some are guildless residents meeting relatives. Some tourists take pictures or stare while initiates try not to resent them.

### Authority
Selesnyan law is experienced as communal legitimacy rather than a wall of official paperwork. People know who the voda is, who organizes meals, who trains militia, who mediates disputes.

### Scene pressure
A citizen accused of theft seeks sanctuary among relatives at Vitu-Ghazi. Azorius has lawful authority to arrest them. The vernadi asks for time to establish restitution without removing the person from the community.

The argument is not whether theft occurred. It is what justice is for.

---

## The Canopy

### Canon anchor
A northern neighborhood beneath tall trees overshadowed by Vitu-Ghazi; buildings occupy ground and larger branches, connected by ladders and rope walkways.

### Verticality
This is where Ravnica's vertical city becomes organic.

The PCs can stand on stone street level, hear a conversation above, climb a ladder to a family walkway, then cross through foliage to a home whose front door is thirty feet above the old road.

### Sights
Branch dwellings, rope rails, laundry between trees, baskets raised by pulley, children moving confidently where outsiders grip every handhold.

### Sound
Wood creak, rope strain, leaves, distant hooves below, voices from multiple elevations.

### Hazard
Fire is terrifying here. So are violent aerial creatures. Residents treat water buckets, evacuation routes, and branch maintenance seriously.

### Scene pressure
An old tree has become unsafe after magical contamination. Cutting it protects several homes but destroys dwellings built into its branches. Simic offers a biological treatment with uncertain side effects.

---

## Great Concourse

### Canon anchor
Elevated Selesnyan roadways converge here. On holy days it fills with worshipers; on ordinary days it operates as a major market.

### Visual identity
Polished pale pathways topped with living greenery create an impossible hybrid of road, park, bridge, and shrine.

Trade carts move beside pilgrims. Animals graze in controlled strips while merchants unload goods. Shade and sun shift through cultivated canopy.

### Social rule
Market behavior and sacred behavior overlap. A merchant can haggle loudly one day and attend a rite at the same place another.

### Scene pressure
A commercial dispute erupts during preparations for a holy day. Closing stalls would make room for pilgrims but destroy the week's income for merchants who traveled across districts.

---

## Concordance

### Canon anchor
An eastern neighborhood associated with the old city and among the oldest parts of the precinct.

### Urban age
Do not make “old” mean ruined.

Concordance should feel **adapted**. Doorways altered for new residents. Ancient stone beneath newer wood. Old courtyards serving new purposes. Shrines with layers of repair that visibly come from different centuries.

Residents use history casually because they live inside it.

### Scene pressure
A renovation uncovers an older sealed chamber beneath a family home. Azorius wants archaeological review. Selesnya says the space is connected to local spiritual history. The family wants their kitchen floor back.

---

## Stables and animal economy

Precinct Three feeds and moves more than people.

Ravnica's domesticated beasts range well beyond ordinary horses. Stable work means feed logistics, waste, veterinary care, harness craft, breeding, transport, and complaints from neighbors.

A stable accident can create a city-scale traffic event if the escaped animal is large enough.

## Evening life

The precinct's strongest contrast with dense commercial areas comes after work.

Communal gatherings, storytelling, trained animal performances, shared meals, and neighborhood care should make evening feel socially open.

The GM should show why Selesnya's offer of belonging appeals to people.

## Precinct Three GM memory line

**Old homes under living shade, animals and children treated as shared responsibilities, sacred space functioning as market space, and every green refuge carrying a political question about who gets to shape nature.**

# Precinct Four — Named Places, War-Scarred Streets, and Adaptation

Precinct Four is where the Tenth District's tensions become audible. Boros fortifications, Izzet experiments, Gruul incursions, goblin turf, merchants who have learned to defend their own stalls, and constant repair create a culture where adaptability is a civic virtue.

## Sunhome

### Canon anchor
Sunhome is the Boros guildhall: fortress, barracks, command center, and spiritual heart of the Legion.

### Approach
The district begins to organize itself around defensibility before the fortress is reached. Intersections have clear firing lanes. Barricade anchors are built into paving. Supply yards sit close to routes broad enough for troops and freight.

Sunhome should feel less like a palace and more like a place designed by people who expect to be attacked and believe they will hold.

### Sights
Red-white banners, training yards, armored traffic, rocs circling above, weapon racks, memorial plaques, field kitchens, healers moving between wings.

### Sounds
Boot cadence, command calls, armor being repaired, distant horns, wings overhead, mess-hall noise, laughter from off-duty soldiers.

### Smells
Leather, oil, metal, soup, sweat, stone warmed by sun, animal musk.

### Human texture
A recruit is nervous about first patrol. A veteran complains about new doctrine. A quartermaster is more feared than a lieutenant because nobody gets gear without paperwork. A healer knows which officer is sending exhausted soldiers back too quickly.

### Scene pressure
A Boros unit needs an Izzet device for an urgent defense. The device has not passed testing. The commanding officer is willing to accept risk to soldiers; the quartermaster refuses to authorize equipment that might endanger civilians near deployment.

---

## Nivix

### Canon anchor
Nivix is one of Ravnica's tallest towers and the Izzet guildhall, filled with laboratories, testing facilities, and living quarters.

### The tower as machine
Nivix should never be still.

Light moves in windows. Steam leaves vents at odd heights. Elevators, platforms, and magical transport connect vertical work zones. Laboratories with incompatible hazards are separated by architecture that looks improvised until a researcher explains the rationale.

The building has a pulse: scheduled energy demand, experimental surges, alarms people react to differently depending on tone.

### Sound
Electrical crackle, pumps, bells, shouted measurements, glass breakage, distant thunder from inside, machinery with rhythmic vibration.

### Smell
Ozone, hot copper, solvents, scorched insulation, coffee, smoke that may or may not indicate danger.

### Social structure
A visitor cannot understand rank merely by clothing. A stained coat can belong to a brilliant director. The real signals are access, who interrupts whom, which lab door opens automatically, and whose questions make technicians stop working.

### Scene pressure
An experiment in one laboratory is drawing power needed by a public system outside. The lab insists shutting down will destroy months of work. Residents outside do not care.

---

## Red Wastes

### Canon anchor
The rubblebelt north of the precinct contains ruined buildings, debris, beasts, elementals, and Gruul camps. Some stubborn non-Gruul residents remain.

### Terrain
This is not empty wilderness. It is **civilization partially withdrawn**.

Roads end abruptly. Foundations remain after upper floors vanished. Trees and brush exploit cracks. Wind crosses spaces that once had roofs. Broken masonry forms ridges and sudden pits.

### Sound
Less constant urban roar, more distant impacts, animals, wind through ruined structures, occasional drums or shouted clan calls.

### Social truth
A surface official may call a structure abandoned while a Gruul family uses it seasonally, scavengers work it weekly, and an old resident still receives mail nearby.

### Scene pressure
Boros wants to demolish a ruined tower used by Gruul scouts. The tower also supports a bridge serving a guildless settlement. Destroying the tactical threat creates a housing crisis.

---

## The Bulwark

### Canon anchor
A reinforced neighborhood between Sunhome and Nivix, home to relatively inexpensive housing and businesses serving workers and professionals tied to the nearby institutions.

### Daily life
The Bulwark is where the grand guildhalls become rent, groceries, shift schedules, repair shops, taverns, boarding houses, and businesses selling things soldiers and researchers need.

A Boros sergeant and Izzet technician may live in the same building and argue about whose institution causes more night noise.

### Sights
Heavy shutters, repaired walls, basement shelters, equipment shops, inexpensive eateries, scorch marks painted over repeatedly.

### Scene pressure
An insurance company refuses renewal after repeated magical damage. The landlord plans to evict tenants and convert the building to storage because warehouses are easier to insure.

---

## Skarrg

### Canon anchor
The closest thing the Gruul have to a common guildhall: the cratered remains of an ancient palace in the Red Wastes where clans sometimes gather around a perpetual bonfire.

### What outsiders get wrong
Skarrg is not a council chamber with primitive decoration. It is a place where temporary unity is performed through feasting, boasting, challenge, ritual, and strength.

Old grudges remain active. No guarantee of peace exists merely because multiple clans are present.

### Sights and sounds
Gutted architecture blackened by fire. Giant bones. scavenged banners. weapon displays. clan markings layered over older carvings. Meat roasting. Challenges shouted across the ruin. Laughter that can turn into violence.

### Scene pressure
The PCs are invited by one clan. Another clan interprets their presence as an insult. Their host will protect them—but doing so may weaken the host politically.

---

## Tin Street through Precinct Four

Tin Street is trade and nightlife pushed through a dangerous precinct. Boros presence reassures shoppers; goblin gangs and rubblebelt threats keep everyone alert.

Street life changes by hour.

**Morning:** deliveries, cleanup, repair.  
**Afternoon:** trade at full volume.  
**Evening:** food, theater, crowds.  
**Night:** nightlife, deals, patrols, gangs, people who know which blocks to avoid.

The Shattergang Brothers and other criminal actors fit because weapons and explosives are already normal commodities near war-scarred neighborhoods.

## Precinct Four GM memory line

**Fortress shadow, laboratory glare, patched walls, people who know evacuation routes, and commerce surviving because danger has become part of local competence.**

# Precinct Five — Named Places, Knowledge Economy, and Intellectual Rivalry

Precinct Five makes knowledge visible as labor, status, commodity, and crime. Universities, libraries, laboratories, apartments, pubs, magical suppliers, information brokers, and students are packed together with less urban planning than outsiders expect.

## Zonot Seven and Zameck

### Canon anchor
Zonot Seven is the only Simic zonot within the city proper. It contains Zameck, the guildhall. A major chamber close below street level serves the Speakers and visitors; canals connect outward, including toward Precinct Six.

### Entering the zonot
Street-level cues begin subtly: more waterproof clothing, drainage channels, carts carrying sealed tanks, humid air rising from grates.

Then the city opens downward.

The zonot should feel enormous because the old city layers have collapsed into a vertical aquatic habitat. Balconies, bridges, laboratories, pools, pens, homes, and meeting chambers line the descent.

### Sound
Water on stone, pumps, voices echoing from lower walkways, splashes too large to identify, glass being moved, occasional creature calls.

### Smell
Brine, wet rock, disinfectant, algae, animal feed, medicinal herbs.

### Public versus restricted areas
Zameck's visitor spaces are designed for land-dwellers and diplomacy. Deeper facilities assume Simic bodies, specialized equipment, and tolerance for environments outsiders find uncomfortable.

That transition itself communicates culture.

### Scene pressure
An Azorius inspection team has authority to enter a lab but lacks equipment safe for the depth. Simic offers guides. The lead inspector suspects the guides are choosing routes that hide other violations.

Both sides smile politely.

---

## The Blistercoils

### Canon anchor
Gigantic Izzet-operated waterwheels near Zonot Seven generate magical energy for public works, Simic facilities, and Izzet workshops. Excess power is stored below.

### Civic significance
This is an excellent illustration of Ravnican interdependence: an Izzet machine physically adjacent to Simic habitat helps power both public and guild systems.

Residents complain about noise and still panic when the wheels slow.

### Sights
Immense turning structures throwing spray, energy channels, maintenance gantries, workers in protective gear, smaller workshops clustered nearby.

### Sounds
Water thunder, gear rotation, electrical hum, shouted maintenance calls.

### Sensation
Fine spray settles on skin. Railings vibrate. Hair may rise slightly from stored charge.

### Scene pressure
One wheel develops an imbalance. Shutting the array down safely cuts power to several labs and a nearby neighborhood. Keeping it running risks damage to underground storage.

The correct engineering answer and the correct civic answer may differ.

---

## Hightower

### Canon anchor
A western neighborhood dense with educational institutions, from broad universities to specialized schools.

### Daily rhythm
Morning belongs to students racing to lectures. Afternoon belongs to cafes, tutors, demonstrations, and arguments. Night belongs to libraries, pubs, experiments, poetry, and people insisting they are still working.

### Status culture
People display worth through ideas, accomplishment, and association.

Academic gossip is political currency:
- who published first;
- who stole an idea;
- whose patron withdrew funding;
- which professor is quietly recruiting for a guild;
- which student built something dangerous and brilliant.

### Scene pressure
A professor publicly accuses a student of theft. The student actually received the idea from a Dimir source who stole it from somebody else entirely.

---

## Prism University

### Canon anchor
A university focused on broad magical theory rather than a single guild's practical specialization. It maintains formal neutrality while multiple guilds recruit and infiltrate it.

### What neutrality looks like
No single guild uniform dominates. Lecture halls host competing approaches. Student groups form around philosophies. Recruiters sponsor events without officially recruiting.

The institution survives by being useful to everyone.

### Visual identity
Crystal-paned spaces, lecture diagrams, demonstration rooms warded against accidents, courtyards where spell practice is socially normal.

### Scene pressure
A neutral faculty committee is deciding whether to accept an Izzet research grant. Simic objects to the experimental terms. Orzhov offers matching funds with different strings. The students mainly want the equipment.

---

## Ismeri Library

### Canon anchor
One of Ravnica's great libraries, open continuously and secretly important to Dimir communication.

### Public life
At any hour someone is reading, copying, sleeping over a book, arguing with a librarian, looking for a reference, or trying to seem as though they belong.

Night is quieter but not empty.

### Dimir layer
The library is powerful because coded communication hides inside legitimate research behavior. A person checking four unrelated texts might be a student—or assembling fragments of an instruction.

The safest Dimir activity looks like scholarship.

### Smell and sound
Paper, dust, leather, ink, old wood, tea. Pages, chairs, whispered questions, ladders, occasional magical ward tone.

### Scene pressure
The PCs know a coded message is hidden somewhere in the library. Removing every suspect book would expose the method and disrupt public access. Reading it in place means behaving like the agent expected to retrieve it.

---

## Information brokers

Knowledge is a market here. Private investigators, rumormongers, booksellers, academics, spies, and specialists sell different levels of certainty.

The GM should distinguish:
- fact;
- interpretation;
- rumor;
- privileged access;
- dangerous knowledge;
- evidence someone else knows you asked.

## Precinct Five GM memory line

**Crowded minds in crowded buildings, arguments as status displays, knowledge sold by the page or secret, and every quiet library aisle potentially part of a guild war.**

# Precinct Six — Named Places, Labor, Night Danger, and the Undercity Edge

Precinct Six is not simply "the poor industrial area." It is a logistics engine. Warehouses, docks, wide freight roads, modest worker housing, factories, organized crime, Rakdos nightlife, Orzhov ownership, Boros storage and patrols, Golgari access, and undercity monsters all overlap.

The people who live here tend to value practical reliability over respectability.

## Smelting Quarter

### Canon anchor
The manufacturing heart at the north end of the precinct: hot, smoky, active day and night. Foundry Street supports goblin communities, crime, factories, and intense Rakdos presence.

### Shift rhythm
**Dawn:** night workers leave as day crews arrive; food vendors make their best money.  
**Midday:** freight and heat peak.  
**Evening:** workers fill cheap taverns while performers assemble stages.  
**Night:** industrial work continues while Rakdos clubs intensify; Boros watches the line between revel and riot.

### Sights
Furnace glow reflected in low cloud, cranes, carts, molten metal glimpsed behind shields, soot on pale stone, workers with heavy gloves, goblin homes squeezed around industrial structures.

### Sounds
Metal striking metal, steam, warning whistles, shouted load counts, Rakdos drums, cart chains, factory gates.

### Smells
Hot iron, coal or magical combustion, oil, sweat, smoke, street food, alcohol, sulfur after experiments.

### Social rule
People respect someone who can do their job safely under pressure. Wealth alone impresses less here.

---

## Kamen Fortress

### Canon anchor
A Boros garrison positioned to protect Legion interests and watch Rakdos activity around the Smelting Quarter.

### Why residents feel mixed about it
Kamen means patrols arrive faster. It also means residents are routinely observed as potential trouble.

Factory owners appreciate security. Rakdos performers treat it as an audience they did not invite. Goblin gangs map patrol timing carefully.

### Scene pressure
Kamen imposes a temporary curfew after a string of killings. The curfew protects residents from a night predator and destroys income for legitimate night workers.

---

## Gore House and the Demon's Vestibule

### Canon anchor
Gore House is a Rakdos club in a repurposed factory; the area provides an entrance down toward Rix Maadi through the Demon's Vestibule.

### Day
Shuttered doors, cleanup crews, damaged props, deliveries, performers sleeping late, neighbors complaining about last night's noise.

### Night
Heat, music, smoke, crowds, bouncers, performance danger, people arriving because the place is notorious.

The club should feel like a business and community as well as a cult venue.

### The descent
The path toward Rix Maadi should feel like moving away from ordinary municipal Ravnica while still carrying traces of industry: old stairs, brick, chains, heat, echo, graffiti, infrastructure abandoned or repurposed.

### Scene pressure
The PCs need information from a performer below, but Kamen has closed the visible entrance. Another route exists through factory service tunnels controlled by workers who dislike both Boros and Rakdos.

---

## Medori Park

### Canon anchor
Despite its name, this western area was converted from parkland into warehouse use by an Orzhov figure. It is heavily tied to storage and has a strong undead presence at night.

### The name as urban memory
Residents still call it a park because city names outlive land use.

That is deeply Ravnican: history becomes vocabulary after physical reality changes.

### Day
Warehouse clerks, guards, teamsters, loaders, inventories, Boros equipment storage, Orzhov property managers.

### Night
Shutters close. Watch changes. Undead guardians and less controlled dangers emerge. The same broad warehouse lanes that feel merely utilitarian by day become exposed and echoing.

### Scene pressure
A warehouse worker disappears on the night shift. The owner calls it abandonment. Coworkers insist a spirit has been following employees who handle one particular shipment.

---

## Deadbridge Chasm

### Canon anchor
A vast opening into Golgari territory and routes toward Korozda. Mossy stairs and fungal growth line the descent; kraul and Devkarin traffic connect surface and undercity trade.

### First impression
The city suddenly has an edge.

Where most Ravnica hides depth under floors and grates, Deadbridge reveals it.

Bridges span darkness. Stairs cling to walls. Fungal light marks paths below. Smell rises before details become visible.

### Sound
Echoed carts, insect wings, water, voices from far below, bridge traffic overhead, occasional falling debris.

### Smell
Decomposition, damp earth, fungus, sewage, cooking from lower settlements, surface smoke settling downward.

### Social reality
Surface citizens use the chasm as a mental boundary. Golgari traders use it as a road.

What one population calls descent, another calls commute.

### Scene pressure
A body falls from a bridge. Surface law treats the chasm as crime scene boundary. Golgari below recover the corpse first and insist local custom now applies.

---

## Wayport

### Canon anchor
A vertically layered neighborhood rising within the chasm, connected to surrounding city by numerous bridges and handling significant warehousing and interdistrict trade.

### Why it is visually memorable
Wayport makes Ravnica's verticality impossible to ignore.

A warehouse roof on one level can be a loading yard for another bridge. Freight moves horizontally across open depth and vertically through hoists.

### Scene pressure
A bridge closure sends freight through a lower route claimed by Golgari residents. Surface merchants call the route public. Residents disagree.

---

## Benzer's Bridge

### Canon anchor
A broad cargo thoroughfare carrying interdistrict traffic, with a small market and hidden nightlife inside or beneath the bridge structure.

### Day
Freight, shops, food, carts, constant flow.

### Night
Legitimate businesses close. Hidden rooms open. The bridge's interior becomes another district layered beneath the public one.

### Scene pressure
A daytime merchant asks the PCs to retrieve a family member from a pain club hidden under the same stall where they sell lunch.

The merchant knows exactly where the entrance is and has pretended for years not to know.

---

## Labor culture

Precinct Six works because people show up despite heat, danger, theft, ownership disputes, and bad shifts.

A worker who keeps a machine running may have more local respect than a rich visitor.

Use:
- union-like informal crews;

**Freight never stops, furnaces color the night, respectable ownership sits beside practical crime, and every bridge or warehouse has another city underneath it.**

# Precinct One — Guildpact and Ceremony

V2 uses Precinct One as the district's most **institutionally visible** space: formal delegations, civic buildings, prominent plazas, visitors, guild representatives, petitioners, and people who know they are being watched.

The stone is cleaner here because someone is paid to keep it clean. Streets broaden near important buildings. Sightlines are designed to impress. Banners hang where visitors can see them. Guards know the difference between a lost citizen and someone pretending to be lost.

People still eat lunch here. Porters still complain. Copyists have ink on their cuffs. Guards know which cafe serves fastest. Petitioners wait for hours and form temporary communities in queues.

Azorius officials want process. Boros delegates want decisions that protect people. Orzhov advocates want language that protects interests. Dimir messengers carry sealed information. Within minutes, someone asks why they are here. Within an hour, at least one office has a written record. By the end of the day, a rival may know the subject of their visit even if the meeting itself was private.

A Boros captain has brought witnesses after a tenement fire. An Azorius clerk refuses to accept testimony because the emergency declaration used the wrong district code. An Izzet engineer says the fire was caused by unauthorized modifications. The landlord's Orzhov advocate insists the tenants caused it. The witnesses can smell smoke on their own clothes.

Nobody is lying about everything.

# Precinct Two — Civic and Residential Pressure

V2 treats Precinct Two as a place where **the city is lived rather than displayed**: apartments, schools, shops, workshops, cafes, clinics, small offices, local shrines, public services, and endless arguments over shared space.

Balconies. Signs. Laundry. Children. delivery carts. neighbors leaning out windows. Repairs happening while everyone steps around them.

**See:** window boxes, drying clothes, patched plaster, shop awnings, legal notices, pipes disappearing through walls, communal courtyards.

**Smell:** cooking, soap, bakery heat, damp stairwells, animal pens, cheap incense, laundry steam.

Everyone knows something about everyone nearby, but not necessarily correctly. Landlords, shopkeepers, block captains, healers, building superintendents, local gang members, and retired guild employees have influence that formal institutions underestimate.

- rent and repairs;
- a business expanding into shared space;
- noise from a Rakdos rehearsal;
- an Izzet service shutdown;
- a Selesnyan garden blocking planned construction;
- Boros patrol frequency;
- Azorius inspection notices;
- Orzhov debt collectors;
- rumors that a courier tenant is Dimir;
- Simic clinic waste;
- Golgari deliveries nobody wants to see unloaded.

## What the PCs can accidentally break

A heroic chase across rooftops may destroy water tanks. A fireball can ruin three families' laundry and the wooden additions to an old masonry wall. Summoned creatures block stairs. A fight in a shop changes whether that shop survives the month.

## Scene seed

A child is missing. The family blames a Rakdos troupe because a performance passed the block. A Boros patrol believes the child ran away. A Dimir courier quietly knows the child boarded a freight lift. The lift operator will talk only if someone clears his brother's Azorius fine.

The neighborhood solves problems through relationships before institutions.

# Precinct Three — Greenbelts and Communal Space

V2 uses Precinct Three to show that Ravnica's "nature" is not untouched wilderness. It is **nature surviving, being cultivated, being defended, and sometimes reclaiming the city**.

## First impression

Stone does not vanish; it becomes entangled. Roots lift paving. Trees grow through courtyards. Old walls become terraces. Vernadi and community gardens create pockets where sound changes and shade matters.

## Sensory palette

**See:** vines on masonry, green roofs, centaurs moving comfortably through broader paths, birds and insects, white-green banners, water channels, carved benches swallowed by roots.

**Hear:** leaves, conversation, training calls, children, devotional song, hooves, distant city noise softened by vegetation.

**Smell:** wet soil, flowers, compost, cooking herbs, bark, rain, animal fur.

## Selesnyan presence

Selesnyan community spaces can be genuinely welcoming: food, healing, childcare, companionship, training, worship, and safety. The same community can apply intense social pressure toward harmony and common purpose.

Do not reveal the tension by making everyone creepy. Reveal it when the PCs ask someone to place personal desire above the group.

## Gruul friction

Green does not automatically mean Gruul approval. The Gruul can see managed gardens as domesticated nature and another claim that civilization gets to decide where wilderness belongs.

## Simic interest

The Simic see ecological systems, adaptation, and useful biological relationships. Their solutions can help preserve urban green space—or turn it into a research site residents no longer recognize.

## Development conflict

Precinct Three scenes become strong when a practical civic need is real.

A new transit line would shorten commutes for thousands but remove a mature grove. A Selesnyan vernadi refuses. Izzet engineers insist there is no safe alternate path. Azorius planners have approved the project. Gruul saboteurs threaten to destroy the whole works. Local guildless residents are split because they need both shade and transport.

Nobody needs to be secretly evil.

## Linger clock

After ten minutes: someone offers food or asks why the PCs are there.  
After an hour: they are invited to help with something communal.  
After a day: people expect reciprocity.

# Precinct Four — Edges, Garrisons and Rebuilding

V2 treats Precinct Four as a place where **security and reconstruction are visible parts of daily life**. Barricades, damaged structures, garrisons, reclaimed lots, new walls beside old ruins, and contested routes give the precinct a harder rhythm.

## First impression

You can see where the city has been broken and repaired. New stone is a different color. Temporary wooden walkways have become permanent by neglect. Boros positions watch wide intersections. Graffiti appears on fresh walls before the mortar fully cures.

## Sensory palette

**See:** scaffolds, patchwork masonry, drill yards, watch posts, supply carts, rubble sorted for reuse.

**Hear:** hammering, shouted cadence, horns, arguments at checkpoints, masonry saws, cart chains.

**Smell:** dust, sweat, leather, hot metal, soup from field kitchens, wet mortar.

A garrison is not only soldiers. It creates cooks, armorers, healers, messengers, dependents, vendors, and residents who feel safer—or watched.

Boros troops know local trouble spots. Some residents greet them by name. Others go quiet when they turn the corner.

## Gruul pressure

Where construction pushes into ruins or open land, Gruul hostility can be immediate. Their attack may be ideological, territorial, retaliatory, or opportunistic. "Gruul raid" should not automatically mean random violence. Ask what changed recently.

## Displacement

Rebuilding creates winners and losers. A cleared ruin can become housing, a military yard, a market, or a luxury development. People who had informal use of the space may not appear on any Azorius plan.

## Scene seed

Boros engineers are fortifying a bridge after repeated attacks. The new wall blocks a neighborhood's only easy access to a lower market. Residents begin cutting unauthorized doors. An Azorius inspector orders the doors sealed. A Gruul gang watches from nearby ruins and starts leaving tools where residents can find them.

The question becomes who is defending whom from what.

# Precinct Five — Scholars, Laboratories and Zonot Seven

V2 treats Precinct Five as the district's **expertise economy**: universities, laboratories, archives, specialist workshops, clinics, lecture halls, and technical rivalries. Published material places the Simic guildhall **Zameck in Zonot Seven** here.

## First impression

People carry notebooks, cases, specimen jars, instruments, rolled diagrams, and coffee. Clothing shows burns, stains, chemical damage, or careful academic respectability.

A messenger who can navigate faculty offices is as valuable as a spellcaster.

## Sensory palette

**See:** glassware in windows, lecture posters, sealed doors, animated diagrams, water channels, weird equipment being transported under tarps.

**Hear:** bells marking lectures, arguments using vocabulary bystanders do not understand, bubbling tanks, magical hum, applause from a demonstration.

**Smell:** disinfectant, brine, hot metal, ink, ozone, preserved specimens, coffee.

## Izzet and Simic rivalry

The Izzet ask what happens if energy, matter, space, or magic is pushed harder.

The Simic ask what life can become if adaptation is accelerated or redirected.

Both respect expertise and experimentation, but they have different intuitions about what counts as a system and what counts as a subject.

## Zameck and the zonot

A zonot is not merely a basement lab. It descends through city layers toward ancient water, creating its own ecosystem, culture, research spaces, and vertical movement.

Visitors should feel the temperature and humidity change as they descend. Street noise fades. Water sound grows. Architecture becomes more biological. The sense of "city" does not disappear; it transforms.

## Academic politics

Research has patrons, rivals, publication disputes, safety reviews, stolen ideas, student labor, guild priorities, and ethical boundaries. A scientific scene can be political without a villain.

## Scene seed

A Simic clinic has developed a treatment that works, but the organism used to produce it is spreading into municipal water channels. Izzet engineers want to sterilize the lines. Simic researchers insist that will destroy the only viable culture. Azorius officials want the facility closed pending review. Patients are waiting outside.

The PCs arrive while the cure is still being administered.

# Precinct Six — Industry, Deadbridge and the Smelting Quarter

Published material anchors several important identities here: **Deadbridge Chasm occupies much of Precinct Six**, and the **Smelting Quarter** is a Rakdos-heavy industrial neighborhood. V2 leans into Precinct Six as the place where the city's appetite becomes physically visible.

Freight. Heat. soot. cranes. shift workers. deep cuts in the urban fabric. Streets that smell like metal and damp stone at the same time.

The furnace is cooling. If it shuts completely, restarting it will cost a fortune.

## Source Note: 02-Locations/Tenth-District-Living-Gazetteer.md

# Tenth District Living Gazetteer

The Tenth District is not a theme park divided into ten guild zones. All ten guilds operate here, often on the same street, and the district's importance comes from **density of institutions, headquarters, trade, transit, politics, and population**.

## How to think about the six precincts

The published precincts are broad GM canvases rather than sealed boroughs. V2 uses each precinct as a strong scene lens, not a hard border. A single walk can move from polished civic stone to crowded housing, then into a green courtyard, then across a freight viaduct without the city stopping to announce a precinct change.

| Precinct | V2 scene identity | Immediate pressure |
|---|---|---|
| One | Guildpact, ceremony, administration, visitors | access, precedent, public visibility |
| Two | Dense civic/residential life | housing, commerce, services, policing |
| Three | Greenbelts, communal spaces, living city | preservation, growth, territorial stewardship |
| Four | hard edges, garrisons, rebuilding, contested space | security, displacement, militarization |
| Five | universities, laboratories, expertise, specialized institutions | research, ethics, permits, intellectual rivalry |
| Six | industry, Deadbridge, smelting, freight, undercity interface | labor, pollution, crime, decay, infrastructure |

## Crossing precincts

Do not transition with exposition. Transition through **street evidence**.

From One into Two, official stone gives way to signs, balconies, laundry, market awnings, delivery traffic, and people who cannot pause for a delegation.

From Two into Three, the sound softens before the city actually becomes green. Roots lift paving. Courtyards widen. Small shrines and community gardens appear. Birds and insects become audible over wheels.

From Five into Six, academic glass and polished apparatus give way to soot, freight cranes, hot metal, night shifts, broken masonry, and routes that descend rather than rise.

## Neutrality is negotiated

Major trade ways and shared civic spaces function because too many people rely on them for any guild to own them completely. "Neutral" means everyone has reasons not to destroy the space—not that nobody spies, recruits, arrests, bargains, or intimidates there.

## Tenth District background motion

Whenever a scene feels empty, add one of these:

- an Izzet maintenance crew arguing over a conduit while traffic flows around them;
- a Boros patrol escorting an injured prisoner to a healer;
- Azorius clerks attaching legal notices to a condemned balcony;
- Golgari porters delivering cheap food uphill;
- a Selesnyan group carrying saplings toward a new courtyard;
- an Orzhov agent inspecting collateral;
- Rakdos stagehands measuring a plaza for that night's rig;
- Simic technicians transporting a sealed tank that moves from inside;
- Gruul graffiti painted over a redevelopment notice;
- a Dimir courier who looks completely ordinary and is probably doing completely ordinary courier work.

The district feels alive when **guild business overlaps ordinary business in the same frame**.

# The Ten Guildhalls — Experiential Atlas

Guildhalls are not interchangeable headquarters. Each expresses what its guild believes authority should **feel like**.

## New Prahv — Azorius Senate

New Prahv's three enormous towers make government visible from far beyond the doors. The building teaches visitors that law is larger than any one official.

**Approach:** orderly traffic, advocates and petitioners, arresters, messenger lanes, food stalls serving clerks, increasing density of formal notices.

**Inside:** broad public chambers feed into smaller offices and controlled corridors. People are routed by purpose. Waiting is institutionalized.

**Hear:** legal argument, names called, bells, papers, footsteps in echoing halls.

**Smell:** ink, stone, wax, leather files, coffee.

**Who belongs:** judges, scribes, arresters, lawmages, advocates, witnesses, cleaners, pages, lobbyists, petitioners.

**What an outsider gets wrong:** the Senate is not one mind. Sova, Jelenn, and Lyev personnel can disagree bitterly while defending the legitimacy of the same institution.

**Scene:** a field officer has evidence that would justify immediate action but cannot make it admissible without revealing a protected source.

---

## Sunhome — Boros Legion

Sunhome is fortress, barracks, command center, memorial, and spiritual symbol. It is designed around readiness rather than elegance.

**Approach:** patrols, supply wagons, rocs overhead, soldiers on errands, trainees, armorers, messengers, controlled gates.

**Inside:** training yards, barracks, armories, command rooms, kitchens, memorial spaces, medical facilities, equipment stores.

**Hear:** drills, armor repair, horns, mess-hall conversation, wingbeats, commands.

**Smell:** leather, oil, steel, sweat, cooking, animal care.

**Who belongs:** recruits, veterans, angels, quartermasters, healers, skyknights, Wojeks, cooks, armorers, clerks.

**What an outsider gets wrong:** Boros culture is not uninterrupted zeal. A standing army runs on logistics, trust, routine, fatigue, and people arguing about orders.

**Scene:** an exhausted unit is ordered back into the field because no replacement is available. The officer agrees the order is unfair and still believes it is necessary.

---

## Duskmantle — House Dimir

Duskmantle is defined by uncertainty of access. Under Lazav, its hidden undercity presence is protected by memory wards, and most Dimir members have no reason to visit it.

**Approach:** there should not be one reliable approach. A route can begin through an archive basement, an abandoned cistern, an old service tunnel, or a door the traveler later cannot place on a map.

**Inside:** avoid fixed cartography. Use chambers that feel too isolated from surrounding city, quiet workrooms, secure message facilities, memory wards, spaces designed for people who do not need to know who else uses them.

**Hear:** little. Water behind walls, distant footsteps, a door closing somewhere impossible to locate.

**Smell:** damp stone, dust, lamp smoke, clean paper.

**Who belongs:** high-level contacts, mind mages, couriers carrying information rather than parcels, agents who may not know one another.

**What an outsider gets wrong:** secrecy does not mean everyone is theatrical. The most dangerous person can look like the most ordinary clerk in the city.

**Scene:** after leaving, each PC remembers the meeting differently. None of the discrepancies is obviously magical until they compare notes.

Korozda lies within the Golgari undercity network, reached through a descent where surface assumptions about light, waste, ownership, and death cease to apply.

**Approach:** fungal growth thickens, surface stone gives way to older masonry, insect traffic increases, and pathways become recognizable only to people who understand local markings.

**Inside:** the guild's politics should be visible through who controls space: Devkarin, kraul, gorgons, shamans, assassins, workers, undead, and ordinary residents all overlap.

**Hear:** insects, water, low conversation, tools, ritual, distant movement through large underground chambers.

**Smell:** earth, fungus, decomposition, smoke, herbs, wet stone.

**Who belongs:** food growers, reclaimers, shamans, undead laborers, faction representatives, Ochran agents, families who may have lived below for generations.

**What an outsider gets wrong:** rot is not disorder. The Swarm can manage biological systems with extreme precision.

**Scene:** a surface delegation calls a chamber unsanitary. The local grower responds by listing three upper-city neighborhoods currently eating food produced there.

## Skarrg — Gruul Clans

Skarrg is a ruined palace in the rubblebelt and the nearest thing the disparate Gruul clans have to shared guild space.

**Approach:** paved streets become broken, patrol presence drops, sightlines open, scavenged clan marks replace official signage.

**Hear:** fire, drums, laughter, argument, challenges, meat roasting, stone breaking.

**Smell:** smoke, hide, blood, herbs, sweat, rain on rubble.

**Who belongs:** whoever can survive the gathering and has a clan reason to be there.

# Transguild Promenade and Major Trade Ways

## The Promenade as civic bloodstream

The Transguild Promenade crosses the core districts and carries trade, freight, pedestrians, processions, news, demonstrations, and political theater. It is one of the few spaces where nearly every kind of Ravnican expects to encounter every other kind.

The street should feel broader than nearby roads but never empty. Colonnades, small parks, market frontage, freight pull-offs, kiosks, shrines, cafes, and public notices create constant edge activity.

### Sights

Guild insignia beside guildless shop signs. Porters with shoulder yokes. Large freight beasts guided by handlers. Centaurs taking broad lanes. Roc riders high above. Procession banners folded near a staging yard. Azorius notices layered on posts already covered in advertisements. Orzhov offices with discreet entrances two doors from noisy food vendors.

### Sounds

Wheels. Hooves. bargaining. bells. a courier whistle. a distant military cadence. street musicians. an amplified legal notice. a cafe argument about guild politics. the hiss of a pressure vent under the paving.

### Smells

Bread, animal sweat, wet stone, coffee, perfume, hot metal from a repair crew, rainwater, flowers from a public garden, sewer air when a grate opens.

## Why violence changes tone here

People treat the Promenade's shared use as a practical necessity. When open conflict erupts, the important reaction is not only fear. It is disbelief and anger: carts stop, merchants yank shutters closed, couriers detour, Boros and Azorius both rush to control the space, and every guild immediately asks who gains from disrupting a route everyone needs.

## Ten-minute consequences

If the PCs stop traffic for ten minutes:

- a queue forms faster than expected;
- someone with perishable cargo becomes desperate;
- someone important is trapped behind ordinary citizens;
- a pickpocket or spy uses the congestion;
- an official threatens penalties;
- a street vendor makes excellent money;
- a rumor about the incident reaches the next precinct before the party does.

## Processions and public spectacle

On celebration days, the Promenade becomes symbolic ground. Guild banners, military formations, public sermons, civic speeches, seasonal decoration, and Rakdos mockery all compete for attention.

This is a good place to show that Ravnicans understand politics visually. The order of a procession, which guild stands nearest the center, whose banner is larger, and which official is forced to wait are all messages.

## GM encounter frame

A useful Promenade scene contains:

- one urgent traveler;
- one stalled commercial interest;
- one official with limited authority;
- one observer from a rival guild;
- one ordinary citizen who knows the local workaround.

Nothing has to become combat. The pressure is enough.

# Undercity, Verticality and the Buried City

The undercity is not one sewer level. It is **all the older Ravnicas that the current city has built over, beside, through, and forgotten**.

## Layers

A descent might pass:

1. modern cellar and utility corridors;
2. active drains, heat lines, and service passages;
3. abandoned transit;
4. older streets now roofed by later construction;
5. crypts, cisterns, ruins, collapsed plazas;
6. Golgari-controlled farms and settlements;
7. natural caverns or spaces so old that city history becomes uncertain.

The deeper party travels, the less reliable modern jurisdiction becomes.

## Sensory progression

Near the surface: warm pipes, hammering overhead, sewer water, maintenance lamps, chalk marks, service workers.

Deeper: constant damp, fungal light, insects, old stone polished by feet, roots through ceilings, distant water, organic heat from compost, echoes that make distance hard to judge.

Far below: enormous spaces suddenly opening after cramped tunnels; black water; architecture at impossible angles; statues facing walls that did not exist when they were carved; entire facades buried inside newer foundations.

## Golgari presence

The Golgari are not merely monsters living under the city. They operate food, reclamation, waste, fungal cultivation, undead labor, and communities whose relationship to the surface mixes resentment and necessity.

A surface merchant may insult Golgari food in public and still depend on their deliveries.

## Dimir presence

The undercity is useful for routes, caches, surveillance, safe houses, and forgotten access points. Do not make every shadow a Dimir agent. Their power grows when the party cannot tell which ordinary courier tunnel is also an intelligence route.

## Orzhov and the dead

Old burial spaces, forgotten ownership claims, sealed family crypts, and spirit-binding arrangements create legal as well as supernatural dangers. A haunted chamber may be less dangerous than the deed proving somebody still owns it.

## Simic depths

Zonots reveal that the buried city can open into ancient water. This makes "underground" and "aquatic" part of the same vertical geography. Water pressure, flooded ruins, strange adapted life, and scientific access tunnels can intersect Golgari territory.

## The undercity social rule

Surface characters often assume "abandoned" means "unowned." Undercity residents know better.

Before the party camps, breaks a wall, opens a sealed door, or harvests fungus, decide who already uses the space and what they call ownership.

## Scene seed

The PCs descend to repair an Izzet line. The pipe crosses a Golgari growing chamber. The Azorius map says the chamber is abandoned. The Golgari family living there has receipts showing they have supplied a surface soup kitchen for thirty years. The pipe must be shut down tonight or a residential block loses heat.

That is Ravnica: infrastructure, law, class, guild history, and human need in one room.

# Azorius Senate — Deep Dossier

## The Azorius self-image

The Azorius do not wake up thinking, "how can we make life difficult?" They believe civilization is a machine made of rules, expectations, precedent, and predictable remedies. If those disappear, power belongs to whoever can shout, bribe, intimidate, or kill most effectively.

Their deepest fear is **arbitrariness**.

That is why an Azorius official may defend a tedious procedure even when the individual case seems obvious. If the rule can be ignored because this case feels urgent, what stops the next official from ignoring a different rule because a rich patron asks?

## The three Columns

The Senate divides its work among three Columns.

**Sova** is the judicial structure: judges, advocates, prosecutors, legal aides, precedent specialists, librarians, pages, and everyone who turns disputes into decisions.

**Jelenn** is the legislative structure: lawmakers supported by researchers, writers, lobbyists, clerks, and bureaucrats who convert policy desires into actual statutes.

**Lyev** is the enforcement structure: arresters, investigators, officers, lawmages, and increasingly surveillance and precognitive work.

The three Columns produce internal friction. A Lyev officer may think Sova judges free dangerous people on technicalities. A Sova advocate may think Lyev treats suspicion as guilt. A Jelenn drafter may believe both groups keep creating emergencies because they refuse to understand policy.

## New Prahv and visual language

Azorius architecture should communicate **order before comfort**: high pale stone, geometric repetition, controlled entry, clear sightlines, stairs and doors that make people approach authority deliberately.

Uniforms and insignia are legible social signals. A citizen who cannot name an official's exact rank still knows whether they are dealing with a clerk, field officer, lawmage, or someone whose presence means the case is serious.

### Sensory palette

Paper, wax, ink, polished metal, stone cooled by high ceilings, rain on broad steps.

Footsteps echo. Voices lower automatically in formal halls. Bells and magical signals divide time. Queues are visible architecture.

## An ordinary Azorius workday

A junior clerk spends more time correcting filings than changing law. An arrester deals with drunken disputes, property complaints, fraud, missing people, illegal enchantments, and the aftermath of other guild experiments.

Most cases are boring until they are not.

This is important: if every Azorius scene is a grand constitutional dispute, the guild stops feeling like government.

## Why citizens resent them

Azorius systems are designed for repeatability, not mercy. People with education, time, advocates, or institutional familiarity navigate them better. Poor citizens suffer more from delay because missing a day of work matters.

The Senate can sincerely believe everyone receives equal process while ignoring unequal ability to survive that process.

## Why citizens need them

The same bureaucracy can stop an Orzhov foreclosure, invalidate an illegal search, record ownership, constrain a violent guild, protect a witness, or force a powerful official to answer a complaint.

A good Azorius scene allows the law to be protective **before** it becomes obstructive.

## Interaction habits

**With Boros:** respect for public safety, recurring fights over spirit versus letter of law.  
**With Orzhov:** sophisticated legal chess; Azorius hates exploitation but recognizes valid contracts.  
**With Dimir:** institutional paranoia; information that cannot be sourced is difficult to use officially.  
**With Izzet:** permit wars, safety review, emergency exceptions.  
**With Rakdos:** censorship versus public danger versus speech.  
**With Gruul:** often the worst mutual understanding; Azorius sees public disorder where Gruul sees illegitimate occupation.

## How to play an Azorius NPC

Give them a rule they believe in for a reason.

Good dialogue:
- "I am not saying no. I am saying this office cannot say yes."
- "If I waive that requirement, I need something I can cite when the next person asks."
- "You want me to trust your urgency. I want you to understand that everyone in this queue believes their case is urgent."

Bad shorthand: monotone robot who quotes laws because bureaucracy is funny.

## Scene pressure

The PCs need immediate access to a sealed building. The Azorius officer agrees people inside may be in danger but the seal was issued after evidence contamination. Boros wants the door opened. The building owner has an Orzhov advocate. A Dimir informant claims the evidence was planted.

The Azorius question is not "do we care?" It is "what precedent do we create under pressure?"

# Boros Legion — Deep Dossier

## The Boros self-image

The Boros believe justice must be **felt in the world**, not merely written down. If someone is being beaten in an alley, the morally correct response is not to convene a committee.

Their strength is willingness to stand between danger and ordinary people.

Their danger is that moral certainty plus military capability can become permission to act first and justify later.

## Structure and military culture

The Legion is a standing army with garrisons throughout the city. Squads, sergeants, brigadiers, captains, commanders, warleader angels, and specialized orders create clear chains of responsibility.

The **Wojek League** combines military policing and intelligence. Wojeks investigate threats from other guilds and also investigate misconduct inside the Legion.

**Skyknights** mounted on rocs make Boros presence visible above the city. Aerial patrols are a constant reminder that the Legion thinks in terms of rapid response and territorial awareness.

**Sunhome** is not merely a fortress; it is a symbolic center of the Legion's identity and a place soldiers imagine defending even before they ever see it.

## Ordinary life in a garrison

A garrison smells like oil, leather, soup, wet wool, training yards, animal care, and too many people living on schedules.

Soldiers complain about boots, food, leave, impossible orders, and officers. Armorers know which squads abuse equipment. Healers know who is pretending not to be injured. Messengers know which commander reads reports and which skims them.

The Legion should feel like an institution with friendships and bureaucracy, not an endless parade ground.

## Justice versus law

Boros and Azorius overlap because both care about order, but their instincts differ.

Azorius asks: **what process gives legitimate authority?**  
Boros asks: **who is being harmed and what stops it?**

A Boros officer can break a regulation to save lives and then accept punishment afterward because the choice still felt morally correct.

## Angelic leadership

Angels embody the Legion's ideals and can inspire genuine devotion. Their presence should change a room. Mortals straighten posture, arguments stop, and even experienced soldiers reassess what they were about to say.

But angelic perspective can also be strategically distant. A mortal sergeant understands what it means to ask exhausted troops to hold a block for twelve more hours.

## Why citizens love them

When something explodes, when a monster escapes, when a riot becomes murderous, or when somebody powerful is hurting someone visibly weaker, Boros is the guild people expect to arrive.

## Why citizens fear them

Military force has a way of defining problems as threats. Barricades become normal. Search powers expand during emergencies. A neighborhood known for unrest can feel occupied.

## Interaction habits

**Azorius:** necessary sibling rivalry; law versus justice.  
**Izzet:** respect for useful engineering mixed with dread of prototypes.  
**Gruul:** frequent violent contact, but warriors can understand each other's courage more easily than diplomats do.  
**Rakdos:** crowd control, raids, contempt mixed with grudging recognition of bravery.  
**Dimir:** constant counterintelligence anxiety.  
**Selesnya:** cooperation in defense, disagreement over hierarchy and coercion.  
**Orzhov:** Boros hates predatory exploitation but must distinguish crime from ugly legality.

## How to play a Boros NPC

Give them a person or place they are responsible for.

Good dialogue:
- "You can argue jurisdiction after I get them out."
- "I have six people for twelve streets. Tell me what you know that changes where I put them."
- "If you want my soldiers to trust you, stop treating them like pieces."

## Scene pressure

A Boros patrol arrests a known extortionist after a beating. The victim refuses to testify because the extortionist works for an Orzhov creditor who holds the family's lease. The Azorius says the case will collapse. The sergeant knows releasing the suspect puts the family in danger tonight.

Justice is obvious. A lawful path is not.

# Canonical Guild Structures and Roles — Source-First Atlas

This is the **check-the-source-before-you-improvise** companion to the interpretive guild dossiers.

It draws primarily from *Guildmasters' Guide to Ravnica* and the official Planeswalker's Guides. It deliberately preserves the distinct organizational logic of each guild rather than reducing every guild to "leader + officers + members."

# Azorius Senate

## Institutional frame

The Senate is built around three Columns.

### Sova Column

Judicial work:

- judges;
- advocates;
- prosecutors;
- legal aides;
- pages;
- librarians;
- precedent specialists;
- experts;
- arbitration and mediation staff.

### Jelenn Column

Legislative work:

- legislators;
- policy researchers;
- legal writers;
- lobbyists;
- aides;
- clerks;
- analysts.

### Lyev Column

Enforcement work:

- arresters;
- investigators;
- officers;
- lawmages;
- hussars;
- infantry;
- surveillance and precognitive functions.

The three Columns are not three isolated buildings. They are different ways the same institution turns law into lived reality.

## Canonical specialist roles

**Konstructors:** magical architects and builders.  
**Elocutors:** messengers, mediators, negotiators, and communication specialists.  
**Hieromancers:** magic users who sanctify or protect places and things.  
**Lawmages:** coercive or restraining magic used to preserve legal order.  
**Hussars:** mounted Azorius troops.  
**Infantry:** unmounted formations, including tower-shield crowd control.

## Everyday institutional ecology — canon-supported inference

A structure this large necessarily includes:

- records clerks;
- evidence custodians;
- public intake desks;
- court schedulers;
- archive staff;
- custodial workers;
- building maintenance;
- food service;
- messengers;
- translators;
- stable and griffin handlers where relevant.

These jobs are not a new invented Azorius faction. They are the ordinary labor required by the source's own institutional scale.

## Internal conflicts to play

- Sova complains Lyev creates weak cases.
- Lyev complains Sova releases obvious threats.
- Jelenn writes law neither group thinks is practical.
- Elocutors believe everybody would cooperate if people would stop shouting.
- Konstructors are irritated that new legal requirements arrived after construction began.
- a justiciar wants precedent;
- an arrester wants action;
- a legislator wants a problem that can be described cleanly enough to regulate.

- build cases;
- manage civil disorder;
- watch for interguild threats;
- can investigate misconduct inside Boros structures.

A permanent garrison needs:

- animal handlers;

- repair crews;
- mess staff;
- supply officers;
- laundry;

Boros daily life should include far more than marching.

# House Dimir

## Institutional frame

The Dimir explicitly operate in layers.

### Overt layer

Public-facing work:

- couriers;
- investigators;
- reporters;
- archivists;
- librarians;
- information specialists.

This layer is genuinely useful and is part of why Dimir can exist inside ordinary city life.

### Covert layer

- spies;
- assassins;
- infiltrators;
- mind mages;
- handlers;
- coded communication;
- secret drop points.

### Clandestine layer

The deepest network around Lazav, insulated from ordinary agents and sometimes from other Dimir leadership.

## Canonical operating methods

- hidden symbols;
- codes;
- telepathic instructions;
- thought-based messages;
- memory erasure;
- sleeper agents;
- compartmentalized contacts;
- agents embedded in other guilds;
- ordinary cover lives.

## Correct low-level Dimir play

A junior operative should usually know:

- their cover identity;
- the immediate task;
- the delivery or contact method;
- what to do if compromised.

They should **not** know the entire conspiracy.

That is much closer to published Dimir structure than giving every agent encyclopedic secret knowledge.

## Ordinary overt jobs matter

A courier still needs:

- route knowledge;
- door staff relationships;
- schedule discipline;
- weather gear;
- fast food;
- repair for bags and boots;
- information about closures.

Those mundane skills are exactly what make the courier useful to an intelligence network.

# Cult of Rakdos — Deep Dossier

## The Rakdos self-image

The Cult believes every institution eventually reveals absurdity, cruelty, hypocrisy, or appetite beneath its dignified language.

Their response is not reform. It is **spectacle**.

They make power ridiculous, desire visible, danger entertaining, and social pressure explode into performance.

## Civic contradiction

The Guildpact historically gave Rakdos roles in entertainment, mining, and manual labor. That contradiction matters. The same culture associated with grotesque theater also contains people who do physically brutal work the rest of the city needs.

A stagehand may know rigging better than an engineer. A miner may perform knife tricks after shift. A giant hauling a stage is simultaneously laborer, spectacle, and traffic problem.

## Troupes, not bureaucracy

Rakdos organization is loose. Performers move between troupes. Renown matters more than formal rank.

Ringmasters shape shows. Blood witches function as dangerous specialists and ideological tormentors. The deepest authority is still Rakdos himself, whose attention is both coveted and terrifying.

## Sensory palette

Sweat. smoke. singed hair. leather. greasepaint. hot iron. alcohol. perfume over blood.

Chains, drums, laughter, jeering, shouted improvisation, rigging creaks, fire breathing, crowd noise rising before the act even begins.

A Rakdos venue should feel alive **before** it becomes dangerous.

## Humor

Rakdos satire needs a target.

A generic murder show is less Ravnican than a performance that mocks a recent Azorius decree, reproduces an Orzhov foreclosure as slapstick, or stages a grotesque parody of an Izzet disaster everybody remembers.

The audience may include people who hate Rakdos but enjoy seeing their superiors ridiculed.

## Consent and danger

Not every Rakdos performance treats consent the same way. Some troupes thrive on voluntary danger among performers and eager audiences. Others cross into cruelty and murder.

This variation matters. It lets the guild contain ordinary working artists, thrill-seekers, sadists, satirists, laborers, and cult extremists.

## Interaction habits

**Azorius:** natural antagonism over control, censorship, permits, and public danger.  
**Boros:** raids and crowd control, but mutual respect for courage can appear.  
**Izzet:** pyrotechnics, machines, and collaboration until responsibility is discussed.  
**Gruul:** shared hostility to oppressive structures; Rakdos thinks destruction without theater wastes an opportunity.  
**Orzhov:** perfect satirical target because it converts suffering into dignity and paperwork.  
**Selesnya:** mass audiences are attractive; collective harmony is ripe for mockery.

## How to play a Rakdos NPC

Give them a performance instinct, not random insanity.

Good dialogue:
- "If they didn't want to be mocked, they should have written a shorter law."
- "Danger is not the same thing as surprise. The audience paid for danger."
- "You want quiet? Fine. Quiet costs extra."

## Scene pressure

A mobile performance blocks an avenue during an Orzhov eviction protest. The show is viciously funny, the crowd is growing, and the family being evicted has become the center of the act without agreeing to it.

Azorius wants the street cleared. Boros sees a riot forming. The ringmaster insists the performance is the only reason the crowd has not attacked anyone yet.

# Golgari Swarm — Deep Dossier

## The Golgari self-image

The Golgari believe the upper city survives by pretending decay is somebody else's problem.

Food rots. Bodies die. buildings crumble. waste accumulates. wealth changes hands. Empires collapse. The Swarm's answer is not despair but metabolism: **everything discarded becomes input**.

Their dignity comes from doing work other guilds depend on while being treated as beneath them.

## Civic function

The Swarm's ancient responsibilities include agriculture and waste. In practice, Golgari networks process refuse, cultivate fungi, provide basic food, use undead labor, reclaim ruins, and inhabit spaces others abandon.

A poor surface resident may know exactly which Golgari vendor sells the cheapest filling meal while still repeating jokes about what is in it.

## Internal politics

The Swarm is plural rather than unified.

Important blocs include the **Devkarin elves**, **medusas/gorgons**, and **kraul**. Power rises and falls between factions. Jarad, an undead Devkarin, rules in the baseline era, but nobody in the guild mistakes leadership for permanence.

Assassination can be accepted as political change. The **Ochran** embody the threat of organized killing within that culture.

The Swarm survives leadership churn because the work below continues.

## Sensory palette

Damp earth, mushroom spice, insects, compost heat, old stone, underground water.

Bioluminescent fungi. bone worked into tools or decoration. moss on armor. insects moving through living material. The sound of dripping water under constant low labor.

Do not describe Golgari spaces as filthy by default. A mushroom farm can be carefully managed. A rot pit can be organized. Surface eyes may call it disgusting because the process is visible.

## Death

Golgari do not treat death as simple annihilation. Undead labor, necromancy, decomposition, and rebirth all complicate grief.

That does not mean nobody mourns. It means mourning can coexist with practical questions about what happens to a body next.

## Why surface Ravnicans resent them

The Swarm reminds people of dependence on unpleasant systems. It also controls undercity routes and can use hunger, waste, vermin, or decay as leverage.

## Why the Swarm resents the surface

Upper guilds celebrate clean plazas while sending waste downward. They call undercity communities primitive, dangerous, or illegal while benefiting from reclamation.

A Golgari worker can hear moral disgust as class contempt.

## Interaction habits

**Selesnya:** shared reverence for life, deep disagreement over death and managed harmony.  
**Simic:** both understand ecosystems; Simic optimization can look arrogant to Golgari cyclical thinking.  
**Gruul:** common resentment of upper-city civilization, different visions of what replaces it.  
**Orzhov:** disgust toward wealth that refuses to die; practical dealings still happen.  
**Dimir:** territorial and intelligence conflict in forgotten routes.

## How to play a Golgari NPC

Make them practical before gothic.

Good dialogue:
- "You call it refuse because you can afford not to need it."
- "Nothing down here is empty just because your map is blank."
- "Take the body upstairs if you want. The debt, the blood, and the rats remain."

## Scene pressure

A surface district wants the Golgari to increase food deliveries after a warehouse fire. The local Golgari community can do it only by harvesting a fungal bed used to stabilize a collapsing lower tunnel.

Feed the surface now, or protect undercity homes from a disaster nobody above can see.

## Source Note: 03-Guilds/Gruul-Clans-Deep-Dossier.md

# Gruul Clans — Deep Dossier

## The core wound

The Gruul were not created to be "the barbarian guild." Their ancient relationship to natural places became impossible as the city expanded until civilization covered almost everything.

The Gruul experience Ravnica's triumph as **dispossession**.

A new road is not neutral infrastructure if it crosses the last space your clan considers living land. A clean civic plaza may stand where your ancestors camped. A permit proving the city owns a ruin can sound absurd when your family never recognized the authority that issued it.

## No single orderly guild

The Gruul are a collection of clans, not a centralized bureaucracy. Borborygmos of the **Burning Tree** holds influence because strength and rage can align many clans, not because every Gruul obeys a chain of command.

Other clans embody different survival strategies. The **Ghor** are notorious for aggressive raids close to populated districts. The **Scab** use scars and body modification as expressions of rage and identity. The **Slizt** favor ambush, high ruins, and cunning survival.

**Skarrg**, a ruined palace in rubblebelt territory adjoining the Tenth District, can serve as a gathering place.

## Sensory palette

Rubble dust, wood smoke, rain on broken stone, animal hides, crushed herbs, sweat, ash.

Open sky appears where the city expected a building. Trees grow through collapsed walls. Campfires burn in places planners label condemned. Drums or horns carry farther across broken terrain than voices.

## Gruul intelligence

Do not confuse rejection of bureaucracy with stupidity.

A clan that survives in rubblebelts understands unstable masonry, animal routes, hidden water, patrol timing, weather, scavenging, and which new development projects will displace people six months from now.

A Gruul raider may be unable to read an Azorius writ and still understand exactly who profits from it.

## Violence

Gruul violence can be celebratory, retaliatory, territorial, strategic, or nihilistic depending on clan and circumstance.

Before running a raid, answer: **what changed?**

- Was a sacred or useful ruin cleared?
- Did a road cut into clan territory?
- Did Boros arrest someone?
- Did Izzet testing contaminate a water source?
- Is a chieftain proving strength?
- Did another guild deliberately provoke the attack?

Random violence should be the exception, not the only explanation.

## Why citizens fear them

Because their attacks can be direct, physically destructive, and unconcerned with property systems the city depends on.

## Why some citizens sympathize

Guildless tenants facing eviction may understand "the city takes what it wants and calls the paperwork lawful." Workers may envy the clans' rejection of bosses and debt. Rakdos performers may romanticize Gruul freedom without living its hardship.

## Interaction habits

**Azorius:** mutual incomprehension over legitimacy.  
**Boros:** violent contact, but warrior respect is possible.  
**Izzet:** fascination with raw power, hatred of infrastructure encroachment.  
**Selesnya:** both value nature; Gruul sees domestication where Selesnya sees harmony.  
**Golgari:** shared contempt for surface arrogance, different relationship to building and decay.

## How to play a Gruul NPC

Give them a concrete loss.

Good dialogue:
- "Your map begins after you took it."
- "You built a wall, then called me criminal for standing where the wall went."
- "I don't hate your street. I hate that you think the street proves you were always here."

## Scene pressure

An Izzet line is being installed through rubblebelt because it is the only stable route. The line will give heat to thousands. The trench cuts across the clan's winter water source.

If the PCs say the city's need is greater, the Gruul asks a fair question: greater **to whom**?

## Source Note: 03-Guilds/House-Dimir-Deep-Dossier.md

# House Dimir — Deep Dossier

## The first rule

Do not play every Dimir scene as "mysterious spy talks cryptically."

House Dimir has an overt civic history as couriers, information brokers, librarians, investigators, and archivists. The covert network works because those jobs are real and useful.

Most people need messages delivered. Most people need records found. Most people do not want to wonder whether the person performing that service is also collecting leverage.

## Cell logic

Dimir agents are compartmentalized. Orders can arrive through dead drops, codes, thought strands, intermediaries, or contacts who do not know the larger purpose.

An agent may know:

- the task;
- one contact;
- one emergency fallback;
- almost nothing about who ultimately benefits.

This protects the House and also makes ordinary Dimir life psychologically strange. Competence requires acting confidently with incomplete context.

## Lazav and uncertainty

Lazav's shapeshifting leadership makes identity itself politically unstable. A senior agent does not necessarily know whether they have met Lazav. That produces a culture where people evaluate information by usefulness, consistency, and control rather than by trust alone.

## Sensory palette

The Dimir are not always dark blue mist.

Their public spaces can smell like paper, dust, raincoats, messenger leather, ink, old shelves, coffee consumed too quickly. Their covert spaces emphasize absence: clean rooms, changed routes, doors with no sign, a chair recently moved, a message that should not be there.

## Ordinary work

A courier spends the day navigating traffic, gatekeepers, lifts, office hours, angry recipients, and weather. An archivist knows how records are actually filed versus how the catalog claims they are filed.

Those ordinary skills become espionage skills naturally.

## Knowledge as leverage

The Dimir advantage is often knowing **which fact matters to whom**.

A ledger means nothing until someone knows the name hidden in it. A love affair means nothing until a vote is close. A repair schedule means nothing until a theft needs a dark building.

Dimir plots should therefore connect mundane information rather than rely only on magical secrets.

## Why people use them anyway

Information work requires specialists. A person may suspect Dimir involvement and still hire a courier because the delivery arrives on time.

This creates the perfect Ravnican tension: everyone jokes that the messenger is a spy, then gives the messenger a sealed packet.

## Interaction habits

**Azorius:** record versus secrecy; each understands the other's dependence on information.  
**Boros:** counterintelligence cat-and-mouse.  
**Orzhov:** mutual respect for leverage, mutual hatred of owing the other side.  
**Izzet:** technical secrets are valuable, but Izzet researchers are difficult to keep quiet.  
**Golgari:** undercity routes, old secrets, territorial friction.  
**Rakdos:** Dimir secrecy versus Rakdos radical publicity can be unexpectedly useful to each other.

## How to play a Dimir NPC

Decide which layer they are on.

**Public:** efficient, ordinary, slightly over-informed.  
**Low covert:** nervous about procedure, strict about contact rules.  
**Experienced:** asks precise questions and rarely volunteers motive.  
**Deep operative:** can appear emotionally normal because paranoia has become routine.

Good dialogue:
- "I don't need to know why. I need to know whether the package changes hands."
- "Everyone has a secret. Most of them are useless."
- "You are asking who told me. Ask instead why they wanted you to hear it."

## Scene pressure

A courier offers proof that the PCs' target left the district. The proof is real. The courier's price is not money: deliver a second packet to an Azorius clerk and do not open it.

The interesting question is not whether the courier lies. It is what **true information is being used to purchase from the party**.

## Source Note: 03-Guilds/Izzet-League-Deep-Dossier.md

# Izzet League — Deep Dossier

## The Izzet self-image

The Izzet believe ignorance is a solvable problem and reality is a system that can be interrogated, modeled, dismantled, rebuilt, and improved.

Their virtue is **creative competence**.

Their vice is that curiosity can become moral permission. "We need to know what happens" can overpower "we should not make this happen."

## Laboratories as culture

The League is organized around laboratories specializing in fields of research. Published examples include pyrology, storms and electricity, metallurgy, alchemy, orientation, mimeography, continuism, arcane geometry, gravitational inversion, and plasma-dermatology.

The **Izmundi** coordinate laboratories and projects. Senior figures can enter the **Izmagnus**, advising Niv-Mizzet more directly.

The important table implication is that "the Izzet" is not one coherent research department. Two labs can compete, duplicate work, hoard findings, argue methodology, and interpret the same directive differently.

## Niv-Mizzet

Niv-Mizzet is not merely a mascot genius. He defines the guild's scale of ambition. Researchers may measure themselves against a dragon whose intellect, ego, and institutional memory vastly exceed theirs.

That creates a culture of intellectual daring and insecurity.

## Sensory palette

Blue-white electrical light. red-orange furnace glow. copper, brass, glass, ceramic insulators, mizzium housings. warning signs written over older warning signs.

Ozone. hot metal. scorched dust. oil. steam. chemical sweetness. coffee.

Buzzing, humming, venting, shouted counts, sudden silence when everyone realizes a reading is wrong.

## Ordinary work

Most Izzet members do maintenance, measurement, fabrication, calibration, data copying, specimen transport, repair, or routine monitoring. The city depends on this work.

A good Izzet scene begins with someone fixing something that normally works.

## Safety culture

The Izzet are not uniformly reckless. They have people whose entire job is preventing catastrophic failure. The conflict is that the institution also rewards discovery.

A safety technician can be the most frustrated person in the room because they genuinely understand the experiment and still think it should stop.

## Interaction habits

**Azorius:** permits and review versus experimental tempo.  
**Boros:** weapons, emergency engineering, and deep anxiety about prototypes.  
**Simic:** intellectual respect, rivalry over method and ethics.  
**Rakdos:** pyrotechnics and spectacle; both enjoy dramatic energy for very different reasons.  
**Gruul:** raw elemental power is admired; infrastructure encroachment is hated.  
**Dimir:** research secrecy is valuable and leaks are inevitable.

## How to play an Izzet NPC

Give them a hypothesis and a deadline.

Good dialogue:
- "It is stable. Stable is not the same thing as safe."
- "We did not expect that result, which is why it is important."
- "If you shut it down now, we lose the only measurement that tells us whether shutting it down was necessary."

## Scene pressure

A transit lift is failing intermittently. The Izzet team can replace the core safely in twelve hours or test an experimental bypass in twenty minutes. Hundreds of people are trapped on the wrong level. Boros wants emergency use restored. Azorius has already prohibited the bypass design.

The prototype probably works.

## Source Note: 03-Guilds/Orzhov-Syndicate-Deep-Dossier.md

# Orzhov Syndicate — Deep Dossier

## The Orzhov self-image

The Orzhov believe society runs on obligation whether people admit it or not.

Money is only the most visible form. Family duty, reputation, contract, confession, patronage, inheritance, employment, lease, debt, and promise all bind people.

The Syndicate's genius is turning those invisible relationships into **enforceable hierarchy**.

## Church, bank, law office, crime family

The Orzhov are simultaneously religious institution, financial network, legal machine, patronage system, and organized crime structure.

Their churches and basilicas can provide real services: loans, safe storage, legal representation, funeral arrangements, mediation, protection, housing connections, employment, and ritual.

The danger is that assistance is rarely allowed to become a gift when it can become leverage.

## Hierarchy

The baseline era is ruled by the **Obzedat**, undead oligarchs whose continued existence embodies the guild's refusal to let wealth and authority end at death.

Below them, rank matters intensely. Syndics perform low-level functions. Knights, advokists, priests, enforcers, ministrants, and wealthy oligarchs operate within networks where almost everyone owes someone above.

Power is relational: the important question is not merely "how rich are you?" but **whose debt can you call in?**

## Sensory palette

Black and white stone. gold trim. polished wood. heavy doors. incense. wax. perfume. paper. cold metal coins. quiet rooms designed to make visitors conscious of their own breathing.

Servants move discreetly. Guards do not fidget. A clerk may speak softly because the architecture makes shouting unnecessary.

## Religion

Orzhov ritual should not be treated as fake merely because the institution is exploitative. People can sincerely seek absolution, continuity, family blessing, or meaning in a system that monetizes those needs.

A priest may genuinely comfort a mourner and still ensure the funeral debt is properly recorded.

## Debt as social control

Debt becomes frightening when it reshapes choices before anyone issues a threat.

A borrower works a dangerous shift because missing it triggers penalties. A witness stays silent because the landlord is Orzhov-financed. A merchant rejects the PCs because their protection contract discourages "high-risk clientele."

## Interaction habits

**Azorius:** sophisticated legal conflict; both respect documents but disagree about purpose.  
**Boros:** resentment of predatory practice, but Boros cannot simply criminalize every harsh contract.  
**Dimir:** information and leverage trade hands cautiously.  
**Golgari:** philosophical disgust over wealth persisting beyond death; practical funeral and property dealings continue.  
**Rakdos:** contempt versus fascination—Orzhov sells respectability, Rakdos humiliates it.  
**Selesnya:** competing visions of community obligation: debt to hierarchy versus duty to collective.

## How to play an Orzhov NPC

Never make them greedy in only one dimension. Make them understand networks.

Good dialogue:
- "You are free to refuse. The consequence is also yours to choose."
- "A favor forgotten is charity. A favor recorded is civilization."
- "I am not threatening the family. I am explaining what the contract already permits."

## Scene pressure

A clinic is behind on payments after treating riot victims for free. The Orzhov lender offers to forgive half the debt if the clinic signs an exclusive service agreement.

The clinic survives. Prices rise. Poor patients lose access. The contract is legal.

## Source Note: 03-Guilds/Selesnya-Conclave-Deep-Dossier.md

# Selesnya Conclave — Deep Dossier

## The Selesnyan self-image

Selesnya offers something genuinely powerful in Ravnica: a community where food, housing, spiritual meaning, protection, companionship, and purpose are shared.

Its central promise is **you do not have to be alone**.

Its danger is the price of harmony when individual desire conflicts with collective need.

## Vernadi

The Conclave is organized into local communes called **vernadi**, each centered on a great tree and guided by a dryad called a **voda**.

This gives the guild a profoundly local scale. Members can know their spiritual leader personally. Children grow up around the same courtyards, teachers, training grounds, healers, kitchens, and gardens.

Vernadi should feel lived-in, not merely sacred.

## Mat'Selesnya and Trostani

The Worldsoul, Mat'Selesnya, grounds the Conclave's belief that life is interconnected. Trostani embodies guild leadership in intimate connection with that ideal.

This produces a culture where social belonging and spiritual truth reinforce one another. Leaving a vernadi can therefore feel like more than changing affiliation—it can feel like severing connection to family, community, and the world itself.

## Military capacity

Selesnya is peaceful in aspiration, not helpless.

Ordinary members train. The Conclave fields militia, archers, riders, elementals, Ledev guardians, and other defenders. Its strength is numbers, organization, local knowledge, and willingness to mobilize whole communities when threatened.

## Sensory palette

Leaf shade over stone. warm food. wood smoke. wet soil. flowers. wool. animal fur. incense made from herbs rather than heavy resins.

Hooves on paving. communal singing. training calls. children. birds. tools in gardens. many quiet conversations happening at once.

## Why people join

Not because they were "brainwashed," but because the offer is attractive.

A lonely worker gains a home. A poor family gains food security. A frightened citizen gains people who will stand beside them. A spiritual seeker gains a worldview where their life matters as part of something larger.

The pressure becomes visible later, when someone wants to choose something the community believes harms the whole.

## Interaction habits

**Golgari:** shared belief in life's interconnectedness, deep conflict over death and decay.  
**Gruul:** both resist sterile urban domination; Gruul sees Selesnya as domestication.  
**Simic:** cooperation on ecology and health; distrust when adaptation becomes engineering.  
**Boros:** natural defensive allies, different command cultures.  
**Orzhov:** rival models of obligation—communal duty versus debt hierarchy.  
**Rakdos:** mass community provides both audience and ideological target.

## How to play a Selesnyan NPC

Make warmth real.

Good dialogue:
- "Eat first. Then tell me why you came."
- "You keep saying 'my choice' as though choices stop affecting people when they leave your mouth."
- "We can make room for you. The question is whether you can make room for us."

## Scene pressure

A vernadi shelters families displaced by an industrial accident. City officials want the courtyard reopened as an emergency transit route. The voda refuses to move people until permanent housing is guaranteed.

Thousands need the route. Dozens need the shelter. Both are true.

## Source Note: 03-Guilds/Simic-Combine-Deep-Dossier.md

# Simic Combine — Deep Dossier

## The Simic self-image

The Simic believe life survives by adapting.

Ravnica changes faster than natural systems can respond: cities spread, pollution changes habitats, magical accidents create new pressures, disease moves through dense populations, and artificial environments demand new bodies.

The Combine asks whether biology can be helped to keep pace.

Their virtue is **adaptive imagination**.

Their danger is turning a living subject into a design problem.

## Zonots

The Simic organize around **zonots**, vast sinkholes descending through layers of city toward ancient waters. Each zonot is habitat, community, laboratory environment, and political unit led by a **Speaker**.

The Speakers form a chamber and select the Prime Speaker. In the baseline era, Zegana is Prime Speaker.

**Zameck**, in Zonot Seven within Precinct Five, serves as the guildhall and meeting place.

A zonot should feel like another mode of Ravnica rather than an escape from the city: bridges, labs, dwellings, tanks, pools, offices, lifts, and living architecture arranged around depth and water.

## Holdfast and Upwelling

Two philosophical ideas shape Simic thought.

**Holdfast** warns against becoming detached from nature. It values ecological fit, continuity, and natural systems.

**Upwelling** embraces renewal, transformation, and replacing depleted forms with newly invigorated ones.

Both can support adaptation. They disagree about how aggressively change should be directed.

## Clades and projects

Researchers work in clades spanning specialties and zonots, or in shorter projects built around immediate goals. Published examples include Hull, Fin, and Gyre clades, alongside programs such as Guardian and Crypsis.

This creates scientific politics: long-term theory versus urgent application, habitat needs versus military pressure, publication versus secrecy.

## Public health roots

The Simic's original public-health role matters. A Combine clinic can be one of the safest places in a neighborhood. Biomancers can heal injuries, treat disease, and solve environmental problems other guilds cannot.

That makes ethical conflict stronger. The same researcher who saves lives may sincerely propose a modification the patient finds horrifying.

## Sensory palette

Brine. disinfectant. wet stone. living tissue. herbs. shell. glass. cool air near water and warm humid air inside growth chambers.

Bubbling tanks. pumps. quiet technical discussion. splashing below. clicking claws from something not visible yet.

## Interaction habits

**Izzet:** mutual technical respect; life versus mechanism as first instinct.  
**Selesnya:** ecological common ground, conflict over deliberate redesign.  
**Golgari:** shared systems thinking, different attitude toward decay and perfection.  
**Azorius:** inspections, medical regulation, research legality.  
**Dimir:** Crypsis and intelligence work create uncomfortable overlap.  
**Orzhov:** research funding can become dependency.

## How to play a Simic NPC

Give them a variable they are watching.

Good dialogue:
- "The treatment worked. The question is what else it changed."
- "Natural does not mean harmless."
- "You are asking whether it is still you. I am asking whether you are still alive."

## Scene pressure

A worker exposed to magical contamination will die unless a Simic team performs an emergency adaptation. The procedure is likely permanent and visually obvious.

The patient is conscious. Their employer wants them returned to work. Their family is afraid. The biomancer is already preparing the instruments.

# Ten Guilds — A Day in Ordinary Life

These are baseline rhythms for ordinary guild members. They are not scripts; they are anchors for improvisation.

## Azorius junior scribe
**06:30:** breakfast while reviewing today's filing list.  
**08:00:** queues already exist; corrects forms before the office officially opens.  
**10:30:** supervisor sends an urgent precedent request.  
**Lunch:** twenty minutes, mostly spent arguing with a Boros friend about last night's arrest.  
**Afternoon:** copies witness statements, verifies seals, tells six people their cases belong in another office.  
**Evening:** studies for promotion and complains that nobody understands how much chaos the paperwork prevents.

**Fear:** making a mistake that becomes precedent.  
**Want:** a competent supervisor and one uninterrupted afternoon.

## Boros garrison soldier
**Dawn:** formation, equipment check, patrol assignment.  
**Morning:** neighborhood patrol, mostly mundane disputes.  
**Midday:** quick meal, equipment repair.  
**Afternoon:** emergency call becomes hours of crowd control.  
**Evening:** writes the incident report they hate writing.  
**Night:** tries to sleep while another unit drills outside.

**Fear:** being too late.  
**Want:** enough people to cover the sector properly.

## Dimir public courier
**Morning:** collects packets and memorizes route.  
**Day:** offices, shops, residences, station platforms, archives; learns who changed jobs, who is angry, which door is newly guarded.  
**Afternoon:** one delivery contains a coded instruction recognized only because of a tiny mark.  
**Evening:** an ordinary delivery is actually the covert task.  
**Night:** goes home and behaves exactly like someone with nothing to hide.

**Fear:** patterns becoming visible.  
**Want:** to know just enough to stay useful.

## Golgari grower
**Early:** checks humidity, fungal beds, insect activity, contamination.  
**Morning:** receives waste and substrate from upper routes.

**Afternoon:** faction argument interrupts work; the crop does not care about politics.  
**Evening:** repairs chamber supports and eats with family.  
**Night:** surface traders arrive because they prefer not to be seen buying.

**Fear:** upper-city decisions damaging systems nobody above understands.  
**Want:** respect for work already keeping people alive.

## Gruul clan scout
**Morning:** checks territory, water, tracks, new construction.  
**Midday:** reports survey markers near a clan-used ruin.  
**Afternoon:** argues whether to destroy them, observe, or confront workers.  
**Evening:** hunt, repair, stories, challenge.  
**Night:** watches city lights spread into places elders remember as dark.

**Fear:** another piece of ground becoming permanently inaccessible.  
**Want:** the city to stop treating existence as permission to expand.

## Izzet technician
**Start:** calibration.  
**One hour later:** recalibration because another lab changed the load.  
**Midday:** fixes a public system while a researcher complains maintenance interrupts data collection.  
**Afternoon:** discovers anomaly and becomes excited despite knowing it means overtime.  
**Evening:** writes notes nobody will read until something fails.  
**Night:** called back because something failed.

**Fear:** preventable failure blamed on "Izzet chaos."  
**Want:** enough time and power budget to solve the actual problem.

## Orzhov syndic
**Morning:** checks ledgers and appointments.  
**Late morning:** assists a priest with tithes and borrower records.  
**Afternoon:** delivers a polite notice to a client who cannot pay.  
**Later:** a superior asks for a favor outside formal duty; refusal would be remembered.  
**Evening:** reviews personal debts upward in hierarchy.

**Fear:** owing more powerful people than owe them.  
**Want:** leverage and security.

## Rakdos stagehand
**Late morning:** wakes after last night's teardown.  
**Afternoon:** repairs rigging, scavenges materials, argues with a performer about safety.  
**Evening:** hauls stage components into a square.  
**Night:** show begins; everything appears spontaneous because six people worked all day.  
**After midnight:** teardown, injuries, drinks, gossip about who got Rakdos's attention.

**Fear:** dying in a boring way.  
**Want:** a show people remember and a crew competent enough to survive it.

## Selesnyan vernadi member
**Morning:** shared work—garden, kitchen, animal care, craft, teaching.  
**Midday:** communal meal and news.  
**Afternoon:** personal work plus training or spiritual study.  
**Evening:** gathering, mediation, storytelling, worship, shared care.

## Source Note: 04-Interactions/Conflict-Reaction-and-Escalation-Engine.md

# Conflict, Reaction and Escalation Engine

Use this when the PCs create a public event and you need to know how the living city reacts.

## Step 1 — Name the civic problem

Do not start with "which guild is involved?" Start with the problem:

- fire;
- missing person;
- unpaid debt;
- broken lift;
- illegal performance;
- contaminated water;
- disputed ruin;
- escaped creature;
- political demonstration;
- murder;
- closed market;
- experimental accident;
- housing eviction;
- corpse with unclear ownership;
- blocked trade route.

## Step 2 — Identify the first responder

Who arrives first depends on local presence, not abstract jurisdiction.

A Boros patrol may reach a lab explosion before Izzet specialists. A Selesnyan healer may reach a stabbing before Boros. A Golgari crew may notice a sewer collapse before the surface knows anything happened.

The first responder shapes the initial narrative.

## Step 3 — Add the authority dispute

Bring in one organization that says, "this is our matter."

Now the scene has pressure even without combat.

Examples:

- Azorius seals a site Boros wants entered.
- Simic claims a creature as research property.
- Orzhov produces a deed to a building residents occupy.
- Golgari says a tunnel is inhabited despite surface maps calling it abandoned.

## Step 4 — Add the material dependency

What breaks if the scene remains unresolved?

- heat;
- food delivery;
- rent;
- medical treatment;
- freight;
- legal deadline;
- public confidence;
- street access;
- water;
- evidence integrity.

This prevents politics from becoming abstract.

## Step 5 — Add the opportunist

A third actor does not need to cause the crisis. They only need to see a use for it.

Dimir collects information. Orzhov buys distressed property. Rakdos turns scandal into satire. Gruul encourages residents to tear down a barricade. Izzet asks to test equipment under emergency conditions.

## Escalation clocks

### Public attention
1. witnesses stop  
2. crowd forms  
3. rumors spread  
4. guild representatives arrive  
5. story reaches wider district  
6. incident becomes symbolic

### Institutional pressure
1. verbal warning  
2. written notice/order  
3. supervisor called  
4. specialist team deployed  
5. senior official takes control  
6. guild leadership uses outcome politically

### Neighborhood memory
1. curiosity  
2. gratitude or annoyance  
3. names remembered  
4. favors or grudges form  
5. local access changes  
6. PCs become part of neighborhood story

## De-escalation should cost something

Good resolutions involve tradeoffs.

- Open the street, but contaminate evidence.
- Save the clinic, but accept Orzhov financing.
- Preserve the grove, but delay transit.
- Arrest the rioter, but radicalize the neighborhood.
- Release the creature, but embarrass the Simic project.
- Pay the debt, but create a new obligation.

If every problem has a clean answer, the city stops feeling politically alive.

## Source Note: 04-Interactions/Dialogue-Voice-and-Social-Pressure.md

# Dialogue, Voice and Social Pressure

Guild voice should emerge from **what a person notices and what they consider legitimate**, not from catchphrases.

## Azorius

Notices sequence, authority, precedent, definitions, records, exceptions.

- "Who authorized this?"
- "What exactly happened first?"
- "I can make an exception only if I can explain why it remains an exception."

Pressure: make you wait, document you, narrow choices to permitted options.

## Boros

Notices harm, courage, threat, responsibility, who needs protection.

- "Who is still inside?"
- "Tell me what stops the next hit."
- "I will answer for the order. Move."

Pressure: urgency, command presence, moral judgment.

## Dimir

Notices information asymmetry, who knows whom, what detail does not fit.

- "Who else heard that?"
- "You are asking the wrong question."
- "I can prove the fact. The source is not part of the offer."

Pressure: uncertainty, selective truth, social leverage.

## Golgari

Notices waste, cycles, class hypocrisy, what gets discarded.

- "The surface calls it trash after taking what it wants."
- "Nothing here is unused."
- "You want this cleaned. Where do you imagine clean goes?"

Pressure: dependence, uncomfortable material reality, patience.

## Gruul

Notices territory, coercion, construction, ownership claims, weakness disguised as law.

- "Your wall is new. My anger is not."
- "Show me the place before the road."
- "You call it order because you stand on top of it."

Pressure: physical presence, refusal of institutional assumptions.

## Izzet

Notices mechanism, anomaly, possibility, data, failure mode.

- "That should not be happening."
- "Don't touch it unless you want to improve the experiment."
- "We can make it safe or we can make it fast."

Pressure: technical authority, time urgency, curiosity.

## Orzhov

Notices obligations, leverage, hierarchy, assets, who owes whom.

- "Nothing is free. Sometimes the price is merely hidden."
- "You may refuse."
- "The debt existed before I entered the room."

Pressure: consequences that appear voluntary.

## Rakdos

Notices hypocrisy, audience mood, fear, status, comic weakness.

- "Say that again, but louder. The balcony didn't hear you lie."
- "You came to stop the show and became the best part."
- "Everyone likes rules until the rules are about them."

Pressure: public embarrassment, crowd energy, escalation through spectacle.

## Selesnya

Notices relationships, isolation, communal consequences, shared need.

- "Who else is affected?"
- "You do not have to carry this alone."
- "Your choice is yours. Its consequences belong to all of us."

Pressure: belonging, reciprocity, moral community.

## Simic

Notices adaptation, systems, symptoms, variables, unintended effects.

- "What changed after exposure?"
- "Stable is not the same as healthy."
- "The body has already made a decision. We are deciding whether to help it."

Pressure: expertise over intimate bodily questions.

## Cross-guild conversation trick

When two guild NPCs argue, have them use the **same word differently**.

"Safety" means procedure to Azorius, immediate protection to Boros, controlled experiment to Izzet, adaptive resilience to Simic.

"Freedom" means independence to Gruul, appetite to Rakdos, freedom from exploitation to some Boros, freedom from chaos to an Azorius jurist.

That creates ideological texture without speeches.

## How to use this

Do not ask "do these guilds like each other?" Ask:

1. What shared problem forces them into the same room?
2. What does each side consider the **obvious** solution?
3. Which value makes that solution unacceptable to the other side?
4. What third guild profits from delay?
5. Which ordinary citizens bear the cost while institutions argue?

A two-guild conflict feels Ravnican. A three-guild conflict with guildless consequences feels like a city.

# Guildless Citizens and Neutral Spaces

The guildless are not the absence of a faction. They are the majority of ordinary social life that remains after ten institutions stop pretending they own everything.

## Guildless competence

A guildless building superintendent may understand Azorius notices, Izzet plumbing, Orzhov leases, Boros patrol patterns, Golgari food delivery, and which Selesnyan healer will make a house call.

That is a kind of expertise guild specialists often underestimate.

## Why remain guildless?

- family identity;
- distrust of institutional obligation;
- independent profession;
- previous bad experience with a guild;
- mixed-guild family;
- local community stronger than guild identity;
- desire to work across guild boundaries;
- inability to qualify or gain sponsorship;
- refusal to accept ideological expectations.

## Neutral spaces

Neutrality is produced socially.

A cafe used by clerks from three guilds stays neutral because the owner ejects anyone conducting official business at the tables.

A market square stays neutral because all sides need freight to move.

A building can become neutral because the residents collectively refuse uniforms inside.

A bathhouse may ban weapons and insignia not from idealism but because fights ruin business.

## What guildless people think of guilds

Avoid a single "anti-guild" attitude.

A resident can hate Azorius bureaucracy and still want an Azorius judge when the landlord cheats them. They can distrust Golgari food and eat it every week. They can condemn Rakdos violence and know the best show in town. They can fear Simic mutation and bring a sick child to a Simic clinic.

That contradiction is normal urban dependence.

## Neighborhood power

Local influence can come from:

- building owners;
- tenant associations;
- market elders;
- gangs;
- unions;
- religious groups;
- healers;
- schools;
- veterans;
- neighborhood kitchens;
- couriers;
- families who have lived there longer than current maps.

## Running a guildless NPC

Give them a **multi-guild relationship map**.

Example: Mara Venn, bakery owner.

- Boros buys morning bread for the local garrison.
- Her sister is Selesnyan.
- Her lease is Orzhov-financed.
- Izzet heat lines keep the ovens reliable.
- A Dimir courier rents the room upstairs.
- She hates the Azorius inspector who keeps citing her back stair.
- She quietly feeds Golgari children who come up on delivery routes.

Mara is not "neutral." She is Ravnican.

## Source Note: 04-Interactions/Interguild-Case-Studies.md

# Interguild Case Studies

These are models for how Ravnican institutions collide around ordinary civic needs.

## Case 1 — Apartment Fire

**Problem:** fire in a dense residential block.

**Boros:** evacuate now, enter dangerous structure.  
**Azorius:** preserve scene if arson is suspected.  
**Izzet:** isolate heat and gas lines.  
**Orzhov:** owner/insurer asks who is liable.  
**Selesnya:** nearby vernadi shelters displaced families.  
**Dimir:** courier network knows someone left before the fire.

The fire is solved in an hour. The political incident lasts weeks.

## Case 2 — Water Contamination

**Simic:** test bodies and water, identify biological agent.  
**Izzet:** isolate pumps and pressure zones.  
**Golgari:** says contamination began in lower runoff channels.  
**Azorius:** considers quarantine and mandatory inspection.  
**Boros:** worries enforcement will trigger panic.  
**Orzhov:** sells clean water at high price.

The best villain may be nobody. A maintenance shortcut six months ago caused it.

## Case 3 — New Road Through Ruins

**Izzet:** road/utility line is necessary.  
**Azorius:** approvals are valid.  
**Gruul:** territory was never legitimately surrendered.  
**Selesnya:** old trees stabilize soil.  
**Guildless residents:** commute is currently dangerous and long.  
**Orzhov:** already purchased nearby property expecting values to rise.

All parties have facts. The dispute is about which facts count as authority.

## Case 4 — Illegal Clinic

A guildless healer uses Simic methods without official Combine oversight.

The clinic is effective, cheap, and technically illegal.

Simic fears unsafe replication. Azorius wants licensing. Boros sees no immediate victims. Orzhov offers financing in exchange for ownership. The neighborhood hides the healer.

## Case 5 — Rakdos Street Show

The performance has a permit but audience size exceeds projections.

Azorius says capacity has been violated. Boros wants exits cleared. Rakdos says crowd compression began because Orzhov security closed a side street. Orzhov says the closure protects valuable property.

A safety problem becomes a jurisdiction fight in two minutes.

## Case 6 — Missing Courier

A public Dimir courier disappears.

Azorius treats it as missing-person case. Boros suspects abduction. Dimir covertly wants the courier found before authorities inspect the route. Orzhov holds the courier's debt. Golgari witnesses saw somebody descend below street level.

The courier ran away voluntarily.

Now every institution is more interested than before.

## Case 7 — Golgari Food Shortage

A fungal blight reduces deliveries upward.

Selesnya offers surplus food but cannot replace volume. Simic wants samples. Azorius considers ration controls. Orzhov buys remaining stock. Rakdos performers start mocking hoarders.

The crisis reveals how much comfortable neighborhoods depended on food they despised.

## Case 8 — Escaped Simic Organism

Boros wants capture. Simic wants minimal injury. Izzet offers containment apparatus. Rakdos wants spectacle. Selesnya argues the creature is frightened and being driven toward crowds by pursuit.

The creature itself becomes a test of each guild's instinct.

## Case 9 — Debt Protest

Residents block an Orzhov office after mass evictions.

Orzhov presents legal contracts. Azorius confirms many are valid but investigates irregularities. Boros tries to prevent violence. Rakdos amplifies the protest. Selesnya delivers food. Dimir leaks a ledger showing one minister privately waived debts for political allies.

One document changes the moral temperature of the street.

## Case 10 — Ruin Collapse

A lower street collapses into the undercity.

Surface maps call it empty. Golgari families live below. Izzet infrastructure runs through the cavity. Orzhov owns one ancient deed. Azorius records show contradictory boundaries. Gruul claims the ruin predates all current titles.

The rescue comes first. Ownership comes immediately after.

# Interguild Social Stereotypes, Respect, and Misreadings

These are **common attitudes**, not objective truth. Use them to make conversations layered without making every NPC identical.

## Azorius sees…
- **Boros:** necessary but impatient.
- **Dimir:** evidence contamination wearing a coat.
- **Golgari:** difficult jurisdiction below maps.
- **Gruul:** proof civilization needs enforceable boundaries.
- **Izzet:** a permit violation waiting to happen.
- **Orzhov:** people who read every loophole more carefully than the lawmakers.
- **Rakdos:** public danger defended as expression.
- **Selesnya:** well-meaning parallel authority.
- **Simic:** experts who say "medical necessity" when they want an exception.

**Respects:** documentation, predictability, testimony that survives scrutiny.  
**Misreads:** informal legitimacy as lawlessness.

## Boros sees…
- **Azorius:** people who can make justice wait until it dies.
- **Dimir:** useful intelligence from a source that cannot be trusted.
- **Golgari:** uncomfortable allies during disasters.
- **Gruul:** dangerous raiders with courage Boros can understand.
- **Izzet:** brilliant support staff who should stop testing during emergencies.
- **Orzhov:** legal predators.
- **Rakdos:** crowd-disaster generators.
- **Selesnya:** dependable defenders who debate too long.
- **Simic:** lifesaving medics whose experiments need watching.

**Respects:** courage, accountability, protection of weaker people.  
**Misreads:** caution as cowardice and process as indifference.

## Dimir sees…
Every guild as an information system.

Azorius generates records. Boros generates reports. Golgari controls forgotten routes. Gruul creates unpredictable gaps. Izzet leaks novelty. Orzhov catalogs obligations. Rakdos shapes public perception. Selesnya distributes trust. Simic produces restricted knowledge.

**Respects:** people who understand information has cost.  
**Misreads:** genuine openness as either cover or vulnerability.

## Golgari sees…
The surface as a civilization that hides consequences below street level.

Azorius sends regulations downward. Boros sends bodies after conflict. Izzet sends runoff and broken machinery. Orzhov sends debtors and the dead. Selesnya sends organic waste while preaching purity of life.

**Respects:** people who acknowledge dependence.  
**Misreads:** cleanliness as denial rather than sometimes simply good sanitation.

## Gruul sees…
Structures as claims.

A road says "we own movement here." A wall says "we decide who belongs." A permit says "our paper outranks your memory."

**Respects:** direct speech, physical courage, people who admit the cost of what they want.  
**Misreads:** maintenance of existing communities as identical to expansion.

## Izzet sees…
Problems waiting for models.

Azorius limits variables. Boros gives constraints. Simic offers alternate systems. Gruul demonstrates raw forces. Rakdos finds weird applications. Orzhov provides funding with irritating conditions.

**Respects:** competence, novel insight, willingness to test assumptions.  
**Misreads:** emotional or cultural objection as lack of technical understanding.

## Orzhov sees…
Relationships as obligations already present whether written or not.

**Respects:** people who pay debts, honor promises, and understand leverage.  
**Misreads:** gift economies and communal reciprocity as badly documented debt.

## Rakdos sees…
Every institution's dignity as a setup for a joke.

Azorius gives rules to parody. Boros gives heroic imagery to puncture. Orzhov gives hypocrisy. Selesnya gives earnestness. Izzet gives spectacular accidents.

**Respects:** people who can take a joke, own desire, perform under pressure, and refuse false dignity.  
**Misreads:** sincere boundaries as cowardice or repression.

## Selesnya sees…
People as parts of relationships.

**Respects:** reciprocity, care, patience, contribution.  
**Misreads:** strong individual boundaries as alienation.

## Simic sees…
Bodies and environments as interacting systems.

**Respects:** observation, adaptability, consent understood as informed participation, willingness to revise assumptions.  
**Misreads:** attachment to an existing form as fear of improvement.

# Respect changes scenes

NPCs should be capable of revising stereotypes.

A Gruul scout may respect an Azorius official who personally walks disputed ground before ruling.  
A Boros sergeant may trust a Dimir courier who gives actionable truth at personal risk.  
An Orzhov advokist may admire a Selesnyan community that honors promises even without contracts.  
An Izzet researcher may respect a Rakdos rigger whose practical safety knowledge catches a design flaw.

Those moments keep guild identity from becoming caricature.

# Institutional Scenes — Volume 1

## 1. Sova Hearing: The Missing Stair

An apartment owner sealed an exterior stair after repeated thefts. Fire evacuation now fails code.

The owner argues the stair was never part of the leased premises. Tenants have used it for forty years. The original building plans are from a street level that no longer exists.

The judge wants evidence. The residents want the stair reopened today. The owner wants reimbursement. An Izzet inspector says the current internal stair is unsafe anyway.

**Room texture:** high ceiling, ink, benches polished by generations of waiting, whispers cut off whenever the clerk stands.

## 2. Boros Briefing: Six Streets, Four Soldiers

The local captain has four squads and six simultaneous problems. The PCs are present because they know something about one of them.

The captain's map is covered with notes: riot risk, missing child, Gruul movement, broken bridge, Orzhov security request, Simic animal escape.

The scene is about prioritization. Every decision leaves somebody waiting.

## 3. Izzet Safety Review

A prototype passed every designed test and then did something the test did not measure.

Half the team wants to shut it down. Half wants to reproduce the anomaly immediately.

The room contains scorched instruments, three versions of the same diagram, and a mug fused to a table by heat.

No one is stupid. The disagreement is about whether unknown behavior is evidence of danger or opportunity.

## 4. Orzhov Debt Conference

The room is warm, quiet, and comfortable.

The debtor is offered water and a chair.

The advokist explains five repayment plans. Every plan is survivable in isolation. All of them reshape the next decade of the debtor's life.

The cruelty is in how reasonable everything sounds.

## 5. Selesnyan Vernadi Meeting

Food is served before debate.

Residents sit in a circle beneath a living tree. The voda listens while a family asks permission to leave the vernadi and sell their home to an Orzhov-backed developer.

Legally the home is theirs. Socially, the sale would fracture the community courtyard.

Nobody raises their voice.

## 6. Simic Consent Room

A patient needs adaptation to survive.

The biomancer explains the procedure clearly. The patient understands. Their employer insists the alteration may violate workplace rules. The patient's spouse is frightened of permanent change.

The Simic doctor becomes angry—not because anyone questions the science, but because people are discussing property and employment while the patient's condition worsens.

## 7. Dimir Archive Request

The public archive clerk is helpful, ordinary, and slightly bored.

The record the PCs want exists but was moved after a water leak.

The clerk remembers who requested it last month because the requester complained about the wait.

That tiny detail is the entire clue.

## 8. Rakdos Rehearsal

The rehearsal is more disciplined than the performance.

Rigging is checked. Marks are measured. A stage manager screams about safety. The star performer keeps improvising anyway.

The party sees the truth: chaos on stage requires enormous backstage competence.

Then one prop turns out to be an actual confiscated Azorius evidence cage.

# Night and Undercity Scenes

## 1. Shift Change Below

At midnight, a maintenance hatch opens and Golgari workers climb up carrying empty food crates while guildless sanitation workers descend with full waste carts.

For ten minutes, the alley becomes a border crossing between two versions of the city.

A drunk insults the Golgari workers. One sanitation worker tells him to shut up because these are the people who keep his block from drowning in refuse.

## 2. Rain in the Sunken Street

Soft rain falls through an opening three stories above.

The street used to be open sky before newer construction surrounded it. Dim lamps reflect in standing water. A courier waits beneath an arch with no visible door.

The party hears footsteps on an upper level but cannot tell whether they are overhead or across the buried street.

## 3. The Fungal Wake

A Golgari family holds a wake beside luminous fungus beds.

Food is served. Stories are funny. The body is present and will later be returned to the local cycle according to custom.

An Orzhov representative arrives with documents claiming funerary rights under an old debt.

The family does not dispute the signature. They dispute whether the dead can still owe.

## 4. The Last Lift

The final public lift of the night fails between levels.

Inside: a Boros soldier off duty, a Rakdos performer carrying props, an Orzhov clerk, a Selesnyan grandmother, two students, and a cage containing something small from a Simic lab.

The failure itself is ordinary.

The cage is not.

## 5. The Illegal Garden

A patch of pale flowers grows beneath a bridge where no direct sunlight reaches.

Selesnyan gardeners tend it quietly at night because the property owner has ordered the space cleared.

The flowers absorb runoff from an Izzet conduit and are the reason nearby residents stopped getting sick.

The owner has already leased the space for storage.

## 6. The Closed Tunnel

Azorius seal. Boros warning ribbon. Golgari chalk marks. Gruul paint. Dimir symbol that may be fake.

Everyone has marked the same tunnel for a different reason.

The tunnel leads to an older transit platform recently uncovered by a collapse.

Nothing inside is immediately hostile. That is what makes everyone nervous.

## 7. The Smelting Quarter After Show

The performance is over. The real city begins.

Stagehands coil chains. Injured performers drink water. Factory workers arrive for night shift. Vendors count coins. Boros watches from the edge without intervening.

A star performer privately asks the PCs to find a missing stagehand because calling Boros would damage the troupe's reputation.

## 8. The Water Below Zonot Seven

A Simic guide takes the party beneath public areas of Zameck.

Street noise fades. Air becomes damp. Pumps thrum. The water below is black until bioluminescent organisms move and reveal impossible depth.

The guide stops talking like a scientist for the first time and simply says, "This was here before the streets."

# Street Scenes — Volume 1

These are not encounters waiting for combat. They are **living slices of Ravnica** with sensory detail, competing needs, and consequences.

## 1. The Heat Line

**Opening:** Warm vapor rolls from a trench cut across the street. Copper piping glows faintly blue beneath wet cobbles. Thirty residents in coats crowd behind an Azorius barrier while an Izzet technician argues with an arrester.

**Sound:** hissing steam, angry residents, metal tools, a child coughing.

**Situation:** The line feeding three apartment blocks is failing. Izzet can restore heat quickly by bypassing a safety regulator. Azorius has prohibited that model after a previous explosion.

**People:**  
- Senna Vur, Izzet technician: exhausted, competent, wants twenty uninterrupted minutes.  
- Officer Parel, Azorius: has seen the earlier explosion report and refuses to be the official who ignores it.  
- Old Mikk, tenant: his spouse is ill upstairs.

**If PCs help:** choose speed, legality, evacuation, or a clever alternative. Any success makes one institution remember them.

## 2. The Bread Queue

**Opening:** Rain has turned the market awning into a drum. The line for Golgari ration bread bends around a shuttered bakery.

**Smell:** wet wool, mushroom broth, sour yeast.

**Situation:** A surface warehouse fire destroyed normal supplies. Golgari sellers doubled deliveries, but an Orzhov creditor has seized the bakery ovens over unpaid debt.

A Boros patrol is present because tempers are rising. The seizure is legal.

**Truth:** the bakery owner gave away food during the fire instead of making loan payments.

**Pressure:** feed people now, respect property, or challenge the contract.

## 3. The Moving Stage

A giant carries half a Rakdos stage down an avenue while stagehands run beside it tightening cables.

The troupe has a permit for the next square, but a damaged bridge forces a detour through a Selesnyan neighborhood during evening meal.

The performers are rude but not initially violent. Residents are furious because the last unscheduled show damaged a community garden.

The interesting solution is logistical and social: alternate route, compensation, temporary dismantling, or turning the move itself into a show.

## 4. The Wrong Door

A Dimir courier knocks on the PCs' door, realizes immediately it is the wrong apartment, apologizes, and leaves.

Ten minutes later, an Azorius investigator asks whether anyone delivered a sealed packet.

The courier genuinely made a mistake. The packet was intended for the identical apartment number on the old lower street beneath the building.

Now the PCs know a lower street exists.

## 5. The Fallen Centaur

A centaur slips when a freight lift jerks unexpectedly. The injury blocks a narrow market lane.

A Simic medic begins treatment. Boros clears space. Merchants complain because the blockage is destroying the day's trade.

The lift operator insists the machine was serviced yesterday.

An Izzet tag proves that is true.

The cause is not sabotage but a building owner who overloaded the lift to avoid paying for a second freight run.

## 6. The Eviction Choir

Orzhov enforcers arrive to remove a family from a tenement. Selesnyan neighbors stand in the stairwell singing quietly.

Nobody attacks.

The song makes the enforcers hesitate because forcing passage will be ugly and public.

Azorius confirms the eviction order is valid. A Rakdos performer has begun attracting a crowd outside.

The party can solve the family, the debt, the crowd, or the law—but probably not all four at once.

## 7. The Garden Under the Road

Survey marks appear through a community garden. Izzet plans show a new utility route. Selesnya says the mature trees stabilize the block's old foundation.

Both are correct: the road needs the line and the roots are structurally important.

Gruul graffiti appears overnight: **LET IT FALL.**

A resident admits they invited the Gruul to scare the planners.

## 8. The Unclaimed Body

A dead man is found beneath a bridge.

Boros believes he was murdered. Orzhov claims the body under a funeral contract. Golgari says the body was left in undercity territory and has already begun reclamation rites. Azorius seals the scene.

The dead man carried three guild insignia, none registered to his name.

Dimir has not arrived, which is itself suspicious.

## 9. The Beautiful Monster

A Simic transport tank cracks in a plaza. A juvenile krasis escapes.

It is frightened, not hostile.

Children love it. Merchants do not. Boros wants the square cleared. Simic wants the creature alive and uninjured. A Rakdos scout offers the handlers a fortune to rent it for a show later.

The creature keeps returning to a fountain because the water calms it.

## 10. The Permit Nobody Read

A street festival has been operating under the same Azorius permit for twenty-three years.

A new clerk notices that the original text accidentally authorizes "unrestricted public assembly until sunset on the final day of Ravnica."

Nobody knows what that sentence legally means.

Three political groups immediately decide it means exactly what they need.

Three days later, Selesnyan gardeners discover the bridge blocks sunlight to a courtyard greenhouse.

A Boros veteran has accumulated Azorius fines for feeding stray rocs near a restricted landing zone.

They are not harmless.

The patient's public persona becomes a medical privacy problem.

## Canon anchors used by the one-shot

The adventure fits naturally into published Ravnica because:

- the Tenth District is a dense center of guild activity;

- Gruul clans occupy ruins, rubblebelts, and contested urban edges;
- Rakdos stages can appear in streets and plazas and are moved between venues;
- Precinct Six and the Smelting Quarter support the industrial/nightlife tone used by the chase.

## Campaign inventions that must remain labeled as campaign material

- Chemister Olya Renn;
- Tavin Rell;
- the specific sabotage;
- the orientation prism/core used by the scenario;
- the precise leyline behavior;
- the specific chase path;
- the named supporting NPCs created for the one-shot;
- exact layouts of workshops and annexes.

## Hydrating the opening workshop

Do not describe it only as "Izzet lab under attack."

### Before the attack

**Sight:** overhead gantries, suspended instrument cages, chalk calibration marks, mizzium housings, bundled cables, cargo platform waiting by the door.

**Sound:** transformer hum, a repeating alignment chime, technicians calling measurements, freight from Augustin Station beyond the wall.

**Smell:** hot dust, machine oil, ozone, coffee gone cold.

**Ordinary work:** one technician logs data, one argues with a supplier, one worker is fixing a mundane lift.

Then the sabotage matters because it disrupts a workplace the players briefly understand.

## Hydrating the chase

Every leg should change **social context**, not just terrain.

### Near Augustin Station

Crowded, scheduled, transit-oriented. People hate being blocked.

### Through a Rakdos street performance

The chase becomes public entertainment. Bystanders choose sides. Performers improvise around danger. The party can gain or lose crowd support.

### Toward the Smelting Quarter

Heat, shift labor, freight, soot, cranes, foundry noise. Workers care whether the chase interrupts production.

### Orientation annex

Spatial instability should sit on top of mundane abandonment: old notices, broken furniture, obsolete instruments, evidence that people once worked routine shifts here.

## Tavin Rell in a living city

Tavin works better if he uses Ravnica rather than merely running across it.

He knows:

- which checkpoint can be delayed by paperwork;
- which Rakdos troupe will turn pursuit into spectacle;
- which freight route avoids Boros;
- which Dimir courier can move a message;
- which guildless mechanic will help for cash without asking questions.

That makes him a Ravnican smuggler rather than a generic thief.

## Consequence memory

After the one-shot, record:

- which guild saw the PCs act;
- what damage they caused;
- who they helped;
- which local worker remembers them;
- whether the prism returned to Izzet hands;
- whether any institution now considers the party useful, dangerous, indebted, or embarrassing.

Those are hooks for the next visit.

# Original Ravnica Trilogy Era — Deep Overlay

This is an **era overlay**, not the V2 default present.

Use it for games set around *Ravnica: City of Guilds*, *Guildpact*, and *Dissension*, especially the Decamillennial crisis and its aftermath.

## Source priority

1. the original novels if they are available to the GM;
2. the official retrospective **Ravnica, Then and Now**;
3. later official setting material describing consequences of the trilogy;
4. V2 synthesis only where published material is silent.

Official retrospective:
https://magic.wizards.com/en/news/feature/ravnica-then-and-now-2012-08-29-0

---

# The great difference: the old Guildpact is part of reality

For nearly ten thousand years, the Guildpact is not merely a treaty that politicians agree to honor. It is a magically binding structure assigning functions and constraining conflict among the guilds.

That changes the psychology of the city.

Later Ravnicans know the system can break.

Many people before the Decamillennial have spent their entire lives—and all recorded family memory—inside a civic order that seems almost as permanent as gravity.

A clerk can say, "That cannot happen under the Guildpact," and mean more than "it would be illegal."

## What ten thousand years of institutional continuity feels like

- guild functions are treated as facts of life;
- old procedures survive because nobody remembers a world without them;
- ancient boundaries can matter more than the current buildings standing on them;
- rival guilds have grudges older than modern neighborhoods;
- paruns and founders are not remote mythology when some guilds still maintain direct cultural continuity with them;
- guild symbols function like parts of the city's visual grammar.

A person can hate the system while finding it almost impossible to imagine Ravnica without it.

---

# The Decamillennial is not one festival

The ten-thousand-year anniversary of the Guildpact should alter the city for weeks.

## Before the celebration

Expect:

- repair crews beautifying major routes;
- guild delegations arriving;
- merchants ordering commemorative goods;
- Azorius issuing temporary traffic and gathering rules;
- Boros increasing patrols;
- Orzhov selling premium ceremonial services;
- Rakdos preparing enormous counter-celebrations;
- Selesnyan vernadi planning communal observances;
- Izzet building things that officials did not ask to be built;
- Golgari workers handling the increased waste and food demand;
- Dimir exploiting movement and information volume;
- Gruul responding to a city celebrating the very system that dispossessed them.

## On the streets

People complain about crowds.
Children collect guild-themed trinkets.
Inn prices rise.
Couriers make money.
Pickpockets make more.
Veterans tell stories.
Old people argue about how earlier anniversaries were better.

The crisis becomes more painful if the city first feels festive.

---

# Agrus Kos and the Wojek perspective

The official retrospective centers hardened Boros Wojek **Agrus Kos** in the unraveling of Szadek's plan.

That is a vital campaign model.

A constitutional catastrophe can begin as police work.

Start with:

- a body;
- a witness;
- a crime scene that makes no sense;
- conflicting jurisdiction;
- a superior who wants the case closed;
- evidence pointing toward a guild nobody thinks should be relevant.

The investigator does not know they are "saving the Guildpact." They are trying to explain the next piece of evidence.

## Trilogy-era Boros tone

The Wojek is especially valuable because it places the player between:

- guild loyalty;
- law;
- street reality;
- military hierarchy;
- corruption;
- the needs of ordinary citizens.

Use precinct relationships, informants, beat knowledge, and old grudges.

A veteran Wojek knows which tavern will talk, which alley always produces false witnesses, and which Azorius clerk can find a record without making the request disappear for three days.

---

# Szadek and a truly hidden Dimir

House Dimir's cultural condition in this era is not identical to later Return-era assumptions.

The original arrangement made the Dimir's existence itself part of ancient secrecy. Knowledge that the guild is active can be genuinely destabilizing.

## Play tone

Later-era citizens can joke that the courier is probably Dimir.

In an earlier game, discovering an organized Dimir operation should feel more like discovering that an old conspiracy theory is real and structurally embedded in the city's founding.

That changes how evidence works.

A witness saying "Dimir did it" may sound unhinged.
An investigator may suppress the claim because writing it down makes them look incompetent.
A senior official may know more than they admit.

## Dimir horror in this era

The strongest Dimir horror is epistemic:

- memories are unreliable;
- official history omits something that nevertheless shaped the city;
- the person who helped the investigation may have guided it deliberately;
- an apparent breakthrough may be part of the enemy's plan.

Do not solve this only with invisible assassins.

---

# Grand Arbiter Augustin IV and the danger of institutional certainty

The official retrospective makes clear that the end of the old Guildpact involves more than one villain.

Azorius ambition matters.

This is an excellent era for showing how a guild can sincerely believe its own institutional virtue so completely that it becomes capable of monstrous overreach.

## Azorius tone before the break

The Senate has ten thousand years of evidence that structured law is what keeps the city from destroying itself.

That makes the institution:

- deeply confident;
- procedurally sophisticated;
- conservative about systemic change;
- capable of treating dissent as proof that stronger law is needed.

The frightening Azorius official is not frothing with power.

They are calm.
They have a legal theory.
They can explain why what they are doing is necessary.

---

# The moment the old Guildpact breaks

Do not make every street instantly become a war zone.

The first effect should be **uncertainty**.

People have habits built over ten millennia.

The guilds still have:

- offices;
- uniforms;
- payroll;
- barracks;
- temples;
- laboratories;
- debt;
- farms;
- patrols;
- internal chains of command.

Those do not disappear.

What disappears is certainty about the invisible structure behind them.

## Questions ordinary people start asking

- Does this contract still bind?
- Can that guild refuse its old civic role?
- Who settles an interguild dispute now?
- Are old territorial restrictions still meaningful?
- Can a guild retaliate openly?
- Can an arrest be resisted if the underlying authority changed?
- What happens to a debt defined by Guildpact-era law?
- Who owns a building whose title depends on an ancient compact?
- What does "illegal" mean when the deepest legal foundation is broken?

These are campaign hooks before armies ever mobilize.

---

# Institutional inertia keeps Ravnica alive

A city this large cannot stop functioning because the metaphysical constitution failed.

Workers still go to shift.

Izzet technicians still repair pressure lines.
Golgari still process refuse.
Boros still respond to violence.
Azorius clerks still open offices.
Orzhov still collect payments.
Selesnya still feeds communities.
Simic still treats patients.
Rakdos still performs.
Dimir still delivers messages.
Gruul still resents the walls.

For a time, Ravnica survives because people keep doing what they did yesterday.

That is both reassuring and dangerous: an institution can continue enforcing a rule after the rule's original foundation has vanished.

---

# Teysa Karlov and Orzhov political intelligence

Official retrospective material places **Teysa Karlov** among the important figures navigating the changing order.

She is useful for showing that an Orzhov character can be:

- legally sophisticated;
- ambitious;
- critical of parts of the institution;
- loyal to Orzhov interests;
- capable of reform without becoming non-Orzhov.

## Table lesson

Do not treat "reformer" as "secretly good person who agrees with another guild."

An Orzhov reformer may want:

- more efficient debt;
- less wasteful cruelty;
- clearer rights because clear rights make contracts easier to enforce;
- new leadership because the current leadership is bad for business.

Reform can remain culturally Orzhov.

# Momir Vig, old Simic confidence, and Project Kraj

The old Simic Combine carries a different historical emotional temperature than the later Zegana-era guild.

Under **Momir Vig**, directed biological improvement and guild ambition can become terrifyingly expansive.

**Project Kraj** matters not only as a creature or event but as institutional memory.

It represents the possibility that:

- the research worked in important ways;
- the scale of ambition was catastrophic;
- the guild's idea of improvement became detached from limits.

## Before Kraj

Play confidence.

Researchers believe biological design can solve systemic problems.

## After Kraj

Play argument.

Some conclude:
- the method was wrong.

Others:
- the leadership was wrong.

Others:
- the project failed only because it was compromised.

Others:
- the old Simic identity itself must be rebuilt.

Later Holdfast thinking is richer when this wound exists behind it.

# Rakdos and catastrophic survival

Rakdos as an ancient parun gives the Cult a different relationship to institutional crisis.

The city can experience enormous political change while the demon at the center of the Cult remains a living connection to the original compact.

If Rakdos is injured, incapacitated, or withdrawn during the era's disasters, the guild does not simply vanish.

Rings, performers, laborers, bloodwitches, clubs, and local power structures continue.

This is a recurring Ravnican theme:

**guildmaster crisis does not mean civic function disappears.**

# Agyrem and the politics of the dead

The original trilogy changes Ravnica's relationship to ghosts and the realm associated with them.

If using Agyrem directly, maintain a separate era-state document for:

- what ordinary citizens believe happens after death;
- what Orzhov spirits can do;
- whether ghosts are visible/common;
- which places have spiritual overlap;
- which events have already occurred.

Do not import later assumptions backward without checking the chosen date.

# War of the Spark — Deep Era Overlay

This is a **later emergency overlay**, not the default V2 Living Guildpact present.

The public *War of the Spark: Ravnica* story sequence shows the city experiencing invasion as a civic catastrophe: Planeswalkers appear across Ravnica, the Dreadhorde attacks civilians, guild loyalties remain fractured, and leaders must build cooperation while normal urban assumptions collapse.

Primary official public stories:

- *Old Friends and New*
- *The Path to Opulent*
- *Rallying the Reluctant*
- *Desperate Operatives*
- *Operation Desperation*
- *Ashes*

---

# 1. The war begins in places people used yesterday

One reason the official stories are valuable is that the invasion does not occur on an abstract battlefield.

The Transguild Promenade, Tenth District Plaza, Orzhova, guild territories, streets, gardens, and civic spaces become combat zones.

That means invasion imagery should always preserve the ordinary layer underneath it.

A broken wall was somebody's apartment.
A plaza was a meeting place yesterday.
The fallen cart was carrying food.
The bodies in the street include people who were trying to free relatives from rubble.
A new monument or pyramid displaces whatever stood there before.

## GM technique

For every war-torn location, state:

1. what it was used for yesterday;
2. what happened to it;
3. who is trying to use it now.

Example:

Yesterday: café terrace beside a transit route.  
Now: shattered tables and lazotep fragments.  
Current use: Boros medics have turned the cellar into a casualty station while the owner keeps asking whether anyone has seen her cook.

This prevents the war from becoming generic fantasy rubble.

---

# 2. Planeswalkers arrive as incomprehensible refugees and weapons

Official War fiction shows off-world Planeswalkers appearing in the middle of Ravnican public space.

Most ordinary citizens do not possess a complete cosmological model for what is happening.

They see:

- strangers materialize;
- unfamiliar clothing and species;
- unusual magic;
- people who clearly do not know the city;
- veteran Ravnican leaders treating some of these strangers as strategically vital.

## Immediate civilian reactions

- assume Izzet teleportation until evidence says otherwise;
- offer help if the stranger looks injured;
- move away if the arrival damages property;
- report the event;
- exploit confusion;
- ask which guild is responsible;
- later realize there are too many arrivals for an ordinary magical accident.

The revelation that Ravnica is suddenly full of off-world people should be social before it becomes lore exposition.

---

# 3. The Immortal Sun makes the plane a trap

During the active War-era trap, Planeswalkers can arrive but cannot simply leave Ravnica.

For MTGFoundry, this is a **timeline condition**, not a permanent rule of the plane.

## What that changes

A planeswalker accustomed to escape suddenly needs:

- shelter;
- food;
- local allies;
- maps;
- healing;
- information;
- a way to distinguish guild insignia;
- somebody who can explain which street is safe.

That puts powerful off-worlders into the hands of ordinary Ravnicans.

A guildless café owner may become important because three Planeswalkers are hiding in the cellar.

A Dimir courier may become strategically valuable because they can move someone through streets the visitor cannot navigate.

---

# 4. The Beacon creates political blame

Official War material connects the Izzet, Ral Zarek, and the Beacon that summons Planeswalkers.

Whatever the exact technical responsibility, public understanding will be simpler and harsher.

Citizens hear:

- Izzet device;
- Planeswalkers appeared;
- invasion followed.

Those facts are enough for rumor.

## Postwar arguments

"Izzet brought them here."

"No, Izzet tried to warn us."

"Ral knew."

"Ral saved my block."

"Your block survived. Mine burned."

This is how large magical events become neighborhood politics.

---

# 5. Guild distrust does not disappear because Bolas is worse

The official stories explicitly show that guilds are not instantly united.

Some are compromised.
Some retreat to defend their own territory.
Some need to be persuaded.
Some have leadership serving or influenced by Bolas.
Some simply do not trust the coalition being proposed.

That is the correct tone.

## Tactical unity, emotional distrust

A Boros commander may accept Rakdos fighters while assigning troops to watch them.

An Azorius survivor may work with Gruul while still calling them unlawful.

A Selesnyan healer may treat an Orzhov knight and refuse the knight's offer of a binding repayment.

A Dimir operative may provide life-saving intelligence while refusing to explain how it was obtained.

The invasion creates cooperation without erasing identity.

---

# 6. Orzhova becomes sanctuary, treasury, fortress, and political stage

Official War fiction moves through **Orzhova, the Cathedral Opulent** while efforts are being made to rally guild support.

During invasion, an Orzhov cathedral can serve many functions at once:

- shelter;
- command point;
- financial vault;
- negotiation site;
- fortified interior;
- source of magical resources;
- center of hierarchy.

## Ordinary people inside

Do not fill it only with leaders.

Use:

- borrowers trapped away from home;
- servants;
- clerks preserving ledgers;
- wounded enforcers;
- priests organizing food;
- families who fled nearby streets;
- wealthy patrons demanding private protection;
- spirits continuing old duties as though invasion were simply another breach of contract.

## Postwar leverage

Every emergency promise can become a future claim.

An Orzhov official who supplies food may later insist the transaction was recorded.

Another may deliberately waive payment and gain enormous social capital.

The guild is not required to make the same choice everywhere.

---

# 7. Rakdos becomes useful without becoming orderly

The War gives the Cult a situation where traits normally feared by the city can become military assets:

- willingness to confront terror;
- appetite for spectacle;
- physical daring;
- chaotic movement;
- contempt for authoritarian pretension;
- comfort with violence.

That does not turn Rakdos into Boros.

## A Rakdos defense scene

A Ringmaster does not say, "Form ranks."

They turn a street into a performance arena, lure Eternals into a kill zone, ignite scenery, and use an audience's panic as energy for coordinated movement.

Some civilians cheer.
Others are nearly killed by the solution.

Both responses are valid.

---

# 8. Selesnya's distributed community is wartime infrastructure

A vernadi can rapidly become:

- shelter;
- kitchen;
- field hospital;
- muster point;
- childcare site;
- information node;
- defensive position.

Selesnya's strength during civic collapse is that its communities already know how to feed, house, train, and organize people locally.

## The cost

Destroying a vernadi does more than remove military capacity.

It can remove:

- home;
- temple;
- family network;
- food;
- identity;
- spiritual connection.

A displaced Selesnyan survivor may experience the loss as several kinds of homelessness at once.

---

# 9. Golgari becomes responsible for what the surface cannot bear to look at

War produces:

- bodies;
- sewage failure;
- contaminated water;
- broken foundations;
- flooded lower levels;
- food disruption;
- abandoned animals;
- unstable ruins.

These are all problems the undercity understands.

Even when the Golgari leadership is politically complicated, local Swarm communities can become essential simply because they know how to process catastrophe.

## Postwar resentment

The surface may praise heroic guilds publicly while quietly expecting Golgari workers to remove the dead.

That hypocrisy should matter.

---

# 10. Boros fights the war it always imagined—and pays for it

The invasion validates Boros fears about external catastrophic violence.

It also pushes the Legion toward the most dangerous edge of its virtue: decisive force under impossible conditions.

## Show both

Heroism:
- holding evacuation routes;
- rescuing civilians;
- standing against Eternals;
- aerial deployment;
- exhausted soldiers refusing to abandon a block.

Cost:
- emergency requisition;
- demolished buildings to create defensive space;
- civilians ordered from homes;
- confused chains of command;
- friendly fire;
- triage;
- trauma;
- officers making decisions they will review for years afterward.

Boros becomes compelling when courage and coercion occupy the same uniform.

---

# 11. Azorius continuity becomes a form of survival

During invasion, procedure can look absurd.

It can also preserve civilization.

Azorius workers record:

- missing people;
- deaths;
- emergency authority;
- prisoners;
- damaged property;
- evacuation zones;
- supply transfers;
- orders;
- later testimony.

Those records become the foundation for rebuilding.

## Two Azorius reactions

**Emergency pragmatist:** suspends normal process because survival requires it.

**Continuity defender:** preserves process because if every emergency action becomes unrecorded precedent, postwar abuse will be impossible to untangle.

Neither needs to be a caricature.

---

# 12. Dimir information suddenly has obvious humanitarian value

In normal politics, secret information is leverage.

During invasion, route knowledge can save hundreds of lives.

Dimir assets may know:

- which tunnels remain open;
- where enemy movement is concentrated;
- which guild leader can still be reached;
- which officer is compromised;
- which building has a hidden exit;
- which warehouse actually contains food.

A spy can spend years building a secret and then burn it in one minute to open an evacuation route.

That does not make the House altruistic.

It demonstrates that information is power because power sometimes means **keeping people alive**.

---

# 13. Simic emergency medicine pushes ethics to the edge

The War places extreme demand on:

- trauma treatment;
- antidotes;
- biological adaptation;
- environmental survival;
- rapid field medicine;
- creation or deployment of defenders.

A procedure too risky for ordinary approval may be the only option during an invasion.

## Postwar question

If an emergency adaptation saved hundreds of people, should it remain available?

The Simic argument after the crisis can be fiercer than the argument during it.

Some say:
- the measure proved itself.

Others:
- wartime consent was compromised.

Others:
- the emergency revealed that old rules were too conservative.

Others:
- survival cannot justify permanent normalization.

That is excellent campaign politics.

---

# 14. Gruul participation does not equal acceptance of the city

A Gruul warrior can fight Bolas without deciding civilization is legitimate.

The logic can be simple:

"No dragon from another world gets to own the ruins I already hate."

That is enough.

## Wartime alliances

Gruul knowledge of rubble, unstable structures, hidden routes, and improvised movement can be extremely useful.

After victory, the alliance may collapse the moment Azorius planners propose rebuilding every destroyed block exactly as before.

The war can actually sharpen the question:

**what should be rebuilt, and who gets to decide?**

---

# 15. War creates new neighborhood reputations

Afterward, people remember specific acts.

"This was the street the Boros held."

"That café hid walkers."

"The Orzhov opened their cellar."

"The Azorius officer unlocked the gate without waiting for authorization."

"The Rakdos troupe led the Eternals away."

"The Selesnyans fed us."

"The Golgari came up at night and removed the bodies."

"The Izzet line exploded—but it also powered the evacuation lift."

These memories become political facts even when no official history records them.

---

# 16. Postwar rebuilding is the next campaign

The invasion ends before its consequences do.

Rebuilding creates conflicts over:

- property ownership when records are destroyed;
- who pays for repair;
- whether emergency shelters become permanent;
- whether ruined land returns to previous owners;
- whether Gruul claim newly open rubble;
- whether Simic biological defenses are removed;
- whether Boros keeps emergency powers;
- whether Azorius investigates wartime abuses;
- whether Orzhov emergency loans are collectible;
- whether Dimir secrets exposed during the war remain useful;
- whether Rakdos heroes are suddenly expected to behave respectably.

The end of the war is the beginning of a huge civic campaign.

# An Ordinary Week in the Tenth District

This file is table synthesis built from established Ravnican institutions, amenities, guild functions, and official-fiction behavior. It is deliberately mundane. The point is to show what the city feels like **when no adventure has announced itself yet**.

# Seleszeni 12 — First Day

The morning begins with rain.

Not a storm. Persistent cold drizzle that makes the upper bridges glossy and turns every open drain into a line of moving gray water.

## 06:00 — Freight before breakfast

Precinct Two is already awake.

A bakery opens its street shutters while a Golgari delivery team moves sealed food containers from a lower access ramp.

A centaur courier complains that someone parked a produce cart across the broad lane.

An Izzet maintenance crew has three paving stones lifted beside a utility conduit. One apprentice is holding an umbrella over the open access panel instead of over herself.

A Boros patrol finishing night watch buys coffee.

None of these people are thinking about guild politics.

They are thinking about:
- wet boots;
- late deliveries;
- breakfast;
- whether the repair crew will still be blocking the corner when morning traffic peaks.

## 08:30 — The queue

An Azorius inspection closes one entrance to a public lift.

The inspection is routine.

The consequences are not.

Within fifteen minutes:
- office workers are late;
- a teacher has twelve children gathered in the wrong place;
- a loxodon porter cannot fit easily through the alternate stair;
- a merchant begins shouting;
- a street vendor moves closer to the queue and triples sales.

The inspector is not malicious.

The lift's emergency brake actually failed a test.

Nobody in line cares until the inspector explains what the brake does.

## 12:15 — Lunch politics

Two Izzet researchers sit outside a café arguing about whether a malfunction counts as a successful test if it revealed a previously unknown property.

At the next table, an Orzhov advokist is reviewing a lease.

Across the street, a Selesnyan volunteer is distributing small bowls of soup to people displaced by a building repair.

A Dimir courier leaves three sealed packets at three different offices and eats standing up.

This is what interguild life usually looks like:
**shared pavement, separate purposes**.

## 18:00 — Home traffic

Rain stops.

Steam rises from warm grates.

Balconies open.
Laundry appears.
Restaurants fill.

A Rakdos crew measures a small square for an evening performance.

An Azorius local official tells them the permit allows a stage but not open flame.

The Ringmaster says the torches are "illumination."

The official says everyone involved knows exactly what open flame means.

They negotiate.

Nobody draws a weapon.

## 23:30 — The same street

The bakery is closed.

The café is louder.

A Boros patrol now walks the opposite direction.

The Rakdos stage is gone.

There is a scorch mark where the official said there would not be open flame.

A sanitation worker makes a note.

# Dhazo 3 — Second Day

## 07:00 — Household trouble

A tenement's heat fails on three upper floors.

The landlord says the Izzet utility connection is responsible.

The Izzet utility desk says the building's internal line is private property.

The tenants say the pipes are cold and they do not care whose legal problem that is.

By noon:
- Azorius receives a complaint;
- an Orzhov representative reminds the landlord that loan covenants require habitability;
- a local Izzet technician agrees to look unofficially after shift;
- a Selesnyan family lends blankets;
- a guildless plumber says everybody is overcomplicating a cracked valve.

The plumber is right.

The cracked valve was caused by vibration from a nearby Izzet test.

Everybody is also right.

## 16:00 — Children know first

Children discover that warm vapor is escaping from a maintenance grate behind the building.

They turn it into a game.

Adults turn it into evidence.

# Prahz 19 — Third Day

## 09:00 — Market morning

The market smells like:
- wet vegetables;
- coffee;
- mushroom broth;
- baking bread;
- animal sweat;
- soap;
- cheap perfume.

A Boros veteran sells used buckles and repaired leather.

A Selesnyan grower argues amiably with a Golgari mushroom seller about compost.

A Simic hybrid shops for fruit while a child stares at their gills until the child's parent apologizes.

The hybrid laughs.

A street preacher uses the moment to begin explaining that all bodies reveal spiritual debt.

The crowd collectively decides not to engage.

## 11:30 — Small crime

A purse is stolen.

The victim shouts.

A guildless teenager points toward the wrong alley because the thief is their cousin.

A Dimir courier saw the real route and does not want involvement.

A Boros patrol arrives.

An Azorius arrester happens to be nearby and immediately objects to the patrol questioning a minor without proper process.

The stolen purse contains:
- twelve zibs;
- a work key;
- a medicine note;
- no grand conspiracy.

It matters enormously to the owner.

## 14:00 — The consequences of twelve zibs

The owner misses an appointment because the work key is gone.

The employer docks pay.

The clinic cannot find the original medicine authorization.

A clerk later waives the replacement fee.

That clerk may never know they just prevented a family from missing rent.

Small institutional kindness can matter as much as heroism.

# Mokosh 8 — Fourth Day

## 05:30 — Precinct Six shift change

Foundry workers emerge into cool air already smelling of hot metal.

Some are Rakdos.
Some are Izzet.
Some are guildless.
One is Selesnyan and leaves immediately for morning communion.

An Orzhov agent waits outside for a worker who missed two loan payments.

A laborer quietly signals the debtor through a side exit.

The agent notices.

They do not confront the laborer.

They write down the name.

## 09:00 — Deadbridge freight

A Golgari crew brings reclaimed material upward.

Surface buyers inspect:
- fungal fiber;
- salvage metal;
- treated bone;
- compost substrate;
- recovered stone.

Nobody uses the word "garbage" while negotiating price.

## 15:00 — A maintenance problem becomes a political problem

An industrial drain backs up.

The surface foreman says Golgari blockage.

The Golgari crew says the drain was narrowed by a new Izzet sleeve.

Izzet says the sleeve met specification.

Azorius says the permit was conditional.

The original permit copy is in an office across the district.

The smell gets worse.

By evening, a guildless worker has removed the obstruction while four institutions argue about who is authorized to reimburse him.

# Paujal 21 — Fifth Day

## 10:00 — Vernadi morning

A Selesnyan vernadi is busy, not silent.

People:
- cook;
- mend clothes;
- train;
- teach children;
- prune growth;
- treat minor injuries;
- argue about chores;
- pray;
- plan a communal meal.

A visitor expecting mystical serenity discovers that spiritual community includes deciding who cleans the kitchen.

## 13:00 — Recruitment without pressure

A guildless worker eats there regularly.

Nobody has demanded they join.

A friend casually asks whether they have considered staying permanently.

The worker says no.

The friend says, "All right."

Three weeks later the question will feel different because the worker now knows everyone's names.

That is how belonging grows.

## 20:00 — Defense drill

Training begins.

The same courtyard that served lunch becomes a militia space.

Weapons come out of storage.
Children move to supervised rooms.
Routes are cleared.

The transition takes minutes because it has been practiced.

An outsider realizes the peaceful commune has been military infrastructure the entire time.

# Cizarm 5 — Sixth Day

## 07:30 — Precinct Five academic traffic

Students and researchers fill cafés.

A Simic technician carries a sealed case with warning markings.

An Izzet attendant carries six notebooks tied with cord because the laboratory copying device is broken.

A professor is late.

Three students immediately invent reasons involving Dimir sabotage.

The professor simply overslept.

## 10:00 — Clinic

A warehouse worker arrives at a Simic clinic with chemical burns.

The biomancer:
- asks precise questions;
- treats pain;
- explains options;
- requests permission to take a tissue sample.

The worker hears:
"You can save me."

The researcher also hears:
"This exposure pattern is scientifically valuable."

Both are true.

The scene is Ravnican when the medical miracle and research appetite exist together.

## 17:00 — Research ethics in a tavern

Two Simic scientists disagree about a proposed adaptation.

Neither is a villain.

One believes the change is too permanent to recommend casually.

The other believes refusing a proven adaptation because it looks unfamiliar is prejudice disguised as caution.

A guildless bartender listens for twenty minutes and finally asks whether the patient has been invited to the argument.

Silence.

# Tevnember 14 — Seventh Day

## 08:00 — Guildpact administration

At the Chamber, the queue already contains:

- a Boros territorial complaint;
- a Golgari delegation;
- an Izzet request for emergency variance;
- an Orzhov contract dispute;
- a Selesnyan petition;
- two guildless complainants who have been told three times their case belongs somewhere else.

Lavinia or another administrator sorts the morning into:
- matters Jace must hear;
- matters that can be mediated;
- matters that should never have reached this office;
- matters nobody wants to admit are urgent.

The grand constitutional center of Ravnica begins with triage.

## 11:00 — One case becomes a neighborhood

A property dispute concerns six feet of alley.

On paper, trivial.

In practice:
- it is the only freight access to a bakery;
- an Orzhov lender owns the adjacent warehouse;
- the old deed references a wall removed fifty years ago;
- a Golgari tunnel runs below;
- an Izzet utility line runs through the disputed foundation;
- Boros wants the alley kept open as an emergency route.

Six feet.

Five guild interests.

Forty residents.

That is Ravnica.

## 19:00 — Week's end

People go home.

Some do not.

Night shifts start.

A courier runs.
A stage assembles.
A patrol changes.
A Golgari access gate opens.
A laboratory light stays on.
A clerk takes unfinished work home.
A family eats late because the lift broke again.

Somewhere, a famous person is making a decision that could alter the balance of ten guilds.

Most people will hear about it tomorrow.

Tonight, they need sleep.

# Living in Ravnica — Player Reader

This is for players whose characters should feel as if they **grew up on Ravnica** rather than arrived five minutes before the campaign.

It contains ordinary knowledge, habits, assumptions, and social expectations. It avoids GM-only secrets.

# You have almost never seen an empty horizon

The first fact of your life is city.

You grew up with:

- walls;
- roofs;
- towers;
- domes;
- chimneys;
- pipes;
- bridges;
- balconies;
- statues;
- elevated roads;
- market awnings;
- windows looking into other windows;
- streets built above older streets.

Even open places are framed by construction.

A green courtyard has rooftops behind its trees.
A ruined block has bridges crossing overhead.
A plaza opens toward several different street levels.
A park feels special precisely because vegetation interrupts masonry.

When an off-worlder stares too long at the skyline, locals notice.

# You think vertically

"Next door" can mean a different level.

A destination might be directly below you but twenty minutes away because the nearest stair is three blocks over.

A broken lift is not a curiosity. It can ruin hundreds of commutes.

A bridge closure can separate people who live only a few hundred feet apart.

You routinely ask:

- which level?
- above or below the viaduct?
- street entrance or lower entrance?
- public stair or guild access?
- near the old arch or the new one?
- before or after the freight ramp?

Locals navigate by layered landmarks because a flat map only tells part of the truth.

# Guilds are not abstract political parties

Even if you are guildless, guilds touch your day.

You might:

- drink water through Izzet-maintained infrastructure;
- see Boros patrols;
- file a permit or complaint with Azorius;
- owe money to an Orzhov lender;
- buy cheap Golgari food;
- attend a Selesnyan healer or garden;
- use Simic medicine;
- work beside Rakdos laborers;
- hire a courier who may or may not be Dimir;
- pass the remains of a Gruul raid on the way to work.

- your meals;
- your debts;
- your protection;
- your rank;

Some spend years trying.

- families;
- neighborhoods;
- professions;
- trade networks;
- clubs;
- local leaders;
- landlords;
- mutual-aid arrangements;
- criminal protection;
- teachers;
- temples or beliefs;
- social traditions.

A guildless neighborhood can have stronger local identity than a guild district.

Priests.

Your neighbor might owe their life to a Simic clinic.

You may also know someone whose treatment left a visible adaptation.

The question "would you let Simic change you to save you?" is not abstract in Ravnica.

People have family stories about it.

# Orzhov respectability is part of its power

An Orzhov basilica does not have to look criminal.

It can be:

- beautiful;
- organized;
- safe;
- wealthy;
- quiet;
- professionally staffed.

The danger is that all of those qualities can make obligation feel legitimate.

# Boros heroism is part of its power

People really are saved by Boros soldiers.

That is why criticizing Boros excess can be socially complicated.

A resident can believe:

"They saved us during the riot."

and

"They had no right to search every apartment."

at the same time.

# Azorius fairness can be real and still hurt

A rule can apply equally and harm people unequally.

A wealthy person can survive a six-week delay.

A worker who misses wages cannot.

A character who grew up around Azorius systems should understand that injustice can emerge without a corrupt judge.

# Dimir jokes are social camouflage

People can joke:

"Of course the messenger is Dimir."

Most of the time, they are joking.

The joke persists because the public knows information guilds and courier networks exist, while the deeper structure remains intentionally hard to understand.

# Famous people are not part of your daily life—until suddenly they are

You know names like:

- Jace Beleren;
- Aurelia;
- Niv-Mizzet;
- Trostani;
- Lazav;
- Rakdos;
- Jarad;
- Zegana;
- Isperia;
- Borborygmos;
- the Obzedat.

But knowing a guildmaster's name does not mean you know what their current plan is.

Most citizens experience leadership through:

- orders;
- rumors;
- speeches;
- changed patrols;
- policy;
- prices;
- recruitment;
- ceremonies.

When a guildmaster appears personally, that is an event.

# The Living Guildpact is both constitutional miracle and inconvenient human being

Jace Beleren embodies extraordinary authority.

He is also one person.

He can be:

- absent;
- tired;
- overwhelmed;
- late;
- unreachable.

Guilds can wait for rulings.

Lavinia and other officials can spend their lives managing the fact that the supreme constitutional mechanism sometimes leaves the plane.

You have an opinion about this, even if the opinion is only:

"I wish the guilds would stop blocking my street every time they have a constitutional dispute."

# Planeswalkers are not ordinary public knowledge in the same way guilds are

The city contains people who know more about the Multiverse than others.

Do not assume every baker understands what a Planeswalker is.

A strange arrival is more likely to be explained first through familiar categories:

Izzet accident.
Teleportation.
Dimir trick.
Guild magic.

Multiversal truth is unevenly distributed.

# Death is political

Who handles a body can matter.

- Azorius records.
- Boros investigates or honors service.
- Orzhov manages inheritance, debt, ritual, spirits.
- Golgari understands decay and reclamation.
- Selesnya places death in communal and spiritual context.
- Simic may investigate cause or disease.
- Dimir may care what information died.
- Rakdos may interpret mortality through spectacle.
- Gruul practices vary by clan.

A funeral can involve several guilds without anyone drawing a weapon.

# You know the calendar affects the street

The Ravnican year has twelve months and 365 days.

The civic year turns around the Guildpact celebration at the end of Zuun and beginning of Seleszeni.

Even if you do not care about politics, major civic dates change:

- traffic;
- work shifts;
- prices;
- patrols;
- visiting family;
- public performance;
- crowds.

Festivals are infrastructure events.

3. **A map that does not mark elevation is only half a map.**

6. **The Transguild Promenade belongs to everybody precisely because nobody can afford to let one guild own it completely.**

14. **A Wojek and an Azorius officer can agree that you are in trouble for completely different reasons.**

15. **If Boros arrives first, the immediate problem gets smaller and the number of witnesses gets larger.**

92. **If a guild dispute reaches Jace, it was probably already somebody's problem for days, weeks, or years.**

93. **A Planeswalker appearing from nowhere is less immediately believable to most citizens than an Izzet accident.**

94. **Outsiders reveal themselves by looking up too long, asking where the city ends, or assuming all undercity routes connect.**

95. **Another sign of an outsider is asking which guild "controls" an entire district.**

96. **All ten guilds can matter on the same street.**

97. **No guild is only its worst stereotype, because the city depends on too many of their ordinary members.**

98. **No guild is only its best civic function, because every institution can convert necessity into power.**

99. **The city existed before your problem and will continue after it. People around you are trying to finish their own day while you save the world.**

100. **If a scene could happen in any fantasy city after removing the guild names, you are not done describing Ravnica yet.**

# Canon and Synthesis Policy

V2 is intentionally deeper than a sourcebook summary, so every section should distinguish **what is published** from **what is inferred for play**.

## Labels

### Canon
Directly supported by official Ravnica setting or fiction.

Examples: the ten guilds, their civic histories, the Tenth District, the Transguild Promenade, zibs and zinos, the Living Guildpact baseline, Izzet laboratory names, Zameck in Zonot Seven, Deadbridge Chasm, the Smelting Quarter.

### Campaign
Facts established by MTGFoundry / Glass Run material.

Examples: Olya Renn, Tavin Rell, the orientation prism, exact leyline anomalies, specific one-shot events.

### Synthesis
A practical inference that connects canon facts without claiming publication.

Example: describing how a broken public lift creates class pressure, or how an Azorius permit dispute would affect a neighborhood.

### Original scene
Dialogue, sensory prose, invented citizens, and scene seeds written to make the setting playable.

## Rule for deep description

Sensory and social detail can be invented **when it grows from canon structure**.

It is reasonable to describe an Izzet utility site as hot, metallic, loud, and ozone-scented because the guild uses elemental engineering and public works. It is not acceptable to invent a named canon institution and present it as published fact.

## Era discipline

The default V2 era is the Living Guildpact baseline reflected in *Guildmasters' Guide to Ravnica*: Jace embodies the Guildpact and is frequently absent.

War of the Spark facts, post-war leadership, and original Ravnica-trilogy conditions must not silently replace this baseline.

## Copyright discipline

Do not reproduce full copyrighted source text.

Use:
- concise paraphrase;
- fact extraction;
- short quotations only when truly useful;
- source titles and links;
- original prose for read-aloud and scenes.

The objective is a setting bible for play, not a copied book.

# Expanded Source Map — Ravnica V2

V2 follows a **source-first rule**:

> Use published setting material and official fiction wherever they can answer the question. Use synthesis only to connect published facts into playable daily life or to fill genuine gaps.

The goal is not to replace Ravnica with generated lore. It is to make the published city **usable at the table**.

# Provenance labels

## CANON

Explicitly established by an official published source.

Examples:
- Zameck is in Zonot Seven.
- the Izzet maintain a Gravitational Inversion workshop near Augustin Station.
- the Azorius Senate has three Columns.
- the Golgari provide basic food and manage waste.
- Rix Maadi is the Rakdos guildhall.

## CANON-SUPPORTED INFERENCE

Not explicitly narrated in detail, but required or strongly implied by a canonical institution.

Examples:
- a permanent Boros garrison needs quartermasters and cooks;
- New Prahv generates demand for copyists, advocates, messengers, and food vendors;
- a Simic zonot needs maintenance workers and ordinary residents in addition to researchers.

These are not claimed as named canonical facts.

## TABLE SYNTHESIS

Created for play where sources are silent.

Examples:
- the exact name of an invented café;
- the current tenant dispute on a particular block;
- the smell in a made-up stairwell;
- the personalities of new local NPCs.

Table synthesis should fit established Ravnican systems and remain easy to replace if later canon supplies an answer.

## CAMPAIGN CANON

True in MTGFoundry campaign material, but not presented as Wizards canon.

Examples from *The Glass Run*:
- Chemister Olya Renn;
- Tavin Rell;
- the orientation-prism theft;
- the specific sabotage;
- the campaign's exact chase sequence.

## ORIGINAL FICTION

New vignettes, scene prose, dialogue examples, and stories created to demonstrate setting behavior.

Original fiction should never be silently presented as a published event.

# What to extract from fiction

Do not mine official stories only for plot.

For each scene, record:

## Physical city detail

- architecture;
- level changes;
- rain;
- light;
- crowd density;
- transit;
- what ordinary people are carrying;
- how long movement takes.

## Speech

- how a guild member talks when not explaining their guild;
- how professionals address superiors;
- how insults reference guild stereotypes;
- when someone uses a title versus a first name.

## Ordinary labor around the plot

- guards;
- clerks;
- couriers;
- servers;
- laborers;
- attendants;
- healers;
- witnesses;
- drivers;
- maintenance workers.

## Institutional pressure

- who has authority;
- who thinks they have authority;
- what document matters;
- who has to wait;
- which guild gets blamed;
- what changes if the famous protagonist leaves.

That four-part extraction is more valuable for a living-world campaign than copying plot summaries.

# Conflict-resolution hierarchy for V2

When two details conflict:

1. explicit current campaign instruction;
2. selected campaign era;
3. official source specific to that era;
4. official source describing the same guild/location from nearby eras;
5. *Guildmasters' Guide to Ravnica* baseline;
6. V2 canon-supported inference;
7. V2 table synthesis.

A later source does not automatically overwrite an earlier-era campaign.

A War of the Spark leader state must not silently appear in a Living Guildpact game.

A Return-era guildhall should not be projected backward into a pre-construction scene without checking chronology.

## The Secretist material

## Reading practice

For every story, extract four things:

1. **physical city detail**;
2. **how a guild member speaks when not explaining lore**;
3. **what ordinary work happens around the plot**;
4. **what institutional pressure exists even when nobody is fighting**.

Those four categories improve table play more than plot summary.

## Source hierarchy

When sources conflict:

1. explicit current campaign instruction;
2. chosen campaign era;
3. official source specific to that era;
4. broader official setting source;
