# Original Ravnica Trilogy Era — Deep Overlay

This is an **era overlay**, not the V2 default present.

Use it for games set around *Ravnica: City of Guilds*, *Guildpact*, and *Dissension*, especially the Decamillennial crisis and its aftermath.

## Source priority

1. the original novels if they are available to the GM;
2. the official retrospective **Ravnica, Then and Now**;
3. later official setting material describing consequences of the trilogy;
4. V2 synthesis only where published material is silent.

Official retrospective:
https://magic.wizards.com/en/news/feature/ravnica-then-and-now-2012-08-29-0

# The great difference: the old Guildpact is part of reality

For nearly ten thousand years, the Guildpact is not merely a treaty that politicians agree to honor. It is a magically binding structure assigning functions and constraining conflict among the guilds.

That changes the psychology of the city.

Later Ravnicans know the system can break.

Many people before the Decamillennial have spent their entire lives—and all recorded family memory—inside a civic order that seems almost as permanent as gravity.

A clerk can say, "That cannot happen under the Guildpact," and mean more than "it would be illegal."

## What ten thousand years of institutional continuity feels like

- guild functions are treated as facts of life;
- old procedures survive because nobody remembers a world without them;
- ancient boundaries can matter more than the current buildings standing on them;
- rival guilds have grudges older than modern neighborhoods;
- paruns and founders are not remote mythology when some guilds still maintain direct cultural continuity with them;
- guild symbols function like parts of the city's visual grammar.

A person can hate the system while finding it almost impossible to imagine Ravnica without it.

# The Decamillennial is not one festival

The ten-thousand-year anniversary of the Guildpact should alter the city for weeks.

## Before the celebration

Expect:

- repair crews beautifying major routes;
- guild delegations arriving;
- merchants ordering commemorative goods;
- Azorius issuing temporary traffic and gathering rules;
- Boros increasing patrols;
- Orzhov selling premium ceremonial services;
- Rakdos preparing enormous counter-celebrations;
- Selesnyan vernadi planning communal observances;
- Izzet building things that officials did not ask to be built;
- Golgari workers handling the increased waste and food demand;
- Dimir exploiting movement and information volume;
- Gruul responding to a city celebrating the very system that dispossessed them.

## On the streets

People complain about crowds.
Children collect guild-themed trinkets.
Inn prices rise.
Couriers make money.
Pickpockets make more.
Veterans tell stories.
Old people argue about how earlier anniversaries were better.

The crisis becomes more painful if the city first feels festive.

# Agrus Kos and the Wojek perspective

The official retrospective centers hardened Boros Wojek **Agrus Kos** in the unraveling of Szadek's plan.

That is a vital campaign model.

A constitutional catastrophe can begin as police work.

Start with:

- a body;
- a witness;
- a crime scene that makes no sense;
- conflicting jurisdiction;
- a superior who wants the case closed;
- evidence pointing toward a guild nobody thinks should be relevant.

The investigator does not know they are "saving the Guildpact." They are trying to explain the next piece of evidence.

## Trilogy-era Boros tone

The Wojek is especially valuable because it places the player between:

- guild loyalty;
- law;
- street reality;
- military hierarchy;
- corruption;
- the needs of ordinary citizens.

Use precinct relationships, informants, beat knowledge, and old grudges.

A veteran Wojek knows which tavern will talk, which alley always produces false witnesses, and which Azorius clerk can find a record without making the request disappear for three days.

# Szadek and a truly hidden Dimir

House Dimir's cultural condition in this era is not identical to later Return-era assumptions.

The original arrangement made the Dimir's existence itself part of ancient secrecy. Knowledge that the guild is active can be genuinely destabilizing.

## Play tone

Later-era citizens can joke that the courier is probably Dimir.

In an earlier game, discovering an organized Dimir operation should feel more like discovering that an old conspiracy theory is real and structurally embedded in the city's founding.

That changes how evidence works.

A witness saying "Dimir did it" may sound unhinged.
An investigator may suppress the claim because writing it down makes them look incompetent.
A senior official may know more than they admit.

## Dimir horror in this era

The strongest Dimir horror is epistemic:

- memories are unreliable;
- official history omits something that nevertheless shaped the city;
- the person who helped the investigation may have guided it deliberately;
- an apparent breakthrough may be part of the enemy's plan.

Do not solve this only with invisible assassins.

# Grand Arbiter Augustin IV and the danger of institutional certainty

The official retrospective makes clear that the end of the old Guildpact involves more than one villain.

Azorius ambition matters.

This is an excellent era for showing how a guild can sincerely believe its own institutional virtue so completely that it becomes capable of monstrous overreach.

## Azorius tone before the break

The Senate has ten thousand years of evidence that structured law is what keeps the city from destroying itself.

That makes the institution:

- deeply confident;
- procedurally sophisticated;
- conservative about systemic change;
- capable of treating dissent as proof that stronger law is needed.

The frightening Azorius official is not frothing with power.

They are calm.
They have a legal theory.
They can explain why what they are doing is necessary.

# The moment the old Guildpact breaks

Do not make every street instantly become a war zone.

The first effect should be **uncertainty**.

People have habits built over ten millennia.

The guilds still have:

- offices;
- uniforms;
- payroll;
- barracks;
- temples;
- laboratories;
- debt;
- farms;
- patrols;
- internal chains of command.

Those do not disappear.

What disappears is certainty about the invisible structure behind them.

## Questions ordinary people start asking

- Does this contract still bind?
- Can that guild refuse its old civic role?
- Who settles an interguild dispute now?
- Are old territorial restrictions still meaningful?
- Can a guild retaliate openly?
- Can an arrest be resisted if the underlying authority changed?
- What happens to a debt defined by Guildpact-era law?
- Who owns a building whose title depends on an ancient compact?
- What does "illegal" mean when the deepest legal foundation is broken?

These are campaign hooks before armies ever mobilize.

# Institutional inertia keeps Ravnica alive

A city this large cannot stop functioning because the metaphysical constitution failed.

Workers still go to shift.

Izzet technicians still repair pressure lines.
Golgari still process refuse.
Boros still respond to violence.
Azorius clerks still open offices.
Orzhov still collect payments.
Selesnya still feeds communities.
Simic still treats patients.
Rakdos still performs.
Dimir still delivers messages.
Gruul still resents the walls.

For a time, Ravnica survives because people keep doing what they did yesterday.

That is both reassuring and dangerous: an institution can continue enforcing a rule after the rule's original foundation has vanished.

# Teysa Karlov and Orzhov political intelligence

Official retrospective material places **Teysa Karlov** among the important figures navigating the changing order.

She is useful for showing that an Orzhov character can be:

- legally sophisticated;
- ambitious;
- critical of parts of the institution;
- loyal to Orzhov interests;
- capable of reform without becoming non-Orzhov.

## Table lesson

Do not treat "reformer" as "secretly good person who agrees with another guild."

An Orzhov reformer may want:

- more efficient debt;
- less wasteful cruelty;
- clearer rights because clear rights make contracts easier to enforce;
- new leadership because the current leadership is bad for business.

Reform can remain culturally Orzhov.

# Momir Vig, old Simic confidence, and Project Kraj

The old Simic Combine carries a different historical emotional temperature than the later Zegana-era guild.

Under **Momir Vig**, directed biological improvement and guild ambition can become terrifyingly expansive.

**Project Kraj** matters not only as a creature or event but as institutional memory.

It represents the possibility that:

- the research worked in important ways;
- the scale of ambition was catastrophic;
- the guild's idea of improvement became detached from limits.

## Before Kraj

Play confidence.

Researchers believe biological design can solve systemic problems.

## After Kraj

Play argument.

Some conclude:
- the method was wrong.

Others:
- the leadership was wrong.

Others:
- the project failed only because it was compromised.

Others:
- the old Simic identity itself must be rebuilt.

Later Holdfast thinking is richer when this wound exists behind it.

# Rakdos and catastrophic survival

Rakdos as an ancient parun gives the Cult a different relationship to institutional crisis.

The city can experience enormous political change while the demon at the center of the Cult remains a living connection to the original compact.

If Rakdos is injured, incapacitated, or withdrawn during the era's disasters, the guild does not simply vanish.

Rings, performers, laborers, bloodwitches, clubs, and local power structures continue.

This is a recurring Ravnican theme:

**guildmaster crisis does not mean civic function disappears.**

# Agyrem and the politics of the dead

The original trilogy changes Ravnica's relationship to ghosts and the realm associated with them.

If using Agyrem directly, maintain a separate era-state document for:

- what ordinary citizens believe happens after death;
- what Orzhov spirits can do;
- whether ghosts are visible/common;
- which places have spiritual overlap;
- which events have already occurred.

Do not import later assumptions backward without checking the chosen date.

# Street-level scenes by phase

## Before the Decamillennial

A baker curses temporary parade restrictions.
A Wojek doubles patrol.
A Rakdos troupe advertises a satire of the celebration.
An Orzhov church sells commemorative indulgence.
A Selesnyan family prepares food for visitors.
A Gruul scout marks new civic construction as a target.

The city feels confident enough to make jokes about itself.

## During the hidden crisis

Rumors contradict.
Authorities tell citizens the celebration remains safe.
Investigators receive orders that make less sense the deeper the case goes.
Dimir becomes a word some people refuse to say seriously.

## Immediately after the break

Nothing looks different for one terrible moment.

Then people begin discovering which rules were habits, which were contracts, and which were magic.

A landlord tests an old restriction.
A patrol crosses a boundary it never crossed before.
A guild refuses service.
An Orzhov advocate argues that an obligation still survives.
A clerk says no one has issued new instructions.

This is how constitutional collapse becomes a street scene.

# How to convert V2 baseline files to this era

Keep:

- city verticality;
- ordinary urban life;
- guild civic functions;
- most architectural identity;
- multi-guild dependence;
- neighborhood complexity.

Change:

- Jace/Lavinia/Chamber references;
- guild leadership;
- Dimir public awareness;
- Simic philosophy and leadership;
- exact Orzhov politics;
- post-Mending assumptions;
- anything dependent on later Return-era rebuilding.

Always ask: **has the event that created this later institution happened yet?**
