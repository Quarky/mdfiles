# Guild Interaction Matrix

Ravnican guild relations are not fixed ally/enemy tags. Two guilds can cooperate intensely on one problem and sabotage one another on the next. Use the **cooperation axis** and **friction axis** together.

| Pair | Natural cooperation | Deep friction |
|---|---|---|
| Azorius–Boros | emergency response, warrants, public order | procedure versus immediate justice |
| Azorius–Dimir | records, investigation, controlled intelligence | verifiable evidence versus secrecy and manipulation |
| Azorius–Golgari | sanitation, food safety, burial/property disputes | surface law applied to undercity customs |
| Azorius–Gruul | rare ceasefires and negotiated access | legitimacy itself; Gruul rejects the authority writing the rule |
| Azorius–Izzet | permits, infrastructure standards, public works | experimentation outruns regulation |
| Azorius–Orzhov | contracts, courts, property, banking rules | Orzhov excels at exploiting legal structure without respecting its purpose |
| Azorius–Rakdos | event permits, crowd plans, censorship hearings | freedom/satire versus safety/control |
| Azorius–Selesnya | public welfare, parks, mediation | formal law versus local communal authority |
| Azorius–Simic | medicine, public health, research regulation | safety review versus adaptive experimentation |
| Boros–Dimir | intelligence on threats | Boros hates dependence on unverifiable sources |
| Boros–Golgari | disaster recovery, corpse retrieval, defending poor districts | prejudice, undercity sovereignty, use of undead |
| Boros–Gruul | temporary battlefield truces, respect for strength | raids, territorial defense, militarized repression |
| Boros–Izzet | emergency engineering, armor, weapons, transport | prototypes endanger troops and civilians |
| Boros–Orzhov | protection of property and civilians | protection becomes extortion; justice conflicts with valid but predatory contracts |
| Boros–Rakdos | event security, confronting common threats | crowd violence and disdain for discipline |
| Boros–Selesnya | neighborhood defense, evacuation, militia coordination | centralized command versus communal decision-making |
| Boros–Simic | combat medicine, rescue adaptations | concern over engineered soldiers and uncontrolled organisms |
| Dimir–Golgari | undercity routes, hidden caches, forgotten records | territory, secrets, and who owns abandoned space |
| Dimir–Gruul | deniable information and common enemies | Gruul independence makes long-term manipulation unreliable |
| Dimir–Izzet | technical intelligence, stolen research | Izzet gossip and public experimentation ruin secrecy |
| Dimir–Orzhov | dossiers, leverage, financial intelligence | neither side wants to owe the other |
| Dimir–Rakdos | spectacle as cover, rumor amplification | Rakdos publicity destroys careful control |
| Dimir–Selesnya | community intelligence and discreet mediation | dense trust networks resist infiltration |
| Dimir–Simic | Crypsis, research theft, biological surveillance | Simic records create evidence Dimir may want erased |
| Golgari–Gruul | resentment of upper-city expansion, survival in ruins | cycle/reclamation versus pure destruction |
| Golgari–Izzet | industrial waste reuse, underground infrastructure | contamination and careless disposal |
| Golgari–Orzhov | funerary logistics, old property and spirits | Golgari accepts death; Orzhov monetizes refusal to relinquish status |
| Golgari–Rakdos | labor, underground venues, acceptance of death | pointless killing wastes what Golgari would reclaim |
| Golgari–Selesnya | agriculture, life cycles, green space | Selesnya idealizes life and harmony; Golgari centers decay and inequality |
| Golgari–Simic | ecosystems, disease, adaptation, fungi | Simic language of perfection can sound arrogant and linear |
| Gruul–Izzet | respect for elemental power, rare practical repairs | infrastructure expands the city into clan space |
| Gruul–Orzhov | almost none beyond temporary bargaining | property, debt, hierarchy, and urban ownership embody everything Gruul hates |
| Gruul–Rakdos | anti-authoritarian action, riots, common targets | Rakdos wants a stage; Gruul may want the stage destroyed |
| Gruul–Selesnya | nature and resistance to sterile development | Selesnya domesticates and organizes nature |
| Gruul–Simic | survival knowledge, hostile-environment expertise | engineered adaptation feels like civilization rewriting nature |
| Izzet–Orzhov | financing, contracts, industrial ownership | research priorities distorted by profit and liability |
| Izzet–Rakdos | pyrotechnics, rigging, spectacle | neither wants responsibility after the show goes wrong |
| Izzet–Selesnya | water, heat, bridges, green-city projects | efficient infrastructure can erase living space |
| Izzet–Simic | science, complex systems, mutual admiration | mechanism/energy versus organism/ecology; rivalry over whose method is truly elegant |
| Orzhov–Rakdos | patronage, venues, entertainment for elites | Rakdos humiliates hierarchy and exposes hypocrisy |
| Orzhov–Selesnya | charity, housing, communal services | debt obligation versus reciprocal communal duty |
| Orzhov–Simic | research funding, clinics, medical contracts | bodily survival becomes collateral |
| Rakdos–Selesnya | huge audiences, festivals, public ritual | collective harmony versus radical appetite and mockery |
| Rakdos–Simic | strange creatures, body performance, practical collaboration | living subjects treated as art props or spectacle |
| Selesnya–Simic | ecology, healing, adaptation, green infrastructure | natural harmony versus deliberate redesign |

## How to use this

Do not ask "do these guilds like each other?" Ask:

1. What shared problem forces them into the same room?
2. What does each side consider the **obvious** solution?
3. Which value makes that solution unacceptable to the other side?
4. What third guild profits from delay?
5. Which ordinary citizens bear the cost while institutions argue?

A two-guild conflict feels Ravnican. A three-guild conflict with guildless consequences feels like a city.
