# History, Political Memory, and What People Remember

Ravnica's history is not a list of ancient dates. It is **present in institutions, ruins, family stories, neighborhood grudges, public holidays, and arguments over what the Guildpact was supposed to mean**.

## The original Guildpact

More than ten thousand years of relative guild order created an expectation that the ten-guild system is not merely political. It is the shape civilization takes.

Even citizens who dislike guilds may think in guild categories because the institutions have existed longer than almost any family, district boundary, or neighborhood government.

The original pact did more than prevent war. It assigned civic functions. Over millennia, guilds grew away from those original roles while still using them as justification.

## The Decamillennial rupture

The breaking of the old Guildpact is recent enough to be political memory, not myth.

Older adults may remember a time when the magical order felt more fixed. Younger adults grew up in the uncertainty after its collapse.

That generational difference matters. An older Azorius official can sincerely say, "we used to know what the limits were." A younger official may regard constant institutional improvisation as normal.

## The Implicit Maze and the Living Guildpact

Jace's rise as Living Guildpact restored a binding mechanism in a radically personal form.

This should feel strange even years later. A plane-wide constitutional principle is now embodied by a man who can leave the plane.

Ravnicans debate this constantly:

- Is Jace's absence negligence or unavoidable reality?
- Should guilds wait for his judgment?
- Can precedent substitute for his presence?
- Does his authority weaken local institutions or save them from deadlock?
- What happens if a guild decides absence equals opportunity?

## History in the built environment

A wall can be older than the office using it. A shrine can predate the street around it. A buried plaza can preserve inscriptions from earlier guild arrangements.

When players investigate a place, ask what political era physically shaped it.

## Historical memory by guild

**Azorius:** precedent and the fear of returning to chaos.  
**Boros:** remembered sacrifices, battles, and institutions that failed to protect people.  
**Dimir:** history is information; what is forgotten can be more valuable than what is remembered.  
**Golgari:** all structures decay; history proves their philosophy.  
**Gruul:** history is dispossession—evidence that the city kept expanding into what was once theirs.  
**Izzet:** history is a record of solved and unsolved technical problems.  
**Orzhov:** history is debt, inheritance, ownership, and continuity of power.  
**Rakdos:** history is material for satire; yesterday's sacred institution becomes tonight's punch line.  
**Selesnya:** continuity of community and Worldsoul outlasts political cycles.  
**Simic:** history reveals changing environments and the cost of failing to adapt.

## GM use

Whenever a conflict feels flat, add an older version of the same conflict.

Today's housing dispute stands on yesterday's burned guildhall. Today's laboratory occupies an old court. Today's road crosses land a clan remembers differently.

Ravnica becomes deep when present politics has archaeological layers.
