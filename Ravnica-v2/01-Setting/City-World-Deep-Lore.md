# City-World Deep Lore

## Ravnica is urban geology

Ravnica covers the known world in construction layered over construction. The important idea is not simply "a very large city." It is that **the city has become terrain**. Streets can be rooftops of older neighborhoods. Cellars open into forgotten plazas. A sewer line can intersect a shrine, a collapsed viaduct, and a Golgari fungus field before reaching a modern pumping station.

People navigate by districts, precincts, trade ways, towers, guildhalls, bridges, and neighborhood names because a flat map cannot express the city well. "Across town" can mean climbing two hundred feet, crossing three bridges, descending through a market hall, and taking a public lift to a lower street that technically lies beneath the block you started in.

## The ten-guild settlement is in everything

The Guildpact historically assigned civic functions to the guilds, but millennia of ambition, interpretation, and institutional drift turned those functions into entire subcultures.

That means guild conflict is rarely cleanly separated from public necessity. If the Izzet stop maintaining a conduit because an Azorius order freezes access, apartments lose heat. If the Boros seal a street after a Gruul attack, merchants lose a day's income. If the Golgari stop processing waste or moving food upward, the poor feel it first. If Orzhov credit vanishes, some businesses close while others are bought cheaply. If Selesnyan growers refuse a redevelopment, a neighborhood may lose both a garden and a militia post.

The city survives because rivals keep performing work they resent one another for controlling.

## The core and the wider plane

The central city is divided into ten enormous districts, all crossed by all ten guilds. The Tenth District is especially politically dense because the guilds maintain important headquarters there and because many famous civic locations cluster nearby.

The Transguild Promenade links the core districts and functions as trade artery, parade route, meeting ground, and symbolic neutral space. Its neutrality is not absolute peace; it is a shared recognition that everyone loses if the arterial route becomes a permanent battlefield.

Beyond the core lie more districts and old urban centers absorbed by expansion. Ravnica has neighborhoods whose identities predate modern administrative boundaries. A resident may identify more strongly with Deadbridge, a smelting quarter, a moss haven, or a local vernadi than with a formal district number.

## The Living Guildpact problem

The current baseline is precarious rather than post-apocalyptic. Jace can render judgments with extraordinary authority, but he is often absent. This creates a strange constitutional culture:

- everyone agrees that a supreme mechanism exists;
- almost nobody can reliably access it;
- every guild therefore keeps its own lawyers, precedents, leverage, investigators, and contingency plans;
- local officials must decide whether to act now or wait for impossible certainty.

The result is a city where procedure and improvisation coexist. A neighborhood survives because people develop customs faster than central authority can settle disputes.

## A city of competence

Ravnica should not feel like a collapsed setting where nobody knows how anything works. It is ancient, crowded, dangerous, and politically dysfunctional, but it also supports central heating, plumbing, lifts, mass markets, guild hospitals, public law, freight networks, universities, military garrisons, organized waste processing, restaurants, theaters, parks, and powerful magical engineering.

Its danger is often the danger of **complex systems under rival ownership** rather than simple absence of civilization.

## What outsiders notice first

An off-world traveler usually notices three things:

1. the skyline seems to continue forever;
2. magic is embedded in ordinary civic systems rather than confined to wizards;
3. everyone appears to understand institutional signals the outsider cannot read—uniform cuts, insignia placement, document colors, queue etiquette, which alley belongs to whom, which bell means inspection, which bell means fire, and which bell means somebody important is arriving.

A good first session lets the outsider learn those signals through consequences.
