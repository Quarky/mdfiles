# Religion, Death, and Meaning

Ravnica does not have one simple religious structure. Guild philosophy, spirits, community ritual, ancestral practice, personal belief, and public institutions overlap.

## Orzhov faith

The Orzhov turn religion into hierarchy, debt, ritual, and institutional continuity.

Their services can be emotionally real. A mourner can receive genuine comfort in a basilica and still leave owing money.

The Deathless demonstrate that wealth and authority can persist beyond death, which gives Orzhov theology a terrifying material proof: the powerful literally refuse to leave.

## Selesnyan spirituality

Selesnya offers communion with Mat'Selesnya and a lived sense of belonging to a greater whole.

Ritual is communal rather than transactional. Meals, gardens, training, singing, and daily work can all become spiritual practice.

The tension appears when the collective's understanding of harmony conflicts with individual choice.

## Golgari relationship to death

The Golgari refuse to treat death as opposite to life. Decay, necromancy, fungi, insects, and new growth belong to the same cycle.

This does not erase grief. It changes what grief means.

A Golgari funeral may focus less on preserving a body and more on ensuring that what remains re-enters the cycle properly.

## Rakdos and mortality

Rakdos culture looks at the certainty of death and concludes that solemnity is often hypocrisy.

That can produce courage, hedonism, satire, nihilism, art, cruelty, or a refusal to waste the present.

## Boros moral certainty

Boros justice can function like faith even when not formal religion. Angels make transcendent ideals physically visible. Soldiers can believe sacrifice matters because justice is larger than personal survival.

## Secular citizens

Many Ravnicans are practical rather than doctrinal. They attend an Orzhov funeral because family expects it, ask a Selesnyan healer for help, buy Golgari food, and curse every guild equally.

## Spirits and the dead

Because ghosts, undead, necromancy, and magical afterlife effects are real, death creates legal as well as spiritual problems.

Who owns a dead person's property if the person still manifests as a spirit? Does a debt survive? Who controls remains? Can testimony from the dead be admitted? Which guild has jurisdiction?

These are ordinary Ravnican questions with extraordinary mechanics.
