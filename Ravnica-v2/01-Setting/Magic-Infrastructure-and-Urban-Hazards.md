# Magic Infrastructure and Urban Hazards

Magic in Ravnica is not only adventuring magic. It is civic engineering, medicine, law, transport, communication, agriculture, entertainment, and industrial process.

## Izzet infrastructure

The Izzet League builds and maintains major systems while also treating the city as an irresistible research environment. Heating, plumbing, pressure systems, magical power, transport experiments, metallurgy, weather control, and spatial research can all sit uncomfortably close to residential life.

The important tone is **competent volatility**. Izzet systems usually work. The city depends on them. The same culture that keeps them running also tolerates experimentation that would terrify a normal municipal authority.

### What an Izzet utility site feels like

Copper and blue-white light. Mizzium housings. Warning glyphs layered over handwritten modifications. Technicians arguing while repairing something live. Heat through walls. The smell of hot dust and ozone. Pipes vibrating at a frequency residents recognize as either normal or extremely not normal.

## Simic civic biology

The Simic began from public-health concerns and now combine medicine, adaptation, biomancy, environmental engineering, and biological research. Their work can solve real urban problems: disease, pollution, hostile environments, injuries, food resilience.

A Simic answer often asks, "why make the environment safe for the body when the body can be made safe for the environment?" That question can be humane, visionary, unsettling, or all three.

## Selesnyan living infrastructure

Gardens, vernadi, trees, communal spaces, healing, food production, and green corridors are not decorative. They cool streets, provide gathering space, organize neighborhoods, and can become militia positions quickly.

## Golgari metabolic infrastructure

The Golgari treat decay as a resource flow. Waste becomes substrate, food, fungus, soil, labor, and new life. Upper-city residents often disdain the undercity while depending on it materially.

## Common urban magical hazards

- unstable elemental pressure in a conduit;
- misaligned lift enchantment;
- animated legal seal preventing access;
- escaped weird;
- biomantic runoff changing vermin;
- old ward interacting with new construction;
- spirit bound to property terms nobody currently understands;
- Selesnyan roots breaking into older masonry;
- undercity gas ignited by careless magic;
- spatial distortion near an orientation experiment;
- overlapping guild enchantments producing contradictory effects.

## Repair culture

Ravnicans are not amazed when magic breaks. They are irritated. They know which guild to blame, which office to call, which shortcut a technician will take, and how long a permit will delay repairs.

That is a useful tonal difference from high fantasy where magical failure automatically feels mystical. Here, a floating streetlamp flickering may be the equivalent of a bad transformer—until the Izzet apprentice says the current is flowing backward.

## GM escalation ladder

Start with inconvenience, not explosion.

1. hum changes;
2. lights flicker;
3. residents complain;
4. a technician arrives;
5. two guilds disagree about responsibility;
6. system fails dangerously;
7. evidence suggests sabotage, negligence, or something older under the street.

The city becomes believable when catastrophe grows out of ordinary maintenance.
