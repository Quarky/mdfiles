# People, Identity, Class and Belonging

## Species is visible; citizenship is social

Ravnica is cosmopolitan. Humans, elves, goblins, loxodons, minotaurs, vedalken, centaurs, hybrids, and many other beings share streets built over millennia.

Do not treat species as neighborhood decoration. Architecture and etiquette adapt around bodies. A loxodon may need stronger floors and wider seating. A centaur changes how a tavern, stair, alley, or lift works. Goblins know maintenance spaces larger folk ignore. Flying creatures create rooftop access patterns. A Simic hybrid may need a humid room or unusual food. These are ordinary design problems in a city that has accommodated strange bodies for a very long time.

## Guild identity

Guild membership can be occupation, family tradition, religion, political identity, social network, housing system, or all of them. Leaving a guild may therefore resemble changing careers, converting religion, leaving a military, breaking with family, and losing insurance at the same time.

Guildless does not mean cultureless. Many guildless citizens have strong neighborhood identities, professions, family networks, unions, criminal ties, clubs, schools, and political opinions.

## Class

Ravnica has wealth stratification despite advanced civic amenities.

A poor resident can live in sturdy construction with heat and plumbing and still be one missed payment from eviction. A wealthy resident can live above the crowd with private lifts, servants, magical climate control, legal retainers, and enough institutional relationships to make problems disappear.

The useful contrast is not medieval squalor versus magic luxury. It is **dense urban inequality inside a sophisticated city**.

## Reputation

People read insignia, uniform quality, posture, vocabulary, documents, companions, and confidence. A Boros breastplate polished by an armorer signals something different from a dented local garrison kit. An Orzhov functionary carrying no visible guards may be more dangerous than one surrounded by them.

## Belonging is local

Ask an NPC where they are from and they may answer with a neighborhood before a district. Ask who they trust and the answer may be a building superintendent, vernadi healer, street vendor, former squadmate, guild clerk, or cousin—not a guildmaster.

This matters because large institutions are experienced through small relationships.

## Conversation cues by social position

**Clerk:** precise about process; knows which rule is routinely bent.  
**Laborer:** knows what systems actually fail and which supervisors lie.  
**Merchant:** tracks patrols, permits, customer mood, and credit.  
**Student:** overconfident about theory, underexposed to consequences.  
**Veteran:** compares current crisis to three older crises.  
**Guildless tenant:** understands overlapping jurisdictions because they live with them.  
**Wealthy patron:** treats access as a thing that can be purchased indirectly.  
**Courier:** knows routes, timing, door staff, and where nobody asks questions.

## The Ravnican social test

Before an NPC answers the PCs, decide:

- what institution can hurt them;
- what institution can help them;
- what local person they cannot afford to alienate;
- what stereotype they hold about the PCs' apparent guild;
- what practical thing they need today.

That produces setting-specific behavior faster than assigning alignment.
