# Civic Life, Economy, Housing and Transit

## Money is only one layer of obligation

Ravnicans use **zibs and zinos**, but purchasing power also comes from guild access, housing rights, credit, work assignments, sponsorship, permits, favors, ration entitlement, and debt.

A character with coin can still be unable to buy laboratory time, legal access, a military escort, or a safe introduction. Conversely, a broke guild member may eat, sleep, travel, and obtain equipment through institutional support while becoming increasingly obligated to superiors.

## Food

Ravnica eats constantly because Ravnica works constantly.

Street food should be everywhere: breads, filled pastries, skewers, stews, roasted vegetables, fried dough, strong coffee, tea, cheap beer, preserved fruit, and foods specific to species or neighborhood.

The Golgari provide a survival floor for people who cannot afford better. Their contribution is easy for comfortable citizens to joke about until supply is interrupted. Selesnyan cultivation, guildless farms and gardens, trade networks, and specialist growers provide variety beyond subsistence.

A food shortage in Ravnica is therefore political immediately. The question is rarely only "is there food?" but **which route failed, which guild controls the warehouse, who is hoarding, whose permits were suspended, and which neighborhood is considered expendable?**

## Housing

Housing stacks vertically and socially.

- upper floors can mean light, air, status, and exposure to wind;
- lower floors can mean convenience, damp, noise, and proximity to freight;
- basement levels may be older than the building above them;
- wealthy blocks have lifts, attendants, better heat, and private courtyards;
- poor tenements may still have solid masonry and public utilities but suffer crowding, maintenance delays, predatory landlords, and disputed access.

A family can have lived in the same "building" for generations while the street level outside moved upward through reconstruction.

## Work

Guild identity often overlaps with profession but never perfectly. Guildless people do enormous amounts of essential labor. The city contains clerks, cooks, porters, cleaners, teachers, builders, scribes, mechanics, teamsters, nurses, performers, undertakers, messengers, guards, repair crews, lawyers, bookkeepers, printers, brewers, cobblers, animal handlers, lift operators, landlords, students, academics, street vendors, and people doing three jobs badly to keep a room.

When improvising an NPC, give them a **work problem before a faction problem**.

## Transit

Walking remains fundamental, but vertical transit changes the city. Public and private lifts, ramps, broad freight lanes, stairs, bridges, elevated roads, tunnels, and major trade ways produce different traffic cultures.

Centaurs and large-bodied species shape street width and queue design. Flying creatures and mounted aerial patrols create a second traffic layer. Freight beasts need turning space. A single broken lift can force hundreds of residents onto stair routes never designed for that volume.

The Transguild Promenade acts as an artery through the core districts. On major days it becomes procession space; on ordinary days it is commerce, freight, gossip, and neutral encounter territory.

## Leisure

Ravnicans have cafes, restaurants, literature, opera, symphonies, arenas, clubs, illegal fights, religious gatherings, parks, street theater, and Rakdos spectacles. "Urban fantasy" here includes the ability to finish a shift, complain about your supervisor over coffee, attend a performance, and argue about politics on the way home.

## The practical GM rule

For every neighborhood, decide:

- what people eat cheaply;
- where they wash;
- how they get to work;
- who owns most housing;
- what service fails first;
- what people do for fun;
- what residents complain about even when no adventure is happening.
