# Precinct One — Named Places, Street Texture, and Scene Use

Precinct One is the **Guildpact Precinct**, the district's center of wealth, formal diplomacy, public display, finance, and interguild politics. Its defining contrast is that immaculate order depends on an enormous invisible labor force and on political arrangements that are far messier than the avenues look.

## Tenth District Plaza

### Canon anchor
The plaza is a major meeting, recreation, and shopping space maintained with magical cleanliness and heavily watched by Azorius arresters. It functions as neutral ground for guilds and factions.

### What the PCs see
The first impression is **space**. In most of Ravnica the skyline presses inward; here the city deliberately opens. Broad paving creates enough distance to see whole facades. Fountains, trimmed gardens, pale stone, monuments, shaded benches, and carefully placed trees make the plaza feel designed rather than accumulated.

Neutrality has visible infrastructure. Guard posts are positioned for clear sightlines. Carriage drop-offs do not block pedestrian routes. Public notices are mounted in standardized frames. Street vendors are fewer and better dressed than elsewhere because permits and rents select for wealth.

### Sound
Conversation carries because there is less enclosing street noise. Carriage wheels sound crisp on clean paving. Arresters' boots are distinct. Diplomats speak in low voices because they know other diplomats are listening. A distant fountain can be heard between conversations.

### Smell
Clean stone after rain, clipped vegetation, perfume, cafe coffee, carriage animals kept unusually well groomed, faint incense from nearby Orzhov properties.

### What ordinary people do here
- tourists take in architecture;
- government clerks cross between offices;
- diplomatic aides wait for meetings;
- wealthy clients meet advocates;
- couriers hand off sealed packets;
- gardeners and cleaners work constantly but are socially ignored;
- prospective employees wait outside institutions they hope will change their lives.

### Social rule
Drawing a weapon is not merely dangerous; it announces that the person cannot function under shared civic norms. Even rivals who would happily sabotage one another elsewhere have reasons to preserve the plaza.

### Scene pressure
A Gruul representative arrives under negotiated safe passage. A wealthy spectator deliberately provokes them, assuming arresters will intervene if violence starts. The representative knows that retaliating would confirm every stereotype used to justify excluding Gruul from civic space.

The PCs can stop the provocation, defend the representative's dignity, expose the spectator, or watch a diplomatic incident form in real time.

## Chamber of the Guildpact

### Canon anchor
A monumental meeting hall reached from the plaza. The Living Guildpact has an office here but is rarely present; functionaries attempt to manage interguild disputes in his absence.

### What the building means
The Chamber is Ravnica's constitutional contradiction in stone: a place built to serve authority that is theoretically supreme and practically unavailable.

Every waiting room therefore contains two conversations:
1. the dispute people officially came to settle;
2. the question of whether anybody present can actually settle it.

### Street-to-hall transition
Outside: banners, carriage traffic, petitioners, guards.  
Grand stair: conversations become quieter; people sort themselves into delegations.  
Entry hall: insignia, clerks, schedules, waiting benches, messengers.  
Upper levels: less public movement, more aides and functionaries carrying unresolved problems from office to office.

### Sound
Pages turning. Shoes on immense floors. Clerks calling names. Delegates rehearsing language. Echoes from a chamber too large for most meetings held inside it.

### Human texture
A junior functionary may know more about daily interguild disputes than a guildmaster because they see the paperwork from all ten factions.

A cleaner knows which delegations throw documents after meetings.

A cafe worker nearby knows which diplomats stay after formal sessions to negotiate privately.

### Scene pressure
Three guilds petition over the same infrastructure failure. The office cannot issue Living Guildpact judgment. It can only broker a temporary agreement. Every party wants the "temporary" language written so it later becomes precedent.

## Plaza East

### Canon anchor
Government officials live here alongside temporary diplomatic housing; stately residences mix with more modest apartments, and carriage transit to the central plaza is common.

### Street texture
Morning is busiest. Carriages arrive in waves. Servants move luggage. Diplomatic guards learn unfamiliar routes. Clerks leave home carrying document cases and return with more than they started.

The neighborhood looks affluent without being purely luxurious. Apartment buildings are well maintained. Door staff recognize uniforms. Corner cafes specialize in fast breakfasts for people who cannot be late to formal appointments.

### Scene pressure
A visiting delegation loses a sealed case between its residence and carriage. The case is politically sensitive but not dangerous. The local carriage driver remembers a child running into the road, a clerk changing seats, and an Orzhov servant offering to help.

Nobody saw a theft.

## Plaza West

### Canon anchor
Prestigious nightclubs and restaurants cluster here, many run by the Orzhov; permanent residents are exceptionally wealthy.

### Day versus night
By day, Plaza West can feel almost sleepy: deliveries, cleaning crews, cooks, florists, servants, and discreet security preparing for evening.

After dark it becomes a choreography of status. Carriages queue. Door staff recognize regulars. Musicians arrive through service entrances. Guests are judged before they reach the threshold.

### Sound
Soft music through walls, carriage doors, laughter, restaurant kitchens, private arguments that become silent when staff enter.

### Smell
Wine, grilled food, perfume, wax, flowers, polished wood, rain on carriage roofs.

### Social pressure
Admission is a political fact. Being seen entering the wrong club can matter. Being denied entry can matter more.

### Scene pressure
The PCs need to meet someone who refuses a public audience. Their target is dining in an Orzhov-owned restaurant where every staff member is professionally discreet and socially observant.

Getting in is easy with money. Getting in without announcing themselves is harder.

## Plaza South — “Downside”

### Canon anchor
A commercial district along the Transguild Promenade, with specialty shops serving tourists and wealthy customers. It has more permanent residents than the other plaza neighborhoods.

### Why it matters
This is where Precinct One becomes economically comprehensible. Artisans, merchants, service workers, and residents turn elite demand into actual goods.

The wealthy may call it Downside because it is less prestigious. Residents may hear that name as evidence that people who depend on their labor still consider them socially lower.

### Scene pressure
A specialty crafter refuses an Orzhov buyout that would let them retire comfortably because the shop employs two generations of neighborhood families. Their children think the refusal is selfish.

No villain is required.

## Orzhova — Church of Deals

### Canon anchor
The Orzhov guildhall stands on the western edge of Tenth District Plaza. Its cathedral-like architecture houses elite gatherings and the Obzedat; opulent mausoleum spaces lie below.

### What it feels like
The exterior announces that finance, religion, and eternity are one institution.

Inside, visitors should feel **courteously evaluated**. Floors gleam. Clerks speak softly. Knights stand still enough to become part of the architecture. Servants anticipate needs before guests ask.

The mausoleum below changes the emotional temperature. Wealth does not disappear underground; it intensifies. The dead remain socially present.

### Sound
Footsteps softened by rugs, low prayer, coin and pen at distant desks, occasional ghostly speech with no visible speaker.

### Smell
Incense, wax, old stone, perfume, paper, preserved flowers.

### Scene pressure
A poor debtor and a wealthy oligarch wait in the same antechamber. Both are treated politely. Only one can afford the consequences of waiting.

## Vizkopa Bank

### Canon anchor
A monumental Orzhov bank and mint guarded by gargoyles and indebted spirits.

### Read it as infrastructure
Vizkopa is not merely a dungeon with money. Businesses, landlords, guild projects, inheritances, and private fortunes depend on what happens inside.

A rumor that Vizkopa will freeze credit can change markets before any official announcement.

### Scene pressure
The PCs need to stop a fraudulent withdrawal. The fastest way is to freeze an entire account network, which would also block payroll for hundreds of unrelated workers until review is complete.

The bank official is willing to do it—if the PCs sign responsibility for losses.

## Precinct One GM memory line

**Clean stone, expensive quiet, visible order, invisible labor, and every polite interaction carrying institutional weight.**
