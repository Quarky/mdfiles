# Precinct Two — Named Places, Neighborhood Life, and Pressure

Precinct Two is one of the best places to show that Ravnica can be orderly, prosperous, domestic, and corrupt at the same time. Professionals, officials, retired soldiers, working families, small businesses, and criminal influence all coexist beneath the visual authority of New Prahv.

## New Prahv

### Canon anchor
The Azorius guildhall is the tallest structure in the Tenth District and is formed around three enormous towers corresponding to the Senate's three Columns.

### What the skyline does
New Prahv is orientation as much as architecture. Residents know where they are by which side of the towers they can see. Shadows move across neighborhoods as the day changes.

From far away, the three-column form makes Azorius government look unified.

Inside, the three branches argue constantly.

### Street approach
The closer the PCs get, the more everyday business becomes procedural. Messenger lanes appear. Notice boards multiply. Uniforms become common. Food sellers specialize in quick meals for officials. Copyists, advocates, witnesses, petitioners, and people carrying bundles of evidence crowd approaches.

### Sound
Bells, carriage wheels, clerks reading names, pages running messages, doors closing, legal argument escaping through open windows.

### Smell
Paper, ink, rain, stone, coffee, dust from old files, leather document cases.

### Scene pressure
A witness needs to reach a hearing before the filing window closes. A local protection crew blocks the quickest street but will not cause violence within sight of New Prahv.

The conflict is about time, intimidation, and who is willing to make the threat public.

## Whitestone

### Canon anchor
An orderly northern neighborhood of pale houses developed to house people working in New Prahv. Earlier residents were displaced during its construction, many toward denser Griffin Heights.

### Surface impression
Whitestone appears to prove that planning works: aligned facades, maintained walks, tidy drains, predictable services.

The deeper story is displacement. Older residents remember what stood here before. Newer residents may not know.

### Sights
Alabaster rows, trimmed stoops, identical drainage channels, small family gardens, official workers walking the same morning routes.

### Social texture
Neighbors care about reputation. A resident can resent the Senate and still call an arrester when someone parks a freight cart illegally.

### Scene pressure
A century-old compensation claim resurfaces because a newly discovered property marker suggests the original relocation survey was wrong.

The present homeowners did nothing wrong. The displaced family's descendants are also right.

## Griffin Heights

### Canon anchor
A southern neighborhood with modest houses, corruption, and strong Orzhov influence. Residents may pay protection to Orzhov knights and develop genuine loyalty to them.

### Do not play it as “the bad neighborhood”
People live here because it is home.

There are bakeries, children, retired soldiers, mechanics, clerks, small gardens, neighborhood arguments, and people who know which Orzhov collector is cruel and which one quietly gives extensions.

Corruption works because it is relational.

### Sound
Doors open onto conversation. Children in streets. shop bells. neighborhood gossip. distant station horns.

### Scene pressure
Boros plans a raid on an Orzhov protection crew. Residents oppose the crew's extortion but fear what happens when the people who currently deter burglary vanish overnight.

The PCs can help build a replacement before the raid or dismiss the concern and learn why residents were afraid.

## Augustin Station

### Canon anchor
The Tenth District's major airship station sits on the western end of Griffin Heights. Numerous platforms handle balloons, gondolas, and enormous Simic-bred flying creatures; traffic continues around the clock.

### First impression
This is **Ravnica in motion above the streets**.

Passengers drag luggage. Freight crews shout weights. Handlers soothe giant creatures. Porters compete for work. Announcements overlap. Families embrace at arrivals while business travelers push around them.

### Visual field
Platforms project into open air. Rigging lines sway. Balloon envelopes loom overhead. Creatures large enough to carry compartments shift their weight against restraints. Below, the city drops away in layers.

### Sound
Wind, ropes, bells, animal calls, platform numbers, arguments over baggage, warning horns, distant machinery.

### Smell
Oil, animal musk, rain, hot food, rope fiber, ozone from nearby Izzet work.

### Ordinary workers
- platform dispatcher;
- beast handler;
- baggage porter;
- ticket clerk;
- maintenance technician;
- street vendor who knows every delayed flight;
- unofficial guide who recognizes lost visitors instantly.

### Why it is politically useful
Stations create plausible contact between every guild, outsiders, smugglers, couriers, officials, and ordinary travelers.

Security must stay functional without making travel impossible.

### Scene pressure
An incoming passenger is wanted by Azorius. Boros has been told they are dangerous. The station master refuses to halt all traffic without a written order because a shutdown will strand hundreds of passengers and live cargo.

Meanwhile the person may already be on a connecting platform.

## Statue of Agrus Kos and neighborhood memory

A public statue or memorial is not just scenery. Boros veterans leave small tokens. Students sit nearby. Tourists ask who the figure was. Someone uses the base as a meeting point because everyone knows it.

When historical figures remain in public space, they shape how current institutions justify themselves.

## Precinct Two GM memory line

**Serviceable homes, civic pride, long commutes, old favors, protection money, and the shadow of law literally falling across the neighborhood.**
