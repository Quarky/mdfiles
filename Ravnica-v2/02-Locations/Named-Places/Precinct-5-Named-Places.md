# Precinct Five — Named Places, Knowledge Economy, and Intellectual Rivalry

Precinct Five makes knowledge visible as labor, status, commodity, and crime. Universities, libraries, laboratories, apartments, pubs, magical suppliers, information brokers, and students are packed together with less urban planning than outsiders expect.

## Zonot Seven and Zameck

### Canon anchor
Zonot Seven is the only Simic zonot within the city proper. It contains Zameck, the guildhall. A major chamber close below street level serves the Speakers and visitors; canals connect outward, including toward Precinct Six.

### Entering the zonot
Street-level cues begin subtly: more waterproof clothing, drainage channels, carts carrying sealed tanks, humid air rising from grates.

Then the city opens downward.

The zonot should feel enormous because the old city layers have collapsed into a vertical aquatic habitat. Balconies, bridges, laboratories, pools, pens, homes, and meeting chambers line the descent.

### Sound
Water on stone, pumps, voices echoing from lower walkways, splashes too large to identify, glass being moved, occasional creature calls.

### Smell
Brine, wet rock, disinfectant, algae, animal feed, medicinal herbs.

### Public versus restricted areas
Zameck's visitor spaces are designed for land-dwellers and diplomacy. Deeper facilities assume Simic bodies, specialized equipment, and tolerance for environments outsiders find uncomfortable.

That transition itself communicates culture.

### Scene pressure
An Azorius inspection team has authority to enter a lab but lacks equipment safe for the depth. Simic offers guides. The lead inspector suspects the guides are choosing routes that hide other violations.

Both sides smile politely.

## The Blistercoils

### Canon anchor
Gigantic Izzet-operated waterwheels near Zonot Seven generate magical energy for public works, Simic facilities, and Izzet workshops. Excess power is stored below.

### Civic significance
This is an excellent illustration of Ravnican interdependence: an Izzet machine physically adjacent to Simic habitat helps power both public and guild systems.

Residents complain about noise and still panic when the wheels slow.

### Sights
Immense turning structures throwing spray, energy channels, maintenance gantries, workers in protective gear, smaller workshops clustered nearby.

### Sounds
Water thunder, gear rotation, electrical hum, shouted maintenance calls.

### Sensation
Fine spray settles on skin. Railings vibrate. Hair may rise slightly from stored charge.

### Scene pressure
One wheel develops an imbalance. Shutting the array down safely cuts power to several labs and a nearby neighborhood. Keeping it running risks damage to underground storage.

The correct engineering answer and the correct civic answer may differ.

## Hightower

### Canon anchor
A western neighborhood dense with educational institutions, from broad universities to specialized schools.

### Daily rhythm
Morning belongs to students racing to lectures. Afternoon belongs to cafes, tutors, demonstrations, and arguments. Night belongs to libraries, pubs, experiments, poetry, and people insisting they are still working.

### Status culture
People display worth through ideas, accomplishment, and association.

Academic gossip is political currency:
- who published first;
- who stole an idea;
- whose patron withdrew funding;
- which professor is quietly recruiting for a guild;
- which student built something dangerous and brilliant.

### Scene pressure
A professor publicly accuses a student of theft. The student actually received the idea from a Dimir source who stole it from somebody else entirely.

## Prism University

### Canon anchor
A university focused on broad magical theory rather than a single guild's practical specialization. It maintains formal neutrality while multiple guilds recruit and infiltrate it.

### What neutrality looks like
No single guild uniform dominates. Lecture halls host competing approaches. Student groups form around philosophies. Recruiters sponsor events without officially recruiting.

The institution survives by being useful to everyone.

### Visual identity
Crystal-paned spaces, lecture diagrams, demonstration rooms warded against accidents, courtyards where spell practice is socially normal.

### Scene pressure
A neutral faculty committee is deciding whether to accept an Izzet research grant. Simic objects to the experimental terms. Orzhov offers matching funds with different strings. The students mainly want the equipment.

## Ismeri Library

### Canon anchor
One of Ravnica's great libraries, open continuously and secretly important to Dimir communication.

### Public life
At any hour someone is reading, copying, sleeping over a book, arguing with a librarian, looking for a reference, or trying to seem as though they belong.

Night is quieter but not empty.

### Dimir layer
The library is powerful because coded communication hides inside legitimate research behavior. A person checking four unrelated texts might be a student—or assembling fragments of an instruction.

The safest Dimir activity looks like scholarship.

### Smell and sound
Paper, dust, leather, ink, old wood, tea. Pages, chairs, whispered questions, ladders, occasional magical ward tone.

### Scene pressure
The PCs know a coded message is hidden somewhere in the library. Removing every suspect book would expose the method and disrupt public access. Reading it in place means behaving like the agent expected to retrieve it.

## Information brokers

Knowledge is a market here. Private investigators, rumormongers, booksellers, academics, spies, and specialists sell different levels of certainty.

The GM should distinguish:
- fact;
- interpretation;
- rumor;
- privileged access;
- dangerous knowledge;
- evidence someone else knows you asked.

## Precinct Five GM memory line

**Crowded minds in crowded buildings, arguments as status displays, knowledge sold by the page or secret, and every quiet library aisle potentially part of a guild war.**
