# Scene Hydration Checklist

Before a scene is table-ready, fill these fields. This is intentionally stricter than the older guide.

## Physical place

- What is above the PCs?
- What is below them?
- What older layer of the city is visible?
- What piece of infrastructure is functioning in the background?
- What does the street/building do when no adventurers are present?

## Sensory layer

Provide at least:

- 3 visible details;
- 2 simultaneous sounds;
- 1 smell;
- 1 tactile or temperature detail;
- 1 sign of crowd movement.

Avoid decorative description that does not affect play. A lift bell matters because somebody is arriving. Warm pavement matters because the heating main runs beneath it.

## Ordinary people

Name:

- a worker;
- a resident/customer/patient/petitioner;
- someone with informal local influence.

Each needs a current practical goal unrelated to the main quest.

## Guild overlap

At least two guilds should touch most civic scenes indirectly even if only one is physically present.

Example: the Simic clinic is financed by Orzhov credit, inspected by Azorius, connected to Izzet water, and receives low-cost Golgari reagents.

## Stakes

Write all three:

- **personal:** who is hurt?
- **material:** what service/property/resource is at risk?
- **institutional:** which guild gains or loses authority?

## PC consequence

For each likely PC approach, decide what changes socially.

- comply;
- threaten;
- bribe;
- expose;
- fight;
- help;
- walk away.

Winning initiative is not a complete consequence model.

## Five-minute continuity

If PCs leave and return five minutes later, what changed?

Someone called a supervisor. The crowd grew. A worker fixed half the problem. A rumor started. A door was sealed. The patient worsened.

## One-day continuity

If PCs return tomorrow, who remembers them and how?

That answer is the living world.
