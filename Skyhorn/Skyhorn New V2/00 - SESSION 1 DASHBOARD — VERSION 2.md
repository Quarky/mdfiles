# 00 - SESSION 1 DASHBOARD — VERSION 2

## What This Is

Run Session 1 from this file as a live table reference. Gundbarg is already under strain when the characters arrive: crews are stranded, workers are losing pay, merchants are sitting on cargo, and families are waiting for ships that should have returned. The town keeps moving anyway.

The Level 5 pregenerated characters and their personal hooks are assumed. PC and core adventure NPC stat blocks stay in Foundry, so this guide concentrates on decisions, conversations, scene pressure, consequences, and what changes around the party while they act.

## Where Session 1 Should End

**Recommended endpoint:** The party reaches Skyhorn Lighthouse after the attack on the *Annabel Lee*, secures the landing area, sees the damaged lighthouse above them, and ends on the choice to ascend.

This gives Session 1 a complete dramatic arc:

1. Gundbarg is hurting.
2. The party learns why people are afraid.
3. The party chooses to get involved.
4. A local conflict tests how they deal with people before monsters.
5. They leave the safety of town.
6. The sea itself appears to attack them.
7. They arrive at the isolated lighthouse with proof that the danger is real.

## Session 1 Core Truths

These are the facts behind the rumors. Keep them straight even when NPCs don't know them.

- Skyhorn Lighthouse—called the **Eye of the Storm** by locals—is functioning poorly, not fully dark.
- The harbor is slowing toward a standstill because captains are afraid to sail near the lighthouse.
- The merchant vessel **Jade Lion** is overdue and was pulled toward the lighthouse by an unnatural wave.
- **Benji Frogwater** wants his son **Raf** and the crew back alive. He's willing to pay capable people to act.
- **Captain Sheila Coppertree** is willing to sail toward Skyhorn and is personally concerned for **Captain Aryn Hest**.
- **Lucien Skyhorn** is still alive at the lighthouse when the adventure begins.
- The "sea monster" is not a natural beast. Water elementals are being weaponized against ships.
- Eelfolk are responsible for the attack on the lighthouse and the use of the bowl below it.
- **Gaspar and Neeno are not connected to the eelfolk plot.** They're opportunistic local trouble on the way to the real threat.

### The Smugglers (GM Background)

**Optional expansion, not part of the published adventure:** a smuggling operation has been running supplies toward the eelfolk without understanding the full operation. These smugglers aren't true believers in anything—they're profiteers who found a buyer and stopped asking questions. But now their most recent shipment has gone missing. The contact didn't show. The drop point was empty. And one of their ships—the *Slippery Eel*—has sailed toward Skyhorn to find out what happened.

The smugglers are nervous. They don't know what's really on the island. They don't know about the bowl or the prisoners or the full eelfolk operation. They just know their money stopped flowing and someone needs to answer for it.

This is background texture for Session 1. The *Slippery Eel* may already be anchored on the far side of Skyhorn Island when the party arrives. If the players investigate the dock or the waters around the lighthouse carefully, they might spot signs of a second vessel. Don't call attention to it—let it be a detail they discover or miss.

## Northern Canon Decision

The consolidated source notes contain an older Leilon/Barge Yard adaptation alongside the later northern material. For this campaign:

- **Use Gundbarg.**
- Use the dark finger-fjords, grey cliffs, sleet, pine smoke, smoked fish, longships, Cracked Cask, Widow's Shroud, Jarl's watchtower bell, and northern harbor culture.
- Ignore Leilon-specific shallow mud flats, barge-yard logistics, and Leilon council references unless you deliberately reuse a detail under a northern name.

## Session 1 Safety Valve

If the group stalls, don't add more mystery. The adventure already has enough uncertainty. Move information toward them:

- Benji approaches them.
- A dockworker mentions Sheila is the only captain still willing to sail.
- Lucien's speaking-gull message reaches the Cracked Cask.
- A watch bell rings while the lighthouse beam visibly stutters.
- Sheila enters the tavern long enough to say, "If any of you are actually going to help, meet me at the *Annabel Lee* before the tide turns."

When play stalls, move one reliable fact toward the party. The decision should be what to do about the crisis, not whether they can locate the plot.
