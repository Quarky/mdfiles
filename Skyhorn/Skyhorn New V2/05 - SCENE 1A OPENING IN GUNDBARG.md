# 05 - SCENE 1A: OPENING IN GUNDBARG

## Scene Goal

Establish that Gundbarg is a living place under pressure, not yet a dungeon entrance. Give the players enough sensory information and human stakes that their chosen hooks matter before Benji speaks.

## Foundry

Activate **Gundbarg — Fjord Harbor** first if you want a wide establishing shot, then move to **The Cracked Cask — Tavern** once the PCs enter.

## Opening Structure

Use three quick beats:

1. The harbor still works.
2. Something is wrong offshore.
3. The PCs see how the problem reaches ordinary people.

### READ ALOUD — Harbor Establishing Shot

> Sleet rattles off Gundbarg's roofs and runs in cold lines between the dock planks. The harbor is crowded, but too many sails are tied down. Dockhands shift cargo that has nowhere to go while captains argue under awnings.
>
> When the fog opens, the beam from Skyhorn Lighthouse reaches across the water. It is there, but weak—and the timing is wrong.

## Immediate Ambient Events

Use one or two, not all at once.

### A Crate Argument

A southern merchant insists his cargo must be moved under cover first because it's "worth more than fish." A local dockworker responds, "Then ask your cargo to lift itself."

**Why it matters:** Dan, Cassian, Rurik, Sarduuk, Mara.

### The Missed Bell

Two sailors stop mid-conversation because the lighthouse beam should have swept across the harbor by now. A few seconds later it does.

**Why it matters:** Tark, Borrik, Aelar, Lethiel.

### A Child Counting Ships

A child points to vessels and asks an adult, "Which one is the *Jade Lion*?" The adult answers, "Not here."

**Why it matters:** Valemon, Mara, Elira, Cadwallon.

### The Silent Gulls

A cluster of gulls fights over fish scraps near shore. None fly toward open water.

**Why it matters:** Lethiel, Nymera, Kreslin.

### A Smuggler's Worry (Optional)

A dockworker mentions that the *Slippery Eel* left two days ago and hasn't come back. "Odd cargo outfit. Never unloads in daylight. Captain's a nervous little man named Fennick." No one pays much attention—just another ship gone quiet near Skyhorn.

**Why it matters:** Nymera, Cassian, anyone investigating unusual ship movements.

## Expected Player Actions and Reactions

### If PCs Immediately Ask About the Lighthouse

Locals provide overlapping but incomplete answers:

- "The beam started acting wrong before the harbor stopped."
- "Ships started refusing the route after the *Jade Lion* vanished."
- "Ask Sheila if you want the truth. Ask any drunk if you want a monster."
- "Lucien Skyhorn keeps that light. If he's dead, nobody has proved it."

### If PCs Immediately Head for the Docks

Let them. Don't force the tavern first.

On the way, a runner or dockworker can mention Benji Frogwater is paying for information about the *Jade Lion*. Sheila can later say, "You should speak to the halfling before we go. His boy is on that ship."

This preserves Benji without railroading.

### If PCs Try to Investigate Officials

They learn the authorities have insufficient evidence to send a full force into dangerous waters. The watch is focused on preventing harbor violence and keeping essential local traffic moving.

**Don't make officials cartoonishly cowardly.** Their hesitation should feel bureaucratic and risk-averse, not evil.

### If PCs Ask for Supplies

Basic travel and cold-weather gear is available. Shopping only matters if the party spends enough time on it for the harbor situation to advance.

If they spend a long time shopping, advance the Harbor Confidence clock and have news arrive that another captain has cancelled departure.

### If PCs Split Up

Use parallel cuts:

- One group gathers rumors at the Cracked Cask.
- One inspects the harbor/light.
- One finds Sheila.
- One checks manifests or dock records.

Bring them back together before the Gaspar/Neeno confrontation unless the players deliberately avoid one another.
