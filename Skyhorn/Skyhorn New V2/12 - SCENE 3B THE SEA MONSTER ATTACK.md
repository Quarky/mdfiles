# 12 - SCENE 3B: THE SEA MONSTER ATTACK

## Scene Goal

Deliver hard proof that the harbor crisis is real and magical. The encounter should feel like a moving emergency on a ship, not a stationary hit-point exchange.

## GM Truth

The "sea monster" here is a **water elemental** compelled to attack approaching ships.

The party doesn't automatically know that.

## Foundry

Remain on **The Annabel Lee** and open the Water Elemental actor from Foundry when needed.

No stat block is repeated here.

## Detection

Characters with sufficient passive awareness or who actively prepared may see the attack coming.

Describe:

> A ridge of water is moving toward the ship.
>
> Not with the wind.
>
> Not with the tide.
>
> Toward the ship.

If someone actively watches the water and succeeds on the scene's Perception check, the whole party has more time to brace.

## Initial Impact

### READ ALOUD

> The wave reaches the *Annabel Lee* and rises.
>
> For one impossible second it stands taller than the rail, holding its shape against gravity.
>
> Then something like shoulders form inside it.
>
> The sea hits the hull with the sound of a door being kicked open by a giant.
>
> Timber cracks. The deck lurches sideways. Free water explodes across the planks—and part of that water doesn't fall.
>
> It climbs.

Resolve the opening saving throw / prone effect as established in the adventure.

## Sheila's Immediate Reaction

She doesn't freeze.

> "Brace! Brace! BRACE!"

After impact:

> "Dock's ahead! I need three rounds—keep it off my ship!"

This creates the combat clock.

## The Three-Round Ship Clock

### Round 1 — Impact

The crew is disorganized. Loose gear shifts. The elemental tries to seize people near the rail.

**GM focus:** Confusion and immediate rescue.

### Round 2 — Control

Sheila has the ship aimed at the dock. Crew can respond to orders. The elemental keeps pace.

**GM focus:** Choices between offense, rescue, and protecting the helm/rigging.

### Round 3 — Landing Run

The dock is close. Everyone can see slick pilings and cliff stairs beyond.

**GM focus:** Finish the threat or survive contact long enough to land.

If the elemental remains active, it can continue threatening creatures near the dock from the water.

## Dynamic Ship Complications

Use at most one per round if needed.

### Snapped Line

A rope whips loose across the deck. Someone must move or secure it before it tangles crew.

### Sliding Cargo

A crate breaks free and slams toward the rail.

### Crew Overboard

Use only if the party has tools to respond. A crew member clings to line or rail rather than vanishing beneath water immediately.

### Cracked Railing

Creates a dangerous gap but also a clear line for attacks.

### Flooded Footing

Water across the deck makes movement tense; describe it without adding ad hoc penalties unless mechanics support it.

## Expected Player Actions

### Attack the Elemental Directly

Good. Make attacks feel like striking moving pressure and collapsing water.

### Identify It Magically

On success, confirm it's an elemental creature and appears compelled or directed. Don't reveal the exact summoning source.

### Protect the Crew

This matters socially. Sheila remembers it.

### Protect the Helm

If the elemental threatens Sheila, defenders buying her space directly protect the ship's ability to land.

### Use Ropes

Prepared ropes can make overboard rescues automatic or easier rather than requiring improvisation.

### Swing From Rigging

Reward cinematic use. The notes explicitly support acrobatic movement using rigging.

### Dive After Someone

Allow it, but describe the danger of cold water and hostile elemental clearly before the choice.

### Try to Communicate With the Elemental

The elemental is compelled and not behaving as an independent negotiator. Communication may reveal distress/hostility but shouldn't become an easy diplomacy bypass unless a spell specifically supports control.

### Try to Flee Away From Skyhorn

Sheila can attempt it, but the elemental's purpose is to drive off or destroy approaching ships. The party must decide whether escape is worth abandoning the rescue mission.

### Teleport/Fly Directly to Lighthouse

Characters at this level may have limited mobility tricks. Let clever movement work for individuals, but Sheila and crew remain in danger. This creates a genuine choice rather than invalidating the encounter.

## Sheila Combat Dialogue

Use sparingly so she doesn't become a commentator.

- "I need the wheel clear!"
- "Line! Throw a line!"
- "Stay off the starboard rail!"
- "That's it—keep it busy!"
- "Dock in sight!"
- "If you knock it off the ship, I'll buy the drinks!"

## After the Elemental Falls

The elemental collapses into ordinary water.

The **six Beads of Force** remain as treasure per the source notes.

Don't explain why they were present. Let that become an odd detail.

### Sheila After Combat

First she checks ship and crew. Only then does she congratulate the party.

> "Count heads."

Wait.

> "Again."

After everyone is accounted for:

> "Good. Now we can be impressed with ourselves."

If PCs protected crew:

> "You remembered this is a ship, not a battlefield. I noticed."

If they endangered crew recklessly:

> "Next time you throw magic across my deck, look behind the thing you hate."

## If the Elemental Is Not Defeated Before Landing

The ship reaches the dock damaged and under pressure.

Use the dock and pilings as new terrain. The elemental can't simply walk up the cliff like a humanoid attacker; it remains a threat from the water.

This can create a strong cliffhanger if session time is running out.

## If a PC Goes Down

Don't soften normal rules automatically, but remember the scene is full of rescuers, ropes, and allies. Sheila's crew tries to save people when doing so doesn't require them to outfight the monster.
