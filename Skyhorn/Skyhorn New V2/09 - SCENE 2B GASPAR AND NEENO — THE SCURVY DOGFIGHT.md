# 09 - SCENE 2B: GASPAR AND NEENO — THE SCURVY DOGFIGHT

## Scene Goal

Give the party a public social conflict with multiple valid solutions. This isn't filler. It establishes how they use power when frightened people are watching.

## GM Truth

Sheila beat Gaspar and Neeno in a fair dice game. They are now calling the dice weighted because they want a public excuse to take the *Annabel Lee*. The accusation is a pretext; the attempted seizure is the real conflict.

Gaspar is loud and insecure. Neeno is quiet and calculating.

Neeno plans to remove Gaspar eventually and take control for himself.

Four thugs are positioned nearby as backup if the encounter becomes violent.

## Entrance

### READ ALOUD

> Two men step from behind a stack of cargo as though they've been waiting for the exact moment the gangplank became crowded.
>
> The first is tall and hunched, a scar crossing his nose, one hand replaced by a crude hook. He spits between the dock planks before speaking.
>
> The second is a half-orc in a black leather coat polished far too carefully for this weather. His hair is slicked back. He studies one fingernail with a silver pick while the first man talks.

Gaspar points toward Sheila.

> "Ye owe us, Sheila. Ship'll do."

Neeno doesn't raise his voice.

> "We prefer a quiet settlement."

Sheila pockets her dice.

> "Funny. So do I."

## Conflict Score

**Starting score:** 3.

- **0:** Gaspar/Neeno back down, accept a face-saving exit, or fracture enough to withdraw.
- **5:** Combat begins.

### Standard Movement

- Large bribe: -1.
- Successful DC 15 Persuasion or Deception: -1.
- Failed attempt: +1.
- Straight Intimidation usually: +1 because pride and witnesses make them resist.
- DC 15 Insight: Reveal a useful pressure point such as Neeno's betrayal plan, Gaspar's disgrace, or current conflict trajectory.

### Use Judgment

Don't make players roll if they produce decisive leverage and the NPC response is obvious. A clever reveal of Neeno's plan to Gaspar can shift the encounter dramatically.

## Public Stakes

The dock isn't empty.

Witnesses include:

- Sheila's crew.
- Dockworkers.
- Stranded sailors.
- Possibly harbor watch at a distance.
- Merchants protecting cargo.
- Any minor NPC the players befriended earlier.

This means area magic, threats, humiliation, and mercy all have social consequences.

## Gaspar — Conversation Responses

### "What Does She Owe You?"

> "Dice game. Weighted. Cheated us blind."

If asked for proof, his story becomes less precise.

### "How Much?"

He gives a number or says "enough for the ship," exposing that the demand is disproportionate.

### "Why the Ship?"

> "Debt's debt."

He spits.

This is a strong opening for Cassian or Dan.

### If Called a Thief

> "Careful, landlubber."

Conflict tends upward unless the speaker has already established dominance or public support.

### If Offered a Face-Saving Exit

He wants one but can't look eager.

> "Fine. We'll settle it later."

He needs to believe the crowd sees this as postponement rather than surrender.

### If Confronted With His Fallen Status

His temper spikes. Neeno becomes visibly annoyed because uncontrolled anger damages their leverage.

## Neeno — Conversation Responses

### "What Do You Actually Want?"

> "The debt settled."

A pause.

> "And nobody hurt over something simple."

He says this while being the more dangerous of the pair.

### "Would Coin Settle It?"

> "Coin settles many things."

He considers real offers.

### If a PC Notices He's Watching Gaspar

Neeno's eyes return to the speaker immediately.

> "You watch people closely."

### If Accused of Planning to Kill Gaspar

If done publicly and convincingly, Gaspar whirls on him.

Neeno doesn't panic.

> "You're taking a stranger's word now?"

This fractures their unity and can reduce conflict sharply.

### If Offered a Private Future Deal

Neeno listens. He may withdraw now if he thinks survival yields a better future opportunity.

## Sheila During the Conflict

Sheila doesn't need rescuing emotionally. She lets the party act because the confrontation can delay departure and because she wants to see how they handle pressure.

### If PCs Ask Whether She Cheated

She flips one black die onto the back of her hand.

> "No. They lost. They decided losing sounded better if I was a cheat."

Sheila has no interest in re-litigating the game. She cares that two armed men are trying to turn a gambling loss into ownership of her ship.

### If PCs Escalate Recklessly

> "Easy. I need the pier intact after you're done proving yourselves."

### If PCs Resolve It Peacefully

She's impressed.

> "Good. Anyone can make a dock louder."

## Expected Player Approaches

### Persuasion

Appeal to cost, public embarrassment, bad odds, or the fact that Sheila is leaving dangerous waters anyway.

**NPC reaction:** Neeno is receptive to rational exits; Gaspar needs pride managed.

### Deception

Possible lies:

- Harbor watch is already coming in force.
- Sheila's ship is damaged/useless.
- The party represents a powerful patron.
- Neeno has already betrayed Gaspar.

Use normal consequences if lies are later exposed.

### Bribery

Works, but locals notice who paid whom. Sarduuk may treat this as purchased time; Dan/Rurik may dislike rewarding bullying.

### Intimidation

Raw threats tend to escalate because the confrontation is public. Physical demonstrations can rattle Gaspar, but Neeno will calculate whether the threat is real.

### Expose Neeno's Betrayal

This is the cleanest "clever" route.

Gaspar turns on Neeno verbally first. Neeno tries to keep control. If the PCs step back and let them fracture, the pirates may withdraw separately.

### Challenge to Duel

Cassian/Dan may offer a controlled one-on-one alternative.

This isn't in the source mechanics but fits the living world. If used, make the stakes clear before accepting. Gaspar is more likely to accept than Neeno. Neeno may sabotage or refuse unless the deal benefits him.

### Arrest Them

The party has no automatic official authority. The harbor watch will support containment if clear crimes occur but doesn't instantly deputize adventurers.

### Attack Immediately

Combat begins. Have witnesses scatter. Use Foundry actors. Keep civilian positioning meaningful.

## If Combat Begins

Don't repeat stat blocks here.

### Foundry

Open Gaspar, Neeno, and thugs from the Actor directory.

### Tactical Story Goals

- Gaspar tries to shove opponents into water and focuses on Sheila or visibly powerful PCs.
- Neeno stays mobile, uses cover, and preserves his life.
- Backup thugs join once the fight is established.
- Enemies flee or surrender when clearly beaten; they're not suicidal.

### Environmental Details to Use

- Wet planks.
- Mooring lines.
- Cargo crates for cover.
- Narrow gaps between ship and dock.
- Civilians retreating.
- Hanging nets.
- Gangplank.
- Cold black water below.

### Civilian Response

If PCs use dangerous area effects near workers, locals react even if tactically effective. This is a living harbor, not an empty arena.

## Aftermath Branches

### Peaceful Resolution

Dockworkers resume breathing. One sailor gives an approving nod. Sheila says:

> "Good. Let's spend our violence where it earns something."

Harbor Confidence improves slightly.

### Pirates Flee Humiliated

Gaspar leaves loudly promising future trouble. Neeno leaves quietly and remembers faces.

### Pirates Arrested or Restrained

The harbor watch takes custody if the PCs can articulate the offense. The watch is irritated if the party expects them to adjudicate an entire gambling history on the dock.

### Pirates Killed

The mood is grim, not celebratory. If combat was clearly self-defense, the watch doesn't treat PCs as murderers, but public killing changes first impressions.

### Sheila Is Injured

She can still sail if the injury is minor and Foundry mechanics allow; otherwise the party may need to stabilize the situation before departure. Don't strand the adventure because of an unlucky pre-voyage exchange.
