# 17 - DERAILMENT AND CONTINGENCY TOOLKIT

## If the PCs Refuse the Adventure

Don't force them magically.

Escalate the world naturally:

1. Benji tries another appeal.
2. Lucien's gull message confirms active attack.
3. Sheila states she's going because Aryn is missing.
4. Harbor consequences become visible.

If they still refuse, the adventure ends or changes direction. Agency includes walking away.

## If the PCs Want Their Own Ship

Let them investigate that option. Most captains refuse Skyhorn waters. Hiring a different ship should cost more and produce a less capable/less personally invested crew.

If they succeed, Sheila can become an allied parallel captain or remain in Gundbarg. Don't invalidate their plan simply because the printed adventure expects her ship.

## If the PCs Want to Walk/Fly/Teleport to Skyhorn

The island is offshore. At level 5, full-party magical bypass is difficult but creative spells may help individual scouting.

Let scouting reveal exterior information without removing the need to deal with distance, sea, and survivors.

## If They Attack Gaspar and Neeno Before Talking

Run combat. Bystanders scatter. Sheila is irritated at the lost time unless the pirates clearly presented immediate lethal danger.

This changes reputation but doesn't block departure.

## If They Befriend Gaspar or Neeno

Possible, especially with Neeno. They remain self-interested criminals unless events change them over time.

Don't reveal Neeno's betrayal plan automatically just because he's friendly.

## If They Recruit Gaspar/Neeno for the Voyage

Sheila refuses to put people who just tried to seize her ship into trusted crew roles.

A very persuasive party might convince her to allow them as watched passengers, but this creates extra tension and isn't recommended unless the players genuinely enjoy it.

## If They Want the Watch to Handle the Pirates

The watch can intervene if summoned, but reaching the point of formal intervention takes time. Neeno may withdraw rather than wait.

This is a valid de-escalation path.

## If They Spend Hours Researching the Lighthouse

Give useful context, then summarize diminishing returns.

They can't research the current eelfolk occupation from town because the town doesn't know it.

Advance weather and harbor clocks; Sheila eventually announces departure window.

## If They Scry or Send Magical Messages

Honor spell capabilities.

If they can contact Lucien directly, he confirms the lighthouse is under attack by sea-creatures and needs help. He's under pressure and can't deliver the entire later exposition safely.

If they contact Aryn/Raf through appropriate magic, respond according to their current captive/hiding state without revealing more than they can reasonably say.

## If the Party Destroys the Elemental Immediately

Good. Don't artificially prolong it. The three-round approach can still continue as a tense ship-control sequence or be compressed if everyone is safe.

## If the Party Cannot Defeat the Elemental

Possible outcomes:

- Sheila reaches the dock while it remains active.
- Party escapes onto dock while it threatens from water.
- Ship is forced to retreat.
- Creative control/escape tactics work.

Avoid a total campaign stop unless the group accepts the consequences of a full retreat.

## If the Annabel Lee Is Badly Damaged

Sheila stays behind to stabilize it. This strengthens rather than weakens the reason she doesn't accompany the lighthouse assault.

The party may worry about return transport; good. It adds urgency without changing the dungeon.

## If a PC Wants a Short Rest Before the Lighthouse

Allow the decision and make the tradeoff clear:

- Ship needs inspection anyway.
- Unknown people may be in danger above.
- The lighthouse remains active for now.
- Waiting isn't automatically wrong, but time may matter.

Don't invent an arbitrary instant failure solely to punish resting.

## If the PCs Investigate the Slippery Eel

If they spotted the smuggler ship and want to investigate:

- The *Slippery Eel* is anchored in a hidden cove on the far side of the island.
- Fennick and two crew are ashore, looking for their missing shipment contact.
- They are armed and nervous. They would rather talk their way out than start a fight, and none of them lies especially well under pressure.
- They don't know about the eelfolk, the bowl, or the prisoners. They just know their buyer stopped paying.
- If pressed hard, they might reveal they were delivering "special cargo" to someone on the island. They don't know who or what.
- This is a thread for Session 2+ if the players pull on it.
