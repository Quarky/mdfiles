# Scene 3: Sea Monster Attacks

## Immersive Area Description

The *Annabel Lee* cuts through the ocean with a swift, bold path. Her sails are full, and the crew moves with practiced efficiency. [Captain Sheila Coppertree](#captain-sheila-coppertree-cg-half-elf-spy) stands at the helm, one hand on the wheel, the other shielding her eyes against the spray.

**The Voyage:** Three hours at sea. The coastline fades behind the party, replaced by open water. The fog thins, then thickens again. The lighthouse grows larger on the horizon — a dark silhouette against a grey sky.

**The Lighthouse Island:** Steep, rocky, 100 feet in diameter. A 40-foot wooden dock juts out from the base, its planks green with algae. A staircase of worn stone switchbacks up the cliff face. At the top, a crumbling stone wall surrounds the lighthouse grounds. The gate door flaps open and closed in the wind — a broken gull wing.

**The Lighthouse Itself:** 60 feet tall, made of grey stone stained black by salt spray. Its beam blazes defiantly into the sky — a single bright eye in the gloom. But something is wrong. The beam flickers, pulses erratically. It's not the steady rhythm of a well-maintained light.

**The Bay:** Empty. No ships at anchor. No rowboats. The *Jade Lion* is nowhere to be seen — it's hidden in a narrow cove on the opposite side of the island.

*[Enhancement] The sea is eerily still. No waves, no ripples. The water is dark, almost black. A faint green glow pulses beneath the surface about 80 feet from the dock. The air smells of ozone and brine — like before a lightning strike. The crew has gone quiet. Even the gulls are silent.*

## The Water Elemental Attack (Official)

A **water elemental** in the bay attacks any incoming ships once they are within 80 feet of the docks.

**Detection:** Characters whose passive Perception is 12 or higher notice a strange wave slicing toward the ship against the tide. *[Enhancement] The wave has a vaguely humanoid face with empty, swirling eyes. It moves with purpose — not like a natural current.*

**Initial Impact:** The elemental slams into the ship with a sickening crunch — wood splinters, and the ship lurches. It boils out of the sea, a column of churning water with two arms that end in crushing fists.

**Saving Throw:** Surprised PCs must pass a DC 15 Dexterity saving throw or fall prone. Characters who noticed the elemental and brace themselves (stated intention before the impact) have advantage on the save.

## Combat Tactics (Official + Enhancement)

- The water elemental is tall enough to attack creatures on the edge of the ship without leaving the water. It can reach over the railing.
- On its first turn, it uses its **Whelm** action on two PCs at once. (Whelm: The elemental tries to drown a creature — DC 15 Strength save or take 2d8+4 bludgeoning damage and be grappled. A grappled creature starts suffocating.)
- It is compelled by its summoner to fight until destroyed.
- Sheila steers for the safety of the docks. It takes **three rounds** to get there. She won't move her ship again until she can inspect the damage.
- *[Enhancement] If a PC falls overboard (pushed or knocked), the elemental attempts to grapple them (escape DC 14) and drag them under. Other characters can make a DC 12 Strength check to pull the victim back aboard using a rope or boat hook.*

## Example Dialogue & Mannerisms

**Sheila (shouting):** *“Brace yourselves! Something's coming!”* (She spins the wheel hard to port.)

**Crew member (pointing):** *“By the gods — look at the water!”*

**Sheila (after impact):** *“Everyone hold on! We're heading for the dock — three minutes, maybe less!”*

**Sheila (to party, if they're fighting):** *“I can't leave the wheel! You have to stop that thing!”*

**If a PC falls overboard:** *“Man overboard! Throw a line!”*

**After the elemental is destroyed:** *“Good work. Now let's get to that lighthouse before anything else shows up.”*

## Example Player Interactions

- **Spot the elemental early:** A PC who actively searches the water (action) can make a DC 13 Perception check. On success, the whole party has advantage on the Dex save.
- **Protect the crew:** The crew are non-combatants (bandit stats, but cowardly). If too many are killed, the ship may have trouble sailing later. Sheila will notice and be grateful if the party protects them.
- **Use the ship's features:** The ship's ballista (if present) deals 3d10 piercing damage but takes an action to load and an action to fire. The rigging can be swung from for acrobatic attacks (advantage on melee attack if the player describes a swing).
- **Repel the elemental:** A *create or destroy water* spell can blast a hole in the elemental (automatic 4d8 damage, no save). A *control water* spell can redirect it.
- **Rescue a drowning ally:** A character can use an action to throw a rope (DC 10 Dexterity check to land near the victim) or dive in (requires swim speed or risking drowning).

## Treasure (Official)

Upon defeat, the water elemental dissolves into a spray of foam and salt, leaving a lingering smell of rain. In its place, a pouch floats to the surface containing **six Beads of Force**.

*[Enhancement] The pouch is warm to the touch. The beads are clear glass spheres with swirling blue mist inside.*

## DM Prep Help

- **Battle map:** Draw the ship's deck with railings, mast, rigging, and helm. Mark where the elemental is (in the water, but can reach up).
- **Monster card:** Water elemental stats (MM) — AC 14, HP 114, resistances to nonmagical weapons, immunities to poison and exhaustion.
- **Underwater rules cheat sheet:** Print a small card for swimming, holding breath, and underwater combat.
- **Timing:** The three rounds should feel urgent. Use a timer or count aloud to build tension.
- **Music:** Stormy sea ambience or fast-paced combat music.

## GM Notes

- **Pacing:** This encounter should feel desperate and fast. Describe the elemental's attacks as crashing waves and crushing pressure. Use phrases like “The water itself is trying to kill you.”
- **Sheila's role:** She won't abandon the wheel unless absolutely necessary. She shouts orders but trusts the party to fight.
- **Alternative outcomes:** If the party destroys the elemental quickly, Sheila still needs three rounds to reach the dock — but they arrive safely. If the elemental is still active after three rounds, it follows the ship to the dock, attacking anyone on the pier. It can't leave the water, but it can reach onto the dock.
- **Player death:** If a PC drowns, consider having the elemental spit them back up when destroyed — unconscious but alive (death saves required).
- **Aftermath:** The ship has minor damage (a few broken planks, wet cargo). Sheila will need time to inspect it, giving the party an excuse to explore the lighthouse without her.
