# SKYHORN LIGHTHOUSE — POST-LANDING FULL LIVING-WORLD GM MASTER


## Purpose

This is the complete GM-facing continuation beginning when the *Annabel Lee* reaches Lighthouse Island. It joins the established Session 2 and Session 3 material into one continuous runbook and adds a master living-world layer so the island does not wait in frozen encounter rooms.

Use this file for:

- the damaged landing and decision to climb;
- the island exterior and broken gate;
- the occupied lighthouse;
- Lucien Skyhorn and the beacon;
- the hidden flooded caves;
- the wounded *Jade Lion* and Raf;
- Aryn Hest and the captive crew;
- Grigor and the summoning chamber;
- the Bowl of Commanding Water Elementals;
- evacuation, salvage, return to Gundbarg, rewards, rumors, and future hooks.

The party uses the established pregenerated characters and D&D 5e 2014 rules. Actors, items, spells, maps, cards, portraits, tokens, and handouts remain in the Foundry module; this file is the table-running layer and does not replace those assets.

## Canon and Campaign Labels

- **Published fact:** stated by the adventure.
- **Campaign-established:** fixed by the Northern Fjords version, pregens, prior sessions, or existing handouts.
- **Living-world addition:** GM-facing activity, dialogue, or consequence added to make the location behave like a real place.
- **Optional pressure event:** movable material that may be used when pacing needs motion.

Living-world additions must not overwrite a published fact or dictate a player character's thoughts, dialogue, or choices.

## Start Point

Default start:

- The *Annabel Lee* has survived the water elemental.
- Sheila has reached the island dock.
- The ship is damaged but not sinking.
- The lighthouse beacon still burns.
- The stone stair climbs forty feet to the island top.
- The gate in the ten-foot wall stands broken.
- No enemy is visibly guarding the exterior.
- The absence of guards is evidence of disorder, not safety.

If Session 1 ended later, begin at the exact established position and carry forward injuries, expended resources, damaged rigging, discovered clues, relationships, and optional smuggler developments.

## Player Agency Rule

Describe situations, pressures, and what NPCs are doing. Do not say a PC climbs, agrees, enters, trusts Lucien, frees a captive, protects the bowl, or returns to Gundbarg until that player's declared action establishes it.

For each scene:

1. Establish what is visibly happening.
2. Let the players choose an approach.
3. Resolve the approach honestly.
4. Advance the world according to elapsed time, noise, survivors, and messages.
5. Describe consequences through the environment and NPC behavior.

A missed check changes cost, detail, position, time, or danger. It does not erase the only path to the adventure.

# MASTER LIVING-WORLD STATE

## Phase 1 — The Landing

**Island:** Wind, spray, and the active beacon make the island feel exposed rather than abandoned.

**Annabel Lee:** Sheila and the crew secure lines, inspect hull damage, clear broken rigging, and establish whether the ship can evacuate anyone.

**Lighthouse:** Eelfolk above are looting and arguing. They do not yet understand who has arrived unless the approach was loud.

**Caves:** Captives remain guarded. Grigor has not been freed. The bowl remains usable.

**World move:** If the party talks for too long at the dock, a sailor reports a practical repair problem, a loose line snaps, or something scrapes beyond the broken gate.

## Phase 2 — The Ascent

**Island:** The beam crosses the party at regular intervals, bleaching color from stone and making every shadow momentarily sharp.

**Annabel Lee:** A lookout watches the stairs. Sheila does not abandon the deck while the ship is vulnerable.

**Lighthouse:** Raiders drift toward the main hall if they hear climbing, shouted orders, broken windows, or force against the door.

**Caves:** The deeper group does not automatically learn about the arrival. Warning must be carried.

**World move:** Use one sign that ordinary life existed here before the occupation: a split basket, a weather ledger under a stone, a torn drying line, or a crushed herb planter.

## Phase 3 — Breaching the Lighthouse

**Island:** The exterior becomes quiet behind the party. Weather and the beam continue even if combat stops.

**Annabel Lee:** Repairs advance. If the party sends word, Sheila reallocates crew for evacuation or defense.

**Lighthouse:** Eelfolk behave like occupiers interrupted during an argument, meal, search, or rest. Survivors try to regroup or warn allies; they do not fight to the last without reason.

**Lucien:** Hears violence below, checks the barricade, preserves the beacon, and tries to judge whether the newcomers are rescuers or a new threat.

**World move:** A loud fight advances alert. A quiet victory creates a valuable window before the remaining occupants understand what happened.

## Phase 4 — Contact with Lucien

**Island:** The beacon is visibly a maintained machine and public safety system, not merely a magical set piece.

**Lucien:** Exhausted, concise, suspicious of carelessness, and relieved only after he sees credible evidence of rescue.

**Caves:** Time still moves. The eelfolk do not instantly begin the final ritual solely because the party is talking, but warning and delay matter.

**World move:** Lucien supplies information in answer to questions and the party's observations. He does not deliver one uninterrupted exposition speech.

## Phase 5 — Cave Descent

**Lighthouse:** Secured areas remain secured unless the party left survivors, ignored entrances, or allowed a warning to pass.

**Annabel Lee:** Repair clock continues. Sheila prepares blankets, water, light, rope, and space for injured survivors once she knows captives exist.

**Caves:** Noise travels through stone and water. Sharks investigate disturbance as animals conditioned by feeding, not as moral villains.

**World move:** Water, darkness, vertical space, equipment protection, and communication matter before initiative does.

## Phase 6 — The Wounded Jade Lion

**Cove:** The damaged ship, missing mast, stripped cargo spaces, and abandoned work tell the story before Raf appears.

**Raf:** Hides until voices or behavior convince him the deck is safe. He is frightened, hungry, observant, and able to provide what he personally witnessed.

**Aryn and crew:** Still alive deeper in the caves.

**World move:** Finding Raf converts an abstract rescue into named people in immediate danger.

## Phase 7 — Area 8 and the Captive Crew

**Captives:** Exhausted people watch for a real opening. They are not scenery and do not all react identically.

**Aryn:** Acts as a captain even without equipment. He assesses who can move, who needs help, and whether a runner can warn Area 9.

**Eelfolk:** Their first priority is warning the summoning room, not dying in place.

**World move:** If warning gets through, the final room prepares. If it does not, stealth and surprise remain earned advantages.

## Phase 8 — The Summoning Room

**Grigor:** A coerced sailor acting from fear of torture. He continues the ritual until someone stops, incapacitates, reassures, or physically separates him from it.

**Scourge and raiders:** Use cover, pressure weak-looking targets, and protect the ritual long enough to produce another elemental.

**Bowl:** A fixed rescue tool being misused. It is not portable treasure.

**World move:** Combat, persuasion, movement, concentration, cover, rescue, and ritual interruption are simultaneous problems.

## Phase 9 — After the Threat

**Crew:** Immediate needs are weapons secured, wounded stabilized, names counted, exits checked, water provided, and fear reduced.

**Lucien:** Wants to know the bowl is safe and the beacon remains functioning.

**Aryn:** Wants every surviving crew member and recoverable cargo returned to the *Jade Lion* without causing another death.

**Sheila:** Wants a seaworthy evacuation plan, not a celebratory speech.

**World move:** Let the rescue breathe. The silence after violence is a scene.

## Phase 10 — Return and Consequences

**Gundbarg:** News reaches the harbor unevenly. Families, dockworkers, merchants, officials, and storytellers react for different reasons.

**Benji:** Looks for Raf before cargo or payment.

**Survivors:** Correct some rumors and create new ones.

**Party reputation:** Depends on what they protected, how they treated captives and animals, whether they preserved the bowl, and whether they returned the cargo.

**World move:** The town changes because named people came home.

# MASTER ISLAND ACTIVITY CYCLE

Use one activity cycle whenever the party completes a major scene, spends about ten to twenty minutes debating/searching, takes a short rest, or creates a loud event.

Roll or choose one result appropriate to the current phase.

| d12 | Living-world activity |
|---:|---|
| 1 | The lighthouse beam sweeps through spray, briefly revealing movement or damage that darkness concealed. |
| 2 | A repair call rises from the *Annabel Lee*; the work is progressing, but the ship remains physically present in the story. |
| 3 | Stone carries a distant impact, shout, dragged object, or slamming door from another area. |
| 4 | Wind changes and forces everyone to notice exposure, wet clothing, unsecured gear, or failing flame. |
| 5 | An enemy survivor changes position, seeks companions, conceals evidence, or attempts to warn the caves. |
| 6 | A mundane remnant of Lucien's household appears: a tool, note, meal, mended garment, planter, or maintenance mark. |
| 7 | Water rises, recedes, or surges in a way that alters sound and footing without changing the map geometry. |
| 8 | A captive quietly tests a restraint, signals another prisoner, or prepares to act if help arrives. |
| 9 | An NPC asks a practical question that demands a choice: continue, rest, carry loot, send word, guard an exit, or move wounded people. |
| 10 | An animal reacts honestly to scent, blood, noise, or magic. It is information, not prophecy. |
| 11 | Something useful is visible but costs time to recover, inspect, or protect. |
| 12 | The immediate area falls unnaturally quiet because another group has begun listening. |

Do not use this table to add random combat. Use it to show that other people and systems continue to act.

# MASTER NPC VOICES

## Sheila Coppertree

**Voice:** Direct, maritime, unsentimental without being cold.

**Wants now:** A stable ship, accurate information, and survivors brought back alive.

**Useful lines:**

> "Tell me what you need, not what you hope."

> "I can keep her here. I cannot make damaged timber stop being damaged."

> "If you find people, send a name and a number. We will make room."

## Lucien Skyhorn

**Voice:** Precise, weary, protective of responsibilities rather than possessions.

**Wants now:** The beacon kept alive, the bowl protected from misuse, and the occupation ended.

**Useful lines:**

> "The light is not decoration. While we speak, somebody is steering by it."

> "Ask what you need. Then decide quickly."

> "The bowl was built to bring ships home."

## Raf Frogwater

**Voice:** A frightened child trying to report facts correctly because adults need them.

**Wants now:** Safety, his father, and assurance that the crew has not been abandoned.

**Useful lines:**

> "I stayed where Captain Aryn told me until I couldn't hear him anymore."

> "They took the others. I counted until I couldn't see them."

> "Is my father here?"

## Captain Aryn Hest

**Voice:** Economical command language, gratitude shown through work.

**Wants now:** Crew counted, wounded moved, ritual stopped, ship recovered if possible.

**Useful lines:**

> "Good. You're real. Cut the people loose who can walk first."

> "Two of them run for that door if fighting starts."

> "Celebrate when everyone has sky above them."

## Grigor

**Voice:** Fragmented, focused on avoiding renewed torture.

**Wants now:** To survive the next seconds and believe stopping will not condemn him.

**Useful lines:**

> "If I stop, they start again."

> "You don't know what they did when I failed."

> "Get me away from it. Don't just tell me."

## Eelfolk Prisoner or Surrendering Raider

**Voice:** Defensive, clan-centered, practical.

**Wants now:** Survival, escape, or leverage.

**Useful lines:**

> "You call it theft because your dead put stones around it."

> "The scourge promised the sea would answer us."

> "Your sailors summoned it too."

Do not use one captive to speak for every eelfolk or to erase their responsibility for raids, torture, and coercion.

# MASTER DESCRIPTION PALETTE

## Exterior

Use two or three details:

- salt spray whitening dark stone;
- rope snapping against a mast below;
- wet steps reflecting the beacon;
- gulls circling land but not open water;
- wind flattening grass around the broken wall;
- a maintained path disrupted by recent tracks.

## Occupied Lighthouse

Use two or three details:

- domestic furniture repurposed as a barricade;
- wet footprints drying in overlapping layers;
- fish oil, smoke, salt, and opened stores;
- fresh damage against old, well-kept stone;
- household objects handled without understanding;
- the mechanism overhead continuing its steady labor.

## Flooded Caves

Use two or three details:

- cold water swallowing light;
- sound arriving from the wrong direction;
- old masonry beneath natural stone;
- mineral streaks and tide lines;
- bubbles caught under ledges;
- distant wood striking rock in the hidden cove.

## Aftermath

Use two or three details:

- people saying names while counting;
- shaking hands becoming useful hands;
- blankets, rope, water, and improvised stretchers;
- the smell of wet wood and blood after magic ends;
- ordinary voices returning one at a time;
- the lighthouse beam still crossing the water.

# REST, RETREAT, AND DELAY

## Short Rest

A short rest is possible only in a secured area with watches and controlled entrances.

During it:

- *Annabel Lee* repair advances;
- Lucien's relief may improve if contacted;
- escaped enemies may warn deeper groups;
- captives remain under pressure;
- Area 9 preparations advance if the cave group has been alerted.

Do not punish a necessary rest with invented monsters. Apply the established clocks.

## Long Rest

A long rest on the island is a major strategic decision.

Reasonable consequences:

- the final group completes preparations;
- captives may be moved, separated, or more heavily guarded;
- the bowl may be used again;
- weather changes;
- Sheila must decide whether the ship can remain exposed;
- Gundbarg goes another night without reliable news.

A long rest should not automatically kill every captive, but it cannot freeze the crisis.

## Retreat to Annabel Lee

The party may retreat.

Sheila asks for:

- number of enemies seen;
- number of survivors found;
- whether anyone can pursue them;
- whether the bowl can threaten the ship again;
- how long they need.

A retreat may permit repairs, triage, and a better plan. It also gives warned enemies time.

# PART I — LANDING, LIGHTHOUSE, LUCIEN, CAVES, AND RAF

# 00 - SESSION 2 DASHBOARD

## What This Session Covers

Session 2 begins on Skyhorn Island after the *Annabel Lee* survives the water elemental attack and reaches the lighthouse dock.

The session is about **turning the mystery into a concrete rescue problem**.

By the end of the session, the party should understand most of the following:

- The lighthouse is occupied by eelfolk.
- Lucien Skyhorn is alive and has kept the beacon burning.
- The eelfolk found the Bowl of Commanding Water Elementals in the flooded caves below the lighthouse.
- The bowl was historically used to help ships, not attack them.
- The current raiders are forcing captives to perform the summoning.
- The *Jade Lion* was dragged into a hidden cove.
- Raf Frogwater survived aboard the damaged ship.
- Captain Aryn Hest and the surviving crew were taken deeper into the caves.

That is enough revelation for one session. Do not rush the final hostage/summoning-room sequence unless the group is moving unusually quickly. Session 3 is stronger if it begins with the party knowing exactly whom they are trying to save and why time matters.

## Recommended Endpoint

**Full-session endpoint:** The party finds the damaged *Jade Lion*, locates Raf, and hears his account of the crew being taken into the caves.

End on the decision to go back into the caves for Aryn and the prisoners.

A strong final line is simply:

> Raf wipes his nose on his sleeve and looks toward the cave mouth.
>
> "They took everybody else in there."

Do not add a speech after it. Let the players react.

## Alternate Endpoints by Runtime

| Available play time | Good endpoint |
|---|---|
| 2.5–3 hours | Lucien opens the lantern-room door and reveals the bowl and hidden caves |
| 3.5–4 hours | Party descends, deals with the flooded cavern, and reaches the old cave dock |
| 4.5–5.5 hours | Party reaches the *Jade Lion*, finds Raf, and learns Aryn is alive |
| 6+ hours | Allow Area 8 / hostage rescue to begin, but consider stopping before the summoning room |

## Session Arc

1. **Island aftermath** — the *Annabel Lee* is damaged and the party has to choose how quickly to move.
2. **The ascent** — the lighthouse looks active, but the grounds feel abandoned.
3. **Occupied home** — the eelfolk have turned the main hall into a raider camp.
4. **The survivor above** — Lucien is alive, exhausted, armed with knowledge, and still keeping the light.
5. **The truth** — the bowl, the hidden caves, and the captured ship become one connected problem.
6. **Descent** — the party enters flooded caves where the consequences of the occupation are visible.
7. **Wounded Lion** — they find the ship, then the child who survived by hiding.

# 01 - SESSION 2 ONE-MINUTE QUICK REFERENCE

## Start State

The *Annabel Lee* is at the island dock.

Sheila is alive unless Session 1 changed that.

The ship has taken damage from the elemental attack. Sheila's crew can keep working while the party climbs.

The lighthouse is sixty feet tall. The beacon still burns.

The stone stairs switch back forty feet from the dock to the top of the island.

A ten-foot crumbling wall encloses the top. Its gate is broken open.

No eelfolk stand guard outside.

That absence should feel wrong.

## Main Lighthouse Threat

Area 1 begins with:

- 4 eelfolk.
- 2 eelfolk hunters.
- Reinforcements from Area 2 on round 2 if combat breaks out:
  - 4 more eelfolk.
  - 2 more hunters.

If the party moves quietly, use group Stealth against the hunters' passive Perception 12.

If they smash their way in, neither side is surprised.

## Key Access DCs

- Outer stone wall: **DC 10 Strength (Athletics)** to scale.
- Exterior lighthouse wall: **DC 20 Strength (Athletics)** to climb.
- Main lighthouse door: **DC 18 Strength** to force.
- Stairwell windows: **DC 12 Strength** to break or **DC 15 Dexterity** to open.
- Lantern-room windows: same DCs.
- Lantern-room iron door: **DC 25 Strength** to force.
- Hidden floor grate in Area 3: **DC 20 Intelligence (Investigation)** if the party has not learned its location from Lucien.
- Ancient cave wall at Area 6: **DC 13 Strength (Athletics)** to climb.
- Swim through the strong channel to the *Jade Lion*: **DC 13 Strength (Athletics)**; fail by 5+ = 7 (2d6) bludgeoning damage from the rock walls.
- Find Raf's cabinet before he reveals himself: **DC 14 Intelligence (Investigation)**.

## Lucien's Essential Information

Lucien can tell them:

- Eelfolk entered the caves and found the Bowl of Commanding Water Elementals.
- His family has protected it for six generations.
- The fixed bowl can draw on the sea to summon water elementals twice each day.
- His family used those elementals to aid ships in distress.
- Elementals can break free when a summoner loses control.
- Lucien believes the eelfolk are forcing captives to perform the summoning.
- He saw an elemental tow a merchant vessel around the back of the island.
- The hidden cave entrance is beneath a heavy barrel in Area 3.
- The caves are flooded.

If the party agrees to stop the eelfolk and protect the bowl, Lucien gives each PC a **Potion of Water Breathing**.

## Cave Essentials

- Ladder descends 10 feet into water.
- Water is 50 feet deep.
- Ceiling is 40 feet above the water.
- Dim underwater visibility is 30 feet.
- Three hunter sharks investigate noise in the water and attack within 30 feet.
- Sharks flee toward Area 7 if reduced below 15 HP.
- Four submerged loot chests are present.
- Treasure totals 400 gp in fine goods and includes a **Dagger of Venom**.

Area 6:

- Small sandbar.
- Rickety dock.
- Two rowboats.
- Ruined 20-foot wall from an old sunken tower.

Area 7:

- *Jade Lion* at cove mouth.
- One mast snapped and missing.
- Heavily looted.
- No crew visible.

# 03 - SESSION 2 LIVING-WORLD CLOCKS

## Clock A — Lighthouse Alert

This tracks what the eelfolk believe is happening in the tower.

Start according to how the party arrives:

- **0 — Unaware:** quiet landing, careful ascent, no shouting, no obvious intrusion.
- **1 — Suspicious:** normal start if the elemental attack or docking made enough noise to carry.
- **2 — Alert:** party breaks doors, shouts, uses loud magic outside, or leaves obvious bodies.
- **3 — Organized:** survivors inside stop looting and start defending routes.
- **4 — Alarmed:** the remaining raiders know intruders are in the lighthouse and prioritize warning the cave group.

### What the Clock Changes

It changes behavior, not total encounter count.

**At 0–1**
- Main-hall eelfolk are arguing, eating, or picking through the house.
- Hunters are less disciplined.
- Area 2 reinforcements may take the published round to arrive.

**At 2**
- Main-hall creatures are watching entrances.
- A hunter may take cover immediately.
- Nobody is automatically surprised.

**At 3**
- Reinforcements are already awake and armed.
- One creature may try to disengage toward the store room or cave route if things go badly.

**At 4**
- Any surviving eelfolk focus on carrying warning downward.
- If the party later reaches the hostage area, assume the deeper group has had time to prepare unless the PCs prevented the warning.

Do not add extra monsters because the clock rose.

## Clock B — Annabel Lee Repair

Start based on Session 1 damage:

- **0 — Immediate danger:** serious hull/rigging problem.
- **1 — Stabilized:** not sinking; not ready to leave.
- **2 — Seaworthy with caution:** Sheila could evacuate if necessary.
- **3 — Ready:** not pretty, but functional.

Advance this clock whenever the party spends meaningful time inside the lighthouse or caves.

Sheila and the crew are not idle.

### What Sheila Does

At 0–1:
- Orders bilge checks.
- Resecures rigging.
- Replaces damaged rail and line.
- Keeps one sailor watching the stairs.

At 2:
- Sends someone up the first part of the stairs if the party has been gone a long time.
- Prepares blankets, water, and a place for survivors.

At 3:
- Keeps the ship ready to leave but does not abandon the party.

If the PCs send a message back about prisoners, Sheila reorganizes the deck immediately for casualties.

## Clock C — Lucien's Endurance

Lucien has been living on goodberries and water for days.

This clock is about exhaustion and patience, not hit points.

Start at **2 — Worn thin**.

- **1 — Relieved:** after he knows real help has arrived.
- **2 — Worn thin:** default.
- **3 — Frayed:** prolonged fighting or repeated attempts to force his door.
- **4 — Desperate:** he believes the party may be hostile or the tower is about to fall.

At higher states, Lucien becomes shorter and more suspicious. He still thinks clearly.

Do not make him babble or collapse for drama.

## Clock D — Cave Noise

Start at 0 when the party first descends.

Increase when:

- Someone jumps or falls into water.
- Heavy loot is dragged.
- Thunderous magic is used.
- A body hits the water.
- A boat bangs hard into the dock.
- Several characters swim carelessly.

At **1**, ripples and distant movement appear.

At **2**, the sharks begin closing.

At **3**, the sharks are actively hunting in the party's vicinity.

At **4**, any remaining sharks track wounded swimmers aggressively.

A cautious party can delay the encounter or change its shape.

## Clock E — Tide and Weather

Do not change the published map geometry unless you want a harder navigation game.

Use this clock for atmosphere:

- Wind shifts.
- The lighthouse beam cuts through spray.
- Cave water surges higher against stone.
- The hidden cove grows louder.
- The *Annabel Lee* strains against its lines.

Advance it after major scenes.

It reminds the table that the island is in open northern water.

## Clock F — Optional Smuggler Nerves

Use only if the *Slippery Eel* thread is active.

Start at **1 — Waiting**.

# 04 - SKYHORN ISLAND: WHAT IS HAPPENING WITHOUT THE PCS

## The Eelfolk Occupation

The eelfolk are not standing in assigned encounter squares between turns.

During the session:

- Some in Area 1 pick through furniture and household goods.
- Hunters argue over what to do about Lucien.
- Raiders from Area 2 drift in and out of the barracks.
- Someone occasionally tests the route upstairs, then backs off from the barricaded lantern room.
- Food stores in Area 3 are being torn open rather than carefully inventoried.
- Traffic between the upper lighthouse and caves is intermittent.
- Deeper below, captive sailors remain under guard.
- The bowl remains in use as part of the eelfolk's larger plan.

If the party is loud, this behavior tightens into defense.

If the party is quiet, they can catch the occupation in an unguarded moment.

## Lucien

Lucien keeps the beacon lit because ships still depend on it.

He sleeps in short stretches when he can.

He listens for movement below.

He has enough magic to make a forced entry unpleasant, but not enough strength to retake the building alone.

His stubbornness is practical. If he dies and the beacon goes dark, the problem becomes larger than the island.

## Sheila and Her Crew

Sheila's priority order is:

1. Keep the *Annabel Lee* from becoming the next wreck.
2. Keep her crew alive.
3. Stay close enough to evacuate survivors.
4. Get Aryn back if the party finds him.

She does not follow the adventurers room by room.

This gives the PCs space to lead while preserving Sheila as a meaningful ally.

## The Sharks Below

They have been fed human remains.

That does not make them agents of the eelfolk.

They associate sound, blood, and disturbance with food.

Lethiel or any character using appropriate animal magic can learn that distinction.

## The Beacon

The beam itself is bright once the party reaches the island.

Earlier irregularity came from the siege, Lucien's limited ability to maintain the mechanism, weather, and interference around the tower.

Do not turn the light into a second unrelated curse.

# 06 - SCENE 1: THE DOCK, DAMAGE, AND THE DECISION TO CLIMB

## Scene Goal

Give the party thirty seconds to feel the aftermath of Session 1, then hand control back to them.

Do not bury the start under recap.

## Foundry

Activate **Skyhorn Lighthouse — Exterior Approach**.

Keep the *Annabel Lee* visible if the scene supports it.

## Opening Image

Use the exact state left by Session 1.

If the party ended on the dock:

>
> The lighthouse beam passes over the deck.
>
> Somewhere inside the tower, something heavy scrapes across a floor.

Then stop.

## Sheila's Immediate Priorities

Sheila checks:

- hull;
- rigging;
- crew count;
- whether anyone is still in the water;
- whether the party can move.

If a PC asks what she wants:

> "Find Lucien. Find Aryn if he's here. I'll keep our way home floating."

If the party wants her to come:

She looks at the damaged ship first.

> "I leave this deck now, you may come back with survivors and no ship to put them on."

- one team checks below deck;
- another cuts damaged rigging free;
- one sailor watches the water with a crossbow;

- spare rope, lanterns, and blankets are set aside.

The crew has work.

## Expected Player Actions

### "We Take a Short Rest"

Allow it if they want.

Make the cost visible:

- wind worsens;
- eelfolk alert may increase if the landing was noisy;
- the ship repair clock advances;
- the party hears another dull impact from inside the tower.

Do not trigger a punitive surprise attack solely because they rested.

### "We Heal the Crew First"

Good.

Sheila appreciates it.

Mara or anyone with medical skill can improve crew readiness without a formal check if there is no uncertainty.

### "We Inspect the Ship"

Borrik, Tark, or a practical sailor can help.

Useful finding:

The ship can be made safe enough to remain at dock. Whether it can immediately fight another elemental is a different question.

### "We Send a Familiar / Scout Ahead"

Good plan.

The main hall is cluttered and occupied; line of sight remains limited.

### "We Circle the Island Before Climbing"

Let them.

- the coastline is steep;
- surf pounds most approaches;

If they find the *Jade Lion* first, use the alternate-flow section later in this guide.

### "We Shout for Lucien"

No answer from the grounds.

Wind and thick stone swallow most sound.

If they shout near an upper window, Lucien may eventually respond.

### "We Look for Tracks"

Evidence is messy because of rain and spray.

Still, a careful search finds:

- webbed or clawed prints in sheltered mud;
- drag marks from cargo;
- signs of repeated movement through the broken gate.

No one needs to roll to learn that creatures have been moving between the gate and lighthouse.

# 07 - SCENE 2: ASCENT TO THE BROKEN GATE

## Scene Goal

Build tension with evidence, not a jump scare.

## Physical Layout

- Dock at waterline.
- Slippery stone stairs switchback 40 feet upward.
- Top plateau is about 100 feet in diameter.
- Crumbling wall is 10 feet high.
- Gate hangs broken.
- Lighthouse rises 60 feet beyond.

## Sensory Details

Use a few:

- stair edges polished slick by spray;
- dead seaweed caught above normal tide line;
- oil smell faintly mixed with ozone;
- shell fragments crushed into mud;
- one section of wall blackened by electrical scorching;
- a gull carcass caught behind a stone;
- wet footprints where no boot tread appears;
- a splintered chair leg lying outside where it should never have been.

## The Broken Gate

The gate itself does not need a check.

If characters inspect it, it was forced from the lighthouse side of the island during the attack.

Do not over-explain the exact moment.

## Climbing the Wall Instead

The surrounding wall can be scaled with **DC 10 Strength (Athletics)**.

A success gets the character up.

A failure should usually mean delay, noise, or a wet slide unless the fiction makes a dangerous fall plausible.

## Looking at the Lighthouse Exterior
The main door is barricaded.

No guard waits outside.

That is because the eelfolk are operating inside and below, not because the exterior is safe by design.

The exterior lighthouse wall can be climbed with **DC 20 Strength (Athletics)**.

## Windows

Two windows roughly 40 feet up open to the stairwell between the main level and lantern room.

Lantern-room windows are about 60 feet up.

Published access:

- **DC 12 Strength** to break the glass.
- **DC 15 Dexterity** to open a window without simply smashing it.

Climbing to them is still a separate problem.

## If a PC Watches the Beam

From here, the beacon is bright.

Its ongoing operation points to an active keeper.

Tark/Borrik can tell that whatever caused the weak appearance from Gundbarg was not "the lamp is dead."

Aelar may read the same fact emotionally: someone is still doing the work.

## If They Search for Bodies

None lie openly outside.

That absence should remain unexplained until later evidence shows that people were dragged, captured, or killed elsewhere.

## If They Listen at the Main Door

On a good quiet moment, they hear:

- voices in an unfamiliar language;
- chair legs or benches scraping;
- something hard dropped on stone;
- occasionally a sharper argument.

A character who knows Eelfolk understands the content.

A character using appropriate translation magic can learn that the raiders are angry about "the old light-man" above and have failed to kill him.

They also mention hunger, loot, and "below," but do not conveniently explain the bowl to each other.

# 09 - SCENE 3: ENTERING THE LIGHTHOUSE

## Scene Goal

Reveal that the lighthouse is occupied, establish eelfolk behavior, and let the party choose stealth, force, magic, or an alternate route.

## Main Door

The main lighthouse door is barricaded.

**DC 18 Strength** opens it by force.

A forced entry is loud.

If the party breaks through, neither side gains surprise from the entry alone.

## Alternate Entry

Possible approaches:

- exterior climb to a window;
- open/break a stairwell window;
- magic that bypasses barriers;
- familiar scouting;
- creative rope work;
- entering the caves first through a route discovered from outside.

Do not insist on the front door.

## Main Hall Read-Aloud

If the party enters Area 1:

> The first thing you see is what happened to the room.
>
> Barrels have been split open and chairs broken for no useful reason. Long tables sit crooked across the floor. A wall hanging bearing a nautilus shell has been ripped down and dragged through muddy footprints.
>
> The second thing you see is what did it.
>
> Wet, rubbery figures crowd the tables and the far side of the hall. Some have shields and long knives. Two leaner ones hold spears and keep glancing toward the stairs.
>
> One of them stops talking.
>
> Then the room turns toward you.

If the party enters unseen, stop before the final two lines and ask what they do.

## What the Eelfolk Are Talking About

If understood:

- Lucien is still above.
- They have not gotten through his final barricade.
- Some want to starve him out.
- Others want to set fire to something.
- At least one is more concerned with loot below.
- None wants to be the first to test the hot iron door again.

They do not recite plot exposition.

## Published Combat Shape

The initial room contains:

- four basic eelfolk;
- two hunters.

On round two, unless circumstances prevent it:

- four more basic eelfolk;
- two more hunters arrive from Area 2.

Use Foundry for mechanics.

## Eelfolk Tactical Intent

Basic raiders:

- rush exposed targets;
- use numbers;
- try to keep strong melee PCs occupied.

Hunters:

- overturn tables for cover;
- throw from behind obstacles;
- move to maintain distance.

If the fight goes badly:

- one may try to run deeper;
- one may surrender if communication is possible;
- a hunter may dive through a route or window if escape is credible.

Do not play every raider as suicidal.

## Environmental Use

The hall offers:

- overturned tables;
- broken furniture;
- narrow passages;
- stairs;
- spilled oil or food;
- torn tapestries;
- partial cover;
- objects that can be shoved or climbed.

Players should be allowed to improvise without needing a bespoke subsystem.

## If the Party Uses Area Magic

Remember this is a stone building with oil, wood, cloth, and a working lighthouse above.

Fire has consequences.

Do not arbitrarily burn the whole tower down, but make uncontrolled flame feel relevant.

## If the Party Tries to Talk

Without shared language, this goes nowhere beyond posture, gestures, and obvious threats.

With shared language:

The eelfolk are hostile raiders who believe they currently own the place.

A wounded or isolated one might bargain for escape.

The group as a whole is unlikely to abandon the island because a stranger asks politely.

A convincing threat backed by overwhelming force may cause individuals to flee.

## If the Party Wants to Bypass the Fight

Possible:

- stealth along edge routes;
- window entry above Area 1;
- magic;
- wait for some raiders to move;
- lure part of the group outside.

If they reach Lucien without clearing the hall, remember those eelfolk still exist.

They can cut off retreat later.

# 10 - AFTERMATH IN AREA 1

## Scene Goal

Give the room enough detail to feel like a home that was invaded.

Do not turn every broken object into a clue.

## Visible Evidence

- Nautilus-shell Skyhorn crest on shredded tapestry.
- Broken household furniture.
- Food opened and wasted.
- Wet tracks.

Yes.

The building was active and maintained.

The exact number of days is difficult to determine from the room alone.

There are signs of violence, but the main hall does not provide a clean body count.

Movement patterns go:

- toward Area 2;
- toward Area 3;

The hidden grate itself is not obvious without investigation or Lucien's directions.

After combat, let the lighthouse beam pass across a window.

The room brightens for one second.

Then it moves on.

That is enough.

# 13 - SCENE 4: THE STAIR AND LANTERN-ROOM DOOR

## Scene Goal

Lucien does not know who is coming up the stairs.

Before the spell lands, give a perceptive party a chance to hear him.

A beat.

> "Next strike, I make the door hotter than your hands."

Lucien asks concrete questions.

> "Where did you dock?"

> "What attacked you?"

He is tired, not foolish.

## If They Climb to the Window

Lucien can open it from inside after deciding they are allies.

This bypasses the door cleanly.

# 14 - SCENE 5: LUCIEN SKYHORN AND THE LANTERN ROOM

## First View

> Heat rolls out when the door finally opens.
>
> The lantern room smells of hot metal, lamp oil, wet wool, and the sharp green scent of crushed berries.
>
> A massive lamp turns around a metal post, sending the beam through salt-streaked glass and out over the sea.
>
> Beside it stands a short old man with white hair in every direction, grey eyes, and one hand still held ready for a spell.
>
> He looks past you toward the stairs before he looks at your faces.
>
> "How many?"

He means eelfolk.

Let the party answer.

## Lucien at the Table

### Appearance

- Short.
- Wild white hair.
- Grey eyes.
- Clothing chosen for work, patched repeatedly.
- Hands blackened with oil and soot.

### Manner

- Talks with his eyebrows as much as his hands.
- Defaults to explanations when nervous.
- Stops mid-lecture when he remembers someone is bleeding.
- Corrects nautical or natural-history mistakes automatically.
- Has not slept properly.

### What He Wants Right Now

- Confirm the upper tower is safe.
- Keep the beacon running.
- Get the eelfolk away from the bowl.
- Rescue anyone still alive.
- Keep the bowl from becoming a permanent weapon.

## Immediate Reaction to the PCs

If they cleared the hall:

Relief shows, briefly.

> "Good. Good. Shut the door."

Then he asks what happened to the ship at the dock.

If they bypassed the hall:

> "There are still six—no, more than six—below us."

He wants to know what routes remain dangerous.

## The Beacon

If asked why he stayed:

> "Because ships don't stop needing a light because I'm frightened."

Leave it there unless someone asks more.

## Lucien Conversation — What Happened?

### "Who Attacked?"

> "Eelfolk. Shore raiders. They came through the caves, not the front gate."

He describes them as violent shoreline and cavern hunters.

### "Why the Lighthouse?"

> "They found something underneath it."

He is reluctant for about one breath, then decides secrecy has already failed.

### "What Did They Find?"

> "A bowl my family should have guarded better."

Then explain the Bowl of Commanding Water Elementals.

## The Bowl — Clean Explanation

Lucien tells them:

- It has been in the caves for six generations.
- It is fixed in place.
- It can draw on the surrounding sea.
- It can summon water elementals twice in a day.
- The Skyhorn family used those elementals to help stricken ships reach safe water.
- A summoned elemental can break free when the person directing it loses control.

Do not make the bowl inherently evil.

The problem is who controls it and how.

## "Why Didn't You Tell Gundbarg About It?"

Lucien takes a moment.

> "Because a tool that can drag a sinking ship off rocks can also drag a sound ship onto them."

> "My family chose secrecy."

If pressed:

> "Today is a fairly good argument that secrecy wasn't enough."

He is capable of admitting that.

## "Can the Bowl Be Moved?"

The source establishes that it is fixed in place.

Lucien says:

> "Not by any safe method I know."

Do not invent a portable version.

## "Why Not Destroy It?"

The source does not give a definitive answer.

Lucien can honestly say:

> "I've never tried."

If players push:

> "It's tied into old stone, old magic, and the sea below us. Smashing unknown magic because I'm frightened would be a poor final Skyhorn decision."

That keeps the question open.

## "How Are the Eelfolk Controlling the Elementals?"

Lucien explains the problem.

> "At first, I think they tried it themselves."

> "Some of them died."

> "Now I think they're making prisoners do the summoning."

This is his reasoning, not direct eyewitness certainty.

## "Prisoners?"

Lucien saw an elemental seize a merchant ship and tow it toward the rear of the island.

> "It went into the hidden cove."

That is his strongest evidence.

## "The Jade Lion?"

If the party names it:

> "I didn't see the name from here. Merchant rig. One mast still standing when it vanished behind the rock."

Do not let him know more than he could know.

## "Where Are the Caves?"

The trap door is under the heavy barrel in Area 3.

He tells them exactly.

No DC needed afterward.

## "How Bad Are the Caves?"

> "Flooded. Deep in places. Tidal."

He does not know current enemy positions.

## "Will You Come With Us?"

Default answer:

Lucien stays with the beacon.

> "If I leave the light, I trade one rescue for the next wreck."

He is also exhausted.

If the party has a credible way to keep the beacon operating safely without him, he can reconsider.

If you allow him to accompany them:

- keep him support-focused;
- do not let him solve encounters;
- remember he has been trapped and underfed for days.

The cleanest table choice is usually to leave him above.

## Potions of Water Breathing

If the party agrees to protect the bowl and deal with the eelfolk, Lucien gives **one Potion of Water Breathing to each PC**, per the adventure.

If Lethiel can cast *water breathing*:

Do not make that spell choice feel wasted.

Lucien still offers the potions.

The party can:

- save them;
- give them to future survivors;
- use the spell and hold potions as emergency resources.

## Valemon's Seed

If Valemon asks for help:

Lucien examines the container and gives practical preservation guidance.

Possible short exchange:

> "Forest stock?"

Valemon answers.

> "Then stop treating salt like weather. Salt stays."

Lucien recommends:

- rinse exposed surfaces with clean fresh water;
- keep the roots insulated from rapid temperature shifts;
- avoid the lantern room's dry heat;
- use damp moss or cloth around the container during the return voyage.

He cannot restore a dead seed or guarantee its survival.

## Lethiel and the Lighthouse

If Lethiel raises ecological concerns, Lucien is willing to discuss them after the immediate crisis.

He sees the lighthouse as part of an obligation to sailors and the coast.

He does not claim human structures are automatically harmless.

## Lucien's Wife

This is private.

Do not have him volunteer it because a player asked one empathetic question.

If trust develops and someone asks why he insists on saving everyone:

Lucien may admit that he still blames himself for his wife's death, despite knowing he could not have prevented it.

Keep this brief.

It is character texture, not a required revelation.

# 17 - SCENE 6: DESCENT INTO AREA 5 — SOMETHING IN THE DEEP

## Scene Goal

Change the adventure's physical language.

The lighthouse was doors, stairs, tables, and stone.

The caves are depth, echo, limited sight, water, and uncertain footing.

## Foundry

Activate **Skyhorn Lighthouse Caves**.

Place the party at Area 5.

## Read-Aloud

> The trap door opens on cold air and the smell of deep saltwater.
>
> A rusty ladder drops ten feet before disappearing into black-green water.
>
> Beyond it, the cavern opens far wider than the room above should allow. Every sound comes back twice: once from stone, once from water.
>
> Somewhere out in the dark, something large turns just beneath the surface.

If Cave Noise is still 0, omit the last line until they actually disturb the water.

## Published Environment

- Ladder descends 10 feet.
- Water is 50 feet deep.
- Cave ceiling reaches 40 feet above the surface.
- Underwater visibility is dim to 30 feet before other light modifiers.
- Swimming without a swim speed costs extra movement under normal 2014 rules.
- Breath holding uses normal 2014 suffocation rules.

Most parties will have Water Breathing available from Lucien or Lethiel.

That removes suffocation pressure.

It does not remove:

- darkness;
- movement;
- combat limitations;
- separation;
- current;
- fear of deep water.

## Before Anyone Enters

Ask:

- Who carries light?
- Who goes first?
- Is anyone using rope?
- Are they swimming, using a boat if available, or trying another route?
- Who has water breathing active?

This is enough planning.

Do not demand a formation diagram unless the group likes one.

## The Sharks

Three hunter sharks are in the cavern.

Published behavior:

- they investigate sounds in the water;
- attack when they come within 30 feet of creatures in the water;
- focus on wounded targets due to Blood Frenzy;
- flee toward Area 7 if reduced below 15 HP.

Use Foundry for numbers.

## Making the Sharks Feel Alive

Before initiative, show signs:

- a fin breaks the surface and vanishes;
- light catches an eye and then nothing;
- a rope suddenly shifts sideways;- tiny fish scatter;
- something bumps stone below.

The sharks are hunting animals.

They do not roar.

## Avoiding Combat

The published encounter expects danger, but player abilities should matter.

Possible solutions:

- remain out of the water where possible;
- move quietly and minimize splashing;
- use food as diversion;
- *speak with animals*;
- frighten or repel them with magic;
- use a rowboat from Area 6 once reached;
- create an alternate traversal method.

If the party avoids them intelligently, let that work.

## Lethiel and Speak with Animals

The sharks communicate simple ideas:

- hunger;
- blood;
- feeding place;
- loud creatures bring food;
- deeper water leads out.

They cannot describe the eelfolk's political goals.

A shark might recognize the scent/shape of eelfolk as "the ones who bring meat."

## If Someone Is Wounded

Now the sharks become noticeably more direct.

Describe the change before exploiting Blood Frenzy mechanically.

> The nearest shadow turns the instant blood reaches the water.

## If Someone Falls Unconscious in the Water

This is dangerous.

Use normal rules.

Other PCs have ropes, swimming magic, potions, and allies; give them the chance to rescue without softening the threat invisibly.

Raf hides in a secret cabinet.

Do not make it cute.

# 26 - EELFOLK BEHAVIOR REFERENCE

## General

Eelfolk in this adventure are aggressive shoreline raiders.

They are comfortable in wet spaces and use the caves as home terrain.

They are not mindless.

## When Winning

- press isolated targets;
- use numbers;
- hunters keep distance;
- try to drive intruders away from routes downward.

## When Losing

- fall back;
- seek water;
- warn deeper allies;
- abandon loot;
- surrender only when escape is impossible and communication exists.

## What They Value

In the context of this raid:

- food;
- plunder;
- control of the bowl;
- territorial dominance;
- survival.

## What They Fear

- uncontrolled water elementals;
- fire in confined areas;
- being cut off from water;
- stronger leaders;
- the scourge below;
- Lucien's magic after repeated failed attempts to reach him.

## What They Know About Humans

Enough to recognize weapons, doors, ships, cargo, prisoners, and bargaining behavior.

Do not play them as animals wearing equipment.

## Language Barrier

Most speak Eelfolk.

If no PC understands it, gestures and combat intent still communicate basic ideas.

Translation magic should open new options.

# PART II — CAPTIVE CREW, SUMMONING ROOM, BOWL, EVACUATION, AND RETURN

# 00 - SESSION 3 DASHBOARD

## What This Session Covers

Session 3 begins after the party has found the damaged *Jade Lion* and learned from Raf that Captain Aryn Hest and the surviving crew were taken farther into the caves.

This is the rescue-and-resolution session. Session 1 was uncertainty. Session 2 was discovery. Session 3 is about people who are still alive, a ritual already underway, and choices that cannot all be delayed until the party feels perfectly ready.

The core sequence is:

1. Return from the *Jade Lion* toward the occupied cave interior.
2. Reach Area 8 and find the surviving crew.
3. Prevent the two guards from warning Area 9 if possible.
4. Learn that another summoning is already in progress.
5. Decide whether to free everyone first or move immediately.
6. Enter Area 9 and stop Grigor's forced ritual.
7. Defeat or scatter the remaining eelfolk.
8. Decide what to do with the Bowl of Commanding Water Elementals.
9. Get the survivors, cargo, and ships back under control.
10. Return to Gundbarg and let the living world react.

## Recommended Endpoint

For a normal 4.5–5.5 hour session, finish with the party back in **Gundbarg**.

Do not rush the return. The emotional payoff is not only that the monsters are defeated. It is Benji seeing Raf alive, Sheila seeing Aryn walk off the *Jade Lion*, exhausted sailors reaching familiar ground, and Gundbarg learning that the danger strangling its harbor was real and has been stopped.

A good final image:

> By the time the ships reach Gundbarg, the fog has lifted enough for the Eye of the Storm to be seen behind them.
>
> The beam crosses the mouth of the fjord once.
>
> This time, nobody mistakes it for a warning.

Then let the players say what their characters do next.

## Alternate Endpoints by Runtime

| Available play time | Good endpoint |
|---|---|
| 2.5–3 hours | Stop after Area 9 and the bowl decision |
| 3.5–4 hours | Finish rescue and ship evacuation; end as the convoy leaves Skyhorn |
| 4.5–5.5 hours | Return to Gundbarg and resolve Benji, Sheila, Aryn, and public aftermath |
| 6+ hours | Add downtime conversations, personal-hook follow-through, and campaign hooks |

# 03 - SESSION 3 PRESSURE CLOCKS

## Clock A — Area 9 Warning

Start at **0** if no eelfolk escaped from Session 2.

Start at **1** if the party has been loud in the caves.

Start at **2** if an eelfolk runner already escaped toward the final rooms.

### 0 — Unaware

Area 8 guards are inattentive enough to be caught off guard. Area 9 focuses on Grigor and the bowl.

### 1 — Nervous

Area 8 guards watch the routes. Area 9 still performs the ritual normally.

### 2 — Expecting Trouble

Area 8 guards run immediately when they see the party. Area 9 responds quickly if warned.

### 3 — Warned

Area 9 takes cover and prepares. Begin the two-minute pre-summoning window.

### 4 — Ritual Completed Before Entry

A water elemental has already been summoned. It can move into Area 8 under eelfolk coercion.

Do not add a second elemental unless the fiction and bowl usage clearly justify it.

## Clock B — Grigor's Ritual

Once Area 9 combat begins and Grigor is still performing the ritual:

- **Turn 1** — first stage.
- **Turn 2** — water rises and reacts.
- **Turn 3** — the bowl begins to pull sea-water unnaturally.
- **Turn 4** — ritual completes at the end of Grigor's turn.

If concentration is broken before completion, the ritual stops.

"Halted" can mean:

- physically pulled from the bowl;
- knocked unconscious;
- reassured that the eelfolk cannot punish him;
- restrained;
- convinced to stop;
- concentration broken by combat;
- magic that prevents the action.

Do not require the party to damage him.

## Clock C — Prisoner Safety

Start at **2 — exhausted but alive**.

Pressure rises if:
- combat spills directly into tied prisoner groups;
- area effects catch them;
- an elemental reaches Area 8;
- nobody cuts them loose while the room becomes unsafe.

Safety improves if:
- Mara or another healer triages;
- groups are untied;
- someone escorts them toward the cove;
- weapons are kept away from panicked captives until people can think clearly.

This is not a hidden hit-point tax. It is a reminder that twelve bound civilians occupy the battlefield.

## Clock D — Evacuation

- **0:** all still tied / trapped.
- **1:** Area 8 secured.
- **2:** first group moving toward Area 7.
- **3:** survivors aboard *Jade Lion* or moving toward *Annabel Lee*.
- **4:** all living survivors accounted for.

Aryn can manage much of this once freed.

## Clock E — Tide

Use tide for atmosphere and logistics:
- stronger pull through cove;
- rowboats knocking against dock;
- louder surf masking speech;
- harder cargo movement.

Do not change map geometry mid-combat without warning.

## Clock F — Gundbarg Response

This starts once a returning ship is visible in the fjord.

1. Lookouts notice.
2. A ship is recognized.
3. Crowd begins to form.
4. Survivors disembark.
5. Story spreads faster than facts.

# 44 - QUICK GM RULINGS

## Cutting Bonds
No roll needed under no pressure.

In initiative, use an appropriate action/object interaction ruling.

## Carrying Injured Sailors
Roll only when terrain, current, or combat makes the outcome uncertain.

## Trade Goods
Bulky. Use labor and boat trips, not exact pound-by-pound accounting unless desired.

## Grigor's Concentration
Treat the ritual as concentration-like per the adventure's description.

## Elemental Hostility
If control breaks, Grigor is the immediate target. The elemental is still dangerous to everyone.

## Surprise
Use 2014 surprise rules. Scourge passive Perception is 13.

## Cover
Stalagmites and broken tower crenellations provide half cover.

## Aryn
Starts with no equipment.
