# 06 - SCENE 1B: THE CRACKED CASK

## Scene Goal

Make the crisis personal through Benji, then let the players choose how much rumor-gathering they want before heading to Sheila.

## FOUNDry

Activate **The Cracked Cask — Tavern**.

## Room Tone

The Cracked Cask should feel **warm, crowded, and tense**.

### READ ALOUD — Entering the Cracked Cask

> The Cracked Cask hits you with heat first.
>
> Wet cloaks steam near the hearth. Pine logs pop in the fire. The room smells of dark ale, smoked fish, wool, and too many people who expected to be somewhere else by now.
>
> Sailors occupy tables in whole crews rather than mixing. Conversations lower whenever the door opens, then resume after everyone decides the newcomer is not carrying news from the lighthouse.
>
> One person does not fit the room at all: a halfling in a velvet merchant’s coat, spectacles fogged from the heat, pacing beside a table covered in ledgers he has stopped pretending to read.

That is **Benji Frogwater**.

## What Is Happening Without the PCs

- Benji has already asked several crews for help and been refused.
- Yrsa is allowing him to occupy a table because throwing him out would be cruel and bad for business.
- Two sailors are arguing over whether the *Jade Lion* was attacked by a creature or by water itself.
- A southern merchant wants the harbor reopened for commercial reasons and keeps saying so too loudly.
- A local family listens for any mention of missing ships.
- If the party waits long enough, Lucien’s speaking-gull message can arrive outside or at an open window.

## Benji Frogwater — Fast Roleplay Card

**Presentation:** round spectacles, red nose, merchant coat slightly too large, ink-stained fingers, wedding ring twisted repeatedly.

**Emotional center:** he lost his wife; Raf is all he has left. Do not play him as comic relief.

**Wants:** a capable group to investigate and rescue survivors.

**Offers:** 200 gp each to investigate; another 200 gp each if the crew and Raf return safely. He can offer a letter of credit or future favor if that better motivates the party.

**Does not do:** accompany them, command them, hide the danger.

## Benji Opening

If the party approaches him:

> “You’re armed, and nobody in here is trying to throw you out. That puts you ahead of most of the people I’ve asked.”
>
> He pushes his spectacles up, realizes his hand is shaking, and grips the edge of the table instead.
>
> “My ship is called the *Jade Lion*. My son Raf is aboard. Captain Aryn Hest is no fool, and he did not simply sail into bad weather and forget the way home.”
>
> “I need someone to go toward Skyhorn and find out what happened.”

Then state the reward plainly.

## Benji Conversation Tree

### “Why was your son on the ship?”

Benji pauses before answering.

> “Because he begged me to stop treating the sea like something that only exists on invoices.”
>
> “Aryn agreed to take him. One voyage. Safe route. Experienced crew.”
>
> “I said yes.”

If a PC presses blame, Benji accepts responsibility rather than becoming defensive.

> “If you’re asking whether I regret it, yes. If you’re asking whether making me regret it harder brings him home, no.”

### “What was the Jade Lion carrying?”

> “Trade goods. Valuable enough to matter to my business. Not valuable enough to matter more than the crew.”

If Sarduuk or another merchant-minded PC asks intelligently, Benji can provide manifests and cargo categories without revealing future treasure placement.

### “When was it last seen?”

Benji gives the last reliable departure/route information available in your campaign timeline. The source establishes that it is overdue but does not require a specific number of days. Keep the answer relative if you have not fixed dates:

> “Long enough that Aryn would have sent word if he could.”

### “What did witnesses see?”

> “A wave moving the wrong direction. That is the part everyone agrees on before they start adding teeth and tentacles.”

### “Do you believe in the sea monster?”

> “I believe my son is missing. I don’t need the correct name for what took him before I pay someone to look.”

### “Why aren’t the authorities going?”

> “Because officials need proof before they risk people. I understand the reasoning. I hate the reasoning.”

### “What do you know about Lucien Skyhorn?”

> “Keeper of the lighthouse. Druid. Old family. Stubborn reputation. If anyone knows what happened out there, he does—assuming he can still answer.”

This is a clean insertion point for **Valemon** and **Lethiel**.

### “What do you know about Sheila Coppertree?”

> “She’s willing to sail. That currently makes her either the bravest captain in Gundbarg or the worst judge of danger. I am hoping for the first.”

### “Can you pay more?”

Benji’s reaction depends on motive.

**Professional negotiation:** he can discuss letters of credit, merchant introductions, or a limited increase.

**Predatory leverage:** he becomes colder.

> “My son is missing, not auctioned.”

He may still hire them if they are his best chance, but trust drops.

### “Can we inspect your records?”

Yes. Benji produces shipping papers.

Useful outcomes:

- **Nymera:** can compare marks/times against her own thread.
- **Elira:** gets names and documented route information.
- **Tark/Borrik:** can identify supply or lighthouse-related entries if relevant.
- **Sarduuk:** learns merchant relationships without needing a roll.

Do not place the main solution in paperwork.

## Benji Reactions by PC Style

| PC behavior | Benji reaction |
|---|---|
| Compassionate | Relaxes enough to answer clearly. |
| Highly professional | Grateful; gives documents and stops repeating emotional details. |
| Mercenary but fair | Accepts it. “Coin is honest if the work is honest.” |
| Mocking superstition | Fine, unless they mock frightened families. |
| Mocking his fear | Trust drops sharply. |
| Promises Raf will definitely live | Benji does not believe certainty. “Don’t promise what the sea hasn’t given you yet.” |
| Says they will try | This is enough. He values honesty. |

## Rumor Cluster in the Cask

Do not gate all information behind rolls. A social check changes **quality, detail, and trust**, not whether the adventure remains playable.

### Rumor 1 — The Monster Story

A braggart claims enormous tentacles, red eyes, and a ship swallowed whole.

**Truth:** exaggerated or invented.

**Insight:** easy to tell the storyteller is enjoying attention more than remembering trauma.

### Rumor 2 — The Wrong-Way Wave

A nervous sailor saw the *Jade Lion* pulled toward Skyhorn by water moving against wind and tide.

**Truth:** broadly accurate.

**Line:** “I know currents. That wasn’t one.”

### Rumor 3 — The Light

A lighthouse supplier or old sailor says the beam’s irregularity began around the same general crisis window as the missing ship reports.

**Truth:** useful correlation, not proof of cause.

### Rumor 4 — Lucien

People disagree whether Lucien is dead, besieged, hiding, or “talking to gulls.”

**Truth:** he is alive and resisting, but town does not know.

### Rumor 5 — Sheila

“Coppertree will sail through anything if she thinks someone she cares about is on the other side.”

**Truth:** emotionally true; also she is competent, not suicidal.

## The Seagull Message

Use this if the party needs stronger proof or discovery motivation.

A gull lands on a sill, table, sign, or nearby crate. It opens its beak and a weary human voice emerges:

> “This is a message from Lucien Skyhorn, from the lighthouse. We’ve been attacked by creatures from the sea. Please—send help before it’s too late. I beg any who hears this…”

Then the gull returns to being a gull.

### NPC reactions

- Benji goes completely still.
- A skeptical sailor stops smiling.
- Yrsa says, “Well. That narrows the argument.”
- Someone in the room immediately asks whether “creatures from the sea” means the rumored monster.
- Another points out Lucien said **creatures**, plural.

### GM use

This should shift the room from speculation toward action without solving the threat.

## Expected Player Actions

### Interview every witness

Let them gather texture, then summarize once repetition sets in:

> “After another twenty minutes, the stories stop adding new facts. The reliable core is the same: the *Jade Lion* went toward Skyhorn, the water behaved unnaturally, and the light has been failing.”

### Ask whether anyone else will go

Nobody with a normal commercial reason wants to risk it. Sheila is the exception.

### Recruit townspeople

Most civilians decline. A few offer supplies or information. Do not swell the party with disposable NPC combatants.

### Demand official authority

The watch can give informal permission to investigate but is not needed for the adventure. Avoid turning this into paperwork.

### Ignore Benji

The world continues. Benji seeks someone else, but Sheila may approach the party independently because of Aryn/Lucien.
