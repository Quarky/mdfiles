# 02 - GUNDBARG LIVING WORLD STATE

## The Settlement Today

Gundbarg has not collapsed. That matters. A frightened but functioning town feels more alive than a place where everyone waits for heroes.

The harbor crisis has created **friction**, not paralysis:

- Fisher crews are arguing over whether to work close to shore.
- Long-distance captains refuse routes that take them near Skyhorn.
- Dockworkers have fewer ships to unload and are losing wages.
- Merchants are paying storage costs on cargo that cannot move.
- Families with sailors overdue are becoming angry at officials who can offer no answers.
- Inns are fuller than normal because crews cannot leave.
- Food is still available, but prices on imported goods are beginning to creep upward.
- Local boats continue short runs inside safer fjord waters, proving that people are afraid of **Skyhorn’s waters**, not of every patch of sea.
- The lighthouse beam is visible from parts of town. People pretend not to look at it and then look anyway.

## The Physical Feel

### READ ALOUD — First View of Gundbarg

> Grey cliffs rise on both sides of the harbor like walls built by something too old to remember builders. Dark water pushes into narrow finger-fjords between them. Sleet needles sideways across the piers. Northlander longships with carved prows share the harbor with southern cogs riding too high because their holds cannot be unloaded fast enough.
>
> The air smells of wet rope, pine smoke, tar, iron, and smoked fish. Somewhere above the harbor, a heavy bell sounds once from a watchtower.
>
> No one runs. No one screams. Gundbarg is still working.
>
> It is simply working with the strained silence of a place waiting for something else to go wrong.

## The Eye of the Storm

Locals call Skyhorn Lighthouse the **Eye of the Storm**. It is not a poetic tourist name; it is practical superstition born from generations of reliance.

Most locals believe some version of the following:

- If the Eye burns steadily, ships find their way home.
- If the Eye weakens, the sea is “looking back.”
- Experienced sailors will mock that saying in daylight and still touch iron when the beam skips.
- Younger locals use the expression casually; older captains do not.

### What the PCs can actually observe

From a clear enough harbor angle, a careful observer can see that the beam is **not fully extinguished**. It is weaker than expected and occasionally irregular.

A PC with a technical or magical mindset does not need a roll to conclude that “failing” and “cursed” are not the same diagnosis.

## Widow’s Shroud

Low fog over the harbor is called **the Widow’s Shroud**.

The name is folk vocabulary, not proof of supernatural danger. Use it to show culture:

- Dockworkers use the phrase as weather shorthand.
- Tourists and southern merchants repeat it too dramatically.
- Northlander sailors become annoyed when outsiders treat every bank of fog as a ghost story.
- After dark, even skeptics watch the water longer than they need to.

## What the Crisis Costs Ordinary People

Use one or two of these details whenever the scene needs grounding:

- A dockworker calculates aloud how many meals a lost day’s wage represents.
- A fish seller cuts prices late because unsold fish will spoil.
- A southern merchant loudly complains about storage fees while two families nearby listen with obvious contempt.
- A child asks when a parent’s ship is coming back and gets no answer.
- A sailmaker has work repairing idle sails but knows that is temporary comfort.
- An innkeeper runs out of cheap rooms because crews are stranded.
- A healer has received more small injuries from anxious dock laborers rushing work around understaffed crews.
- A local priest or shrine keeper has more offerings than usual and fewer coins in the poor box.

These are especially useful for **Handsome Dan, Sister Mara, Rurik, Cadwallon, and Aelar**.

## Optional Living-World NPCs

These are **session additions**, not required source canon. Rename freely. They exist to prevent every conversation from routing through the four core NPCs.

### Yrsa Veld — Proprietor of the Cracked Cask

- Broad-shouldered Northlander woman in her fifties.
- Keeps a rag tucked into her belt and remembers who has paid.
- Believes panic is bad for business but lying to customers is worse.
- Does not give free drinks to heroes, only to people who come back alive.
- Knows Benji has been asking everyone for help.
- Knows Sheila has not left because she wants capable backup, not because she is afraid.

**Voice:** dry, economical.

**Line:** “Drink, don’t drink. Help, don’t help. Just decide before the harbor freezes from indecision.”

### Eirik Ropehand — Dock Foreman

- Thick beard full of sleet and sawdust.
- Keeps three different work crews busy repairing things that do not urgently need repairing because idle workers get frightened.
- Knows which captains refused to sail and which did so reluctantly.
- Has answered too many of Borrik’s or Tark’s questions if either PC claimed that connection.

**Line:** “Wood breaks where you can see it. Men break where they think nobody’s looking.”

### Tova Arnsdottir — Fish Seller and Sailor’s Sister

- Has a brother aboard a different ship currently refusing to leave port.
- Thinks Benji’s wealth means he can make people listen faster, but does not resent his fear for Raf.
- Will challenge anyone who talks about “trade” as though that is the only thing at risk.

**Line:** “Cargo doesn’t write home. People do.”

### Old Njal Fen — Retired Lighthouse Supply Hand

- Missing two fingers to a winch accident.
- Delivered supplies to Skyhorn years ago.
- Remembers Lucien as “stubborn enough to argue with a storm.”
- Cannot confirm what is happening now.
- Gives Borrik/Tark useful mundane context: salt ruins everything eventually; the tower survives because someone is always maintaining it.

**Line:** “Lighthouses don’t fail all at once. First somebody stops replacing one little thing.”

### Jessa Bellward — Harbor Watch Runner

- Young, cold, and taking the crisis more seriously than her superiors.
- Carries messages between the watchtower and docks.
- Will appear if violence at the docks begins to spread.
- Has no interest in arresting heroes or pirates if everyone backs down; she wants bloodshed contained.

**Line:** “I don’t care who started it. I care what catches fire next.”
