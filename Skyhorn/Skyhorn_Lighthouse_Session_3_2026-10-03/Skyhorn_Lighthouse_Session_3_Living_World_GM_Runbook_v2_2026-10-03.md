## Session Start State

[MODULE][CAMPAIGN] Session 3 normally begins after the party finds the damaged *Jade Lion* and Raf tells them Captain Aryn Hest and the surviving crew were taken deeper into the caves. Do not reset the cave. Preserve the actual Session 2 state: escaped eelfolk, surviving sharks, treasure moved or left, Fennick status, party resources, Water Breathing duration, *Annabel Lee* condition, and Raf's location.

If Session 2 ended before Raf, run only the missing material needed to reach that information and then enter Scene 0. If Session 2 already entered Area 8, begin from the exact initiative/social state where play stopped.

## Five Facts to Keep Visible

1. The hostages are alive.
2. Stopping Area 8 runners changes Area 9, but failure does not end the adventure.
3. Grigor is being coerced; he is not the villain.
4. The summoning completes at the end of Grigor's fourth turn of unbroken concentration once the final confrontation is underway.
5. Returning to Gundbarg is part of the session's emotional payoff, not bookkeeping.

**Failure-forward rule:** a runner escaping, the ritual completing, lost cargo, or a damaged relationship changes the next scene; none of those outcomes should halt the story. Use harder states, visible consequences, and altered NPC trust rather than dead ends.

## Compact Pressure Track

- **Area 9 warning:** unaware → nervous → expecting trouble → warned → ritual completed before entry.
- **Grigor:** Turn 1 water rises; Turn 2 the chamber answers; Turn 3 a shape begins forming; Turn 4 completes at end of his turn.
- **Prisoners:** bound civilians remain physically present; danger rises if combat, area effects, or an elemental reaches them.
- **Evacuation:** Area 8 secured → first group moving → survivors aboard vessels → all living survivors accounted for.
- **Gundbarg:** lookouts notice → ships recognized → crowd forms → survivors disembark → story outruns facts.

## Player/GM Information Boundary

**Player-facing when encountered:** sensory descriptions, visible injuries, Aryn/Grigor/Sheila/Benji/Raf dialogue, obvious room geometry, visible bowl behavior, public harbor reaction, and earned reward descriptions.

**GM-only until learned:** exact ritual timing, passive Perception 13 for the scourge, treasure values, bowl removal consequence, enemy morale logic, NPC private motives/fears, future hooks, and the consequence ledger. Do not expose production/provenance labels to players.

## Full Scene Walkthrough

1. Start on the exact image where Session 2 ended.
2. Ask who has Raf and what immediate promise or instruction is given to him.
3. Re-state only the sensory change in the cave, not a full recap.
4. Move through cleared space quickly; show one or two consequences of prior choices.
5. If sharks survived, keep them animals rather than forcing another set-piece fight.
6. If Fennick is active, decide what he can plausibly do with the elapsed time.
7. Before Area 8, give the party one short preparation beat.
8. End when the door opens, the party announces itself, or a scout reveals the captive room.

## Purpose

Do not make the walk through already-cleared caves feel like a second dungeon crawl.

The party has a destination now.

Use the route to show consequence and urgency.

## If Sharks Survived

They remain animals. If blood or noise draws them, they may shadow the rowboats. They do not need another full encounter unless the party re-enters the water carelessly.

## If Treasure Was Left Behind

It remains where the players left it unless someone had enough time and access to move it.

## If Fennick Is Active

The smuggler may leave, hide, or try to recover evidence of his deliveries. He does not suddenly become an eelfolk lieutenant.

## Quiet Details

Use one or two:
- small pieces of broken rope drifting from deeper inside;
- fresh blood on an oarlock;
- distant Eelfolk speech that vanishes under the tide;
- a lantern flame leaning toward a passage before the draft is felt;
- one human boot print in wet sand and no second print.

Do not turn every detail into a clue.

# Scene 1 — Area 8: Captive Crew and Captain Aryn

**Map / Foundry:** `Skyhorn Lighthouse Caves`, Area 8. Key zones are the entry, three prisoner groups, the two guards, the runner lane, and the Area 9 door. Put bound civilians on the map if area effects or movement will matter.


**Provenance:** [MODULE] core hostage encounter; [CAMPAIGN] PC hooks; [ADDITION] living-world crew detail/dialogue.

## Quick-Glance Scene Card

| Field | At the table |
|---|---|
| Scene goal | Rescue twelve sailors while stopping two guards from warning Area 9 |
| Active NPCs | Aryn, twelve commoners, two eelfolk guards |
| Main threat | Both guards run for Area 9 |
| Main choice | Block runners, free captives, fight, talk, or divide effort |
| Exit state | Warning level, crew condition, and Aryn's plan |

## Full Scene Walkthrough

1. Describe captives as distinct people in groups, not one hostage token.
2. Show the two guards' proximity to the Area 9 route.
3. The instant the party is identified as rescuers, guards try to warn the next chamber.
4. Aryn uses his body and position to trip a runner who passes close; he is unarmed, not passive.
5. Let PCs prioritize: runner control, guard defeat, restraints, shielding captives, or securing exits.
6. Captives who are freed do not all become combatants. Some help others; some freeze; some move when commanded clearly.
7. Once immediate danger passes, Aryn gives the urgent fact first: Grigor is being forced to summon another elemental.
8. Count survivors, identify serious injuries, distribute simple tasks, and keep the two-minute decision visible if warning passed.
9. Aryn answers tactical questions quickly and postpones biography.
10. End with a clear decision: Area 9 now, Area 8A, triage, or retreat.

# Scene 2 — Area 8A: Plunder and the Two-Minute Window

**Map / Foundry:** same cave scene. Area 8A is an unlocked side room; keep the visible route back to Area 8 and the route toward Area 9 clear so time and carrying distance are legible.


**Provenance:** [MODULE] 5,000 gp trade goods and ritual urgency; [ADDITION] logistics and reaction framing.

## Quick-Glance Scene Card

| Field | At the table |
|---|---|
| Scene goal | Make cargo recovery compete honestly with ritual urgency |
| Value | 5,000 gp in Jade Lion trade goods |
| Door | Unlocked from Area 8 |
| Pressure | If warned, Area 9 can complete ritual after about two minutes |
| Exit state | Cargo touched/marked/moved and elapsed time |

## Full Scene Walkthrough

1. Make the unlocked door and visible cargo obvious; do not hide the moral/logistical choice.
2. Aryn identifies it as ship cargo but immediately prioritizes the ritual.
3. Describe goods as chests, barrels, and trade inventory—not piles of coins.
4. Let the party inspect or mark cargo quickly without forcing full recovery.
5. If they begin moving it, state weight, distance, boats, and time.
6. If Area 9 was warned, announce the time pressure through sound, Aryn, or ritual signs.
7. Do not pause the ritual while players debate.
8. Smart preparation—unbarred route, crew assigned, magical transport—can reduce later recovery work.
9. End when they commit to the final chamber or accept the consequence of delay.

## Aryn's Position

> "That's ours. It can stay ours for two more minutes. Grigor may not have two."

## Living World

- Freed sailors can begin organization, but exhausted commoners cannot silently move 5,000 gp of goods instantly.
- Ritual sound or water response grows if time advances.
- If the party chooses cargo first, record that choice without moral narration; show consequences through the next scene.

If Area 9 was warned, the party has roughly two minutes before the source's forced summoning can complete and an elemental can crash back into Area 8.

# Scene 3 — Area 9: The Summoning Room

**Map / Foundry:** `Skyhorn Lighthouse Caves`, Area 9. Make the entry lane, stalagmite/ruin half-cover, hunter positions, scourge position, Grigor, fixed bowl, and route back toward Area 8 immediately readable.


**Provenance:** [MODULE] final encounter, Grigor, scourge, ritual and elemental; [ADDITION] round signals/dialogue.

## Quick-Glance Scene Card

| Field | At the table |
|---|---|
| Scene goal | Stop the scourge, save Grigor, and prevent or survive the summoning |
| Active NPCs | Eelfolk scourge, eelfolk, hunters, Grigor |
| Environment | Stalagmites, ruined circular chamber, half cover, fixed bowl |
| Ritual | Completes at end of Grigor's fourth turn of unbroken concentration |
| Main choices | Attack, interrupt, reassure/remove Grigor, control cover, protect captives |
| Exit state | Threats, elemental, Grigor, bowl, and survivors recorded |

## Full Scene Walkthrough

1. Choose the unalerted or alerted opening based on actual warning.
2. Describe the chamber geometry, bowl, defenders, and Grigor before initiative.
3. Show coercion physically: his posture, injuries, counting, and fear of stopping.
4. If unalerted, reward the party's success with position and surprise according to the established state.
5. If alerted, defenders use half cover and readied actions; do not add creatures.
6. Start the four-turn ritual clock where the fiction requires. Make the count visible to the GM and legible through description to players.
7. The scourge pressures a weak-looking target; ordinary eelfolk gang stronger threats; hunters throw from cover for three rounds before entering melee.
8. Allow many ways to stop Grigor: reassurance once punishment is blocked, forced movement, grapple, incapacitation, concentration break, or severing access.
9. Threatening him can work mechanically but makes rescuers another source of fear and changes aftermath trust.
10. If the elemental appears, determine whether Grigor maintains control. Each round carries the published 30% loss chance; loss makes him its immediate hostile focus.
11. Use the room as three simultaneous problems: defenders, ritual, and vulnerable people.
12. End only after immediate hostile action stops and someone checks whether Grigor is alive.

>
> At the center, a bowl sits in cracked alabaster tile, filled with water that moves though the air is still.

## Elemental Consequences

- While control holds, the elemental acts under coercion.
- If control breaks, Grigor becomes its first target.
- An elemental may crash into Area 8, endangering captives.
- The party can still separate Grigor, protect him, or break the combat's logic instead of only reducing hit points.

## Eelfolk Behavior

## 26 - EELFOLK BEHAVIOR REFERENCE

### General

Eelfolk in this adventure are aggressive shoreline raiders.

They are comfortable in wet spaces and use the caves as home terrain.

They are not mindless.

### When Winning

- press isolated targets;
- use numbers;
- hunters keep distance;
- try to drive intruders away from routes downward.

### When Losing

- fall back;
- seek water;
- warn deeper allies;
- abandon loot;
- surrender only when escape is impossible and communication exists.

### What They Value

In the context of this raid:

- food;
- plunder;
- control of the bowl;
- territorial dominance;
- survival.

### What They Fear

- uncontrolled water elementals;
- fire in confined areas;
- being cut off from water;
- stronger leaders;
- the scourge below;
- Lucien's magic after repeated failed attempts to reach him.

### What They Know About Humans

Enough to recognize weapons, doors, ships, cargo, prisoners, and bargaining behavior.

Do not play them as animals wearing equipment.

### Language Barrier

Most speak Eelfolk.

If no PC understands it, gestures and combat intent still communicate basic ideas.

Translation magic should open new options.
