# 00 - SESSION 3 DASHBOARD

## What This Session Covers

Session 3 begins after the party has found the damaged *Jade Lion* and learned from Raf that Captain Aryn Hest and the surviving crew were taken farther into the caves.

This is the rescue-and-resolution session. Session 1 was uncertainty. Session 2 was discovery. Session 3 is about people who are still alive, a ritual already underway, and choices that cannot all be delayed until the party feels perfectly ready.

The core sequence is:

1. Return from the *Jade Lion* toward the occupied cave interior.
2. Reach Area 8 and find the surviving crew.
3. Prevent the two guards from warning Area 9 if possible.
4. Learn that another summoning is already in progress.
5. Decide whether to free everyone first or move immediately.
6. Enter Area 9 and stop Grigor's forced ritual.
7. Defeat or scatter the remaining eelfolk.
8. Decide what to do with the Bowl of Commanding Water Elementals.
9. Get the survivors, cargo, and ships back under control.
10. Return to Gundbarg and let the living world react.

## Recommended Endpoint

For a normal 4.5–5.5 hour session, finish with the party back in **Gundbarg**.

Do not rush the return. The emotional payoff is not only that the monsters are defeated. It is Benji seeing Raf alive, Sheila seeing Aryn walk off the *Jade Lion*, exhausted sailors reaching familiar ground, and Gundbarg learning that the danger strangling its harbor was real and has been stopped.

A good final image:

> By the time the ships reach Gundbarg, the fog has lifted enough for the Eye of the Storm to be seen behind them.
>
> The beam crosses the mouth of the fjord once.
>
> This time, nobody mistakes it for a warning.

Then let the players say what their characters do next.

## Alternate Endpoints by Runtime

| Available play time | Good endpoint |
|---|---|
| 2.5–3 hours | Stop after Area 9 and the bowl decision |
| 3.5–4 hours | Finish rescue and ship evacuation; end as the convoy leaves Skyhorn |
| 4.5–5.5 hours | Return to Gundbarg and resolve Benji, Sheila, Aryn, and public aftermath |
| 6+ hours | Add downtime conversations, personal-hook follow-through, and campaign hooks |

- Aryn Hest is alive among the prisoners.

# 01 - SESSION 3 ONE-MINUTE QUICK REFERENCE

## Area 8 — Hostages

Present:

- twelve human commoner sailors tied in groups of four;
- Captain Aryn Hest;
- two eelfolk guards.

The guards try to reach Area 9. Aryn is unarmed and injured, but he tries to trip a guard that runs close to him.

- Door from Area 8 is unlocked.

## Area 9 — Summoning Room

Features:

- clustered stalagmites;
- ruined circular chamber;
- broken tower crenellations;
- half cover from stone and stalagmites;
- Bowl of Commanding Water Elementals set into cracked alabaster tile;
- eelfolk scourge;
- eelfolk and hunters per Foundry/map placement;
- Grigor kneeling at the bowl.

Grigor uses the **Bandit** chassis with no weapons. He completes the summoning at the end of his fourth turn of unbroken concentration.

The eelfolk scourge closes on a vulnerable-looking PC. Basic eelfolk gang up on strong melee characters. Hunters use cover and throw javelins for about three rounds before joining melee if the battle still supports that plan.

## Aftermath

### Bowl

If pried out:
- it loses all magical properties.

If the party tries to take it:
- Lucien is angry;
- he gives only a terse thank-you and goodbye;
- no Mariner's Coin reward.

If left in place:
- Lucien gives each PC one Mariner's Coin.

### Mariner's Coin

Hammered copper coin stamped with a shining lighthouse.

When presented to a non-hostile sailor, it grants advantage on one Charisma check to influence that sailor.

The source does not say the coin is consumed. Do not invent charges unless you deliberately house-rule them.

### Jade Lion

Aryn can get the surviving crew and cargo aboard by rowboat and sail the ship back to port for repairs.

### Return

- *Annabel Lee* can sail home.
- Benji pays 200 gp per PC for Raf's safe return.
- He pays another 200 gp per PC for surviving crew and recovered goods.
- The original adventure has Benji send four soldiers and one captain to repair and guard Skyhorn afterward.
- In the Gundbarg version, treat that as a locally organized guard detail funded or arranged by Benji unless your campaign already gives him private soldiers.

# 03 - SESSION 3 PRESSURE CLOCKS

## Clock A — Area 9 Warning

Start at **0** if no eelfolk escaped from Session 2.

Start at **1** if the party has been loud in the caves.

Start at **2** if an eelfolk runner already escaped toward the final rooms.

### 0 — Unaware

Area 8 guards are inattentive enough to be caught off guard. Area 9 focuses on Grigor and the bowl.

### 1 — Nervous

Area 8 guards watch the routes. Area 9 still performs the ritual normally.

### 2 — Expecting Trouble

Area 8 guards run immediately when they see the party. Area 9 responds quickly if warned.

### 3 — Warned

Area 9 takes cover and prepares. Begin the two-minute pre-summoning window.

### 4 — Ritual Completed Before Entry

A water elemental has already been summoned. It can move into Area 8 under eelfolk coercion.

Do not add a second elemental unless the fiction and bowl usage clearly justify it.

## Clock B — Grigor's Ritual

Once Area 9 combat begins and Grigor is still performing the ritual:

- **Turn 1** — first stage.
- **Turn 2** — water rises and reacts.
- **Turn 3** — the bowl begins to pull sea-water unnaturally.
- **Turn 4** — ritual completes at the end of Grigor's turn.

If concentration is broken before completion, the ritual stops.

"Halted" can mean:

- physically pulled from the bowl;
- knocked unconscious;
- reassured that the eelfolk cannot punish him;
- restrained;
- convinced to stop;
- concentration broken by combat;
- magic that prevents the action.

Do not require the party to damage him.

## Clock C — Prisoner Safety

Start at **2 — exhausted but alive**.

Pressure rises if:
- combat spills directly into tied prisoner groups;
- area effects catch them;
- an elemental reaches Area 8;
- nobody cuts them loose while the room becomes unsafe.

Safety improves if:
- Mara or another healer triages;
- groups are untied;
- someone escorts them toward the cove;
- weapons are kept away from panicked captives until people can think clearly.

This is not a hidden hit-point tax. It is a reminder that twelve bound civilians occupy the battlefield.

## Clock D — Evacuation

- **0:** all still tied / trapped.
- **1:** Area 8 secured.
- **2:** first group moving toward Area 7.
- **3:** survivors aboard *Jade Lion* or moving toward *Annabel Lee*.
- **4:** all living survivors accounted for.

Aryn can manage much of this once freed.

## Clock E — Tide

Use tide for atmosphere and logistics:
- stronger pull through cove;
- rowboats knocking against dock;
- louder surf masking speech;
- harder cargo movement.

Do not change map geometry mid-combat without warning.

## Clock F — Gundbarg Response

This starts once a returning ship is visible in the fjord.

1. Lookouts notice.
2. A ship is recognized.
3. Crowd begins to form.
4. Survivors disembark.
5. Story spreads faster than facts.

# 14 - SCENE 2: AREA 9 — THE SUMMONING ROOM

Run the climax as three overlapping problems:

1. Stop the ritual.
2. Survive the eelfolk.
3. Keep Grigor alive if possible.

## Terrain

Important features:
- stalagmite clusters;
- ruined round chamber;
- old tower crenellations;
- half cover;
- bowl at rear;
- damp uneven stone.

Use Foundry placement for exact actors.

## Bowl Visual Clock

As ritual advances:
- Turn 1: surface trembles.
- Turn 2: water rises without spilling.
- Turn 3: cold mist forms and nearby puddles creep toward the bowl.
- Turn 4: summoning completes.

## Scourge

Opening instinct:
- close on a vulnerable-looking target;
- protect the ritual;
- disrupt casters.

After the first round, let it react to actual threats.

## Basic Eelfolk

They try to gang up on strong melee targets or whoever blocks access to Grigor.

## Hunters

Use cover and javelins.

Published behavior says they throw for three rounds before joining melee, but they can react if trapped or if the battlefield collapses.

## Grigor

Turn 1:
- counts;
- hands shaking.

Turn 2:
- hears the fight;
- does not look away.

Turn 3:
- may hesitate if someone directly convinces him the scourge cannot punish him.

Turn 4:
- summoning completes if uninterrupted.

## Ways to Stop Him

- pull him away;
- break concentration;
- restrain;
- knock unconscious;
- calm him;
- convince him;
- silence/control magic;
- physically block ritual access.

Do not require damage.

Threatening him can also work, but it tells Grigor the rescuers are another group using fear. That should matter afterward.

## If a PC Attacks the Bowl

The source says removal from the ground kills its magic, but it does not give artifact HP.

Do not invent a hidden durability system unless needed.

Describe damage honestly and decide whether the action disrupts Grigor based on what was actually struck.

# 15 - GRIGOR

## Published State

Grigor is a chaotic-neutral human sailor using the **Bandit** chassis with no weapons.

He is browbeaten, frightened, and continuing the ritual because he expects torture if he stops.

Run him as a captive caught inside the enemy's plan, not as a collaborator.

## Appearance

Useful campaign detail:

- salt-stiff hair;
- split lip;
- rope burns at the wrists;
- bruised knees from repeated ritual posture;
- pale fingertips from gripping the bowl's rim;
- torn *Jade Lion* sailor clothing.

These are presentation details, not a new mechanical condition set.

## First Reaction When the Ritual Stops

Do not have him instantly deliver exposition.

Pick one:

- crawls backward from the bowl;
- covers his head;
- vomits;
- keeps counting for several seconds;
- asks whether the scourge is really dead.

## If Someone Says "You're Safe"

He looks toward the scourge first.

That tells the table more than a speech.

## If the Elemental Is Already Summoned

Grigor is terrified of losing control because he knows what uncontrolled elementals do.

A useful line:

> "Don't hit me."

He means that breaking his concentration may turn the elemental loose on him.

## If Concentration Breaks

The elemental becomes hostile toward Grigor.

That does not make it friendly to anyone else.

Its immediate fury is directed at him.

## After Combat

Whoever stopped him shapes his reaction:

- calm instruction: trust comes fastest;
- physical restraint: shaken but understands;
- knocked unconscious: confused on waking;
- threatened into stopping: compliant but afraid;
- injured intentionally: wary even after rescue.

## What He Knows

- the eelfolk forced him through the ritual;
- the scourge punished resistance;
- elementals can become uncontrollable;
- other captives were threatened when he resisted;
- he does not understand the bowl's history.

## What He Does Not Know

- how the bowl was made;
- whether it can be safely dismantled;
- full eelfolk clan structure;
- whether a stormcaller leads a larger group.

# 16 - IF THE ELEMENTAL IS SUMMONED

## This Is a Harder State, Not a Failure State

Do not treat the completed ritual as "the party lost."

It changes the battlefield.

## Visual

> The bowl's surface drops all at once.
>
> Every puddle in the chamber pulls toward it.
>
> Then something vast answers from beyond the stone.

## Control

While Grigor maintains control, the elemental acts under coercion.

If control breaks, Grigor becomes its immediate target.

The party can:

- protect him;
- kill or banish the elemental;
- move him;
- restrain or redirect;
- exploit the elemental's movement through the eelfolk if the geometry supports it.

Do not turn it into a friendly ally.

## If It Reaches Area 8

Bound or recently freed prisoners are in danger.

Aryn starts moving people immediately.

This is the consequence of whether the party created evacuation space before chasing the ritual.

# 19 - THE BOWL OF COMMANDING WATER ELEMENTALS

## What the Party Sees

> The bowl is broad, low, and set directly into cracked alabaster tile.
>
> The water inside it is clear now.
>
> Nothing moves.
>
> For the first time since entering the caves, it looks like an ordinary basin.

## Source Facts

- It is fixed in place.
- It loses all magic if pried out of the ground.
- Lucien strongly objects to anyone trying to take it.

## If They Inspect It

Appropriate magical study can establish:

- powerful elemental/conjuration magic;
- enchantment integrated with this location;
- connection to surrounding sea;
- evidence of recent repeated use.

The source does not provide a portable item stat block.

Do not invent one automatically.

## If They Remove It

Tell them what that physically means:

- lever it up;
- crack surrounding alabaster;
- break the fixed installation.

If successfully removed:

- it becomes nonmagical.

## If They Want to Destroy It In Place

The source does not give a result.

Treat that as an actual campaign decision rather than pretending the published adventure answered it.

Three sensible approaches:

### Conservative
Lucien asks them to stop and study it before damaging an unknown fixed enchantment.

### Destructive
Allow destruction to end the local magic if you deliberately want that outcome.

### Future Quest
Lucien agrees the bowl should be decommissioned, but wants a safe method researched.

This is strong follow-up material for Lethiel, Borrik, Tark, Elira, or Kreslin.

## If They Want to Take It

Once pried loose, it is nonmagical.

That fact should resolve most "we take the artifact" plans.
