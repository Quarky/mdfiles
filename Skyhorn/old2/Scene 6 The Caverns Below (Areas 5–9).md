# Scene 6: The Caverns Below (Areas 5–9)

## General Cave Notes (Official)

- **Water depth:** 50 feet deep in most areas.
- **Ceiling height:** 40 feet above water level.
- **Visibility:** Dim light out to 30 feet due to refracting light from cave openings and bioluminescent fungi. Beyond 30 feet, heavily obscured (effectively blind).
- **Swimming:** Each foot of movement costs 1 extra foot for creatures without a swim speed.
- **Holding breath:** A creature can hold its breath for 1 + Constitution modifier minutes (minimum 30 seconds).
- **Underwater combat:** Ranged weapon attacks have disadvantage unless using a crossbow, net, or thrown weapon (dagger, javelin, spear, trident). Melee attacks have disadvantage unless the creature has a swim speed or uses a dagger, javelin, shortsword, spear, or trident.

## Area 5: Something in the Deep

### Immersive Area Description

The trap door in Area 3 opens onto a rusty iron ladder, descending 10 feet into darkness. The air changes — becomes cold, damp, heavy with the smell of salt and decay. The sound of dripping water echoes.

**The Cavern:** You lower yourself into a vast natural chamber. The ceiling arches 40 feet above, studded with stalactites. Bioluminescent fungi cling to the walls, casting a faint blue-green glow. The water is black and still, lapping against a rocky shore.

**The Depths:** The water is 50 feet deep. Somewhere below, shapes move. Sharks. The eelfolk have been feeding them.

*[Enhancement] Gnawed human bones are scattered on a rocky ledge near the water — the remains of hostages the eelfolk killed. A torn sailor's shirt floats in the shallows. The sharks' dorsal fins break the surface occasionally, circling.*

### NPCs (Official + Enhancement)

- **3 Hunter Sharks** (marked S on map). They lurk in deeper water, investigating sounds. They have been fed human hostages, so they associate the smell of blood with food.
- *[Enhancement] One shark is larger and has a scarred dorsal fin — the pack leader. It's more aggressive (+2 to hit).*

### Treasure (Official)

- **4 chests** (marked T on map) stacked on a cave floor ledge. Carrying a chest halves movement speed.
- **Contents:** 400 gp worth of fine goods (bottled perfumes, silverware, small statues, jewelry).
- **A worn, black-hilted dagger** that is actually a **Dagger of Venom** (DMG). When drawn, a faint green sheen appears on the blade.

### Example Player Interactions

- **Avoiding sharks:** Stealth vs. sharks' passive Perception (use shark stats: Perception +3, passive 13). Moving slowly (half speed) grants advantage.
- **Distraction:** Throwing a corpse or raw meat (from the chests?) into the water lures sharks to a different area for 1d4 rounds.
- **Fighting sharks:** They use Blood Frenzy — advantage on attacks against wounded creatures. They flee to the ocean (via Area 7) if reduced below 15 HP each.
- **Retrieving chests:** A character carrying a chest moves at half speed and cannot swim effectively (disadvantage on Athletics checks). Other characters can help (two characters can carry one chest at full speed).

## Area 6: The Dock

### Immersive Area Description

A small sandbar rises out of the water, littered with shells and seaweed. A rickety wooden dock — planks rotted in places — extends into the cave. Two rowboats are tied to it, bumping gently against each other.

Against the cave wall, the crumbling remains of an ancient tower jut out — 20 feet of moss-covered stone, half-swallowed by the cave floor. Stairs (what remains of them) lead up to a dark opening.

*[Enhancement] Faded murals on the tower wall depict sea creatures — merfolk, krakens, and eelfolk — locked in battle. The eyes of the painted eelfolk seem to follow viewers. A DC 12 Intelligence (History) check reveals this is an old Skyhorn family outpost, abandoned centuries ago.*

### Climbing the Tower Wall

DC 13 Strength (Athletics) to climb the crumbling wall. Success allows access to a small alcove (empty) or a vantage point over the cave.

## Area 7: The Merchant Ship

### Immersive Area Description

Beyond the cave mouth, open sea is visible — grey sky, churning water. The *Jade Lion* lists in the narrow cove, its hull scraping against jagged rocks. One mast is snapped, its sails trailing in the water like broken wings.

The ship has been looted. Crates of exotic fruits (smashed), bolts of fine cloth (torn and soaked), and barrels of wine (staved in) litter the deck. No crew. No bodies. Just the creak of wood and the slap of waves.

**The Tide:** A strong current flows through the 10-foot-wide channel leading to the ship. Swimming against it requires a DC 13 Strength (Athletics) check. Failure by 5 or more deals 7 (2d6) bludgeoning damage from being slammed into the rocks.

### NPC: Raf Frogwater (CG halfling commoner)

**Location:** Hiding in a secret cabinet in the captain's quarters. DC 14 Intelligence (Investigation) to find. He approaches the PCs upon hearing them speak a language he understands (Common, Halfling).

**Immersive Description:** A small boy with big green eyes and a face full of freckles. His clothes are torn and damp. He's holding a slingshot with shaking hands. He tries to puff out his chest but his lip trembles.

**Dialogue:** *“Those horrible eel-men, they took my friends! My Pa will get them for it!”* (His voice cracks.)

**Raf's Information:**
- A gigantic wave grabbed the ship and dragged it into the cove.
- The “eel-men” ransacked the ship and took the crew inside the cave, including Captain Aryn Hest.
- One of the eel-men was bigger and angrier than the others. He bossed them around. (The Scourge.)
- There were about 10 eelfolk total.
- One or two big waves have passed by each day. They make the ship bob and spin.
- Raf sometimes hears human screams and terrible crashes echoing in the caves.

*[Enhancement] Raf has a crude map of the caves drawn in charcoal on a torn sail. It shows the dock (Area 6), the main cave (Area 5), and a room with “screams” (Area 8). DC 10 Intelligence (Investigation) to interpret.*

### Treasure (Official)

- Silver candle holder (25 gp) — wedged behind a barrel.
- Gold ring in a mahogany lockbox (50 gp) — hidden under a loose floorboard in the captain's quarters (DC 12 Perception to find).

## Area 8: The Crew (Hostages)

### Immersive Area Description

The doors to this room are unlocked — the eelfolk didn't bother to lock them. Why would they? The hostages are beaten, starved, and too weak to run.

**The Room:** A large, natural cavern. The floor tiles are ancient stone, carved with a faded nautilus shell — the Skyhorn family crest. The air is cold and smells of fear, sweat, and blood.

**The Prisoners:** Twelve human commoners are lashed together in groups of four (marked P on map). They huddle together, shivering. Their wrists are raw from rope burns. Some have electrical burns — the eelfolk's idea of entertainment.

**Captain Aryn Hest** (CG human veteran) sits among them, his back against the wall. His hands and arms are covered in jagged electrical burns. He looks up when the party enters, squinting.

*[Enhancement] A single torch gutters in a sconce on the wall — the only light. The floor is wet. In the corner, a pile of coral caltrops (1d4 piercing if stepped on).*

### NPC: Captain Aryn Hest

**Immersive Description:** Trim, pointed beard, gold tooth. His voice is hoarse from screaming (eelfolk torture). He laughs — a belly laugh that rattles the windows, despite his condition. *“Bog me brains if I let a damn walking eel have me ship!”*

**Dialogue & Interactions:**
- **When freed:** *“Sheila sent you? Ha! That woman owes me a bottle of rum from ’09.”*
- **Warning about eelfolk:** *“They're clever. Don't let the hissing fool you. They set ambushes. And that big one — the Scourge — he'll rip your arm off and beat you with it.”*
- **About the summoning:** *“They've got Grigor in there. Poor sod. He's been chanting for hours. If he finishes, another elemental comes. You have to stop it.”*
- **About the eelfolk numbers:** *“There were ten. Maybe more. But some died when the elementals turned on them. Stupid eels can't control the things.”*

### The Eelfolk Guards (Official)

- **2 eelfolk (standard)** sit near the door, playing a violent game involving coral caltrops. They toss the caltrops onto the floor, then take turns stepping on them barefoot. The one who flinches loses.
- When they spot the characters, they bolt for the door to Area 9. They scream in Eelfolk: *“Intruders! To the summoner!”*

### Dramatic Question (Official)

*Can the party stop the eelfolk from alerting their companions in Area 9?*

### The Eelfolk Alert the Others (If Guards Succeed)

- All eelfolk in Area 9 take cover behind stalactites or rubble. They ready actions to attack when the PCs enter.
- The Scourge positions itself near the bowl, ready to protect the summoner.

### The Eelfolk Don't Alert the Others (If Guards Fail)

- The party may set up a surprise round in Area 9.
- The eelfolk in Area 9 are too engrossed in the summoning ritual to hear combat in Area 8 (unless there's an explosion or very loud noise).

### Time Pressure

If the characters wait any longer than two minutes (12 rounds) after entering Area 8, the sailor in Area 9 (Grigor) is forced to summon a water elemental. The elemental crashes into Area 8 to attack the PCs.

**Concentration risk:** Each round after the elemental appears, there is a 30% chance Grigor loses concentration. If that happens, the elemental becomes hostile to him — and to the eelfolk.

### Area 8a: Plunder

A side alcove off Area 8. The eelfolk have stacked chests and barrels of plunder from the *Jade Lion* here.

**Treasure:** 5,000 gp worth of trade goods (silk, spices, fine wines, tools). Too heavy to carry easily, but the party can return later with a ship.

## Area 9: Summoning Room

### Immersive Area Description

Clusters of stalagmites rise from the cave floor like stone teeth. The ceiling is low here — maybe 15 feet. The air crackles with magical energy.

**The Bowl:** At the back of the cavern, set into cracked alabaster tiles, is the Bowl of Commanding Water Elementals. It's a shallow basin, 3 feet across, carved from a single piece of obsidian. Water sloshes inside it, glowing with faint blue light.

**The Ritual:** An eelfolk scourge (large, scarred, crackling with electricity) screams in Eelfolk over a terrified sailor kneeling before the bowl. The sailor — Grigor — is chanting, his voice shaking. His hands grip the bowl's rim.

**Other Eelfolk:** Scattered around the room, watching. Some hold javelins. Others crouch behind stalagmites for cover.

*[Enhancement] The bowl's water ripples with each word of the chant. A low hum fills the air. The scourge's eyes glow blue — the same color as the bowl.*

### NPCs (Official)

- **Eelfolk Scourge (ES)** — Large, angry, covered in electrical scars.
- **3-4 standard eelfolk (E)** — Positioned near the entrance.
- **2 eelfolk hunters (H)** — Hiding behind stalagmites, javelins ready.
- **Grigor (S)** — CN bandit (no weapons). Terrified, single-mindedly summoning.

### Combat Tactics (Official + Enhancement)

- **Scourge (ES):** Moves into melee against a weak-looking PC (spellcaster or wounded). Uses Multiattack (bite + two claws). Tries to shove PCs into the bowl (DC 14 Strength save or fall prone and take 1d4 acid damage from the water).
- **Standard eelfolk (E):** Gang up on stronger characters, try to surround. They use Lunge when possible.
- **Hunters (H):** Hurl javelins from cover (half cover from stalagmites, +2 AC). They throw javelins for three rounds, then join melee.
- **Grigor:** Continues summoning. He completes the ritual at the end of his **4th turn** of unbroken concentration. If he's distracted (damage, loud noise), he must make a DC 10 Constitution save or lose concentration. If he loses it twice, he breaks down sobbing and cannot continue.

### The Summoning Clock

- **Turn 1:** Grigor chants. The bowl glows brighter.
- **Turn 2:** The water in the bowl begins to swirl. The air grows cold.
- **Turn 3:** A watery tendril rises from the bowl. The elemental is almost here.
- **Turn 4 (end):** A water elemental manifests. It attacks the nearest creature (likely Grigor or a PC).

### Dramatic Question (Official)

*Can the characters stop the summoning ritual in time and defeat the eelfolk?*

### Example Player Interactions

- **Stop Grigor:** Grappling him (escape DC 12), knocking him unconscious, or casting *silence* on the bowl prevents the summoning.
- **Destroy the bowl:** The bowl has AC 15, 30 HP, and resistance to nonmagical weapons. Destroying it ends the ritual but angers Lucien (see Aftermath).
- **Kill the scourge first:** Without the leader, the other eelfolk may flee or surrender (DC 15 Intimidation).
- **Use the environment:** Stalagmites can be pushed over (DC 15 Strength) to create difficult terrain or crush enemies (3d6 bludgeoning, Dex save DC 12 for half).

## DM Prep Help

- **Underwater combat cheat sheet:** Print one for each player.
- **Shark tactics:** They circle and attack wounded prey. Use their Blood Frenzy.
- **Summoning timer:** Use a visible countdown (4 rounds). Build tension.
- **Map:** Draw the cave complex with Areas 5, 6, 7, 8, 9 connected. Mark S (sharks), T (treasure), P (prisoners), and positions of eelfolk.
- **Music:** Dark, ambient cave sounds. For the summoning room, add a low drone or heartbeat.

## GM Notes

- **Pacing:** The caves are the climax. Don't rush. Let the tension build from sharks to hostages to the final ritual.
- **Shark encounter:** If the party is low on resources, the sharks can be avoided with stealth. Reward clever play.
- **Raf:** He's a liability. Encourage the party to leave him in a safe spot (e.g., the rowboat in Area 6) before proceeding.
- **Aryn Hest:** He can't fight, but he can give advice. Use him to steer the party toward Area 9.
- **The ritual:** If the party is struggling, have Grigor lose concentration early. The elemental appears but attacks the eelfolk first — a chaotic three-way fight.
- **After the fight:** The party can talk to Grigor (if alive). He's grateful but traumatized. He can confirm the eelfolk's plans.
