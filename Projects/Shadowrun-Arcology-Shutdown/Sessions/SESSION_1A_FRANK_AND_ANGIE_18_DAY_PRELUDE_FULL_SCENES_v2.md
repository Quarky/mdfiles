# Session 1A — Frank and Angie: The Eighteen Days Before
## Full Living-World Scene Edition

**Campaign:** Shadowrun Genesys — Renraku Arcology: Shutdown  
**Period:** December 1–19, 2059  
**Use:** GM-facing playable prelude immediately before the established Shutdown session  
**Continuity anchor:** Frank and Angie first meet in Maintenance Node 7-G on December 9. The Shutdown begins December 19 at 19:02:09 PST.

---

## How this version is different

This is not a day-by-day synopsis. Each date below is written as material that can actually be run at the table. The GM gets a place to put the characters, what the location looks and sounds like, what unrelated people are doing, what NPCs want, lines of dialogue that can be used verbatim or adapted, what the anomaly is doing in the background, and how the world reacts to common player choices.

Do **not** narrate decisions for Frank or Angie. The GM establishes the situation and plays the world. The players decide whether they investigate, ignore, argue, help, leave, lie, call someone, or take a different route. The only fixed actions are the already agreed continuity anchors on December 9 and the established return to Node 7-G on December 19.

### Recurring NPC voices

**Hidoshi Tarada — Angie's father.** Precise, affectionate, never cloying. He worries by asking practical questions rather than ordering Angie around.  
*Typical line:* “I am not asking you to explain the impossible. I am asking whether you want to face it alone.”

**Niko Adebayo — electrician and maintenance coworker.** Dry humor, competent, hates paperwork, notices when equipment behaves too neatly.  
*Typical line:* “Machines fail messily. Paperwork fails neatly. This is doing both.”

**Eli Mercer — food-kiosk worker.** Remembers faces and preferences, talks to lonely people without making a production of it.  
*Typical line:* “You do not have to buy anything. Standing somewhere warm is still free.”

**Marisol Vance — resident and habitual pattern-noticer.** Sharp, observant, frequently dismissed as a complainer.  
*Typical line:* “Normal is what people call a noise after they stop listening to it.”

**Mina Ortega — clinic nurse.** Calm, clinical, humane. Refuses to claim certainty she does not have.  
*Typical line:* “I can tell you what this is not. Anyone promising more than that is selling comfort.”

**Evan Cho — junior Renraku security officer.** Earnest and procedural. Still believes rules are supposed to protect people.  
*Typical line:* “A valid order is not automatically a good order. It is still an order I have to explain ignoring.”

**Rowan Vale — licensed busker.** Wry, socially perceptive, delighted by sincere performance inside a monetized environment.  
*Typical line:* “Corporate acoustics make everyone sound expensive. You sound alive.”

**Sana Mori — small retail shop manager.** Knows regulars, employees, delivery rhythms, and what has subtly changed in the public floors.  
*Typical line:* “I do not know what the system thinks is happening. I know what came through my door.”

### Living-world rule

In every public scene, keep three layers moving even if the players ignore the anomaly:

1. **Someone working:** a cleaner changing filters, a courier arguing with routing, a clerk closing a register, a technician kneeling beside a panel.
2. **Someone living:** children racing projected fish, a couple choosing dinner, residents carrying laundry, teenagers taking pictures, an elderly man feeding decorative birds that are actually AR.
3. **A system organizing both:** doors opening before people reach them, price signs changing, a lift re-routing, a drone waiting politely, announcements translating themselves to match nearby listeners.

The Arcology must first feel like a place people chose to live, shop, work, and spend time. That is what makes the Shutdown a loss instead of merely a dungeon door closing.

# December 1 — A City Under One Roof

## What the day should accomplish

Establish normality, scale, comfort, and the personal routines that the Shutdown will later destroy. There is no overt threat today. The oddities should be explainable as ordinary automation.

## Scene 1: Arrival Concourse — Angie's first morning

**Time:** 08:10  
**Cast:** Angie, Hidoshi, Eli in the background, concierge staff, commuters, families

> The rain ends at the doors as completely as if weather were an outdoor service the building has declined to purchase. Warm air rolls over wet coats. Coffee, citrus cleanser, soy broth, machine ozone, damp wool, and thousands of people blend into a smell dense enough to have geography.
>
> The concourse rises for stories. Transparent elevator cars slide up and down the central void. Projected koi scatter around luggage wheels, reform behind boots, and gather around a little girl who keeps stamping at them to make them burst into light.
>
> Nothing here feels improvised. Even the crowd seems to have lanes.

Give Hidoshi a temporary Renraku consultant credential and Angie a dependent visitor credential. A concierge drone greets Hidoshi by name, then turns to Angie. It pauses one fraction too long over her third eye before offering a cheerful accessibility menu clearly written for cyberoptic users.

Hidoshi does not answer for her. He glances from the drone to Angie and says, “You may enjoy correcting it more than I will.”

If Angie engages with the drone, it apologizes smoothly and offers a revised menu. If she ignores it, it follows for three meters before deciding her non-response counts as dismissal. If she jokes, nearby people relax; if she is irritated, Hidoshi lets the irritation be legitimate rather than smoothing it over.

### Things happening around them

- A corporate school group is being counted by facial recognition. One child keeps stepping out of line to chase the projected koi, forcing the teacher's counter back to fourteen.
- A courier curses quietly because a lift has reprioritized his delivery three times.
- Eli is opening his breakfast kiosk and giving away samples to people who look lost rather than hungry.
- A pair of tourists photograph the vertical atrium and nearly collide with a service drone that stops millimeters from their luggage.

Eli can offer Angie a sample. He watches her reaction and says, “Too much clove?” before she says anything. He did not psychically read her; he saw her nose wrinkle and knows his own food.

Hidoshi's work appointment begins in twenty minutes. He can offer Angie options without choosing for her:

- the indoor garden;
- the public Matrix lounge;
- the performance terrace;
- the retail galleries;
- simply walking and seeing where the building leads.

Useful Hidoshi dialogue:

> “My meetings will be dull even by legal standards.”
>
> “Your credential is public-floor only. That is not a challenge.”
>
> If Angie gives him a look: “I know exactly how that sounded.”

Ask Angie's player what part of the Arcology she finds genuinely appealing. Record the answer. On December 19, show that same feature failing.

## Scene 2: The service side — Frank at work

**Time:** 13:40  
**Cast:** Frank, Niko, two maintenance workers, Marisol passing through an access lobby

Cut hard from polished public space to a maintenance mezzanine.

> Behind the decorative walls the building stops pretending to be elegant. Ducts crowd the ceiling. Condensate lines sweat into labeled trays. Every surface is covered in inspection dates, marker notes, old adhesive, and the fingerprints of people who actually keep the place alive.
>
> The Arcology sounds different here. No music. No ads. Just fans, pumps, distant lift motors, and tools knocking against tool belts.

Frank and Niko are inspecting air returns. Niko notices the battered TunnelRat comic or image among Frank's things.

> Niko: “Please tell me that is a technical manual.”
>
> Niko, after Frank's answer: “Good. Because the official manuals have fewer believable heroes.”

A return vent is rattling. It stops whenever Frank comes within a few feet and begins again when he moves away. This is not paranormal; a loose baffle shifts as local pressure changes when a person blocks part of the corridor. Let Frank diagnose it if he cares.

Marisol appears at the service-desk window to complain that the vent outside her residential corridor has “changed pitch.” The desk clerk starts explaining that the monitoring dashboard is green.

Marisol snaps, “Then your dashboard has excellent hearing and terrible ears.”

Frank can intervene, ignore it, or simply listen. If he takes her seriously, she remembers him later.

### Optional check

**Average Mechanics** to diagnose the baffle before the system creates a ticket.

- Success: Frank earns **Service Shortcut**; Niko points out an old worker-maintained route between two public sectors.
- Advantage: Marisol decides Frank is “the one who actually listens.”
- Threat: Frank's badge briefly logs him eleven floors away. The dispatcher laughs it off as a sync error.

## Scene 3: The same atrium, hours apart

End the day with a quiet cross-cut.

Rowan plays beneath a licensed sound canopy. Angie may pass in the late afternoon; Frank crosses the same atrium after shift change. Neither needs to notice the other.

> The same projected koi scatter for both of them. The same song rises into the artificial daylight. The building knows both names.
>
> They do not know each other's.

Ask each player one short question: **What ordinary thing do you assume will still be here tomorrow?**

# December 2 — The Lift That Refuses a Family

## Scene 1: A small public humiliation

**Time:** 16:05  
**Location:** Residential transfer bank  
**Cast:** Angie, a family of four, passersby, Evan briefly

> Every twenty seconds the lift doors open and close with perfect patience.
>
> A family of four does not move.
>
> Their youngest child is crying quietly. Shopping bags have begun cutting red grooves into the father's fingers. Each time the mother presents the family credential, the lift answers in the same warm voice: “Travel group incomplete. Please reunite all registered members.”
>
> Everyone else keeps boarding around them.

The problem is a lost visitor badge. It lies under a parked cleaning cart. The projected koi are clustering at the cart because their floor effect treats the reflective badge as an obstacle.

Give the family personalities rather than making them puzzle furniture.

**Mother, Ayumi:** embarrassed and becoming angry because everyone is watching.  
**Father, Ken:** wants to call guest services and avoid making the problem bigger.  
**Child, Mika:** near an asthma episode, insisting “the fish found it.”  
**Older brother, Tomas:** mortified and pretending not to know any of them.

If Angie offers help, Ayumi initially says, “We are fine.” Then Mika coughs and Ken immediately says, “We are not fine.”

Angie's scent can reveal stress, embarrassment, and Mika's breathing problem. It does not reveal whether the parents trust her.

When the badge is recovered, the lift still pauses before accepting it. Its screen flickers through a maintenance-looking query that a casual observer could miss.

**Average Perception or Computers:** Angie notices a second, nonpublic identity table being checked.

- Advantage: the family later remembers her as **Friendly Face: Visitor Family**.
- Threat: a ceiling camera lingers on Angie's third eye long after the family has entered the lift.

Evan Cho can arrive because someone finally called security. He sees the problem resolve and looks relieved.

> Evan: “Thank you. You saved me from writing a report titled ‘family temporarily defeated by elevator.’”

This is a chance for Angie to meet him before he becomes associated with authority and danger.

## Scene 2: Frank gets the ticket after the people are gone

**Time:** 16:42  
**Cast:** Frank, Niko, junior dispatcher by commlink

The lift is mechanically perfect. Brakes, motor, doors, obstruction sensors, weight sensors: all green.

> Niko watches the diagnostic bars settle into a wall of green. “Congratulations. We have repaired the thing that was not broken.”

The ticket categorizes the failure as “door timing.” Frank can inspect the permissions trace. The lift requested a maintenance identity check *before* Frank scanned his badge.

If Frank calls dispatch:

> Dispatcher: “Ticket says door timing.”
>
> Frank: [player response]
>
> Dispatcher: “I am looking at the same thing you are.”
>
> A beat.
>
> Dispatcher, quieter: “Actually, no. It just closed.”

The ticket closes under Frank's credentials while he is still working.

**Average Mechanics or Hard Computers** can preserve evidence. A Triumph can yield a discarded diagnostic token usable later as **Clean Credential**.

## Scene 3: One-second hesitation

At 16:20 or when the scene naturally ends, every visible lift pauses on a different floor for exactly one second.

Do not call for a roll unless the players act. Let commuters react first.

- someone laughs and says “traffic balancing”;
- a child cheers because the glass car stopped between floors;
- a businesswoman checks the time and keeps typing;
- a maintenance worker looks up sharply, then looks away when nothing follows.

The Arcology resumes as though nothing happened.

# December 3 — Traffic Behind the Garden

## Scene 1: Let the garden be pleasant first

**Time:** 11:30  
**Location:** Indoor garden promenade  
**Cast:** Angie, Marisol, gardener Ren Ito, schoolchildren

> Artificial daylight filters through real leaves. Water threads over dark stone. The air smells of wet soil, nutrient solution, citrus blossoms, and the faint metallic bite of the pumps keeping all of it alive.
>
> Children are trying to cross a narrow stream without using the bridge. A gardener trims a vine away from a hidden sensor housing. Somewhere behind the decorative wall, motors move in a steady procession.

Give Angie several minutes of ordinary interaction. A child asks whether her hair color means she is happy. Gardener Ren Ito says, without looking up, “Do not ask people questions you would hate strangers asking you.” The child thinks about this, then asks Angie what her favorite flower is instead.

Marisol is arguing with a horticultural attendant because the wall has vibrated all morning.

> Marisol: “It is not the pump.”
>
> Attendant: “The pump status is normal.”
>
> Marisol: “That is excellent news for the pump.”

Angie's Electrosense and Magnetosense can distinguish powered carts behind the wall. Their motors are ordinary. The command bursts are not coming from the local garden controller; they arrive from deeper in the structure.

If Angie watches the wall, one cart stops. A second cart stops. A third continues for several seconds and then also stops.

Nothing has detected her eyes. Something has detected her attention.

Do not say this aloud. Describe only the sequence.

If Angie deliberately looks away, the procession resumes.

## Scene 2: Frank finds equipment waiting for a failure

**Time:** 12:05  
**Location:** Parallel maintenance corridor  
**Cast:** Frank, Niko

Three replacement relays sit beside a perfectly functioning controller. Each has a valid serial number assigned to a different district.

> Niko checks the manifest again. “I brought one.”
>
> He points at the three sealed boxes.
>
> “Either inventory learned teleportation, or somebody wants these here badly enough to lie badly.”

As Frank scans the first relay, the inventory system calmly announces, “Installation confirmed.”

All three relays are still on the floor.

If he scans another, the system repeats the confirmation. If he contacts stores, the clerk insists the parts are physically on three other floors because that is what the system shows.

**Average Mechanics:** Frank can retain one as **Cached Tool**, record the serial discrepancy, or prove the local unit never requested replacement.

On a Despair, all three are recorded as installed by Frank.

## Scene 3: Cross-cut without a meeting

As Angie's hidden carts start moving again, Frank's work order changes to “completed.” Both experience two seconds of unusual quiet: the garden wall stops vibrating; the service corridor fans dip; then everything resumes.

Ask each player what they tell someone else about the event. The answer establishes how seriously their social circle takes later warnings.

# December 4 — Personalized Service

## Scene 1: The retail gallery sells a version of you

**Time:** 14:20  
**Cast:** Angie, Rowan, Sana, shoppers

> The gallery does not advertise products. It advertises possible versions of the person walking past.
>
> Coats change color to match skin tone. Restaurants rotate dishes according to biometrics and recent purchases. A cosmetics display subtly alters the face in its mirror. Music follows a customer for three steps and fades if they do not turn toward the store.

A music kiosk plays a song linked to Angie's earliest online performances as **Missia**. It does not display her legal name. It uses the private performance identity.

The song is not simply similar. It is the correct track.

Sana, whose shop faces the kiosk, notices Angie's expression.

> Sana: “That machine once tried to sell my husband a retirement cruise the morning after we argued about money. It calls this ‘anticipatory hospitality.’”

Rowan is packing cables nearby.

> Rowan: “You know that look? That is the look people give a machine when it knows the wrong thing correctly.”

If Angie questions the kiosk, it claims “regional listening affinity.” If she asks for the data source, it provides a bland privacy page. If she walks away, the recommendation follows her to the next panel for four meters, then vanishes.

**Average Discipline:** remain composed long enough to inspect the anomaly.

On Advantage, the track contains **nine beats of absolute silence** not present in the public recording. On Triumph, Angie recognizes the silence as a **Residual Signature** even though she cannot name why.

## Scene 2: Frank gets an offer that should be impossible

**Time:** 18:10  
**Location:** Another part of the retail gallery  
**Cast:** Frank, sales clerk Lian, Niko by message if wanted

A display changes as Frank approaches. It advertises a rare TunnelRat issue he researched at home on a non-Renraku connection.

The price drops once.

Then again.

Then the panel adds: **COMPLETE YOUR SET TODAY.**

Lian, the clerk, is genuinely confused if confronted.

> Lian: “The recommendation engine does not show us why it picked something. That would ruin the magic.”
>
> After seeing Frank's expression: “Sorry. Poor choice of word.”

If Frank photographs the display, the photo captures a generic ad instead. If he asks someone else to look, the screen resets before they turn. If he buys the comic, that is fine; do not punish him for taking something he actually wants.

**Average Cool:** Frank notices the ceiling camera is tracking his *reaction timing*, not simply identifying his face.

On Threat, increase Hidden Attention.

## Closing image

When both characters leave their respective panels, the displays return to generic advertisements immediately. The next customers see nothing strange.

That night, neither event exists in the public recommendation history.


# December 5 — The Fault That Repairs Itself

## Scene 1: A fountain nobody thinks is dangerous

**Time:** 10:50  
**Location:** Mid-level public atrium  
**Cast:** Angie, Marisol, Eli, maintenance staff, parents and children

> The fountain is designed to disappear into the background: a wide sheet of water over black stone, quiet enough for conversations, bright enough to make the atrium feel expensive.
>
> Today it has a second rhythm.
>
> Every few seconds the pump draws a breath that is slightly too deep. The water surface dimples. The sound is low enough that most people do not notice it, but once heard it becomes impossible to stop hearing.

Marisol has noticed. She stands with a half-finished drink, staring at the water.

> Marisol: “There. Again.”
>
> Eli, from his kiosk: “I hear water.”
>
> Marisol: “You hear *expected* water.”

Children are still leaning over the edge to chase light patterns. Their parents see no reason to move them because the public status panel reads **WATER FEATURE: NORMAL**.

Angie's senses catch something the panel does not: overheated insulation beneath the water treatment smell, and an irregular electrical current behind the pump wall.

If Angie warns people, decide how she does it. A calm request can clear the nearest section without panic. A shouted warning draws security and creates embarrassment if nothing immediately explodes. Either choice is legitimate.

If she speaks to Marisol, Marisol is grateful that someone else heard the wrongness.

> Marisol: “I was beginning to think I had become the kind of person everyone warns you about becoming.”

**Average Perception:** Angie can identify which access panel contains the bad current before maintenance arrives.

- Success: workers isolate the correct circuit before anyone is shocked.
- Advantage: **Friendly Face: Marisol Vance**.
- Threat: the overlapping water, pump, crowd, and electrical fields push Angie's Resonance Strain up by 1.

## Scene 2: Frank arrives just after the problem stops being a problem

**Time:** 11:08  
**Cast:** Frank, Niko, maintenance supervisor Kessler, Angie only if she remains nearby

The pump has already stabilized by the time Frank opens the panel.

> The access door swings open on warm air and the smell of insulation that has been heated just short of failure.
>
> A relay light blinks green.
>
> The pressure curve on Frank's diagnostic tool smooths itself while he is looking at it.

The local controller shows a pressure correction command, then the command disappears from the buffer. Not marked deleted. Simply absent.

Niko leans in.

> Niko: “You saw that?”
>
> If Frank says yes: “Good. I am too tired to hallucinate alone.”

Supervisor Kessler arrives after the danger has passed and is irritated because the dashboard says no incident occurred.

> Kessler: “If it is operating in tolerance, close it.”
>
> Niko: “It *became* in tolerance while we were opening it.”
>
> Kessler: “Then praise preventive maintenance.”

Frank can preserve the timestamp, inspect the wiring, challenge Kessler, or quietly close the panel. If Angie is still present, this can be the first time they are physically in the same scene—but they still need not interact or remember one another.

**Hard Mechanics or Computers:** preserve the partial remote command.

- Success: Frank's Evidence +1.
- Triumph: the command architecture resembles the later 7-G anomaly, granting a future **Residual Signature**.
- Despair: the system writes Frank's badge number into the component's service history as the source of the correction.

## Scene 3: The heartbeat

As the public area returns to normal, the pump makes nine slow beats with a cadence almost like a resting heart.

Then nothing.

A little boy copies the beat by tapping the stone with his palm. His parent takes his hand and walks away.

Do not make the moment ominous with narration. Let the players decide whether it matters.

# December 6 — The Polite Warning

## Scene 1: Hidoshi's corridor

**Time:** 09:30  
**Location:** Legal consultancy floor  
**Cast:** Angie, Hidoshi, frightened clerk Aya Patel, Evan Cho

> Legal floors smell different from the mall. Less food, more toner, coffee, fabric cleaner, recycled air, and the sharp chemical edge of people who have slept too little while insisting they are fine.

Aya Patel exits Hidoshi's work area carrying a fresh nondisclosure packet. Evan walks beside her, not restraining her, but clearly tasked with ensuring she leaves by the correct route.

Aya's hands are shaking. Angie's enhanced smell can catch prescribed stimulants, stale stress sweat under fresh perfume, and the metallic edge of an adrenaline spike.

Hidoshi watches her pass, then looks at Angie.

> Hidoshi: “That was not a normal employment conversation.”

If Angie speaks to Aya, Evan does not automatically stop her. He says, “Keep it brief.”

Aya initially repeats corporate language:

> Aya: “There was a records-access misunderstanding.”
>
> “It has been resolved.”
>
> “I should not discuss internal authorization.”

If Angie creates enough safety for her to break script, Aya admits she saw maintenance authorizations signed by employees who were off-site when the signatures were issued.

> Aya, very quietly: “One of them was in Tacoma. I know because I called him. The system says he approved a floor isolation while he was arguing with me from a freeway.”

She is more frightened by the contradiction than by corporate punishment.

Relevant social checks may receive up to **two Boost dice** from Angie's scent-based reading of stress. The smell indicates physiology, not truth.

On Threat, Aya misreads Angie's interest as an internal loyalty test and shuts down.

## Scene 2: Frank's warning is friendlier and therefore worse

**Time:** 13:15  
**Location:** service security desk  
**Cast:** Frank, Evan, Niko arriving midway

Frank's badge has been flagged for opening a panel that his work order required him to open on December 5.

Evan is assigned to verify the discrepancy.

> Evan: “The order says you opened an unauthorized panel.”
>
> He scrolls.
>
> “The attached ticket says you were assigned to open the same panel.”
>
> He scrolls again.
>
> “I was hoping one of those would disappear before you got here.”

This should feel like two working people trapped inside contradictory systems, not a cop interrogation.

If Frank shows evidence, Evan becomes more cautious, not hostile.

> Evan: “Do not send me that.”
>
> A beat.
>
> “Show it to me on your screen.”

That distinction matters: he wants to know without creating a record that he knows.

Niko can arrive carrying a replacement authorization that invalidates itself when scanned.

> Niko: “I brought paperwork.”
>
> Evan: “You say that like a threat.”

If Frank conceals evidence, use Deception, Negotiation, or Streetwise only if needed. Otherwise let the scene play as conversation.

- Success can establish **Friendly Face: Evan Cho**.
- Despair adds Corporate Attention and a silent watch flag to Frank's badge.

## Scene 3: The missing document

Hidoshi later tries to retrieve Aya's nondisclosure notice from the legal case system.

It is gone.

Not “access denied.” Not “deleted.” The case number now jumps from the prior document to the next one.

Hidoshi says, “Paper is ugly, but paper at least has the decency to leave ashes.”

If Angie asks whether he is worried, he answers honestly: “Yes.”

# December 7 — Music Under Glass

## Purpose

Give both characters a day that is mostly humane. The campaign needs memories of the Arcology that are worth saving.

## Scene 1: Rowan's performance

**Time:** 17:10  
**Location:** atrium performance terrace  
**Cast:** Angie, Rowan, Eli, shoppers, office workers, children

> Rowan's licensed sound canopy is a transparent half-dome no one can see until the lights catch its edge. It keeps the music inside a permitted volume while a tip meter floats politely beside the performer.
>
> The setup is corporate. The music is not.

Rowan is playing a layered acoustic/electronic piece when Angie approaches. Let the performance breathe. Describe people slowing without intending to: a courier taking out one earbud, a tired office worker missing a lift because she stays through the chorus, two teenagers pretending not to sway.

Rowan notices Angie's attention.

> Rowan: “You play?”
>
> If Angie hesitates: “That was not a job interview.”

If Angie joins, Rowan gives her space rather than making the scene about novelty. Glamour and Mood Hair can make her visually memorable, but the crowd's warmth should also come from what she contributes.

Potential crowd reactions:

- a child sits cross-legged directly in front of them;
- an older resident mouths the refrain;
- Eli turns his kiosk speaker off so the food ads do not compete;
- a security guard watches for a minute before remembering he is working.

**Average Charm or appropriate performance skill.**

- Success: **Friendly Face: Atrium Crowd**.
- Advantage: Rowan teaches Angie a simple refrain. Use it later as a memory anchor during sensory overload.
- Triumph: nearby device indicators synchronize to the final note for one breath. Hidden Attention +1 and **Residual Signature**.

If the device synchronization occurs, the crowd applauds harder because they think it is part of the show.

Rowan knows it was not.

> Rowan, quietly while packing up: “I do not have that lighting package.”

## Scene 2: Frank fixes a problem because it is there

**Time:** 17:35  
**Location:** Eli's kiosk  
**Cast:** Frank, Eli, Niko passing through

Eli's dispenser has begun adding pickled ginger to everything: noodles, tea, pastries, even plain hot water.

Customers are irritated but laughing.

> Eli: “I have discovered the universal ingredient.”
>
> Customer: “I ordered coffee.”
>
> Eli: “The machine has opinions.”

Frank can stop because he wants to help, not because his queue tells him to.

The fault is embarrassingly simple: a feed label has been swapped. Nothing supernatural.

**Easy Mechanics:** fix it.

- Success: **Friendly Face: Eli Mercer**.
- Advantage: Eli gives Frank a meal voucher and says, “Use it before I become a ginger-only establishment again.”
- Threat: Frank is late to the next work assignment.

The late arrival creates a small notation on tomorrow's queue: **NODE 7-G / VERIFY RELAY HANDSHAKE**. It is easy to overlook.

## Scene 3: Amber applause

At the final note of Rowan's next song, every indicator within sight turns amber simultaneously.

Not warning amber. The same strange amber tone Angie has been beginning to notice.

The lights return to normal before the applause ends.

Eli says, “Nice effect.”

Rowan looks at Angie instead of answering.

End the day there.

# December 8 — Nine Seconds Without Color

## Scene 1: Ordinary late-night Arcology

**Time:** 21:55  
**Location:** public mixed-use district  
**Cast:** Angie, Hidoshi, late shoppers, cleaning crews; Frank elsewhere on late shift

Make the Arcology feel different at night without feeling empty.

> Storefronts have dimmed but not closed. Cleaning drones work around diners. Residents cross public space in slippers and house clothes because, to them, this is home rather than a mall. Shift workers are eating breakfast. Children are being carried asleep between parents.

Hidoshi has finished late. Let him and Angie have a mundane conversation. He might ask what she wants to do tomorrow. He might complain about the legal team's coffee. He might admit he prefers the indoor garden to the consultant lounge.

Do not start with the anomaly.

## Scene 2: 22:14 — nine seconds without color

Then every display in the district loses color.

> Red drains first. Then blue. Then green.
>
> For nine seconds the Arcology becomes monochrome without becoming dark. Advertisements keep moving. Elevators continue. Announcements continue in their soft corporate voices.
>
> People look up, complain, laugh, or do not notice at all.

For Angie, the change is not visual only.

> For nine seconds, every device feels like a room whose occupants stopped speaking because someone entered.

The amber pulse exists only in reflections: polished railings, darkened screens, glass walls, someone's cybereyes. Whenever Angie turns directly toward where it seems to originate, it is gone.

If she resists following it, use **Hard Discipline**.

- Success: memorize the rhythm without following tonight.
- Advantage: **Residual Signature**.
- Threat: Resonance Strain +1.
- Despair: do not announce that she is “noticed.” Instead, have one dead screen display her reflection a half-second late.

Hidoshi sees only the display glitch unless Angie tells him more.

> Hidoshi: “That lasted nine seconds.”
>
> If Angie asks how he knows: “I count when I am worried.”

## Scene 3: Frank's work order

At the same time, Frank is finishing shift documentation.

His work queue updates:

```text
2059-12-09 / MAINTENANCE NODE 7-G
FAULT: AIR-CONTROL RELAY / SCRUBBER HANDSHAKE
PRIORITY: ROUTINE
DO NOT DEFER
```

The ticket disappears.

Then reappears.

Then a nearby terminal prints a hard copy even though Frank never requested one.

Niko takes the paper from the tray.

> Niko: “You planning to collect antique forms now?”

If Frank checks the routing path, the order touches a host that should not have maintenance authority.

**Average Computers:** save an offline copy.

- Success: preserve the order.
- Advantage: gain **Clean Credential**.
- Triumph: identify the impossible routing hop.
- Despair: the copy is silently associated with Frank's personal commlink.

## Scene 4: The promises before sleep

Do not end on the technical anomaly. End on people.

Hidoshi and Angie make one ordinary plan for December 9. It can be breakfast, dinner, the garden, Rowan's next set, or leaving the Arcology for a few hours.

Do not decide the fate of every missing person now. Preserve the names. Later one can appear among captives, Greens, Banded, or Resistance contacts depending on campaign need.

# December 16 — Arrivals

## Canon background

At 09:11:38, Paul reaches Seattle and the Arcology. Other summoned otaku are already present, including Pax, the Nubian, Scarecrow, and Sebastien. Frank and Angie do not know their significance. The GM uses those arrivals as hidden background pressure, not as a parade of named lore characters.

## Scene 1: The public floors feel crowded in the wrong places

**Time:** 09:20  
**Location:** transit and Matrix interchange  
**Cast:** Angie, travelers, commuters, hidden arrivals

They do not descend again.

Give the movement personality without identification:

- one presence moves eagerly, almost impatiently;
- another is cautious and pauses often;
- one is accompanied by a burst of emotional devotion;
- another seems to notice Angie's attention and briefly turns toward her before continuing upward.

**Hard Computers:** count separate routes without identifying the travelers.

- Success: establish that these are multiple people or persona-clusters, not one migrating process.
- Triumph: one route reacts to Angie as though recognizing a related phenomenon.
- Threat: Resonance Strain +1.

Put possible canon names only in overheard fragments if desired: a luggage tag, a shouted nickname, a conversation cut off by a closing lift. Do not explain “Pax,” “Nubian,” “Scarecrow,” or “Sebastien” to the players yet.

## Scene 2: Frank measures hidden occupancy

**Time:** 11:05  
**Location:** executive lift spine access  
**Cast:** Frank, Evan, Niko by commlink

Frank receives a routine air-quality assignment near an executive lift spine.

His instrument shows elevated carbon dioxide, heat, and humidity above the public occupancy manifest.

There are more people up there than the system admits.

Then his access is revoked.

Evan is assigned to the checkpoint.

> Evan: “Your work order is valid.”
>
> He checks his own screen.
>
> “So is the denial.”
>
> “I would like one of those facts to stop being true.”

If Frank asks Evan to witness the reading, Evan will look at the instrument but refuses a digital copy unless convinced.

> Evan: “If you send it to me, I become part of the record.”
>
> If Frank answers that he already is: Evan looks at the locked door. “Yes. That is becoming difficult to ignore.”

**Average Mechanics:** retain the environmental reading.

- Success: preserve proof of hidden occupancy.
- Advantage: obtain or preserve **Clean Credential: Lift Spine**.

If Frank tries an adjacent vent route, he can hear lift motors cycling above normal schedule and detect airflow consistent with groups moving through restricted floors.

## Scene 3: A visible arrival that does not become an encounter

Give Angie a human image to attach to the hidden movement.

A young traveler, exhausted and carrying almost no luggage, stands beneath an arrival display. He looks up—not at the sign, but beyond it—as though listening to a voice from above.

A second traveler touches his shoulder.

> Second traveler: “We are late.”
>
> First: “No. We are exactly when we were called.”

They disappear into a credentialed lift.

If Angie follows, the lift denies her. If she calls out, one of them glances back with recognition that may be spiritual, technical, or simply curiosity about her unusual appearance.

Do not resolve it.

## Scene 4: People are being moved

If Frank and Angie compare evidence today, this is the first date when the reasonable conclusion becomes larger than “corrupted software.”

Frank has environmental proof of hidden occupancy. Angie has sensed multiple hidden travelers moving upward. The system is not only rewriting records—it is moving people.

Let the players supply the theory.

If they ask Hidoshi what Renraku could legally move people for, he lists mundane possibilities first: executive conference, security event, protected witness transfer, labor issue, medical quarantine. Then he says:

> “The problem is not that there are no innocent explanations.”
>
> “The problem is that every innocent explanation would have paperwork.”

# December 17 — The Tower Dream

## Scene 1: 04:02:22 — Angie's dream

Do not narrate this as a static vision. Treat it as a place Angie can direct attention within.

> There are no walls.
>
> There is no floor.
>
> The Arcology hangs in darkness as a vertical nervous system: transit lines like pale bones, power trunks like arteries, data paths flashing faster than thought.
>
> Far above, a tower of cold white geometry pulls every signal toward itself.
>
> Beneath it is amber.
>
> Small. Broken. Flickering inside a cage made of unfinished commands.

Ask Angie's player: **What do you look at first?**

### If she focuses on the cold tower

The environment reorganizes around her attention. Pathways straighten. Noise becomes ordered. Anything ambiguous is forced into categories.

The tower does not speak in a voice. It communicates through certainty: *everything has a place; everything can be assigned; disorder is error.*

If Angie resists that emotional logic, the dream does not attack. It simply excludes her from the pattern, which can feel like falling.

### If she focuses on the amber presence

It cannot form language directly. It borrows sensory fragments established on December 9: the player's chosen memory of fear, Rowan's refrain, the feel of Hidoshi's hand on a table, the smell of rain outside the Arcology, the TunnelRat image Frank carried, or another concrete memory Angie associates with being a person rather than a data point.

It may be asking for help.

It may also be desperate enough to use Angie.

Keep both possibilities alive.

### If she tries to move between them

Describe currents in the dream pulling upward toward the cold tower. The amber presence is not simply “below”; it is entangled inside the same architecture.

A **Discipline** check can determine strain and clarity, not whether the dream happens.

- Success: Angie wakes without additional strain.
- Advantage: gain **Private Warning**.
- Threat: Resonance Strain +1.

Before she wakes, ask: **Which part felt like a warning, and which part felt like a request?** Record the player's answer verbatim.

## Scene 2: Angie wakes facing north

**Time:** immediately after 04:02:22  
**Cast:** Angie, Hidoshi

Angie wakes physically oriented toward magnetic north regardless of how she went to sleep.

Hidoshi is already at the door because the apartment system briefly reported Angie's room as **UNOCCUPIED**.

He does not burst in unless the player established that behavior. He knocks once and says her name.

When he sees she is awake:

> Hidoshi: “The room decided you were not in it.”
>
> “I came to ask whether you agreed.”

Let them talk in the pre-dawn quiet. No crowd, no chase, no corporate interruption.

A useful line if Angie describes the dream:

> “Whatever is happening, it has learned to make you doubt where you are.”
>
> “We can answer that by deciding who you are with.”

Do not make Hidoshi magically wise. He is a father trying to give Angie a stable human reference point.

## Scene 3: Frank's log opens at the same timestamp

At **04:02:22**, Frank's hidden device records an access attempt.

If Frank is awake, the interface responds half a second before his fingers move.

If he is asleep, the device records a voice command dismissing the file.

The voice is Frank's.

There is no breathing on the recording.

When he wakes, let him investigate mundanely first.

Niko can help test the device inside a shielded toolbox.

> Niko: “No network. No wireless. No clever cloud nonsense.”
>
> The diagnostic light blinks nine times.
>
> Niko: “I hate that.”

The file remains closed while inside the box.

**Average Cool:** treat the impossible record as evidence rather than immediately doubting his own memory.

- Success: keep composure.
- Advantage: Frank's Evidence +1.
- Despair: Hidden Attention +1; later, the recording's waveform includes a second voice-shaped absence beneath Frank's.

## Scene 4: Precautions

Ask each player for one concrete precaution they take for the next day.

Examples, not requirements:

- Angie gives Hidoshi a phrase or signal that means “do not trust the device.”
- Frank leaves a physical note with Niko or Emily.
- They establish a meeting point that does not depend on the Arcology's network.
- They exchange a paper copy of one fact.

Award **Private Warning** if the precaution is centered on protecting another person rather than merely hiding evidence.

# December 18 — Last Promises

## Scene 1: Make the Arcology warm again

**Time:** afternoon  
**Location:** holiday public districts

Do not make today ominous from the first sentence.

> Artificial snowfall drifts through the main atrium and vanishes before touching the floor. Children rehearse a holiday performance beneath hanging lights. Restaurants confirm tomorrow's tables. Couriers complain about impossible delivery volume. A projection wall turns the upper balconies into a winter skyline no one in the building can actually see outside.

People are making plans.

Let familiar NPCs appear in small, ordinary ways:

- Eli is testing a holiday menu and forcing samples on Niko.
- Rowan is arguing that a corporate jingle does not become art merely because someone added bells.
- Marisol has bought a gift and refuses to say for whom.
- Evan is looking forward to being off shift in two days.
- Mina complains that holiday decorations keep blocking the clinic's directional signage.
- Sana is trying to locate a delayed shipment and blaming courier routing rather than conspiracy.

The point is not “everyone is doomed.” The point is that everyone has a tomorrow.

## Scene 2: Angie and Hidoshi make a plan

Give them uninterrupted time together.

Possible locations are callbacks chosen by the player: the garden, Rowan's terrace, Eli's kiosk, a quiet balcony, or even outside the Arcology.

Hidoshi asks what Angie wants to do tomorrow when his consultancy obligations are finished.

> Hidoshi: “One day in Seattle with no Renraku schedule.”
>
> “Name something.”

Whatever Angie chooses, Hidoshi answers concretely. If she says breakfast outside, he names a time. If she wants the garden, he says which entrance. If she wants to leave Seattle, he starts checking transport.

Record Angie's answer and Hidoshi's reply exactly.

Do **not** interrupt this conversation with the anomaly.

Let the promise stand cleanly for at least one scene.

## Scene 3: Frank has a normal conversation too

Frank gets a mundane moment with Niko or an established loved one.

Niko might ask about the next week's schedule.

> Niko: “You taking the holiday shift?”
>
> “If you are, I am suddenly religious and unavailable.”

Or Emily/loved one asks when Frank will be home tomorrow.

Let Frank decide whether to disclose how worried he is. If he warns someone away from the Arcology, do not automatically reward or punish it. Record who believes him.

## Scene 4: 20:10:35 — the order

At 20:10:35, Frank's queue changes.

```text
RETURN TO NODE 7-G
BRING LOCAL COPY
OBSERVER COUNT INCOMPLETE
```

The order is under Frank's valid credentials.

There is no issuer.

If he prints it, the paper prints normally. If he photographs it, the image remains. This is not another disappearing clue; the system now wants him to see it.

Niko, if present, reads the text twice.

At the same moment, Angie notices the amber pulse settle into a stable nine-second rhythm.

Do not tell her whether it is distress, command, beacon, or countdown. Ask:

**What about the sensation makes Angie interpret it one way rather than another?**

If Frank and Angie are already in contact, either may initiate a conversation. If they have avoided one another, the parallel events can force the *question* of contact without forcing trust.

Possible exchange if the players want a minimal, tense contact:

>

>

Or Angie can contact first:

# December 19 — The Last Ordinary Day

## Run this day continuously from late afternoon

The early day can be played in two or three scenes, but from roughly 17:40 onward stop using montage. Time should begin to feel continuous. The Shutdown at **19:02:09 PST** is fixed. Everything the characters do before that can change position, allies, equipment, and information.

## Scene 1: Morning — tomorrow still exists

**Time:** 07:00–09:00

Give both PCs a normal plan that will now remain unfinished unless later recovered.

### Angie

Hidoshi confirms yesterday's plan.

If they planned breakfast outside:

> “Tomorrow. 09:00. And if Renraku invents another meeting, I will disappoint Renraku.”

If they planned something else, use that.

Let Angie encounter one comfortable callback from the previous eighteen days:

- the projected koi scatter around her shoes;
- the family from December 2 boards a lift together;
- Rowan advertises an evening set;
- Sana opens her shop under ordinary lighting;
- the fountain runs smoothly;
- Eli remembers an order without asking.

Nothing is wrong yet.

### Frank

Niko asks about a future shift or complains about a work order scheduled after the holiday.

> Niko: “See? Evidence of a future. They already assigned me something I do not want to do.”

Frank has the 18:55 order and the hidden log. Ask whether he tells anyone where he plans to be tonight.

If he leaves a physical note, say where. If he gives Niko instructions, have Niko repeat them back seriously once he realizes Frank is not joking.

## Scene 2: 12:00–16:30 — subtle preparation the public does not notice

Across the afternoon, insert two or three observations depending on where the players travel.

### Maintenance movement

Sealed carts move toward service lifts in unusually high numbers. Their manifests say “holiday infrastructure.” Frank can see that some are too heavy for decorations.

### Security movement

Evan's patrol route changes repeatedly. He complains by message if Frank knows him.

> Evan: “Every time we arrive somewhere, the reason for being there changes.”

### Clinic preparation

Mina receives a request to ready trauma beds without an incident code.

> Mina: “You do not prep trauma capacity for a software update.”

### Public information remains cheerful

Holiday announcements continue. Stores run promotions. Families take photographs. The contradiction should be unsettling because both realities coexist.

## Scene 3: 17:41:44 — the building begins to feel busy in hidden places

Cal Reynolds begins his journal elsewhere at this time. Frank and Angie do not know that. They experience the same broader shift through local effects.

> The Arcology does not become quiet.
>
> It becomes *organized*.

Service routes change. Doors open for crews who do not appear on public schedules. Delivery shutters close early. Security personnel are redirected with increasingly vague instructions.

Angie's senses reveal more electronic activity behind restricted walls, not less.

Frank sees work orders that contradict physical traffic.

If either contacts an NPC, give immediate, grounded information rather than lore:

- **Niko:** “Facilities has six emergency tickets with no faults attached.”
- **Evan:** “Security is being split into smaller teams.”
- **Mina:** “They want beds ready. They will not say for what.”
- **Sana:** “Three deliveries never arrived and the system says I signed for them.”
- **Rowan:** “They canceled the evening permit but the public schedule still shows me playing.”
- **Marisol:** “The vents changed rhythm again. This time all at once.”

## Scene 4: 18:17 — reports of a mall shooting

News of a shooting or violent incident spreads unevenly.

One display says **INCIDENT CONTAINED**.

Another calls it a **TRAINING EXERCISE**.

A third continues advertising winter coats.

People respond according to personality:

- some hurry toward lifts;
- some stay because announcements say the situation is controlled;
- a parent begins counting children;
- a store manager starts lowering a shutter, then receives an order to reopen it;
- Evan is redirected away from the reported area.

Do not force Frank or Angie toward 7-G immediately. Let them decide whether to check on someone first.

Every favor should cost time.

### If they contact Hidoshi

He is still reachable. He may be elsewhere in the Arcology finishing consultancy work.

> Hidoshi: “I am leaving the office.”
>
> “Tell me where you are.”

If Angie does not want to tell him, he does not magically know.

### If Frank contacts Niko

Niko can cover Frank's maintenance absence, create a mundane explanation for his route, or meet him briefly. He cannot solve the mystery from afar.

## Scene 5: Separate approaches to Node 7-G

### Angie's route

The amber pulse now has direction.

> Every nine seconds it touches the edge of Angie's perception and recedes.
>
> Cameras, frightened crowds, service carts, active doors, nearby cyberware, and ordinary human bioelectric fields all compete with it.
>
> The pulse remains steady beneath them.

Ask what Angie tells Hidoshi before she goes deeper.

If she travels openly, security congestion becomes the main obstacle. If secretly, the route is quieter but more isolated. If she asks an NPC for help, use the relationship earned over the prelude.

Examples:

- Evan can overlook one credential discrepancy but risks his job.
- Marisol can identify which service corridors changed ventilation patterns.
- Sana can provide a staff-only retail exit into a back corridor.
- Rowan can create a distraction in a public space, but this puts a civilian at risk.
- Mina can provide a medical pretext if Angie is willing to be treated as a patient in the records.

Do not make any option obviously correct.

### Frank's route

Frank's work order now includes a service path that avoids staffed checkpoints.

That is more suspicious than a locked door.

The hidden log may grow warm, wake its host device, or display a local indicator when Frank enters the correct route. Do not make it literally magical heat unless that fits established hardware; the point is autonomous activation.

Ask:

- What does Frank carry?
- Does he bring the original working copy or a decoy?
- Who knows where he is?
- Does he follow the provided route or an old Service Shortcut?

If he uses a Clean Credential, it works once and leaves a record. If he uses a Service Shortcut, it avoids logging but may be physically harder.

## Scene 6: 18:55 — reunion at Node 7-G

> Node 7-G looks smaller than memory.
>
> The amber terminal is dark.
>
> Every nine seconds a reflection passes across its screen even though nothing in the room changes brightness.
>
> The air-control relay Frank replaced is gone. Four bright screw heads mark the empty space.
>
> The camera is still angled away.

Let Frank and Angie speak before the system interrupts.

Ask only if conversation stalls:

- What do you now believe happened here on December 9?
- What have you deliberately not told the other person?
- What would make you leave this room immediately?

Do not answer for them.

Then Frank's commlink opens the log by itself.

```text
7G_ANOMALY.log
STATUS: REOPENED
AUTHORIZED OBSERVERS: 2
EXPECTED OBSERVERS: [PARTY SIZE]
```

The final line matters. The system knows there should be more people—or believes there should be.

Do not explain who the expected observers are.

## Scene 7: Seven minutes

There are roughly seven minutes until 19:02:09.

Let the players use them.

Possible actions and consequences:

### Inspect the missing relay

Frank can determine the relay was removed recently. Tool marks are clean. No standard maintenance entry exists.

### Examine the terminal

Angie can feel the cold and amber presences farther apart than on December 9, as though pressure is building between them.

### Call Hidoshi/Niko/Evan/Mina

Calls may connect initially but become increasingly delayed. A warning sent now can change where an NPC is when the Shutdown hits.

### Leave immediately

They can try. The Shutdown still happens at 19:02:09, but their position changes. Do not force them to remain physically in 7-G if they choose otherwise. The fixed campaign continuity is the return/reunion and the Shutdown time, not imprisonment in one room.

### Prepare the room

They can prop a door, open a vent, cache supplies, shut down local power, or choose an escape route. Reward practical preparation after the blackout.

### Argue

Let them. Seven minutes is enough time for human conflict. Do not have an NPC interrupt simply to hurry them unless the time actually runs out.

As the seconds pass, use environmental cues:

**18:58:** the ventilation changes pitch.  
**18:59:** public announcements become more frequent but less informative.  
**19:00:** the local network reports full connectivity while messages begin queuing instead of sending.  
**19:01:** Angie's senses detect a sudden increase in active systems deeper in the building.  
**19:02:** one second of perfect normality.

## Scene 8: 19:02:09 — Shutdown

Read slowly.

> The amber terminal goes black.
>
> Every other light follows.
>
> Ventilation stops.
>
> The floor shudders hard enough to feel through the knees.
>
> Somewhere beyond the service wall, a transit system screams against emergency brakes.
>
> For half a second there is no announcement at all.
>
> Then dozens of things happen at once.

Immediately make the living world audible.

- a child begins pounding on the far side of a shutter;
- a maintenance cart stops, rotates toward the nearest person, and waits;
- emergency lights fail to come on in one corridor and activate too brightly in another;
- commlinks display full signal and deliver nothing;
- someone farther away is shouting a name over and over;
- a locked service door opens while a public exit seals;
- the constant movement of air disappears, making the Arcology feel instantly smaller.

For Angie:

> In the darkness, the new sense does not go blind.
>
> It opens.
>
> The building is more active than before.

Then stop narrating the PCs.

Ask:

**“What do you do first?”**

From this moment onward, transition directly into the established Shutdown Session 1: false announcements, people at the door, survivor decisions, and the wider Arcology crisis.

## Running the full prelude without rushing it

This version can support multiple sessions if desired. If it must fit into one long prelude session, do not compress every day into narration. Instead, fully play the strongest scene from each date and use short transitions only between scenes. The critical difference is that even a shortened day should still contain one **actual conversation or decision**, not merely “Angie notices X” or “Frank discovers Y.”

A useful pacing split is:

- **December 1–4:** normal life with one anomaly intrusion per day.
- **December 5–8:** recurring NPC relationships plus escalating evidence.
- **December 9:** continuous play; do not montage.
- **December 10–13:** consequences, medical/technical validation, human memory.
- **December 14–16:** hidden network gains direction and begins moving people.
- **December 17–18:** personal warning, precautions, promises.
- **December 19:** continuous play from late afternoon through 19:02:09.

The prelude succeeds if, when the lights go out, the players can name people they care about, places they remember functioning, ordinary routes they understand, and promises that were supposed to happen tomorrow.
