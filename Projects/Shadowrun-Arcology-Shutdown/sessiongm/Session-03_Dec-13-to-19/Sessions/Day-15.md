**Suggested time:** 08:45–10:30  
**Location:** worker concourse and dispatch terminal  
**Purpose:** absence becomes a human emergency, not just missing roster rows. **Scene status:** optional additive play; cross-check original `December 15` scene order before insertion.

### Open at the table

> There are three untouched lunches in the refrigerator. The duty board is full, yet three lockers remain closed and one partner is waiting with the kind of anger that keeps becoming fear.

**Meanwhile, the living Arcology is already in motion:**
- **Working:** three technicians miss dispatch despite replacement names on the board.
- **Living:** a partner waits by the shift gate carrying a packed lunch.
- **System:** service doors report sealed safe while knocks can be heard from behind one.

### People, agendas, and conversation

Niko needs to know whether colleagues are alive; a duty manager wants a search to go through official channels.

> Partner: “He doesn't just disappear. He complains when he's five minutes late.”

### Playable sequence

1. Preserve canonical vanished-worker reports and Frank's roster contradiction.
2. Confront a real scheduling mismatch and establish the exact last-seen location from witnesses, not system truth alone.
3. Let players decide who to involve and whether the families should be informed right now.
4. Keep multiple possible explanations (administrative transfer, hazard evacuation, equipment access, something worse).

### Meaningful player approaches

- Ask relatives for useful details with sensitivity, file a report, or search a route.
- Treat absence as mundane until there is evidence or take an immediate precaution.

**Useful Genesys/Shadowrun checks:** Average Streetwise/Charm for witnesses, Average Computers for deleted shift paths, Average Perception for tangible signs. Failure yields a slower physical search.

**Pressure and stakes:** Waiting does not delete people; it affects access to the area and how much official support remains.

### Consequences / handoff

A real relationship and a specific area to explore are established.

**Failure-forward safety:** never deny the original fixed event because one check fails. A failure means delay, cost, relationship tension, or loss of one evidence avenue; witnesses, physical traces, and alternative approaches stay available. ## Added scene 2: Voices through a service wall

**Suggested time:** early afternoon, optional if players follow up  
**Location:** sealed utility service bay, branching maintenance corridor  
**Purpose:** absence becomes a human emergency, not just missing roster rows. **Scene status:** optional additive play; cross-check original `December 15` scene order before insertion.

### Open at the table

> At first it is only ventilation. Then the sound repeats in threes: a wrench striking something hollow, too deliberate to be a loose pipe.

**Meanwhile, the living Arcology is already in motion:**
- **Working:** three technicians miss dispatch despite replacement names on the board.
- **Living:** a partner waits by the shift gate carrying a packed lunch.
- **System:** service doors report sealed safe while knocks can be heard from behind one.

### People, agendas, and conversation

One trapped technician wants the people outside to stop resetting a broken door. Evan wants proof before overriding a security closure.

> A voice from inside: “Don't cycle it again. It locks harder every time.”

### Playable sequence

1. Present alternatives: official rescue dispatch, manual bypass, duct crawl, cutting power and opening hydraulics.
2. Inside is a cramped, warm service space with two accessible people; the third worker's location can remain uncertain without asserting a death.
3. A leaking coolant line adds urgency but no cinematic flames; workers are capable adults who help.
4. Evidence of a deliberate administrative disappearance can come later; saving people matters now.

### Meaningful player approaches

- Rescue first, preserve logs, split tasks, or escalate to supervisors.

**Useful Genesys/Shadowrun checks:** Average Mechanics to isolate hydraulic lock; Hard (3 difficulty) Athletics for the duct under pressure; Average Coordination/Medicine to extricate safely. Failure adds strain, tool damage, delay and outside attention; rescue remains achievable with time and help.

**Pressure and stakes:** 3-stage heat/air clock; competent responders arrive if called and given time.

### Consequences / handoff

Survivors, if reached, give imperfect human testimony; whether all three are found becomes a player-driven unresolved thread.

**Failure-forward safety:** never deny the original fixed event because one check fails. A failure means delay, cost, relationship tension, or loss of one evidence avenue; witnesses, physical traces, and alternative approaches stay available. Choose strain/damage only from credible hazards and system-native resolution.
