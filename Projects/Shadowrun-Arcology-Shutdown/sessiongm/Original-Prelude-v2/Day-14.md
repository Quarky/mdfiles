# December 14 — A Direction in the Noise

## Canon background

At 15:23:12, Paul's later record describes hearing the Resonance and identifying Deus by name. Frank and Angie do not know this event. What they experience is a change in the hidden network's behavior: what had felt like scattered anomalies begins to feel coordinated.

## Scene 1: Afternoon crowd, sudden orientation

**Time:** 15:23  
**Location:** public transit concourse  
**Cast:** Angie, hundreds of ordinary commuters, one or two unidentified otaku at a distance

> The concourse is busy enough that nobody owns the sound. Announcements overlap with footsteps, conversation, baggage wheels, a child singing to herself, and the low hum of transit power under the floor.

Angie's background data sense has been noisy all day.

At 15:23:12, it changes.

Not louder.

Aligned.

Multiple faint presences elsewhere in the building orient in the same conceptual direction at once. A few feel fearful. Several feel reverent or intensely expectant.

Do not label them otaku yet.

At the same moment, put one visible person in the crowd who reacts physically: a teenage boy stops walking mid-step and smiles at nothing; a young woman near a kiosk closes her eyes as though hearing music.

They do not need to interact with Angie.

**Hard Discipline:** separate the cold vast presence from the smaller wounded one amid the sudden network response.

- Success: Angie can tell they remain distinct.
- Advantage: she senses that several human minds are voluntarily orienting toward the cold presence.
- Despair: Resonance Strain +1 and Hidden Attention +1. A nearby directory briefly replaces all floor names with vertical arrows pointing upward.

If Angie approaches one of the reacting people, they may simply leave. One can whisper, “Soon,” without elaboration. Avoid turning the scene into a lore dump.

## Scene 2: Frank's offline file changes while offline

**Time:** 15:23:12 or shortly afterward  
**Location:** wherever Frank stored his isolated copy

The file size does not change.

The timestamp does.

When Frank opens it on an isolated device, two new lines exist:

```text
ARRIVAL ACKNOWLEDGED
OBSERVER 2 RETAINED
```

If Frank has multiple copies, only one changes—the one he regards as the “real” working copy or the one he handled most often. Let that make no technical sense.

Niko can witness the metadata without seeing the whole contents if Frank wants partial trust.

> Niko: “Offline means offline.”
>
> If Frank says apparently not: “No. I refuse to let that word become negotiable.”

Frank may destroy a copy, make more copies, move it to paper/optical storage, or contact Angie.

If he reconnects the anomaly log to a live protected system during this period, set Deus Attention to 3 for December 19. Otherwise, do not punish caution.
