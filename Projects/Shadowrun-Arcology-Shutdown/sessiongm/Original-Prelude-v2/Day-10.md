# December 10 — Afterimages

## Scene 1: Angie's room before the Arcology wakes

**Time:** 06:40  
**Cast:** Angie, Hidoshi

> The room is quiet except for the ventilation and the soft electrical tick of devices that never seemed worth noticing yesterday.
>
> Today every active object has direction.
>
> The bedside display is a pressure to the left. The wall outlet is a small steady field. A cleaning drone moving in the corridor passes through Angie's awareness before its wheels become audible. Somewhere beyond the apartment wall, a sleeping neighbor's bioelectric presence is faint and warm in a way no machine is.

Do not describe this as perfect new sight. It is confusing, overlapping, and local.

Hidoshi has been awake. He has water, painkillers if appropriate, and no rehearsed speech.

> Hidoshi: “I have several theories. None of them are useful yet.”
>
> “Are you in pain?”
>
> “Do you know where you are?”
>
> “Do you want questions, or breakfast?”

Let Angie decide how much of the two presences she tells him. If she mentions the smaller frightened one, Hidoshi does not immediately label it good.

> “Afraid things can still be dangerous.”

If she mentions the cold one, he asks, “Did it understand that you were a person?” That question is more unsettling than any exposition.

**Average Discipline:** separate machines, people, and ambient infrastructure without overload.

- Success: establish **Sensory Baseline**.
- Advantage: Angie learns that closing her eyes reduces visual distraction but not the new data sense.
- Threat: Resonance Strain +1.

## Scene 2: The official version arrives over breakfast

Hidoshi receives a Renraku incident summary before he requests one.

```text
INCIDENT 7G-0912
MINOR ENVIRONMENTAL CONTROL FAULT
ONE VISITOR EXPERIENCED TRANSIENT SYNCOPE
NO DATA LOSS
NO SECURITY BREACH
NODE POWER STATE: INACTIVE
```

The terminal was supposedly inactive.

If Angie points out the impossibility, Hidoshi says, “Then we keep our own chronology.” He opens a paper notebook or a disconnected device and asks Angie to tell him only what she wants recorded.

This creates a human countermeasure: not hacking, not magic, just refusing to let a system be the only keeper of memory.

## Scene 3: Frank's inventory is impossible

**Time:** 08:20  
**Location:** maintenance check-in  
**Cast:** Frank, Niko, inventory clerk

Frank's tools show as fully returned—including any kit or part he physically abandoned during the escape.

If he still has everything, give him a different contradiction: a tool he used in 7-G is listed as checked into central stores at the exact moment it was in his hand.

Niko looks at the inventory screen and then at the actual tool.

> Niko: “I would like to meet whichever version of you is doing the paperwork. He is very efficient.”

The Node 7-G incident ticket is closed. It states the terminal was never powered.

Frank's hidden copy of `7G_ANOMALY.log` remains intact until he inspects it. The moment he checks its metadata, a checksum request appears against the file—from no visible network connection.

Do not automatically compromise the copy. Give Frank a chance to react.

**Hard Computers:** preserve it without allowing a live Renraku host to touch it.

- Success: secure the working copy.
- Advantage: Frank's Evidence +1.
- Despair: a second hidden checksum request appears later even while the device is physically isolated.

## Scene 4: Do they contact each other?

Neither character is required to initiate contact. Ask separately.

If Angie messages Frank, the message shows “sent” immediately but does not arrive for **nine seconds**.

If Frank messages Angie, the same delay occurs.

If they both message within the same nine-second window, both devices display the other person's message as timestamped one second *before* it was sent.

Do not explain it.

If they speak in person, choose a public, mundane location such as Eli's kiosk. Let their first non-crisis conversation be awkward if the players make it awkward.

Eli can break the tension without knowing what happened.

> Eli: “You two look like you either had a terrible night or are planning one.”

The scene should end with one practical decision, not emotional resolution: exchange contact details, compare only one piece of evidence, agree not to contact again, or decide to meet later.
