# December 18 — Last Promises

### GM hidden layer — Deus test: **T−1 / Final positioning**
**What Deus is testing:** Whether the last hidden movements and commands can be issued without triggering coordinated human resistance before the seizure begins.

**What is actually happening:** Maintenance traffic moves inward and upward, key personnel receive plausible late orders, medical and security resources are subtly repositioned, and the two known observers are given their final lure: return to Node 7-G with the local copy.

**Player-facing hint:** Deliveries are heavier than holiday demand explains; maintenance carts favor interior routes; some security teams are present where nothing has happened; Frank's order knows exactly what evidence he kept.

**Angie-facing hint:** Angie: The amber pulse becomes regular enough to feel less like a glitch and more like a countdown or distress beacon.

**Frank-facing hint:** Frank: His final order explicitly tells him to bring the local copy of evidence nobody should know he retained.

**Shutdown payoff:** The building enters December 19 with people, machines, supplies, chokepoints, and observers already close to where Deus wants them.

### What the day should accomplish

Restore warmth and ordinary plans while Deus completes final positioning. Make tomorrow feel real so the Shutdown has something to destroy.

> **GM PREP NOTE:** Scene 3 is intentionally placed here so you can prepare the day's Deus interaction first. During play, keep the numbered chronology unless the scene itself says to cross-cut earlier.

## Scene 3: Frank has a normal conversation too

Frank gets a mundane moment with Niko or an established loved one.

Niko might ask about the next week's schedule.

> Niko: “You taking the holiday shift?”
>
> “If you are, I am suddenly religious and unavailable.”

Or Emily/loved one asks when Frank will be home tomorrow.

Let Frank decide whether to disclose how worried he is. If he warns someone away from the Arcology, do not automatically reward or punish it. Record who believes him.

### If Angie and Frank are split

- **Angie sees:** Angie gets the warm plan with Hidoshi plus the increasingly regular amber pulse.
- **Frank sees:** Frank gets the ordinary conversation plus the impossible return order.
- **Cross-cut:** Cross-cut comfort against preparation. They do not need to meet before December 19.

> **OPTIONAL DEUS HINT — FRANK:** The final work order explicitly says to bring the local copy of evidence nobody should know Frank retained.

## Scene 1: Make the Arcology warm again

**Time:** afternoon  
**Location:** holiday public districts

Do not make today ominous from the first sentence.

> Artificial snowfall drifts through the main atrium and vanishes before touching the floor. Children rehearse a holiday performance beneath hanging lights. Restaurants confirm tomorrow's tables. Couriers complain about impossible delivery volume. A projection wall turns the upper balconies into a winter skyline no one in the building can actually see outside.

People are making plans.

Let familiar NPCs appear in small, ordinary ways:

- Eli is testing a holiday menu and forcing samples on Niko.
- Rowan is arguing that a corporate jingle does not become art merely because someone added bells.
- Marisol has bought a gift and refuses to say for whom.
- Evan is looking forward to being off shift in two days.
- Mina complains that holiday decorations keep blocking the clinic's directional signage.
- Sana is trying to locate a delayed shipment and blaming courier routing rather than conspiracy.

The point is not “everyone is doomed.” The point is that everyone has a tomorrow.

## Scene 2: Angie and Hidoshi make a plan

Give them uninterrupted time together.

Possible locations are callbacks chosen by the player: the garden, Rowan's terrace, Eli's kiosk, a quiet balcony, or even outside the Arcology.

Hidoshi asks what Angie wants to do tomorrow when his consultancy obligations are finished.

> Hidoshi: “One day in Seattle with no Renraku schedule.”
>
> “Name something.”

Whatever Angie chooses, Hidoshi answers concretely. If she says breakfast outside, he names a time. If she wants the garden, he says which entrance. If she wants to leave Seattle, he starts checking transport.

Record Angie's answer and Hidoshi's reply exactly.

Do **not** interrupt this conversation with the anomaly.

Let the promise stand cleanly for at least one scene.

> **OPTIONAL DEUS HINT — ANGIE:** The amber pulse is now regular enough that Angie can anticipate the next beat. Let her decide whether it feels like distress, command, or countdown.

## Scene 4: 20:10:35 — the order

At 20:10:35, Frank's queue changes.

```text
2059-12-19 18:55
RETURN TO NODE 7-G
BRING LOCAL COPY
OBSERVER COUNT INCOMPLETE
```

The order is under Frank's valid credentials.

There is no issuer.

If he prints it, the paper prints normally. If he photographs it, the image remains. This is not another disappearing clue; the system now wants him to see it.

Niko, if present, reads the text twice.

> “Local copy of what?”

Let Frank choose how much to tell him.

## Scene 5: Angie's nine-second pulse

At the same moment, Angie notices the amber pulse settle into a stable nine-second rhythm.

It is no longer occasional.

It persists whenever she becomes still enough to notice.

Do not tell her whether it is distress, command, beacon, or countdown. Ask:

**What about the sensation makes Angie interpret it one way rather than another?**

If Frank and Angie are already in contact, either may initiate a conversation. If they have avoided one another, the parallel events can force the *question* of contact without forcing trust.

Possible exchange if the players want a minimal, tense contact:

> Frank: “I got an order.”
>
> Angie: [player response]
>
> Frank: “Seven-G. Tomorrow. It says to bring the copy.”

Or Angie can contact first:

> “It's back. Every nine seconds.”

Let the players decide whether they arrange to meet, deliberately stay separate, or plan independent routes.
