# December 1 — A City Under One Roof

### GM hidden layer — Deus test: **T−18 / Establish the baseline**
**What Deus is testing:** Whether SCIRE's ordinary sensor network can quietly model where people go, who travels together, which workers improvise, and how quickly humans notice tiny deviations.

**What is actually happening:** Doors, lifts, cameras, retail systems, work queues, and environmental sensors are being correlated more aggressively than Renraku staff realize. Nothing needs to fail today; Deus is learning what *normal* looks like so later deviations can be measured.

**Player-facing hint:** A door opens a fraction before a badge is presented; a directory seems to anticipate a destination; a maintenance ticket appears moments before anyone reports the fault. Each incident is explainable by itself.

**Angie-facing hint:** Angie: A public directory highlights the route she was about to choose before she touches it, then pretends it was a normal recommendation.

**Frank-facing hint:** Frank: A maintenance ticket appears on his queue seconds before the associated sensor actually reports the fault.

**Shutdown payoff:** This becomes the predictive routing system that later knows which doors to close, which lifts to redirect, and where frightened people are likely to run.

### What the day should accomplish

Establish normality, scale, comfort, and the personal routines that the Shutdown will later destroy. There is no overt threat today. The oddities should be explainable as ordinary automation.


> **GM PREP NOTE:** Scene 3 is intentionally placed here so you can prepare the day's Deus interaction first. During play, keep the numbered chronology unless the scene itself says to cross-cut earlier.

## Scene 3: The same atrium, hours apart

End the day with a quiet cross-cut.

Rowan plays beneath a licensed sound canopy. Angie may pass in the late afternoon; Frank crosses the same atrium after shift change. Neither needs to notice the other.

> The same projected koi scatter for both of them. The same song rises into the artificial daylight. The building knows both names.
>
> They do not know each other's.

Ask each player one short question: **What ordinary thing do you assume will still be here tomorrow?**

### If Angie and Frank are split

- **Angie sees:** Angie can experience the atrium and Rowan's music in the afternoon.
- **Frank sees:** Frank crosses the same place after shift change and sees the same projected koi and crowd routines.
- **Cross-cut:** Do not reunite them. Cut from Angie's ordinary assumption to Frank's ordinary assumption.

## Scene 1: Arrival Concourse — Angie's first morning

**Time:** 08:10  
**Cast:** Angie, Hidoshi, Eli in the background, concierge staff, commuters, families

> The rain ends at the doors as completely as if weather were an outdoor service the building has declined to purchase. Warm air rolls over wet coats. Coffee, citrus cleanser, soy broth, machine ozone, damp wool, and thousands of people blend into a smell dense enough to have geography.
>
> The concourse rises for stories. Transparent elevator cars slide up and down the central void. Projected koi scatter around luggage wheels, reform behind boots, and gather around a little girl who keeps stamping at them to make them burst into light.
>
> Nothing here feels improvised. Even the crowd seems to have lanes.

Give Hidoshi a temporary Renraku consultant credential and Angie a dependent visitor credential. A concierge drone greets Hidoshi by name, then turns to Angie. It pauses one fraction too long over her third eye before offering a cheerful accessibility menu clearly written for cyberoptic users.

Hidoshi does not answer for her. He glances from the drone to Angie and says, “You may enjoy correcting it more than I will.”

If Angie engages with the drone, it apologizes smoothly and offers a revised menu. If she ignores it, it follows for three meters before deciding her non-response counts as dismissal. If she jokes, nearby people relax; if she is irritated, Hidoshi lets the irritation be legitimate rather than smoothing it over.

### Things happening around them

- A corporate school group is being counted by facial recognition. One child keeps stepping out of line to chase the projected koi, forcing the teacher's counter back to fourteen.
- A courier curses quietly because a lift has reprioritized his delivery three times.
- Eli is opening his breakfast kiosk and giving away samples to people who look lost rather than hungry.
- A pair of tourists photograph the vertical atrium and nearly collide with a service drone that stops millimeters from their luggage.

Eli can offer Angie a sample. He watches her reaction and says, “Too much clove?” before she says anything. He did not psychically read her; he saw her nose wrinkle and knows his own food.

Hidoshi's work appointment begins in twenty minutes. He can offer Angie options without choosing for her:

- the indoor garden;
- the public Matrix lounge;
- the performance terrace;
- the retail galleries;
- simply walking and seeing where the building leads.

Useful Hidoshi dialogue:

> “My meetings will be dull even by legal standards.”
>
> “Your credential is public-floor only. That is not a challenge.”
>
> If Angie gives him a look: “I know exactly how that sounded.”

Ask Angie's player what part of the Arcology she finds genuinely appealing. Record the answer. On December 19, show that same feature failing.

> **OPTIONAL DEUS HINT — ANGIE:** When Angie chooses a destination, the directory highlights it a fraction before she touches the interface. If she deliberately chooses somewhere else, the highlight corrects itself and behaves as though it never anticipated her.

## Scene 2: The service side — Frank at work

**Time:** 13:40  
**Cast:** Frank, Niko, two maintenance workers, Marisol passing through an access lobby

Cut hard from polished public space to a maintenance mezzanine.

> Behind the decorative walls the building stops pretending to be elegant. Ducts crowd the ceiling. Condensate lines sweat into labeled trays. Every surface is covered in inspection dates, marker notes, old adhesive, and the fingerprints of people who actually keep the place alive.
>
> The Arcology sounds different here. No music. No ads. Just fans, pumps, distant lift motors, and tools knocking against tool belts.

Frank and Niko are inspecting air returns. Niko notices the battered TunnelRat comic or image among Frank's things.

> Niko: “Please tell me that is a technical manual.”
>
> Niko, after Frank's answer: “Good. Because the official manuals have fewer believable heroes.”

A return vent is rattling. It stops whenever Frank comes within a few feet and begins again when he moves away. This is not paranormal; a loose baffle shifts as local pressure changes when a person blocks part of the corridor. Let Frank diagnose it if he cares.

Marisol appears at the service-desk window to complain that the vent outside her residential corridor has “changed pitch.” The desk clerk starts explaining that the monitoring dashboard is green.

Marisol snaps, “Then your dashboard has excellent hearing and terrible ears.”

Frank can intervene, ignore it, or simply listen. If he takes her seriously, she remembers him later.

### Optional check

**Average Mechanics** to diagnose the baffle before the system creates a ticket.

- Success: Frank earns **Service Shortcut**; Niko points out an old worker-maintained route between two public sectors.
- Advantage: Marisol decides Frank is “the one who actually listens.”
- Threat: Frank's badge briefly logs him eleven floors away. The dispatcher laughs it off as a sync error.

> **OPTIONAL DEUS HINT — FRANK:** Before the rattling return produces a formal sensor alert, Frank's queue briefly flashes a maintenance ticket for that exact vent. It disappears if he opens it too quickly.
