Know these things without looking them up:

- what Tavin was paid to do;
- why Rava agreed to raid;

# GM START HERE

## What this document is

This is the **during-session story document** for *Leylines and Tigers and Bears, Oh My!* It is deliberately more complete than a session outline. The intent is that you can read a scene before the game, understand what the place feels like and what every important person is trying to do, then run from that understanding when the player inevitably does something the written path did not anticipate.

The adventure is not written as a sequence of mandatory cutscenes. The scene flow is the most likely version of events if the characters follow the problem in front of them. NPCs are allowed to change their minds. Tavin can be caught in the first battle. Rava can become an ally without becoming friendly. Boros can help instead of obstructing. The player can destroy evidence you expected them to preserve. The finale can end in an arrest, a deal, an escape, a destroyed experiment, or a new partnership.

The document gives you enough story to improvise around those changes without losing the logic of the adventure.

## Heading and split contract

This file is designed for modular import.

- **H1 (`#`) is a major split point.** Every H1 can become its own Foundry Journal page or Obsidian note.
- **H2 (`##`) is a major table-running subsection inside that split.** Typical H2 sections are *Scene at a Glance*, *Pre-Session Prep*, *During-Session Flow*, *NPC Conversations*, *Tactical Environment*, and *Developments*.
- **H3 (`###`) is reserved for genuine sub-splits inside a larger H2**, especially individual beats in *During-Session Flow*. H3 is not used as decorative formatting.
- Smaller distinctions use **bold labels**, tables, callouts, or paragraphs rather than additional heading levels.

That keeps the Markdown useful both as a readable GM document and as source material for automatic splitting.


## Adventure-Book Use at the Table

The short tables and beat summaries remain intentionally easy to scan. They are the **reminder layer**.

The fuller prose in this version is the **adventure-book layer**: material prepared before play so you do not have to invent what a place looks like, what an NPC is wearing, how a crowd reacts, what a room smells like, or what somebody says when the player asks the obvious next question.

Use the prepared descriptions in whatever way fits the table:

- read a block nearly verbatim when the player first encounters something;
- paraphrase it when the current situation has changed;
- pull one or two sensory details from it during combat;
- ignore details that no longer make sense because of player action;
- use the longer NPC material to improvise conversations after the written beats diverge.

The prepared text is **not canon over play**. If the player destroys a balcony before its later description matters, the balcony is destroyed. If Rava becomes an ally earlier than expected, later descriptions should reflect that. The point of preparation is to give you enough concrete material that improvisation has something solid to grow from.

### What to describe when you need ten seconds to think

When the player does something unexpected and you need a moment, move the world rather than freezing it.

Describe one of these:

- **height:** another bridge, balcony, gantry, roofline, or street below;
- **infrastructure:** pipework, lifts, public lamps, transit plates, drainage, cables, magical conduits;
- **people:** workers, couriers, soldiers, performers, civilians, guild members, street vendors;
- **sound:** machinery, bells, crowd noise, metal, distant voices, pressure vents, drums;
- **smell:** hot metal, wet stone, soot, food, oil, ozone, vegetation, animal musk;
- **guild behavior:** somebody doing a job because their guild still has a job even while the adventure is happening.

Those details make Ravnica feel inhabited without requiring an exposition speech.

## Adventure premise

The character is already planeswalking when something in Ravnica reaches into the crossing and pulls sideways.

They arrive inside an Izzet research workshop that is already under attack. A Gruul raiding band has broken through one wall. The experiment is failing. A tiger that does not belong on Ravnica lands in the room with the character. In the confusion, a guildless planeswalker named Tavin Rell steals the most useful part of the experiment and runs.

The pursuit moves through the Tenth District: across civic viaducts, into the edge of a rubblebelt where a second displaced animal appears inside a prepared receiver, through a Rakdos street performance that refuses to stop being a performance just because the danger is real, and finally into a damaged Laboratory of Orientation annex where former Izzet chemister Ilyra Kest is trying to prove that repeatable living planar transfer can be engineered.

The joke in the title should slowly become evidence.

The tiger is strange.

The bear proves somebody planned for this.

The leylines are the actual problem.

## Recommended modes

| Mode | Party | What changes |
|---|---|---|
| **Solo L6** | One level-6 Planeswalker, Nemi Arven as persistent guest, up to one earned situational guest | Enemy pressure is redistributed among NPC agendas and environmental objectives. Solo Ilyra is used. Healing and recovery exist in-world. |
| **Standard L9** | 4–5 level-9 characters | Full standard deployments and named NPC stat blocks. Multiple objectives can be handled simultaneously by the party. |

The **story is the same in both modes**. Do not make Solo L6 feel like the simplified version of the real adventure. It should remain busy, vertical, socially complicated, and full of moving parts. The difference is that not every token on the map is trying to attack the same player.

| Stage | Visible problem | Story that develops during play |

## What not to protect

Do not protect these things just because they are useful to the written flow:

- Tavin escaping.
- The Orientation Core remaining intact.
- Rava surviving.
- Ilyra surviving.
- The bear surviving.
- Boros staying neutral.
- Nemi joining.
- Any specific clue object.

Protect **information flow**, not plot objects. If one clue is destroyed, another clue should still exist. If Tavin is captured, the story becomes a counter-raid. If Ilyra dies before explaining herself, her notes and the annex should still tell the story.

## The tone at the table

Ravnica is not medieval Europe with guild logos pasted over it. It is a vertical, overbuilt city-world whose institutions are run by ten enormous guilds. Public infrastructure can be magical. Military patrols can include goblins, minotaurs, humans, and angels. A demonic entertainment guild can legally stage a street show while a giant carries part of the stage. A broken neighborhood can turn into wilderness without ever leaving the city.

The city should feel busy enough that the player is never the only person making decisions.

# RAVNICA FOR A PLAYER WHO DOES NOT KNOW RAVNICA

## The city-world

Ravnica is a city that has grown until "outside the city" has largely stopped being a useful idea. Streets sit above older streets. New buildings grow over buried foundations, forgotten sewers, tombs, transit tunnels, ruined neighborhoods, and older civic works. Towers rise through layers of bridges and suspended platforms while deep below them the city continues into catacombs and undercity.

The Tenth District, where this adventure takes place, is one of the city's great central districts and a particularly intense concentration of guild activity. All ten guilds are present. A character who has never been here should feel less as if they entered a city and more as if they arrived inside a machine that has been running for thousands of years.

Describe height whenever you can. A street might cross a bridge that is itself above another street. A pedestrian lane might have a service gantry under it and a skyway above it. Public pipes and magical conduits cross old stone beside newer metal. A market stall can sit under the shadow of a guild monument while a levitating cargo platform rattles past overhead.

Ravnicans generally do not stop to admire any of this. It is home.

That difference is useful when the player is new to the setting. What is astonishing to them can be ordinary to everyone else.

## The ten guilds without a lecture

The player does not need a ten-guild history lesson at the beginning of the one-shot.

Use the guilds as they become relevant.

The important principle is that the guilds are not adventuring clubs. They are civic institutions, political powers, military forces, industries, religions, research organizations, criminal networks, and social identities. They overlap. They compete. A person can distrust a guild while still depending on the services it provides.

This adventure directly foregrounds four:

- **Izzet League** — public works, magical engineering, infrastructure, and experimental science.
- **Boros Legion** — Ravnica's standing army, driven by justice, protection, discipline, and sometimes dangerous zeal.
- **Gruul Clans** — anti-civilization clans living in ruins and rubblebelts, using scavenged city material while wanting the city's machine broken apart.
- **Cult of Rakdos** — a demonic entertainment guild whose street shows, laborers, miners, performers, and violent spectacle remain woven into Ravnican life.

Other guilds can appear as background texture, but there is no need to turn this one-shot into a setting encyclopedia.

## The Izzet League

The Izzet are the reason Ravnica can have running water, public boilers, levitating platforms, engineered roadways, complicated magical utilities, and laboratories that occasionally put holes in those same things.

Their color identity is Blue and Red, and that tension is visible in how they work. They can be intensely analytical and wildly impulsive in the same conversation. An Izzet researcher might take precise measurements, scribble a careful equation, then immediately decide that the fastest way to test it is to double the power and see what happens.

Their workshops should look used, modified, repaired, and modified again. Copper pipe, mizzium fittings, ceramic insulators, blue-white electrical arcs, scorched metal, colored glass, pressure gauges, suspended apparatus, chalked equations, work tables, and jury-rigged corrections all belong. Goblins often serve as enthusiastic attendants and experimenters. Humans are common researchers. Vedalken often bring greater method and obsessive detail.

This adventure uses two canonical Izzet research traditions as its foundation:

- the **Laboratory of Orientation**, concerned with teleportation and spatial recombination;
- the **Laboratory of Gravitational Inversion**, which studies flight and maintains a workshop near Augustin Station.

The actual planeswalker-catching experiment, orientation core, Ilyra Kest, Tavin's sabotage, and living receivers are original to this adventure.

When Olya talks about something dangerous, she does not sound like a mystic. She sounds like an engineer explaining why the boiler should not currently contain a tiger.

## The Boros Legion

The Boros are Ravnica's standing army. They care about law, but their self-image is larger than law enforcement: they see themselves as protectors of the innocent and an active force for justice.

Visually, Boros should be immediately recognizable. White cloth and pale metal are paired with red and warm gold. Their insignia is sun-like. Their armor tends toward military practicality embellished with angelic or radiant motifs. A Boros patrol does not need to be all human. Minotaurs have a proud martial place in the Legion; goblins and other peoples also serve. Angels occupy the highest symbolic and literal levels of Boros authority.

At street level, Boros soldiers tend to move like people who expect to work as a unit. They secure lanes, establish perimeters, move civilians, ask for clear answers, and care about chain of command. Individual members can be compassionate, rigid, hot-tempered, honorable, suspicious, or overzealous. The guild itself carries a real tension between disciplined order and righteous fury.

That matters for Sergeant Edda Rusk. She is not "the police obstacle." She has received reports of an Izzet explosion, a Gruul raid, an escaped predator, stolen experimental equipment, and armed strangers racing through a public transit route. Stopping the chase long enough to understand what is happening is exactly what a responsible Boros sergeant should do.

A player unfamiliar with Ravnica should learn what Boros is by watching Edda work before anyone gives them a history lesson.

## The Gruul Clans

The Gruul are not simply barbarians who live outside the city, because on Ravnica there is barely an outside left.

They occupy the places the city has abandoned, broken, overgrown, or failed to tame: rubblebelts, ruined districts, collapsed parks, old walls, damaged neighborhoods, and wild corridors between layers of construction. They believe the expanding city has crushed the natural world and turned life into machinery, bureaucracy, property, and containment.

Their appearance should communicate that relationship. Armor can be animal hide, bone, leather, and scavenged pieces of the city they despise. Weapons can be repurposed industrial metal, broken signs, demolition tools, or crude heavy blades. Tattoos, scars, trophies, and physical strength are common expressions of identity.

They do not need to hate the Izzet specifically to attack an Izzet workshop. The Izzet are an excellent symbol of the problem from a Gruul point of view: they find powerful things and put them in devices, pipes, cells, engines, and laboratories.

Rava Kett's raid is therefore credible before Tavin manipulates it. He does not invent Gruul anger. He gives that anger a time, place, and false explanation.

## The Cult of Rakdos

The Rakdos are the guild most likely to make a new player ask, "This is legal?"

The answer is complicated, which is very Ravnica.

The cult is centered on the demon Rakdos and embraces spectacle, impulse, pain, mockery, appetite, and freedom from respectable restraint. It is also historically tied to entertainment, mining, and manual labor. Rakdos performers can be acrobats, fire-eaters, musicians, beast handlers, lampooners, pain artists, spellcasters, and fighters. Their venues use black and red, chains, spikes, curtains, fire, cages, painted faces, and theatrical excess.

Street stages can appear in plazas at night and be gone by morning. Some are carried through the city on giants.

That is exactly what Scene Four uses.

The important thing is not to portray every Rakdos performer as an enemy combatant. Vexa Thorn is running a show full of people who may be reckless, cruel, brave, funny, professional, or all four. When a real chase enters the performance, her first problem is crowd behavior. If she screams that something has gone wrong, hundreds of spectators may panic in a space designed around moving stages, chain swings, fire, cages, and narrow lanes.

So she keeps directing.

It is one of the most Ravnican moments in the adventure: the emergency becomes part of the performance because that is the safest option available.


## Ready-to-Read Ravnica Descriptions

These blocks assume the character has little or no familiarity with Ravnica. If the character knows the plane, shorten them and give the player the name of what they are seeing immediately.

### The first time the city opens around them

> [!read-aloud]
> The first thing that makes Ravnica difficult to understand is that the city does not stop at the street.
>
> Buildings rise above buildings. Bridges carry traffic across other bridges, and far below them another road disappears beneath an arch that might be older than the district around it. Pipes, cables, stone aqueducts, metal service tracks, hanging signs, balconies, roof gardens, and narrow walkways fill the space between towers.
>
> There is no distant wall with countryside beyond it. Wherever you look, the city keeps going.

### Street-level Ravnica

> [!read-aloud]
> At street level, the impossible scale becomes ordinary life. A goblin porter argues over a crate beside a broad-shouldered minotaur in military colors. A loxodon steps carefully around a food cart built for smaller customers. A levitating cargo platform drifts past close enough that the operator leans over the rail to swear at somebody below.
>
> Nobody stops to admire any of it. They are late for work.

### Looking down from a high route

> [!read-aloud]
> The railing gives you a view of three different streets that never meet. One runs beneath you between shopfronts and market awnings. Another crosses it two stories higher on a stone viaduct. Far below both, lamps move through a service tunnel in a line like fireflies.
>
> The city has been built over itself so many times that "ground level" feels more like an opinion than a fact.

## Player-Facing Guild Descriptions

Use these when a guild first becomes relevant. They are deliberately written as things the character can perceive before anybody explains the politics.

### Izzet League — what the character sees

> [!read-aloud]
> The people in the blue-red work coats move with the confidence of technicians who have already survived several things that should have taught them caution. Their belts are crowded with tools, insulated gloves, folded lenses, bits of wire, ceramic components, and metal fittings that hum faintly with contained magic.
>
> Nothing in the workshop looks finished forever. New copper pipe has been bolted to old stone. A polished metal ring has three handwritten corrections chalked across it. A perfectly good control lever has a second lever welded beside it because someone apparently decided the first answer needed another answer.

If the player asks who they are, explain that the **Izzet League** is one of Ravnica's ten guilds: magical engineers and experimenters responsible for much of the city's infrastructure as well as research that routinely goes far beyond infrastructure.

### Boros Legion — what the character sees

> [!read-aloud]
> The soldiers are impossible to mistake for a private gang. Their armor repeats the same white, red, and warm-gold pattern from shield to shield. Sun-like insignia mark cloth panels and breastplates. They spread across the route in practiced positions, leaving clear space behind them for civilians to move away.
>
> They are not all the same people. One is human, another has the heavy horns and shoulders of a minotaur, and a goblin in fitted armor is hauling a barrier into place with grim concentration. What makes them look alike is not ancestry. It is drill.

If asked, explain that the **Boros Legion** is Ravnica's standing army and a major force for public protection and justice. Individual Boros can be compassionate, rigid, zealous, honorable, suspicious, or overbearing.

### Gruul Clans — what the character sees

> [!read-aloud]
> The raiders look as though the city tried to throw them away and they learned to wear the pieces.
>
> Hide and bone sit beside scavenged plates of metal. A shoulder guard was once part of something industrial. One weapon is a length of sharpened city hardware wrapped for a two-handed grip. Tattoos and old scars disappear under leather straps and fur. They move through broken masonry with the familiarity of people who trust ruins more than roads.

If asked, explain that the **Gruul Clans** live in rubblebelts, ruins, overgrown districts, and other places civilization has lost. They see the city and its guilds as a machine that has consumed the natural world.

### Cult of Rakdos — what the character sees

> [!read-aloud]
> At first it looks like a riot designed by somebody with an excellent sense of timing.
>
> Black-red banners hang from moving frames. Performers swing from chains between balconies. Fire rolls over a metal hoop as a painted acrobat dives through it. A caged drake snaps at the air above a lane packed shoulder-to-shoulder with spectators.
>
> Then the rear half of the stage takes a step.
>
> A giant is carrying it.

If asked, explain that the **Cult of Rakdos** is both a demonic cult and an entrenched part of Ravnican entertainment and labor. Their performances can be dangerous by design, but the audience is still an audience, not automatically a mob of enemies.

## How to describe Ravnica without stopping the game

Do not front-load paragraphs of lore.

Give the player one or two setting facts whenever a fact becomes actionable.

When Boros arrives, describe their white-red armor, unit movement, and angelic insignia before saying what they are.

When the character enters Gruul territory, let the paving stones become broken walls and weeds before explaining what a rubblebelt is.

When the Rakdos stage appears, let the player hear drums, chains, crowd noise, and the giant's footsteps before anyone says "Cult of Rakdos."

This document gives you fuller context so that you can answer questions naturally when the player asks.

# CAST, APPEARANCE, AND LIVING AGENDAS

## Chemister Olya Renn

Olya is an Izzet Orientation researcher who is good enough at her job to understand exactly how much she does not understand about what happened tonight.

She dresses for a laboratory rather than a battlefield: reinforced coat, tool harness, insulated gloves, lenses that can be flipped over one eye, and the kind of burns on clothing that nobody in the League bothers apologizing for. Her equipment looks maintained rather than decorative.

Olya's first instinct is not secrecy. It is triage.

She wants injured people moved, unstable systems shut down, the experiment understood, and the core recovered because it contains the best record of the event. She becomes suspicious when evidence points toward deliberate internal sabotage.

She is not trying to create artificial planeswalkers. Her experiment was intended to observe planar transit, not perform it.

**Voice:** fast, practical, irritated by imprecision.

**When someone asks a huge question during a physical emergency:**

> "Yes, I can explain why you're here. I can explain it while you hold that railing, or after it kills Nemi. Pick."

**When she confirms the tiger crossed alive:**

> "Good. Terrible. Useful. Mostly terrible."

**When somebody calls the apparatus a planeswalking machine:**

> "No. If it could do that, tonight would be much worse."

If ignored, Olya does not wait. She stabilizes what she can, sends word through Izzet channels, and starts reconstructing the sabotage herself.

## Nemi Arven

Nemi is a field technician attached to Olya's work. In the Solo L6 mode she is the persistent guest unit, but she should never feel as if she was spawned because the player needs a healer.

She is introduced as somebody in immediate danger who survives because of the character's actions or because Olya redirects resources to save her.

Nemi knows the hardware. She can recognize Izzet component families, operate controls, read a damaged system quickly, and tell when a machine is lying because somebody changed its assumptions. She is not a senior researcher and is not eager to pretend otherwise.

Her bravery is practical rather than theatrical. She is willing to crawl under a sparking relay because she knows which cable is actually dangerous. She is much less comfortable when someone hands her a sword and says "hold the line."

In Solo L6 she carries finite, visible support resources: *Cure Wounds*, a healer's kit, and two potions of healing.

**Voice:** intelligent, slightly dry, more willing than Olya to admit fear.

> "I know how the machine works. I do not know why it has decided the world should have an extra tiger."

If the player treats her like an expendable follower, she pushes back. Tactical control is not ownership.

## Tavin Rell

Tavin is a guildless planeswalker who survives by moving things and information between people who prefer not to be connected.

He has worked around Olya's lab before. That familiarity is why he can move through the opening scene without immediately looking like an intruder.

He took Ilyra's job because he believed he was stealing data and hardware from an experiment, not participating in live-animal transfer tests. He also tipped off Rava's people because he wanted noise and a breach.

He is responsible for the sabotage and for manipulating the raid.

He is not responsible for every thing that happens after it.

That distinction is important because it gives him room to become an ally without turning him innocent.

**Voice:** economical, evasive, funny when cornered because humor buys him thinking time.

> "You want the saboteur or the part? Because I can give you one faster than the other."

When the buyer attacks him:

> "Those aren't yours?"

If told no:

> "Good. They aren't mine either."

Tavin becomes more cooperative when the player protects him after the buyer turns on him. He does not become morally converted. He recognizes that the agreement he made is over.

## Rava Kett

Rava is a Gruul raid leader who believes she is destroying a dangerous piece of Izzet infrastructure before it can be used against the rubblebelt.

Her armor mixes hide, scavenged plating, and city debris made useful by someone who has no respect for the city's intended use of anything. Her people look like they have walked out of a ruined district rather than an organized barracks.

Rava is not stupid and is not a puppet.

Tavin misled her about the experiment and conveniently omitted the theft. Once she realizes that, her anger can change direction very quickly.

She respects strength, but strength to her can include acting decisively under pressure or refusing to let someone else choose your enemy for you.

**Voice:** blunt, physical, impatient with euphemism.

When she sees Tavin take the core:

> "You said we were breaking it."

Tavin:

> "You are."

Rava:

> "Then why are you stealing pieces?"

Rava can fight beside the player later without ever agreeing that the Izzet experiment should exist.

## Sergeant Edda Rusk

Edda is a Boros sergeant who makes Ravnica feel governed rather than empty between encounters.

Her armor is white and red with worn gold trim rather than ceremonial polish. Her people move when she points. She watches civilians as often as she watches armed strangers.

Edda believes in protecting people more than she believes in winning arguments.

She also has no reason to let an unknown planeswalker race through a public route because they say something worse is ahead.

**Voice:** clipped, professional, increasingly direct when time is short.

> "Weapons down enough that I can see your hands. You can keep running when I know what you're running toward."

If told there is no time:

> "Then use fewer words."

If the player earns her cooperation, she does not suddenly become a sidekick. She gives limited authority, opens routes, assigns Kell when appropriate, and expects a report afterward.

## Kell Ardan

Kell is a Boros field officer available as a situational guest in Solo L6.

He is not a second protagonist. He is the kind of soldier Edda can detach for a dangerous assignment because he follows instructions without losing sight of why the instructions exist.

Kell carries a spear, light crossbow, shield, *Healing Word*, and one potion of healing.

He obeys ordinary tactical commands, especially those tied to protecting people, holding ground, or stopping an immediate threat. He will not quietly help murder surrendered prisoners or attack uninvolved civilians because the player controls his token.

**Voice:** calm under pressure, respectful without being deferential.

> "Point me at the part that needs holding."

## Vexa Thorn

Vexa is a Rakdos stage manager.

She does not need to be the most flamboyant person on the stage because she is the person making sure everyone else hits their mark without falling under a giant.

She reads crowds, timing, danger, sightlines, and performer ego quickly. She understands within seconds that Tavin's chase is real.

Her decision to turn it into part of the show is not frivolous. It is crowd control.

**Voice:** theatrical when addressing the crowd, brutally practical when talking to people who can hear her normally.

> "If you make them panic, you get to count the bodies."

If the player gives her something spectacular to redirect attention:

> "Good. Do it again, but three steps left."

## Ilyra Kest

Ilyra is a former Izzet Orientation chemister who believes planar travel is an engineering problem that should not remain the accidental privilege of people born with Sparks.

Her visual language should still be Izzet, but stripped of institutional polish: repurposed apparatus, old tools, altered coat, burned gloves, private corrections written over official diagrams.

She is not trying to steal a Spark.

She wants repeatable living planar transfer.

She understands the suffering caused by failed prototypes and has chosen to treat it as a cost worth paying.

That is what makes her dangerous.

**Voice:** calm, exact, comfortable admitting facts that other villains would evade.

When accused of risking lives:

> "Yes."

Then:

> "So did the first person who crossed a sea. The difference is that we remember the destination and forget the drowned."

When told only planeswalkers can cross safely:

> "You can. That is not the same statement."

If her own people are badly injured, she orders them out. She is reckless with risk. She is not casually indifferent to people she knows.

## Ready NPC Descriptions — Named Cast

These are player-facing first-impression blocks. Use the longer dossiers above for motives and conversation; use these when you need to put a person in the player's head immediately.

**Olya Renn — first sight**

> [!read-aloud]
> The woman at the control bank looks as though the laboratory has been trying to kill her professionally for years and has not yet managed it. A reinforced blue-red coat hangs open over a fitted work harness crowded with tools. One insulated glove is blackened at the fingertips. A stack of lenses can be flipped down over one eye, but she keeps shoving them back up so she can see the entire room.
>
> She does not look at the explosion behind her. She has already decided which explosion matters more.

**Nemi Arven — first sight**

> [!read-aloud]
> Nemi is younger than the senior researchers and dressed for field work rather than ceremony: sturdy boots, a short Izzet work coat, leather tool loops, and protective lenses pushed up into untidy hair. There is soot on one cheek and a fresh tear across one sleeve.
>
> The fear in her face is real. So is the way she keeps looking at the machine while frightened, as though understanding it is how she prevents panic from winning.

**Tavin Rell — first sight**

> [!read-aloud]
> Tavin has the forgettable practicality of someone who makes a living passing through places where he does not want to be remembered. Dark traveling clothes sit under an Izzet-compatible work coat that is just convincing enough at a glance. Nothing on him jingles. Nothing catches unnecessarily on a railing. His eyes keep measuring exits even when he is talking to you.
>
> He looks relaxed in the specific way of a person working very hard at appearing relaxed.

**Rava Kett — first sight**

> [!read-aloud]
> Rava is built like somebody who expects doors to lose arguments. Hide and rough leather cover most of her armor, but plates of scavenged city metal reinforce the places that matter. Red-brown paint and old tattoos cross her arms. Her weapon looks less forged than repurposed from something that once belonged to a bridge or machine.
>
> She is not swinging wildly. She picks a piece of the laboratory, decides it should stop existing, and moves toward it.

**Sergeant Edda Rusk — first sight**

> [!read-aloud]
> The Boros sergeant's white-red armor has been maintained rather than polished. Gold edging is worn at the corners, and the cloth beneath one shoulder plate has been repaired carefully enough to suggest she did it herself. Her shield bears the Legion's radiant insignia.
>
> She keeps turning her head as she speaks, checking civilians, rooftops, exits, and her own soldiers. When she finally looks directly at you, you have the uncomfortable sense that she has already counted the weapons you are carrying.

**Kell Ardan — first sight**

> [!read-aloud]
> Kell wears lighter Boros armor than the soldiers holding the main line. A spear rides easily in one hand; a compact crossbow and a small medical pouch are secured where he can reach them without removing his shield.
>
> He has the expression of a man who has just been ordered to follow a stranger into a bad idea and has decided that complaining will not make the idea better.

**Vexa Thorn — first sight**

> [!read-aloud]
> Vexa is dressed in Rakdos black and red without looking like the person trying hardest to be seen. That is because everybody else on the stage is working for her. A chain of cue tokens hangs at her hip beside a knife, chalk, and a folded length of bright signal cloth. Her makeup is sharp around the eyes and already streaked slightly with sweat.
>
> She looks at a swinging performer, a moving platform, the crowd, Tavin, and you in less time than it takes most people to decide where the danger is.

**Ilyra Kest — first sight**

> [!read-aloud]
> Ilyra still looks Izzet even without a guild insignia. The habits are too deep. Her coat began life as proper laboratory gear before the sleeves were reinforced, the pockets altered, and several official markings cut away. Fine burn scars cross one glove. Chalk dust and metal filings mark the other.
>
> She stands beside the central apparatus with the posture of someone halfway through a calculation, not someone waiting for a confrontation.
>
> When she looks at you, the first emotion on her face is not fear.
>
> It is recognition.

## Ready NPC Descriptions — Supporting Cast

**Buyer Operative**

> [!read-aloud]
> The operative wears dark practical clothing with no obvious guild insignia. The cut is good enough to move in and plain enough to disappear in a crowd. A short blade sits where a casual glance would miss it, and a small case at the belt is built to protect documents or delicate components rather than coins.
>

**Buyer Spellcaster**

>

**Gruul Raider**

> [!read-aloud]

**Boros Legionnaire**

> [!read-aloud]

>

# WHAT HAPPENED BEFORE THE CHARACTER ARRIVES

## Olya's legitimate experiment

The Laboratory of Orientation studies teleportation and spatial recombination. Olya's team has been attempting something narrower than planar transport: detect the magical wake of a planeswalker crossing without creating a route themselves.

A cooperating planeswalker was supposed to travel on a known schedule.

That cooperating planeswalker was Tavin.

The equipment would observe the disturbance and compare it to Izzet teleportation data.

Olya expected a reading.

She did not expect passengers.

## Ilyra's project

Ilyra believes a Planeswalker's passage proves that living matter can cross the Blind Eternities and remain coherent. She does not need to reproduce a Spark if she can reproduce the conditions around a crossing.

Her earlier work was shut down after live-subject tests became unsafe.

She continued privately.

By the time this adventure begins, she has already built crude receivers using stolen and repurposed Orientation components.

The prepared bear site is one of them.

## Tavin's job

Tavin's job sounded simpler than it was.

He would plant a false input in Olya's apparatus, perform the scheduled planeswalk, let the machine gather unusually strong data, steal the Orientation Core, and carry it to Ilyra.

He wanted enough chaos around the lab to hide the theft.

So he gave Rava a version of the truth: the Izzet were testing dangerous spatial machinery near territory the Gruul cared about.

He omitted his own role.

## The unexpected crossing

The character planeswalks at exactly the wrong moment.

Olya's sabotaged apparatus reaches farther than intended and catches a stronger crossing than Tavin's scheduled test.

The character is redirected to Ravnica.

A tiger is caught in the same event.

That is the accident.

The bear later is not.

# SOLO L6 MODE — FIELD TEAM, HEALING, AND COMMAND

## Design goal

Solo mode preserves the original adventure complexity.

It does not reduce each scene to "one player versus one monster."

Instead, the battlefield contains independent NPCs with independent goals. One persistent guest and up to one situational guest give the player enough bodies to interact with multi-level maps without pretending one character can physically be everywhere.

## Persistent guest: Nemi Arven

Nemi is the default persistent guest after Scene One.

The player controls her ordinary tactical movement/actions in combat.

The GM controls her personality, loyalty, fears, opinions, and refusal of orders that would violate who she is.

Her support resources are finite and native:

- *Cure Wounds*;
- Healer's Kit;
- 2 Potions of Healing;
- Arc Pistol;
- Shock Baton;
- Relay Technician.

## Situational guest slot

Only one situational guest should normally be under direct player tactical control at a time.

Possible guests:

- Kell Ardan;
- Tavin Rell;
- Rava Kett;
- another NPC earned through play.

This slot should emerge from choices.

It is not a party-selection menu.

## Command style

Normal tactical orders are free communication.

The player does not spend a Bonus Action every round to tell a guest to do ordinary things.

Examples:

- hold that stair;
- heal me;
- take the relay;
- cover Tavin;
- get the technician out;
- stop that operative.

Unusual out-of-turn coordination can have a cost if a specific scene mechanic requires it.

## Incapacitation

When the player reaches 0 HP, friendly NPCs react according to position and priorities.

Nemi uses healing if she can reach them.

Kell may use *Healing Word*.

A nearby ally may stabilize or drag them.

If no one can help, normal danger remains.

Different scenes also support believable defeat states:

- Boros can take the player into custody;
- buyer operatives may capture rather than kill the unexpected planeswalker;
- Rakdos performers can drag an unconscious body out of the moving crowd lane;
- Rava may leave a defeated opponent because the machine, not murder, was her objective.

These are consequences, not invisible immunity.

# STANDARD L9 MODE — PARTY RUNNING NOTES

## Design goal

Standard mode uses the same story with more simultaneous agency.

A full party can divide between rescue, pursuit, control points, and combat. That means you can activate more of the battlefield at once without increasing raw enemy aggression unnecessarily.

## Party spotlight

Do not let the player with the best movement solve every environmental problem.

Use separate elevation bands, conversations, controls, and NPCs so different characters can matter.

## Healing and failure

Standard D&D party recovery applies normally.

NPC aid still exists because the world remains alive, but it is not intended to replace party healing.

# PLAYER CHOICES THAT SHOULD CHANGE THE WORLD

## The tiger

Saving it gives the city a living example of planar displacement.

Killing it removes that evidence but may be tactically reasonable.

Let NPCs react according to their values without using the adventure to scold the player.

## Rava

If the player treats her as a person with a reason, she can become a useful contact.

If they kill her people indiscriminately, Gruul territory becomes harder.

## Boros

Cooperation creates routes, backup, and legitimacy.

Violence creates warrants, suspicion, and later perimeter pressure.

## Tavin

Protecting him after the buyer turns on him changes what he gives willingly.

Trying to kill him can still produce information, but usually through captured evidence rather than trust.

## Ilyra

Her ideology is intended to create a real question without requiring the player to agree with her methods.

The player can oppose the experiment while agreeing that planar travel should not belong only to people with Sparks.

That tension can survive the one-shot.

# SCENE 1 — BROKEN ARRIVAL WORKSHOP

## Scene at a Glance

The character arrives in Ravnica mid-disaster. The scene should teach four things without a lecture:

1. planeswalking has gone wrong;
2. Izzet science can be brilliant and dangerous at the same time;
3. the Gruul raid has a reason even if the raiders are wrong about the experiment;
4. Tavin is exploiting a crisis that already has its own momentum.

The player should leave this scene with questions, not answers.

## Setting and First Impression

This is an Izzet Orientation workshop operating near the infrastructure around Augustin Station. It is not a clean wizard's laboratory.

The room is tall because equipment needs clearance. Service gantries cross above the main floor. Copper and mizzium conduits disappear into old stone walls. Some parts of the building are older than the current lab and have been repurposed three times. Blue-white electrical arcs crackle between damaged contacts. Red warning lamps turn the smoke orange. A worktable has been shoved against a railing as an emergency brace. A goblin technician is trying to shut a valve with a wrench nearly as large as he is.

Then part of the west wall is simply gone.

Through the breach come Gruul raiders in hide, scavenged metal, bone, tattoos, and pieces of the city turned into weapons. Their presence should visually clash with the lab without feeling like they came from another genre. The lab contains the city. The Gruul wear pieces of it.

The tiger is the only thing in the room that truly does not belong.

## Pre-Session Prep

Have the Broken Arrival map ready with the six selectable mana/map Regions.

Know where these things are before initiative:

- the character's arrival point;
- the tiger;
- Olya;
- Nemi and the trapped technician;
- Rava;
- the direct Gruul threat;
- Tavin's entry route;
- the Orientation Core;
- the containment wing;
- the service route Tavin can use if he runs.

In Solo L6, keep one Gruul raider as the character's immediate enemy. Rava is busy with the machine unless engaged. The Weird is escalation, not starting pressure.

Decide what the character was trying to reach before the interrupted planeswalk. That destination matters emotionally even though Ravnica wins the crossing.

## During-Session Flow

### Beat 1 — The sideways pull

Begin inside the planeswalk.

Give the character a sensory detail that belongs to their Planar Signature or normal way of traveling.

Then something changes direction.

Do not describe it as a hand or hook unless that matches the character's own perception. The important sensation is that their intended destination remains present for one impossible moment while a second place overlays it.

Use the workshop as fragments first: iron grating, blue light, smoke, a falling platform, someone shouting in a language the character may not know, then gravity.

Suggested opening:

> Something catches you between worlds.
>
> Not a person. A direction.
>
> Your destination is still there for one impossible instant, but another place has been laid across it: iron walkways, blue-white light, smoke, a wall breaking inward.
>
> Then gravity chooses for you.
>
> You hit stone.
>
> A platform above you is coming loose. People in white-blue work coats are shouting over a bank of sparking machinery. Huge figures in hide and scavenged metal are coming through a hole in the wall.
>
> And a tiger lands hard enough to skid across the tiles beside you.

Give the player a moment to react before explaining anything.

### Beat 2 — Let the room answer questions through action

If the player asks, "Where am I?" Olya does not stop to explain Ravnica.

She points at the falling platform.

> "Alive. Help me keep it that way."

If the player asks what the raiders are, someone can shout "Gruul" while taking cover.

If they ask what the machine is, the answer can wait.

The first round should establish that several things are happening at once:

- Nemi and another technician are in danger.
- One Gruul raider is closing on an important control position.
- Rava is trying to destroy the apparatus.
- The tiger is frightened by light and noise.
- Olya is trying to stabilize the system.
- Tavin is not yet the obvious center of the scene.

This lets the player choose what they care about before the plot tells them what they should care about.

### Beat 3 — The battle becomes a story scene

Once the player commits to an objective, move the others.

If they rescue Nemi, Olya gains time to notice the false input.

If they fight the Gruul, Nemi may solve part of the platform problem herself.

If they calm the tiger, Rava notices that the newcomer is not simply another Izzet defender.

If they rush the core, Tavin sees them and changes approach.

Tavin should enter as somebody who seems to belong there. He knows the route. He has handled the lab before. In the confusion he can look like another technician or contractor moving with purpose.

Only when he reaches the core, data cylinder, or control assembly should his behavior stop making sense.

### Beat 4 — Rava realizes the raid has a second purpose

The best version of this reveal happens while combat is still live.

Rava sees Tavin taking something.

> "You."

Tavin looks up.

> "Busy."

> "You said we were breaking it."

> "You are."

> "Then why are you stealing pieces?"

That exchange should be short enough not to stop the battle.

It changes Rava's understanding immediately. She may still hate the lab. She now also knows somebody used her.

If the player already captured Tavin, Rava can reach the same conclusion by finding his planted access, hearing his explanation, or recognizing the contact who gave her the timing.

### Beat 5 — Escalation belongs to neglected systems

Do not release the Weird because the encounter needs another monster.

Release it if containment was ignored or damaged.

Do not make the platform collapse because the script says round three.

Collapse it if nobody solved its problem.

Do not have the tiger attack randomly.

It attacks when cornered, struck, blocked from escape, or terrified by a nearby blast.

The environment should feel causal.

### Beat 6 — The fight ends before the scene ends

When immediate violence stops, keep the room moving.

Technicians count injuries.

Somebody curses because a pressure line has burst.

A Gruul survivor drags a wounded clanmate toward the breach.

Olya starts reconstructing the experiment before the player has fully caught their breath.

If Nemi survived, she should be doing something useful immediately rather than waiting to be recruited.

This is the moment the adventure becomes an investigation and pursuit.

## Area-by-Area Living Environment

### Central Orientation Ring

The central ring is the room's visual and mechanical heart. Nested metal arcs stand around a marked floor plate. Suspended glass elements and calibration arms can rotate independently. The core housing sits high enough that a technician normally reaches it from the control dais rather than the floor.

During calm operation, the ring produces a low harmonic vibration. Tonight it surges irregularly. Fine metal tools creep a fraction of an inch across nearby tables whenever the planar field spikes.

Technicians avoid crossing the marked center unless necessary.

### Upper Research Gallery

The upper level contains worktables, reference slates, instrument racks, and the controls least likely to be accidentally kicked by a goblin carrying hot metal.

From here a character can see how vertical the workshop really is. Pipes continue upward through the ceiling. A narrow transit rail exits through an arch toward another structure. The gallery is where a researcher would normally observe the ring rather than stand beside it.

### West Garden Platform

The "garden" is an Izzet safety measure created by somebody who discovered that exhausted researchers make bad decisions. Hardy plants grow in raised stone beds beside benches and a water cistern.

Gruul raiders hesitate here if they have no immediate enemy, because living growth inside the lab complicates their mental picture of the place.

The tiger prefers this area if given a safe route.

### East Transit Platform

This side of the workshop connects to the district's service infrastructure. A loading plate, cargo rail, and crane controls make it possible to move heavy machinery without taking the main stairs.

The platform gives Tavin several believable escape options.

### Lower Service Works

Below the main lab floor, narrow bridges cross machinery, drainage channels, and heavy conduits. It is louder here. Conversation requires raised voices.

This is a good space for a pursuit to become physical rather than purely ranged.

### Containment Side

Reinforced cells hold unstable experimental material and, occasionally, living magical phenomena. Warning bands and thick ceramic insulators make this part of the room look more cautious than the rest of the lab.

That caution is useful information: the Izzet know some of what they work with is dangerous. The problem is not that they are unaware of risk. It is how much risk they consider acceptable.

## Quick Dialogue Reminders

**Olya explaining the Izzet without saying "Izzet lore":**

If the player stares at the machinery:

> "Orientation lab. Teleportation, recombination, direction. Usually the direction is chosen."

If they ask whether disasters like this are normal:

> "This one? No. Disasters? Different question."

**Rava if confronted about the raid:**

> "Your machine reached into somewhere it had no right to touch."

If the player says it is not their machine:

> "Then we're both angry at the right people."

**Nemi after being rescued:**

> "Thank you. I would like to repay that by never doing whatever that was again."

Then she looks at the core housing.

> "Unfortunately, somebody just stole the part that tells us what it was."

**Tavin if caught:**

> "If you want the person who paid for this, keeping me alive is substantially more useful than the alternative."

He is bargaining, not confessing.

## Living World Behavior

Olya prioritizes people over the core if forced to choose.

Rava prioritizes destroying the machine until she learns she was manipulated; afterward she may prioritize Tavin or withdrawal.

Tavin prioritizes leverage. If the core is impossible to take, he takes logs or evidence. If he is captured, he trades information for survival.

The tiger seeks distance and cover.

Nemi follows technical emergencies first. In Solo L6 she becomes the persistent guest because Olya sends someone who understands the hardware, not because Nemi suddenly decides to become an adventurer.

## Tactical Environment

Use the workshop's verticality.

The player should be able to solve problems with movement and objects, not only attacks:

- move the exterior crane;
- brace or cut the falling platform;
- use a maintenance stair or leap between levels;
- vent pressure;
- isolate power;
- redirect the tiger toward the safety garden;
- deliberately open or seal containment;
- destroy machinery to stop a worse failure;
- use summons on separate elevation bands;
- use local teleportation without confusing it with planeswalking.

The Central Orientation Ring is the strongest live-mana area. The safety garden is more mixed. The lower service works expose Colorless and industrial Red/Blue.

## Standard L9 Deployment

Use the standard scene's full encounter placement.

The party can split objectives naturally. Do not increase enemy aggression simply because there are more PCs; let more of the room become active.

Rava can engage one group while Tavin works another route.

## Solo L6 Deployment

The player begins alone.

One Gruul raider is the immediate enemy. Rava does not spend every turn attacking the player unless they make themselves her problem.

Olya takes meaningful actions on her turn.

Nemi becomes available as a guest after the scene if she survives and the relationship makes sense.

If the player is incapacitated, Olya and surviving technicians react if physically able. This is visible rescue behavior, not hidden plot armor.

## Clues and Discoveries

The player should be able to establish at least two of these before leaving:

- the experiment received an input Olya did not authorize;
- Tavin had legitimate or stolen access before the breach;
- the tiger's arrival is tied to the same planar event as the character;
- the Gruul did not know about the theft;
- the core or stolen data is unusually valuable because it records the crossing.

No single check should gate the pursuit.

## Developments and Branches

**If Tavin escapes with the core:** run the default pursuit.

**If Tavin escapes without the core:** he takes the best remaining data. Ilyra still wants him and the information.

**If Tavin is captured:** the adventure becomes a race to the prepared receiver and then a counter-raid on Ilyra. Buyer operatives may come to the party instead of the party chasing Tavin.

**If Rava is killed:** Gruul survivors carry the story of the raid forward. Later cooperation with the clans becomes harder, not impossible.

**If the tiger escapes:** reports spread through Boros channels. This becomes background texture during the pursuit.

## Transition to the Next Scene

Olya finally gives the player the shortest version of the truth:

> "We were watching a planar crossing. Watching. Not pulling."
>
> "Tavin was the scheduled walker."
>
> "You were not."
>
> "Something made the array reach."
>
> "If he has the core, he has the best record we've ever seen of what just happened to you."

That is enough reason to move.

# SCENE 2 — NO SAFE ROAD

## Scene at a Glance

This scene teaches the player that Ravnica reacts.

They are no longer in an isolated workshop. They are moving through a crowded city whose authorities have heard conflicting reports.

The obstacle is not "fight the guards." It is that the Boros Legion has every reason to stop an armed stranger running out of an Izzet disaster.

## Setting and First Impression

The route out of the workshop rises into one of Ravnica's layered transit corridors.

The city opens around the character.

Stone arches support newer metal walkways. Pipes cross building faces. Service tracks run beside pedestrian lanes. Cargo moves on platforms that do not have obvious wheels. Shop signs crowd the lower levels while towers disappear upward behind bridges. Somewhere below, another street is carrying completely unrelated traffic.

Let the player see different peoples without presenting them as exotic exhibits: goblins moving crates, a minotaur arguing with a vendor, human couriers, a loxodon walking carefully through a space built too narrowly for them.

Then Boros closes the route.

Their formation should immediately contrast with the chaos of the Izzet lab. White-red armor. Sun insignia. Shields where they matter. Civilians moved behind a clear line. A sergeant watching everyone at once.

## Pre-Session Prep

Know whether Tavin is physically ahead of the party or whether the player is carrying/custodying him.

If Tavin was captured, buyer observers can be the moving target instead.

Decide whether Edda has already spoken to Olya through official channels. If she has, she is less suspicious of the existence of the Izzet emergency but still suspicious of the unknown planeswalker.

In Solo L6, Kell is available if the player earns Edda's cooperation.

## During-Session Flow

### Beat 1 — Make the city feel bigger than the chase

Give the player a few moments of movement before the checkpoint.

Nemi can answer setting questions while running.

This is where a new player can learn that the Boros are not the same thing as the Azorius government. Boros are soldiers and protectors. The Azorius write and administer much of the law. The distinction only needs to be as detailed as the player asks.

### Beat 2 — Edda closes the route

Edda's people do not immediately attack.

> "Stop the rigs. All of them."

If Tavin is fleeing, he can exploit the moment:

> "They attacked the laboratory."

That lie is plausible because the player is armed, dirty, and coming from the direction of the disaster.

Edda looks at the player:

> "Your version."

Do not ask for a Persuasion check before the player actually says something.

### Beat 3 — Keep the chase moving while the conversation happens

This is where the scene becomes tactical.

If the player stops to explain, Tavin gains distance.

If Nemi is present, she can continue the explanation while the player uses another route.

If the player shows evidence, Edda responds to evidence.

If the player jumps the checkpoint or attacks Boros, the city changes its opinion of them.

Use the upper pedestrian span, central service road, lower maintenance route, and side access as different social and movement choices rather than merely elevation bonuses.

### Beat 4 — Edda chooses what kind of problem this is

Once convinced that a larger threat is real, Edda does not apologize for stopping them.

She adjusts.

> "Kell. With them."

Kell:

> "Understood."

> "If they're lying, bring them back."

That is the natural entry point for Kell in Solo L6.

If the player never earns Boros cooperation, Kell does not join simply because the build expects him.

### Beat 5 — Tavin realizes who the character is

If Tavin did not already understand that the character was the unexpected crossing, let him realize it here.

He sees a Spark-related effect, hears Olya's message, or sees the character interact with planar residue.

> "You were the crossing?"

If confirmed:

> "That explains the tiger."

If asked what that means:

> "It means I was paid substantially less than this job was worth."

That line shifts Tavin from smug thief toward someone who also suspects he was kept in the dark.

# SCENE 3 — THE BEAR SITE

## Scene at a Glance

The tiger could have been an accident.

The bear is proof of intention.

This scene changes the mystery from "what went wrong?" to "who has been testing this?"

## Setting and First Impression

A rubblebelt is not empty wilderness. It is a place where the city lost.

Paving breaks into old foundations. Walls stand without buildings. Vines and tough trees root through masonry. A collapsed transit line may be the highest ground. Water gathers where streets no longer drain correctly. Fires burn in sheltered ruins.

The Gruul fit this environment because it is the version of Ravnica they want to preserve or expand.

As the player approaches, the prepared receiver should look wrong for the place.

It is an Izzet-derived machine without Izzet discipline: metal ring pieces bolted between broken walls, copper braid run through old masonry, chalk corrections, scavenged insulators, and a power source hidden under rubble.

Then it activates.

## Pre-Session Prep

Decide whether Rava is alive and whether she knows Tavin used the raid.

Decide whether Boros support is present.

Place the buyer operative where they can either monitor the receiver or destroy evidence.

The bear should have a visible escape direction.

Do not trap it in the center of the map unless somebody actively blocks it.

## During-Session Flow

### Beat 1 — Let the player notice the machine before it works

Nemi should be the first to recognize pieces.

> "That's ours."

She moves closer.

> "Not ours ours. I mean those parts."

Give the player time to inspect, position, or interrupt.

If they stop the transfer before the bear comes through, that is a legitimate success. The partially transferred signature, prepared ring, and buyer evidence still prove the experiment was intentional.

### Beat 2 — The bear arrives

If it fires:

> The metal does not hum so much as strain.
>
> Blue light jumps around the scavenged ring in short, ugly steps.
>
> The space inside it folds.
>
> Something brown and enormous hits the ground hard enough to scatter broken brick.
>
> The bear looks at Ravnica.
>
> Ravnica looks back.

Do not immediately roll the bear as an enemy.

Give it animal behavior.

It wants away from the machine.

### Beat 3 — The buyer tries to erase the story

The buyer operative's objective is evidence.

They cut a line, burn a plate, grab a record, or attack Nemi if she is about to preserve data.

This is what makes the scene tactically interesting for one character.

The player cannot simply "fight the bear encounter" because the actual adversary may leave while they do.

### Beat 4 — Rava can enter for reasons of her own

If Rava appears, she does not arrive saying she has joined the party.

She sees Izzet-derived equipment operating beside Gruul territory.

> "That one yours too?"

Nemi:

> "No."

Rava:

> "Good."

She hefts her weapon.

> "Then I can break it without another speech."

If the player wants evidence preserved, they now have to negotiate with someone who is completely reasonable from her own point of view.

That is better than a Persuasion gate. Give them a concrete compromise: let Rava break the power system after Nemi copies the data, or let her destroy the receiver ring while the player keeps the control plate.

### Beat 5 — The truth lands

Once the player understands that the receiver was built before the opening accident, give the conclusion plainly.

Nemi:

> "The tiger could have been us."
>
> She points to the ring.
>
> "This wasn't."

That should be the story pivot.

# SCENE 4 — THE RED STAGE

## Scene at a Glance

This scene should feel like Ravnica refusing to stop being itself just because the plot arrived.

The pursuit collides with a Rakdos performance in the Smelting Quarter.

The crowd thinks it is part of the act.

Vexa decides that keeping them believing that may be safer than telling them the truth.

Then the buyer turns on Tavin.

## Setting and First Impression

The Smelting Quarter should feel hotter and denser than the previous scenes.

Industrial light flickers across stone. Furnace glow leaks from vents and workshops. Metal carts rattle. Soot settles on older architecture. Somewhere deeper in the district, Izzet metallurgy and ordinary industry overlap with the neighborhoods that support them.

Then the Rakdos show cuts across the route.

Red curtains hang from moving frames. Chains swing between balconies. Fire performers use spaces that would be illegal in almost any other city. Painted faces and black-red costumes fill temporary platforms. A caged drake snarls above the crowd. The back half of the stage is moving because a giant is carrying it.

The audience is not a cult meeting.

It is a night out.

That distinction matters.

## Pre-Session Prep

Know Tavin's relationship to the player.

If the player has already captured him, replace "chase Tavin" with "buyer tries to seize Tavin/core from the group."

Set Vexa's moving-stage options where you can reach them quickly.

Decide whether the Buyer Spellcaster is active at the beginning or reserved as escalation.

In Solo L6, begin with one buyer operative unless the player has assembled a very strong field team.

## During-Session Flow

### Beat 1 — Enter the show before explaining it

Let the player hear it first.

Drums.

Crowd roar.

Metal chains.

A burst of flame reflected off windows.

Then Tavin goes through a false wall or across a moving platform and the audience cheers because they think that entrance was rehearsed.

The player follows.

They cheer louder.

### Beat 2 — Vexa takes control of the emergency

Vexa spots the difference between performance violence and real violence quickly.

She gets close enough to the player to speak without projecting.

> "If you make them panic, you get to count the bodies."

If the player demands an evacuation:

> "With what exit?"

She points to a lane full of people.

> "Give me a reason for them to look left."

This is an invitation to improvise.

A flashy spell, summon, challenge, illusion, staged duel, or deliberately dramatic entrance can become crowd-control technology.

### Beat 3 — Make the stage move

The map should change even if nobody attacks.

The Main Stage rolls forward.

The Giant Frame changes one route.

A Chain Lane becomes reachable.

Crowd density shifts because Vexa redirects attention.

The player should be thinking about position every round.

### Beat 4 — The buyer attacks Tavin

This should happen after the player has spent enough time treating Tavin as the target.

A shot, spell, or sudden strike comes from the wrong direction.

Tavin reacts:

> "Those aren't yours?"

If the player says no:

> "Good. They aren't mine either."

The buyer calls down something simple:

> "Delivery was the agreement. Survival was not."

That line turns the fight.

Tavin is still responsible for the sabotage.

He is now also a witness somebody wants dead.

### Beat 5 — The player's earlier choices change the team

If the player protected Tavin previously, he is much more likely to cooperate.

If they humiliated or tried to kill him, he may still fight beside them for thirty seconds while looking for his own exit.

If Vexa trusts the player to keep the crowd safe, she can become a map-level ally even without joining the party.

If Kell is present, he may be deeply uncomfortable with everything about the venue while still recognizing Vexa is preventing casualties.

### Beat 6 — End with leverage, not necessarily capture

The scene can end with:

- Tavin captured by the player;
- Tavin willingly cooperating;
- Tavin taken by the buyer;
- the buyer fleeing with the core;
- Tavin escaping but leaving a clue deliberately;
- the player gaining Ilyra's name early.

All routes can move toward the White Bridge / Orientation annex.

# SCENE 5 — THE WHITE BRIDGE

## Scene at a Glance

This scene turns geometry into conversation.

The bridge spatially divides the people who need each other, then forces cooperation while the most important information in the adventure comes out.

For Solo L6, it is especially effective because the character and Tavin can end up isolated together while Nemi works another elevation.

## Setting and First Impression

The bridge is civic Ravnica at its most confident: pale stone, ordered railings, statues or formal architectural elements, long sightlines, and engineered routes crossing a deep urban drop.

Then it duplicates badly.

One section remains where it should be.

Another appears higher and offset.

A maintenance span is visible through a gap where empty air should be.

For a moment, people on different versions of the bridge can see one another without sharing the same path.

The effect should feel like Izzet Orientation science leaking into public architecture.

## Pre-Session Prep

Choose who is physically near Tavin when the fault fires.

Do not always put the protagonist and Tavin together just because the script says so. Use actual positions.

Know which controls can stabilize which spans.

In Solo L6, position Nemi so that the player can direct her to a separate control point.

## During-Session Flow

### Beat 1 — Break the battlefield, not the story

When the fault fires, describe architecture changing rather than teleporting everyone arbitrarily.

A parapet rises thirty feet.

A section slides sideways without moving through the intervening space.

A staircase reaches a landing it was not built for.

Then ask for positions.

The player should immediately see that this is a tactical problem.

### Beat 2 — Give separated allies real jobs

Nemi can reach one control.

Kell can hold a route.

Rava can physically break an obstruction.

Tavin may know how old Orientation controls are supposed to respond.

The character cannot do every job personally.

That is the point.

### Beat 3 — Let the conversation happen in initiative

If Tavin is near the player, this is where he stops deflecting.

> "You keep asking who hired me as if that's the important part."

If pressed:

> "The important part is that they knew exactly when the experiment would fire."

Let that land, then keep the turn moving.

If told about the bear receiver:

> "That was live?"

If yes:

> "That was not in the contract."

If the player protected him at the Red Stage:

> "Ilyra Kest. Orientation chemister. Former. Very former."

This is not a cutscene. If someone is shooting at them, Tavin says these things while taking cover.

### Beat 4 — Make the bridge reward creative movement

The player can:

- activate both controls;
- fly;
- teleport locally;
- use a summon;
- create a physical crossing;
- collapse the wrong segment so the geometry settles;
- intentionally ride a phased span;
- throw or pass equipment between elevations.

Allow solutions that bypass the expected controls.

### Beat 5 — Optional Ramza hook

If Ramza "Zero" Beoulve is the player and the campaign wants his old identity surfacing this early, a buyer operative can recognize the surname from off-plane intelligence.

Do not connect his family secretly to Ilyra.

The operative has seen a report.

During combat:

> "Beoulve?"

If Ramza answers to Zero:

> "Call yourself whatever you like. The report says you died."

That is all.

It is a future thread, not a reveal about this plot.

### Beat 6 — Establish the destination

By the end of the bridge, the player should know enough to choose the annex deliberately.

Tavin can guide them.

Nemi can trace the component style.

Buyer movement can reveal the route.

The plot no longer needs a chase marker.

# SCENE 6 — ORIENTATION VAULT

## Scene at a Glance

The finale is not "fight the evil scientist."

It is a confrontation with a working idea that has become dangerous enough to force a decision.

Ilyra is attempting to prove that controlled living planar transfer can be engineered.

The player must decide whether the experiment should be stopped, saved, stolen, exposed, redirected, or negotiated over.

The annex itself is the second antagonist.

## Setting and First Impression

Outside, the annex looks like a narrow Izzet industrial block that should have been abandoned.

Inside, scale becomes unreliable.

A staircase rises into a room that should be outside the building.

A service bay periodically overlaps with a rooftop garden.

One corridor looks too short until somebody walks it.

The Central Ring dominates the main chamber: nested metal rings, suspended glass, plates adjusted by hand, grounding cables, overwritten markings, and the Orientation Core mounted into machinery that was never designed to receive it.

This is not a gleaming superweapon.

It is research equipment being forced to answer a question.

## Pre-Session Prep

Know the relationship state of:

- Nemi;
- Tavin;
- Rava;
- Kell/Boros;
- Olya;
- Ilyra.

Choose which situational guest is actually present in Solo L6.

Use Solo Ilyra for the solo mode.

Decide how far the experiment has progressed according to elapsed time.

Have the six Orientation Vault mana/map Regions ready. They should matter visibly when the chamber phases.

## During-Session Flow

### Beat 1 — Arrive while people are working

Do not put Ilyra on a throne.

Assistants are moving equipment.

Someone is tightening a mount.

A receiver is charging.

Records are being packed.

If the player walks in talking, Ilyra talks while continuing to work.

If they attack immediately, roll initiative.

Her first line on recognizing the unexpected planeswalker should be:

> "You survived."

If the player answers angrily:

> "That was not an assumption."

### Beat 2 — Let the player understand the room through choices

The room contains several things worth doing:

- stop the active transfer;
- take or destroy the core;
- protect a living subject;
- shut down relays;
- catch Ilyra;
- protect Tavin;
- preserve evidence;
- prevent the annex from damaging the surrounding district.

Do not label these as MMO objectives.

Describe them physically.

Nemi can identify which controls are getting worse.

Tavin can identify which route Ilyra will use if she tries to leave.

Ilyra can keep working while arguing.

### Beat 3 — Ilyra states her case without pretending the harm did not happen

If accused of risking lives:

> "Yes."

Do not soften it.

If the player calls the tiger and bear failures:

> "They survived."

If they say Sparks are different:

> "Different is not sacred."

If they say she had no right:

> "That is the first useful objection you've made."

She is willing to argue about authority, access, and acceptable risk.

She is not willing to pretend the prototypes were harmless.

### Beat 4 — The environment enters the conversation

At the end of each round, or whenever the battle needs to change, activate one spatial event.

**North/East exchange:** two sections of the room trade spatial relationship.

**Gravity turn:** one elevation becomes wall-like or a new walking plane.

**Garden overlap:** living rooftop terrain appears in the west side, creating cover and an exit.

**Mana surge:** one Region changes availability as the relay overfeeds.

**Receiver opens early:** a living creature begins to emerge and becomes an immediate moral/tactical problem.

**Central pull:** loose objects and unstable people slide toward the ring.

These should change decisions, not simply deal automatic damage.

### Beat 5 — Guest units make the battlefield feel like a small command

In Solo L6, this is the payoff for the field-team structure.

The player can send Nemi to a relay while personally engaging Ilyra.

They can order Kell to hold the entry.

They can send Tavin after the core.

They can ask Rava to break the power feed.

Those orders work because the relationships were built in earlier scenes.

Do not make every guest equally willing to do every job.

Rava is excellent at destroying a relay and poor at preserving delicate data.

Nemi is the opposite.

### Beat 6 — Ilyra reacts to consequences

If one of Ilyra's assistants is badly hurt, she tells them to leave.

If the district becomes genuinely threatened, Olya can contact the site:

> "Ilyra. Shut it down."

Ilyra:

> "You would preserve the data."

Olya:

> "I would preserve the building."

That exchange keeps both Izzet researchers from becoming simple moral opposites.

If Tavin confronts Ilyra about the bear:

> "You told me the live test came after delivery."

Ilyra:

> "It did."

Tavin:

> "I was still carrying the delivery."

Ilyra:

> "And it worked."

That can be the exact moment Tavin turns against her.

### Beat 7 — Let the ending emerge from the final decision

Ilyra can lose the fight without the player knowing what to do with the research.

The core can survive while the project fails.

The annex can be destroyed while Ilyra escapes.

The player can make a bargain that horrifies Olya but saves the district.

Do not force the ending into "boss defeated."

# AFTERMATH — RAVNICA KEEPS MOVING

## Immediate city response

Ravnica does not end when the boss fight does.

Boros establishes a perimeter if they have not already become hostile.

Izzet personnel arrive and immediately begin arguing about what counts as recoverable research.

Guildless workers and nearby residents want to know whether the building is safe.

Rakdos performers may already be telling exaggerated versions of the chase.

Gruul witnesses will tell their own version.

If the tiger escaped, that story is spreading too.

## Leyline aftereffects

Stopping the annex does not need to erase every anomaly instantly.

For the next hours or days:

- a fountain runs uphill for several seconds;
- a doorway opens onto the wrong alley;
- unfamiliar birds appear above a precinct;
- an Izzet conduit hums with the wrong resonance;
- one of the authored mana Regions remains unusually strong;
- someone reports the tiger;
- someone reports a bear and gets laughed at;
- a third tiny living thing appears somewhere with no active receiver nearby.

Use one or two. Do not immediately escalate to the Multiverse collapsing.

# DURING-SESSION QUICK FLOW

## Opening

Start in the planeswalk.

Do not brief Ravnica first.

Let the city introduce itself by collision.

## After every battle

Ask yourself:

- who is still pursuing their goal?
- who learned something?
- who changed sides or priorities?
- what did civilians see?
- what physical damage remains?
- what rumor leaves this location before the player does?

## If the player gets ahead of the plot

Reward it.

If they capture Tavin early, move the threat toward them.

If they identify Ilyra early, let them go to the annex early.

If they destroy the core, let Ilyra care about the surviving data.

If they convince Rava immediately, let that matter.

## If the player gets stuck

Use a living NPC, not an invisible quest arrow.

Nemi can identify hardware.

Edda can report a sighting.

Rava can know a rubblebelt route.

Tavin can leave a deliberate sign when he wants the player to reach him before the buyer does.

Vexa can know where the buyer operatives fled because she watches her stage.

# CANON ANCHORS AND ORIGINAL ADVENTURE MATERIAL

## Setting facts used from Guildmasters' Guide to Ravnica

This adventure relies on these Ravnica foundations:

- Ravnica is a city-world built in layers.
- The Tenth District is a central hotbed of guild activity.
- The Izzet League handles public works as well as volatile magical research.
- The Laboratory of Orientation studies teleportation and spatial recombination.
- The Laboratory of Gravitational Inversion has a workshop near Augustin Station.
- The Gruul inhabit ruins, rubblebelts, abandoned districts, and other broken parts of the city and oppose civilization's machinery.
- The Boros Legion is Ravnica's standing army and pursues justice/protection through disciplined military action.
- Rakdos stages can appear in streets and plazas and be moved by giants; the Smelting Quarter is a fitting Rakdos-heavy location.

## Adventure-book source discipline

The Ravnica setting descriptions in this guide use *Guildmasters' Guide to Ravnica* as the setting foundation, especially its material on life in the city-world, the Tenth District, the Boros Legion, Gruul Clans, Izzet League, Cult of Rakdos, and the Laboratory of Orientation/Gravitational Inversion.

The prepared read-aloud prose itself is original adventure text written for this one-shot. It is not quoted sourcebook text.

If a later campaign source contradicts an original detail such as the exact layout of Olya's workshop or Ilyra's annex, the published setting fact takes priority and the adventure-specific description can be adjusted.

# FINAL GM REMINDER

The adventure should feel as though the story keeps happening after initiative is rolled.

When someone changes sides, let it happen on the battlefield.

When an NPC learns they were used, let them react before combat ends.

When the environment changes, let that reveal something about the experiment.

When the player wins early, let the story change rather than quietly restoring the planned path.

The title is playful.

The living world underneath it should be real enough that the player eventually understands why the tiger was funny, why the bear was frightening, and why the leylines matter more than either of them.
