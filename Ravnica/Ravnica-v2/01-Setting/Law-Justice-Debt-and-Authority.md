# Law, Justice, Debt and Authority

Ravnica has several overlapping systems that outsiders often mistake for one "law guild."

## Azorius: legitimacy through procedure

The Azorius Senate creates, interprets, records, and enforces formal law through a vast bureaucratic apparatus. Their power is strongest when everyone agrees that a process must be followed.

At street level, Azorius pressure looks like notices, injunctions, permits, inspections, hearings, sealed doors, controlled queues, arresters, records, witness statements, and an official who refuses to skip step seven because step seven exists for a reason.

Their failure mode is not merely cruelty. It is **procedure becoming more important than the human problem the procedure was invented to solve**.

## Boros: legitimacy through protection and justice

The Boros Legion is a standing army and a force of public protection. A Boros officer is more likely to ask who is in danger right now, who started the violence, and what action ends it.

That makes Boros faster and emotionally legible, but also more willing to use force and to decide that moral urgency overrides technical process.

A classic Ravnican dispute is not "law versus crime." It is an Azorius official saying, "You cannot enter until this order is reviewed," while a Boros officer says, "People are dying inside."

## Orzhov: legitimacy through contract, property, and obligation

The Orzhov create a parallel architecture of power through debt, loans, leases, patronage, religious authority, ownership, and enforceable promises. A person can be legally free and still economically trapped.

The frightening part is not that every Orzhov agreement is fraudulent. Many are valid, useful, and voluntarily entered. The horror comes when dependency makes refusal theoretical.

## The Living Guildpact

Jace's authority sits above normal interguild conflict, but his absence makes ordinary officials reluctant to rely on intervention. Everyone knows a supreme arbiter exists; everyone also knows the dispute in front of them may be settled, exploited, or forgotten long before he returns.

## Jurisdiction scene template

When the PCs cause a public incident, decide:

1. **Who arrives first?** Boros patrol, Azorius arrester, private Orzhov guard, local guild security, neighborhood watch.
2. **Who claims authority?**
3. **What evidence matters to them?**
4. **What outcome do they need before shift change?**
5. **Who benefits if the case is delayed?**

## Documents as drama

Use documents as objects with social weight:

- transit permit;
- guild work order;
- search writ;
- lease;
- debt instrument;
- lab safety variance;
- military requisition;
- medical waiver;
- witness affidavit;
- sealed Guildpact petition.

A document can open a door, but it can also create a paper trail. Players should learn that showing credentials solves the immediate problem while telling institutions exactly where they are.

## Street-level consequence examples

- Bribing a clerk succeeds, but the forged timestamp is later noticed.
- Helping Boros stop a riot earns gratitude and an Azorius inquiry into unauthorized force.
- Paying an Orzhov debt saves a family and transfers the obligation to whoever paid.
- Destroying an illegal lab prevents harm but leaves the surrounding block without a service the lab was secretly providing.
- Refusing to testify protects an ally and makes the PCs unreliable in formal proceedings.

Ravnican law is most interesting when **every solution changes who has leverage next**.
