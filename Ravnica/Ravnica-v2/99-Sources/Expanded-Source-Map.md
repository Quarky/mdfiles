# Expanded Source Map — Ravnica V2

V2 follows a **source-first rule**:

> Use published setting material and official fiction wherever they can answer the question. Use synthesis only to connect published facts into playable daily life or to fill genuine gaps.

The goal is not to replace Ravnica with generated lore. It is to make the published city **usable at the table**.

# Provenance labels

## CANON

Explicitly established by an official published source.

Examples:
- Zameck is in Zonot Seven.
- the Izzet maintain a Gravitational Inversion workshop near Augustin Station.
- the Azorius Senate has three Columns.
- the Golgari provide basic food and manage waste.
- Rix Maadi is the Rakdos guildhall.

## CANON-SUPPORTED INFERENCE

Not explicitly narrated in detail, but required or strongly implied by a canonical institution.

Examples:
- a permanent Boros garrison needs quartermasters and cooks;
- New Prahv generates demand for copyists, advocates, messengers, and food vendors;
- a Simic zonot needs maintenance workers and ordinary residents in addition to researchers.

These are not claimed as named canonical facts.

## TABLE SYNTHESIS

Created for play where sources are silent.

Examples:
- the exact name of an invented café;
- the current tenant dispute on a particular block;
- the smell in a made-up stairwell;
- the personalities of new local NPCs.

Table synthesis should fit established Ravnican systems and remain easy to replace if later canon supplies an answer.

## CAMPAIGN CANON

True in MTGFoundry campaign material, but not presented as Wizards canon.

Examples from *The Glass Run*:
- Chemister Olya Renn;
- Tavin Rell;
- the orientation-prism theft;
- the specific sabotage;
- the campaign's exact chase sequence.

## ORIGINAL FICTION

New vignettes, scene prose, dialogue examples, and stories created to demonstrate setting behavior.

Original fiction should never be silently presented as a published event.

# What to extract from fiction

Do not mine official stories only for plot.

For each scene, record:

## Physical city detail

- architecture;
- level changes;
- rain;
- light;
- crowd density;
- transit;
- what ordinary people are carrying;
- how long movement takes.

## Speech

- how a guild member talks when not explaining their guild;
- how professionals address superiors;
- how insults reference guild stereotypes;
- when someone uses a title versus a first name.

## Ordinary labor around the plot

- guards;
- clerks;
- couriers;
- servers;
- laborers;
- attendants;
- healers;
- witnesses;
- drivers;
- maintenance workers.

## Institutional pressure

- who has authority;
- who thinks they have authority;
- what document matters;
- who has to wait;
- which guild gets blamed;
- what changes if the famous protagonist leaves.

That four-part extraction is more valuable for a living-world campaign than copying plot summaries.

---

# Conflict-resolution hierarchy for V2

When two details conflict:

1. explicit current campaign instruction;
2. selected campaign era;
3. official source specific to that era;
4. official source describing the same guild/location from nearby eras;
5. *Guildmasters' Guide to Ravnica* baseline;
6. V2 canon-supported inference;
7. V2 table synthesis.

A later source does not automatically overwrite an earlier-era campaign.

A War of the Spark leader state must not silently appear in a Living Guildpact game.

A Return-era guildhall should not be projected backward into a pre-construction scene without checking chronology.

---

# Writing rule

Every new V2 file should be able to answer:

- What is explicitly sourced?
- What is inferred?
- What is invented?
- Why does the invented material follow from the established city?

If the answer is unclear, the file is not finished.
