# GM Immersion Standard

A scene is not finished when it has a location name and an encounter. A Ravnica scene should feel as though it existed before the PCs arrived and will keep moving after they leave.

## The five-layer description

When the party enters somewhere new, describe at least three of these layers immediately and let the remaining layers emerge through play.

### 1. Skyline and enclosure

Ravnica is vertically crowded. A street may have five visible eras of construction: an old arch swallowed by an apartment block, a bridge carrying another road above, cables and pipes hung underneath that bridge, roof gardens above that, and a tower vanishing into haze.

Ask: **what blocks the horizon? what hangs overhead? what older thing is the current city built on?**

### 2. Movement

Crowds should move with purpose. Couriers squeeze through. Pack beasts pull carts. Lift cages rattle up facades. Centaurs take wider lanes. Goblins use maintenance shortcuts. Boros patrols create temporary currents in foot traffic. An Azorius inspection can dam an entire avenue into angry queues.

### 3. Sound

Use overlapping sound rather than one signature noise: iron-rimmed wheels, shouted prices, distant bells, steam hiss, roc cries, argument, choral Selesnyan song from a courtyard, Izzet crackle from a conduit, a Rakdos drumline three blocks away, a judge's amplified proclamation, rain in gutters, a troll bellowing from a freight lane.

### 4. Smell and physical sensation

Stone after rain. Coal smoke. hot metal. yeast and coffee. fungal broth. wet greenery. lamp oil. incense. ozone. animal musk. perfume over funeral wax. sewer damp rising from a grate. The city is tactile: vibration from machinery, pressure waves from magic, warm pavement over heating lines, cold drafts from undercity shafts.

### 5. Social pressure

Every public space has rules, even when the rule is that nobody can enforce rules there. Who is watching? Who is entitled to stand where? Who can make a queue move? Who has a badge, tabard, writ, guild insignia, or reputation?

## The three-citizen rule

For any street scene, know three ordinary people:

- someone trying to finish work;
- someone trying to avoid trouble;
- someone trying to turn the current situation into opportunity.

They should not all belong to the guild that "owns" the scene. A Simic clinic might employ guildless cleaners, buy Izzet refrigeration service, owe Orzhov financing, and be under Azorius inspection.

## The consequence ladder

PC action should alter more than hit points.

1. **Immediate:** a door closes, a crowd scatters, a worker helps, a patrol arrives.
2. **Institutional:** a guild marks the PCs as useful, reckless, indebted, dangerous, or deniable.
3. **Neighborhood:** prices, rumors, access, and local trust shift.
4. **Political:** another guild exploits the outcome.
5. **Memory:** Ravnica remembers through records, gossip, debts, favors, scars, and public spectacle.

## Do not flatten the guilds

A Boros soldier can be tired, funny, frightened, kind, authoritarian, or corrupt without ceasing to be Boros. An Orzhov priest can sincerely believe debt creates social order. A Golgari rot farmer can be proud of feeding people. A Rakdos stagehand may care more about safe rigging than murder. A Simic technician may hate uncontrolled mutation.

The guild identity is a **pressure and vocabulary**, not a personality replacement.

## Read-aloud test

Before using descriptive prose, ask: could this paragraph describe Waterdeep, Sharn, or any generic fantasy metropolis if the guild names were removed? If yes, add Ravnican structure: vertical city layers, guild jurisdiction, civic magic, mixed species, overlapping infrastructure, visible old ruins, and social friction.
