# Conflict, Reaction and Escalation Engine

Use this when the PCs create a public event and you need to know how the living city reacts.

## Step 1 — Name the civic problem

Do not start with "which guild is involved?" Start with the problem:

- fire;
- missing person;
- unpaid debt;
- broken lift;
- illegal performance;
- contaminated water;
- disputed ruin;
- escaped creature;
- political demonstration;
- murder;
- closed market;
- experimental accident;
- housing eviction;
- corpse with unclear ownership;
- blocked trade route.

## Step 2 — Identify the first responder

Who arrives first depends on local presence, not abstract jurisdiction.

A Boros patrol may reach a lab explosion before Izzet specialists. A Selesnyan healer may reach a stabbing before Boros. A Golgari crew may notice a sewer collapse before the surface knows anything happened.

The first responder shapes the initial narrative.

## Step 3 — Add the authority dispute

Bring in one organization that says, "this is our matter."

Now the scene has pressure even without combat.

Examples:

- Azorius seals a site Boros wants entered.
- Simic claims a creature as research property.
- Orzhov produces a deed to a building residents occupy.
- Golgari says a tunnel is inhabited despite surface maps calling it abandoned.

## Step 4 — Add the material dependency

What breaks if the scene remains unresolved?

- heat;
- food delivery;
- rent;
- medical treatment;
- freight;
- legal deadline;
- public confidence;
- street access;
- water;
- evidence integrity.

This prevents politics from becoming abstract.

## Step 5 — Add the opportunist

A third actor does not need to cause the crisis. They only need to see a use for it.

Dimir collects information. Orzhov buys distressed property. Rakdos turns scandal into satire. Gruul encourages residents to tear down a barricade. Izzet asks to test equipment under emergency conditions.

## Escalation clocks

### Public attention
1. witnesses stop  
2. crowd forms  
3. rumors spread  
4. guild representatives arrive  
5. story reaches wider district  
6. incident becomes symbolic

### Institutional pressure
1. verbal warning  
2. written notice/order  
3. supervisor called  
4. specialist team deployed  
5. senior official takes control  
6. guild leadership uses outcome politically

### Neighborhood memory
1. curiosity  
2. gratitude or annoyance  
3. names remembered  
4. favors or grudges form  
5. local access changes  
6. PCs become part of neighborhood story

## De-escalation should cost something

Good resolutions involve tradeoffs.

- Open the street, but contaminate evidence.
- Save the clinic, but accept Orzhov financing.
- Preserve the grove, but delay transit.
- Arrest the rioter, but radicalize the neighborhood.
- Release the creature, but embarrass the Simic project.
- Pay the debt, but create a new obligation.

If every problem has a clean answer, the city stops feeling politically alive.
