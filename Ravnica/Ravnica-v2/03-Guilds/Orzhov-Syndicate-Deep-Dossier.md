# Orzhov Syndicate — Deep Dossier

## The Orzhov self-image

The Orzhov believe society runs on obligation whether people admit it or not.

Money is only the most visible form. Family duty, reputation, contract, confession, patronage, inheritance, employment, lease, debt, and promise all bind people.

The Syndicate's genius is turning those invisible relationships into **enforceable hierarchy**.

## Church, bank, law office, crime family

The Orzhov are simultaneously religious institution, financial network, legal machine, patronage system, and organized crime structure.

Their churches and basilicas can provide real services: loans, safe storage, legal representation, funeral arrangements, mediation, protection, housing connections, employment, and ritual.

The danger is that assistance is rarely allowed to become a gift when it can become leverage.

## Hierarchy

The baseline era is ruled by the **Obzedat**, undead oligarchs whose continued existence embodies the guild's refusal to let wealth and authority end at death.

Below them, rank matters intensely. Syndics perform low-level functions. Knights, advokists, priests, enforcers, ministrants, and wealthy oligarchs operate within networks where almost everyone owes someone above.

Power is relational: the important question is not merely "how rich are you?" but **whose debt can you call in?**

## Sensory palette

Black and white stone. gold trim. polished wood. heavy doors. incense. wax. perfume. paper. cold metal coins. quiet rooms designed to make visitors conscious of their own breathing.

Servants move discreetly. Guards do not fidget. A clerk may speak softly because the architecture makes shouting unnecessary.

## Religion

Orzhov ritual should not be treated as fake merely because the institution is exploitative. People can sincerely seek absolution, continuity, family blessing, or meaning in a system that monetizes those needs.

A priest may genuinely comfort a mourner and still ensure the funeral debt is properly recorded.

## Debt as social control

Debt becomes frightening when it reshapes choices before anyone issues a threat.

A borrower works a dangerous shift because missing it triggers penalties. A witness stays silent because the landlord is Orzhov-financed. A merchant rejects the PCs because their protection contract discourages "high-risk clientele."

## Interaction habits

**Azorius:** sophisticated legal conflict; both respect documents but disagree about purpose.  
**Boros:** resentment of predatory practice, but Boros cannot simply criminalize every harsh contract.  
**Dimir:** information and leverage trade hands cautiously.  
**Golgari:** philosophical disgust over wealth persisting beyond death; practical funeral and property dealings continue.  
**Rakdos:** contempt versus fascination—Orzhov sells respectability, Rakdos humiliates it.  
**Selesnya:** competing visions of community obligation: debt to hierarchy versus duty to collective.

## How to play an Orzhov NPC

Never make them greedy in only one dimension. Make them understand networks.

Good dialogue:
- "You are free to refuse. The consequence is also yours to choose."
- "A favor forgotten is charity. A favor recorded is civilization."
- "I am not threatening the family. I am explaining what the contract already permits."

## Scene pressure

A clinic is behind on payments after treating riot victims for free. The Orzhov lender offers to forgive half the debt if the clinic signs an exclusive service agreement.

The clinic survives. Prices rise. Poor patients lose access. The contract is legal.
