# Boros Legion — Deep Dossier

## The Boros self-image

The Boros believe justice must be **felt in the world**, not merely written down. If someone is being beaten in an alley, the morally correct response is not to convene a committee.

Their strength is willingness to stand between danger and ordinary people.

Their danger is that moral certainty plus military capability can become permission to act first and justify later.

## Structure and military culture

The Legion is a standing army with garrisons throughout the city. Squads, sergeants, brigadiers, captains, commanders, warleader angels, and specialized orders create clear chains of responsibility.

The **Wojek League** combines military policing and intelligence. Wojeks investigate threats from other guilds and also investigate misconduct inside the Legion.

**Skyknights** mounted on rocs make Boros presence visible above the city. Aerial patrols are a constant reminder that the Legion thinks in terms of rapid response and territorial awareness.

**Sunhome** is not merely a fortress; it is a symbolic center of the Legion's identity and a place soldiers imagine defending even before they ever see it.

## Ordinary life in a garrison

A garrison smells like oil, leather, soup, wet wool, training yards, animal care, and too many people living on schedules.

Soldiers complain about boots, food, leave, impossible orders, and officers. Armorers know which squads abuse equipment. Healers know who is pretending not to be injured. Messengers know which commander reads reports and which skims them.

The Legion should feel like an institution with friendships and bureaucracy, not an endless parade ground.

## Justice versus law

Boros and Azorius overlap because both care about order, but their instincts differ.

Azorius asks: **what process gives legitimate authority?**  
Boros asks: **who is being harmed and what stops it?**

A Boros officer can break a regulation to save lives and then accept punishment afterward because the choice still felt morally correct.

## Angelic leadership

Angels embody the Legion's ideals and can inspire genuine devotion. Their presence should change a room. Mortals straighten posture, arguments stop, and even experienced soldiers reassess what they were about to say.

But angelic perspective can also be strategically distant. A mortal sergeant understands what it means to ask exhausted troops to hold a block for twelve more hours.

## Why citizens love them

When something explodes, when a monster escapes, when a riot becomes murderous, or when somebody powerful is hurting someone visibly weaker, Boros is the guild people expect to arrive.

## Why citizens fear them

Military force has a way of defining problems as threats. Barricades become normal. Search powers expand during emergencies. A neighborhood known for unrest can feel occupied.

## Interaction habits

**Azorius:** necessary sibling rivalry; law versus justice.  
**Izzet:** respect for useful engineering mixed with dread of prototypes.  
**Gruul:** frequent violent contact, but warriors can understand each other's courage more easily than diplomats do.  
**Rakdos:** crowd control, raids, contempt mixed with grudging recognition of bravery.  
**Dimir:** constant counterintelligence anxiety.  
**Selesnya:** cooperation in defense, disagreement over hierarchy and coercion.  
**Orzhov:** Boros hates predatory exploitation but must distinguish crime from ugly legality.

## How to play a Boros NPC

Give them a person or place they are responsible for.

Good dialogue:
- "You can argue jurisdiction after I get them out."
- "I have six people for twelve streets. Tell me what you know that changes where I put them."
- "If you want my soldiers to trust you, stop treating them like pieces."

## Scene pressure

A Boros patrol arrests a known extortionist after a beating. The victim refuses to testify because the extortionist works for an Orzhov creditor who holds the family's lease. The Azorius says the case will collapse. The sergeant knows releasing the suspect puts the family in danger tonight.

Justice is obvious. A lawful path is not.
