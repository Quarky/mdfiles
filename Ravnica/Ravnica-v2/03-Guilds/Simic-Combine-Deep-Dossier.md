# Simic Combine — Deep Dossier

## The Simic self-image

The Simic believe life survives by adapting.

Ravnica changes faster than natural systems can respond: cities spread, pollution changes habitats, magical accidents create new pressures, disease moves through dense populations, and artificial environments demand new bodies.

The Combine asks whether biology can be helped to keep pace.

Their virtue is **adaptive imagination**.

Their danger is turning a living subject into a design problem.

## Zonots

The Simic organize around **zonots**, vast sinkholes descending through layers of city toward ancient waters. Each zonot is habitat, community, laboratory environment, and political unit led by a **Speaker**.

The Speakers form a chamber and select the Prime Speaker. In the baseline era, Zegana is Prime Speaker.

**Zameck**, in Zonot Seven within Precinct Five, serves as the guildhall and meeting place.

A zonot should feel like another mode of Ravnica rather than an escape from the city: bridges, labs, dwellings, tanks, pools, offices, lifts, and living architecture arranged around depth and water.

## Holdfast and Upwelling

Two philosophical ideas shape Simic thought.

**Holdfast** warns against becoming detached from nature. It values ecological fit, continuity, and natural systems.

**Upwelling** embraces renewal, transformation, and replacing depleted forms with newly invigorated ones.

Both can support adaptation. They disagree about how aggressively change should be directed.

## Clades and projects

Researchers work in clades spanning specialties and zonots, or in shorter projects built around immediate goals. Published examples include Hull, Fin, and Gyre clades, alongside programs such as Guardian and Crypsis.

This creates scientific politics: long-term theory versus urgent application, habitat needs versus military pressure, publication versus secrecy.

## Public health roots

The Simic's original public-health role matters. A Combine clinic can be one of the safest places in a neighborhood. Biomancers can heal injuries, treat disease, and solve environmental problems other guilds cannot.

That makes ethical conflict stronger. The same researcher who saves lives may sincerely propose a modification the patient finds horrifying.

## Sensory palette

Brine. disinfectant. wet stone. living tissue. herbs. shell. glass. cool air near water and warm humid air inside growth chambers.

Bubbling tanks. pumps. quiet technical discussion. splashing below. clicking claws from something not visible yet.

## Interaction habits

**Izzet:** mutual technical respect; life versus mechanism as first instinct.  
**Selesnya:** ecological common ground, conflict over deliberate redesign.  
**Golgari:** shared systems thinking, different attitude toward decay and perfection.  
**Azorius:** inspections, medical regulation, research legality.  
**Dimir:** Crypsis and intelligence work create uncomfortable overlap.  
**Orzhov:** research funding can become dependency.

## How to play a Simic NPC

Give them a variable they are watching.

Good dialogue:
- "The treatment worked. The question is what else it changed."
- "Natural does not mean harmless."
- "You are asking whether it is still you. I am asking whether you are still alive."

## Scene pressure

A worker exposed to magical contamination will die unless a Simic team performs an emergency adaptation. The procedure is likely permanent and visually obvious.

The patient is conscious. Their employer wants them returned to work. Their family is afraid. The biomancer is already preparing the instruments.
