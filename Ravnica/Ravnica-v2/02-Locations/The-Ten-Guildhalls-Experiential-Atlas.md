# The Ten Guildhalls — Experiential Atlas

Guildhalls are not interchangeable headquarters. Each expresses what its guild believes authority should **feel like**.

## New Prahv — Azorius Senate

New Prahv's three enormous towers make government visible from far beyond the doors. The building teaches visitors that law is larger than any one official.

**Approach:** orderly traffic, advocates and petitioners, arresters, messenger lanes, food stalls serving clerks, increasing density of formal notices.

**Inside:** broad public chambers feed into smaller offices and controlled corridors. People are routed by purpose. Waiting is institutionalized.

**Hear:** legal argument, names called, bells, papers, footsteps in echoing halls.

**Smell:** ink, stone, wax, leather files, coffee.

**Who belongs:** judges, scribes, arresters, lawmages, advocates, witnesses, cleaners, pages, lobbyists, petitioners.

**What an outsider gets wrong:** the Senate is not one mind. Sova, Jelenn, and Lyev personnel can disagree bitterly while defending the legitimacy of the same institution.

**Scene:** a field officer has evidence that would justify immediate action but cannot make it admissible without revealing a protected source.

## Sunhome — Boros Legion

Sunhome is fortress, barracks, command center, memorial, and spiritual symbol. It is designed around readiness rather than elegance.

**Approach:** patrols, supply wagons, rocs overhead, soldiers on errands, trainees, armorers, messengers, controlled gates.

**Inside:** training yards, barracks, armories, command rooms, kitchens, memorial spaces, medical facilities, equipment stores.

**Hear:** drills, armor repair, horns, mess-hall conversation, wingbeats, commands.

**Smell:** leather, oil, steel, sweat, cooking, animal care.

**Who belongs:** recruits, veterans, angels, quartermasters, healers, skyknights, Wojeks, cooks, armorers, clerks.

**What an outsider gets wrong:** Boros culture is not uninterrupted zeal. A standing army runs on logistics, trust, routine, fatigue, and people arguing about orders.

**Scene:** an exhausted unit is ordered back into the field because no replacement is available. The officer agrees the order is unfair and still believes it is necessary.

## Duskmantle — House Dimir

Duskmantle is defined by uncertainty of access. Under Lazav, its hidden undercity presence is protected by memory wards, and most Dimir members have no reason to visit it.

**Approach:** there should not be one reliable approach. A route can begin through an archive basement, an abandoned cistern, an old service tunnel, or a door the traveler later cannot place on a map.

**Inside:** avoid fixed cartography. Use chambers that feel too isolated from surrounding city, quiet workrooms, secure message facilities, memory wards, spaces designed for people who do not need to know who else uses them.

**Hear:** little. Water behind walls, distant footsteps, a door closing somewhere impossible to locate.

**Smell:** damp stone, dust, lamp smoke, clean paper.

**Who belongs:** high-level contacts, mind mages, couriers carrying information rather than parcels, agents who may not know one another.

**What an outsider gets wrong:** secrecy does not mean everyone is theatrical. The most dangerous person can look like the most ordinary clerk in the city.

**Scene:** after leaving, each PC remembers the meeting differently. None of the discrepancies is obviously magical until they compare notes.

## Korozda — Golgari Swarm

Korozda lies within the Golgari undercity network, reached through a descent where surface assumptions about light, waste, ownership, and death cease to apply.

**Approach:** fungal growth thickens, surface stone gives way to older masonry, insect traffic increases, and pathways become recognizable only to people who understand local markings.

**Inside:** the guild's politics should be visible through who controls space: Devkarin, kraul, gorgons, shamans, assassins, workers, undead, and ordinary residents all overlap.

**Hear:** insects, water, low conversation, tools, ritual, distant movement through large underground chambers.

**Smell:** earth, fungus, decomposition, smoke, herbs, wet stone.

**Who belongs:** food growers, reclaimers, shamans, undead laborers, faction representatives, Ochran agents, families who may have lived below for generations.

**What an outsider gets wrong:** rot is not disorder. The Swarm can manage biological systems with extreme precision.

**Scene:** a surface delegation calls a chamber unsanitary. The local grower responds by listing three upper-city neighborhoods currently eating food produced there.

## Skarrg — Gruul Clans

Skarrg is a ruined palace in the rubblebelt and the nearest thing the disparate Gruul clans have to shared guild space.

**Approach:** paved streets become broken, patrol presence drops, sightlines open, scavenged clan marks replace official signage.

**Inside:** there is no reception desk. Bonfires, feasts, boasts, challenges, animals, weapons, and clan territory create temporary political order.

**Hear:** fire, drums, laughter, argument, challenges, meat roasting, stone breaking.

**Smell:** smoke, hide, blood, herbs, sweat, rain on rubble.

**Who belongs:** whoever can survive the gathering and has a clan reason to be there.

**What an outsider gets wrong:** lack of bureaucracy does not mean lack of politics. Reputation, kinship, injury, old insult, strength, and hospitality all matter.

**Scene:** two clan chiefs both promise the PCs safe passage, but accepting one chief's protection publicly insults the other.

## Nivix — Izzet League

Nivix is an enormous vertical engine of research. It crackles with power because the guild does not separate its identity from experimentation.

**Approach:** specialist suppliers, technicians, repair carts, unusual deliveries, conduits, vents, warning signs.

**Inside:** laboratories, housing, workshops, testing spaces, energy infrastructure, lifts, observation galleries, and sealed areas arranged by hazards outsiders barely understand.

**Hear:** hum, steam, countdowns, glass, sudden alarms, arguments about measurements.

**Smell:** ozone, metal, solvent, smoke, coffee.

**Who belongs:** researchers, attendants, directors, fabricators, safety technicians, maintenance crews, runners.

**What an outsider gets wrong:** the Izzet are not incompetent mad scientists. Their dangerous work often requires extraordinary competence; the danger comes from what they are willing to attempt.

**Scene:** an alarm sounds. Half the room evacuates instantly. The other half rushes toward the source because that specific alarm means the experiment produced something unprecedented.

## Orzhova — Orzhov Syndicate

Orzhova combines cathedral, political court, elite residence, mausoleum, financial institution, and social theater.

**Approach:** wealth becomes architectural. Security grows quieter rather than louder. Servants, petitioners, debtors, advocates, and elite visitors move through different entrances.

**Inside:** worship, accounts, legal conferences, elite gatherings, funerary spaces, and ghostly oligarchs all occupy the same institutional world.

**Hear:** quiet prayer, soft negotiations, pen on paper, doors closing, footsteps muffled by wealth.

**Smell:** incense, wax, perfume, preserved flowers, old stone, paper.

**Who belongs:** syndics, priests, advokists, knights, servants, debtors, oligarchs, spirits.

**What an outsider gets wrong:** politeness is not kindness, and exploitation need not look violent to be coercive.

**Scene:** a debtor is offered terms that genuinely solve the current crisis and make escape from the Syndicate less likely for the next twenty years.

## Rix Maadi — Cult of Rakdos

Rix Maadi lies deep in the undercity and should feel like the city's suppressed appetite given physical form. The route through the Demon's Vestibule descends away from ordinary streets toward heat, performance, danger, and Rakdos himself.

**Approach:** the deeper route grows warmer. Torn performance banners and old marks turn passage into anticipation. Ordinary city noise disappears behind stone.

**Inside:** vast cavernous scale, natural heat, performance infrastructure, spaces for troupes and blood witches, and the overwhelming knowledge that Rakdos's attention can become the most important fact in the room.

**Hear:** chains, distant music, laughter, screams that may be performance, cavern echo, lava or deep heat.

**Smell:** smoke, sweat, metal, alcohol, burned fabric, sulfur.

**Who belongs:** performers, stage crews, demons, blood witches, attendants, thrill seekers with enough status or foolishness to descend.

**What an outsider gets wrong:** performance does not end backstage. In Rix Maadi, status itself is theatrical.

**Scene:** the PCs are granted an audience, but another troupe turns their arrival into an unscheduled act. Refusing to participate can be more insulting than losing.

## Vitu-Ghazi — Selesnya Conclave

Vitu-Ghazi is city-tree, cathedral, legal-spiritual center, pilgrimage destination, and living proof of Selesnya's argument that civilization and nature can be one system.

**Approach:** vegetation becomes architecture. Routes bend around roots rather than cutting through them. Pilgrims, animals, residents, and merchants share space.

**Inside/public areas:** communal gathering, worship, teaching, mediation, food, visitors, and ordinary vernadi life flow together.

**Hear:** leaves, song, conversation, children, animal movement, market activity.

**Smell:** soil, bark, flowers, food, wool, rain.

**Who belongs:** voda, initiates, healers, families, Ledev, hierarchs, pilgrims, visitors without hostile intent.

**What an outsider gets wrong:** hospitality is not political weakness. A community that feeds strangers can also mobilize thousands of defenders.

**Scene:** a public meal continues while local leaders quietly decide whether to shelter someone wanted by another guild.

## Zameck — Simic Combine

Zameck occupies Zonot Seven in Precinct Five. It is a political meeting place and public-facing threshold to a much larger vertical aquatic habitat.

**Approach:** drainage, humidity, sealed specimen transport, unusual bodies, canals, biological materials.

**Inside:** public meeting areas remain accessible to land-dwellers; deeper facilities increasingly assume aquatic or modified bodies.

**Hear:** water, pumps, soft technical conversation, glass, splashing below.

**Smell:** brine, disinfectant, wet stone, herbs, animal feed.

**Who belongs:** Speakers, researchers, clade members, technicians, hybrids, medical staff, visitors seeking expertise.

**What an outsider gets wrong:** the Combine's strangest work is often grounded in practical health or environmental problems.

**Scene:** the only witness to a crime is undergoing a planned adaptation that will alter memory-related neural structures. Azorius wants testimony now; Simic says delaying the procedure is medically dangerous.

## Guildhall rule

A guildhall scene should reveal **how the guild organizes ordinary labor**, not only how its leaders look. Put cooks, cleaners, technicians, scribes, guards, apprentices, messengers, patients, petitioners, and visitors in the frame. That is what makes the institution feel larger than the adventure.
