# Precinct Four — Named Places, War-Scarred Streets, and Adaptation

Precinct Four is where the Tenth District's tensions become audible. Boros fortifications, Izzet experiments, Gruul incursions, goblin turf, merchants who have learned to defend their own stalls, and constant repair create a culture where adaptability is a civic virtue.

## Sunhome

### Canon anchor
Sunhome is the Boros guildhall: fortress, barracks, command center, and spiritual heart of the Legion.

### Approach
The district begins to organize itself around defensibility before the fortress is reached. Intersections have clear firing lanes. Barricade anchors are built into paving. Supply yards sit close to routes broad enough for troops and freight.

Sunhome should feel less like a palace and more like a place designed by people who expect to be attacked and believe they will hold.

### Sights
Red-white banners, training yards, armored traffic, rocs circling above, weapon racks, memorial plaques, field kitchens, healers moving between wings.

### Sounds
Boot cadence, command calls, armor being repaired, distant horns, wings overhead, mess-hall noise, laughter from off-duty soldiers.

### Smells
Leather, oil, metal, soup, sweat, stone warmed by sun, animal musk.

### Human texture
A recruit is nervous about first patrol. A veteran complains about new doctrine. A quartermaster is more feared than a lieutenant because nobody gets gear without paperwork. A healer knows which officer is sending exhausted soldiers back too quickly.

### Scene pressure
A Boros unit needs an Izzet device for an urgent defense. The device has not passed testing. The commanding officer is willing to accept risk to soldiers; the quartermaster refuses to authorize equipment that might endanger civilians near deployment.

## Nivix

### Canon anchor
Nivix is one of Ravnica's tallest towers and the Izzet guildhall, filled with laboratories, testing facilities, and living quarters.

### The tower as machine
Nivix should never be still.

Light moves in windows. Steam leaves vents at odd heights. Elevators, platforms, and magical transport connect vertical work zones. Laboratories with incompatible hazards are separated by architecture that looks improvised until a researcher explains the rationale.

The building has a pulse: scheduled energy demand, experimental surges, alarms people react to differently depending on tone.

### Sound
Electrical crackle, pumps, bells, shouted measurements, glass breakage, distant thunder from inside, machinery with rhythmic vibration.

### Smell
Ozone, hot copper, solvents, scorched insulation, coffee, smoke that may or may not indicate danger.

### Social structure
A visitor cannot understand rank merely by clothing. A stained coat can belong to a brilliant director. The real signals are access, who interrupts whom, which lab door opens automatically, and whose questions make technicians stop working.

### Scene pressure
An experiment in one laboratory is drawing power needed by a public system outside. The lab insists shutting down will destroy months of work. Residents outside do not care.

## Red Wastes

### Canon anchor
The rubblebelt north of the precinct contains ruined buildings, debris, beasts, elementals, and Gruul camps. Some stubborn non-Gruul residents remain.

### Terrain
This is not empty wilderness. It is **civilization partially withdrawn**.

Roads end abruptly. Foundations remain after upper floors vanished. Trees and brush exploit cracks. Wind crosses spaces that once had roofs. Broken masonry forms ridges and sudden pits.

### Sound
Less constant urban roar, more distant impacts, animals, wind through ruined structures, occasional drums or shouted clan calls.

### Social truth
A surface official may call a structure abandoned while a Gruul family uses it seasonally, scavengers work it weekly, and an old resident still receives mail nearby.

### Scene pressure
Boros wants to demolish a ruined tower used by Gruul scouts. The tower also supports a bridge serving a guildless settlement. Destroying the tactical threat creates a housing crisis.

## The Bulwark

### Canon anchor
A reinforced neighborhood between Sunhome and Nivix, home to relatively inexpensive housing and businesses serving workers and professionals tied to the nearby institutions.

### Daily life
The Bulwark is where the grand guildhalls become rent, groceries, shift schedules, repair shops, taverns, boarding houses, and businesses selling things soldiers and researchers need.

A Boros sergeant and Izzet technician may live in the same building and argue about whose institution causes more night noise.

### Sights
Heavy shutters, repaired walls, basement shelters, equipment shops, inexpensive eateries, scorch marks painted over repeatedly.

### Scene pressure
An insurance company refuses renewal after repeated magical damage. The landlord plans to evict tenants and convert the building to storage because warehouses are easier to insure.

## Skarrg

### Canon anchor
The closest thing the Gruul have to a common guildhall: the cratered remains of an ancient palace in the Red Wastes where clans sometimes gather around a perpetual bonfire.

### What outsiders get wrong
Skarrg is not a council chamber with primitive decoration. It is a place where temporary unity is performed through feasting, boasting, challenge, ritual, and strength.

Old grudges remain active. No guarantee of peace exists merely because multiple clans are present.

### Sights and sounds
Gutted architecture blackened by fire. Giant bones. scavenged banners. weapon displays. clan markings layered over older carvings. Meat roasting. Challenges shouted across the ruin. Laughter that can turn into violence.

### Scene pressure
The PCs are invited by one clan. Another clan interprets their presence as an insult. Their host will protect them—but doing so may weaken the host politically.

## Tin Street through Precinct Four

Tin Street is trade and nightlife pushed through a dangerous precinct. Boros presence reassures shoppers; goblin gangs and rubblebelt threats keep everyone alert.

Street life changes by hour.

**Morning:** deliveries, cleanup, repair.  
**Afternoon:** trade at full volume.  
**Evening:** food, theater, crowds.  
**Night:** nightlife, deals, patrols, gangs, people who know which blocks to avoid.

The Shattergang Brothers and other criminal actors fit because weapons and explosives are already normal commodities near war-scarred neighborhoods.

## Precinct Four GM memory line

**Fortress shadow, laboratory glare, patched walls, people who know evacuation routes, and commerce surviving because danger has become part of local competence.**
