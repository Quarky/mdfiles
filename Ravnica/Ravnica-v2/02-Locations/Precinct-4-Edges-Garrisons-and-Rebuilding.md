# Precinct Four — Edges, Garrisons and Rebuilding

V2 treats Precinct Four as a place where **security and reconstruction are visible parts of daily life**. Barricades, damaged structures, garrisons, reclaimed lots, new walls beside old ruins, and contested routes give the precinct a harder rhythm.

## First impression

You can see where the city has been broken and repaired. New stone is a different color. Temporary wooden walkways have become permanent by neglect. Boros positions watch wide intersections. Graffiti appears on fresh walls before the mortar fully cures.

## Sensory palette

**See:** scaffolds, patchwork masonry, drill yards, watch posts, supply carts, rubble sorted for reuse.

**Hear:** hammering, shouted cadence, horns, arguments at checkpoints, masonry saws, cart chains.

**Smell:** dust, sweat, leather, hot metal, soup from field kitchens, wet mortar.

## Boros culture in the street

A garrison is not only soldiers. It creates cooks, armorers, healers, messengers, dependents, vendors, and residents who feel safer—or watched.

Boros troops know local trouble spots. Some residents greet them by name. Others go quiet when they turn the corner.

## Gruul pressure

Where construction pushes into ruins or open land, Gruul hostility can be immediate. Their attack may be ideological, territorial, retaliatory, or opportunistic. "Gruul raid" should not automatically mean random violence. Ask what changed recently.

## Displacement

Rebuilding creates winners and losers. A cleared ruin can become housing, a military yard, a market, or a luxury development. People who had informal use of the space may not appear on any Azorius plan.

## Scene seed

Boros engineers are fortifying a bridge after repeated attacks. The new wall blocks a neighborhood's only easy access to a lower market. Residents begin cutting unauthorized doors. An Azorius inspector orders the doors sealed. A Gruul gang watches from nearby ruins and starts leaving tools where residents can find them.

The question becomes who is defending whom from what.
