# Precinct One — Guildpact and Ceremony

V2 uses Precinct One as the district's most **institutionally visible** space: formal delegations, civic buildings, prominent plazas, visitors, guild representatives, petitioners, and people who know they are being watched.

## First impression

The stone is cleaner here because someone is paid to keep it clean. Streets broaden near important buildings. Sightlines are designed to impress. Banners hang where visitors can see them. Guards know the difference between a lost citizen and someone pretending to be lost.

A clerk carrying forty pages of sealed documents may have more immediate power than an armed adventurer.

## Sensory palette

**See:** polished pale stone, bronze plaques, steps wide enough for delegations, guarded doors, messenger traffic, queues arranged by purpose, public sculpture commemorating agreements nobody fully agrees about.

**Hear:** shoes on stone, clipped instructions, carriage wheels, controlled conversation, court bells, pigeons or rocs overhead, sudden echo inside large halls.

**Smell:** rain-clean stone, ink, wax, wool uniforms, coffee, expensive perfume, old paper.

## Ordinary life

People still eat lunch here. Porters still complain. Copyists have ink on their cuffs. Guards know which cafe serves fastest. Petitioners wait for hours and form temporary communities in queues.

That ordinariness is essential. Grand politics becomes real when an exhausted parent is trying to get a housing order reviewed before the office closes.

## Guild friction

Azorius officials want process. Boros delegates want decisions that protect people. Orzhov advocates want language that protects interests. Dimir messengers carry sealed information. Selesnyan representatives arrive in groups. Izzet researchers ask for exceptions. Gruul representatives, when they appear at all, are treated as both political actors and security risks.

## If the PCs draw attention

Within minutes, someone asks why they are here. Within an hour, at least one office has a written record. By the end of the day, a rival may know the subject of their visit even if the meeting itself was private.

## A scene to drop in

A Boros captain has brought witnesses after a tenement fire. An Azorius clerk refuses to accept testimony because the emergency declaration used the wrong district code. An Izzet engineer says the fire was caused by unauthorized modifications. The landlord's Orzhov advocate insists the tenants caused it. The witnesses can smell smoke on their own clothes.

Nobody is lying about everything.
