# War of the Spark — Deep Era Overlay

This is a **later emergency overlay**, not the default V2 Living Guildpact present.

The public *War of the Spark: Ravnica* story sequence shows the city experiencing invasion as a civic catastrophe: Planeswalkers appear across Ravnica, the Dreadhorde attacks civilians, guild loyalties remain fractured, and leaders must build cooperation while normal urban assumptions collapse.

Primary official public stories:

- *Old Friends and New*
- *The Path to Opulent*
- *Rallying the Reluctant*
- *Desperate Operatives*
- *Operation Desperation*
- *Ashes*

---

# 1. The war begins in places people used yesterday

One reason the official stories are valuable is that the invasion does not occur on an abstract battlefield.

The Transguild Promenade, Tenth District Plaza, Orzhova, guild territories, streets, gardens, and civic spaces become combat zones.

That means invasion imagery should always preserve the ordinary layer underneath it.

A broken wall was somebody's apartment.
A plaza was a meeting place yesterday.
The fallen cart was carrying food.
The bodies in the street include people who were trying to free relatives from rubble.
A new monument or pyramid displaces whatever stood there before.

## GM technique

For every war-torn location, state:

1. what it was used for yesterday;
2. what happened to it;
3. who is trying to use it now.

Example:

Yesterday: café terrace beside a transit route.  
Now: shattered tables and lazotep fragments.  
Current use: Boros medics have turned the cellar into a casualty station while the owner keeps asking whether anyone has seen her cook.

This prevents the war from becoming generic fantasy rubble.

---

# 2. Planeswalkers arrive as incomprehensible refugees and weapons

Official War fiction shows off-world Planeswalkers appearing in the middle of Ravnican public space.

Most ordinary citizens do not possess a complete cosmological model for what is happening.

They see:

- strangers materialize;
- unfamiliar clothing and species;
- unusual magic;
- people who clearly do not know the city;
- veteran Ravnican leaders treating some of these strangers as strategically vital.

## Immediate civilian reactions

- assume Izzet teleportation until evidence says otherwise;
- offer help if the stranger looks injured;
- move away if the arrival damages property;
- report the event;
- exploit confusion;
- ask which guild is responsible;
- later realize there are too many arrivals for an ordinary magical accident.

The revelation that Ravnica is suddenly full of off-world people should be social before it becomes lore exposition.

---

# 3. The Immortal Sun makes the plane a trap

During the active War-era trap, Planeswalkers can arrive but cannot simply leave Ravnica.

For MTGFoundry, this is a **timeline condition**, not a permanent rule of the plane.

## What that changes

A planeswalker accustomed to escape suddenly needs:

- shelter;
- food;
- local allies;
- maps;
- healing;
- information;
- a way to distinguish guild insignia;
- somebody who can explain which street is safe.

That puts powerful off-worlders into the hands of ordinary Ravnicans.

A guildless café owner may become important because three Planeswalkers are hiding in the cellar.

A Dimir courier may become strategically valuable because they can move someone through streets the visitor cannot navigate.

---

# 4. The Beacon creates political blame

Official War material connects the Izzet, Ral Zarek, and the Beacon that summons Planeswalkers.

Whatever the exact technical responsibility, public understanding will be simpler and harsher.

Citizens hear:

- Izzet device;
- Planeswalkers appeared;
- invasion followed.

Those facts are enough for rumor.

## Postwar arguments

"Izzet brought them here."

"No, Izzet tried to warn us."

"Ral knew."

"Ral saved my block."

"Your block survived. Mine burned."

This is how large magical events become neighborhood politics.

---

# 5. Guild distrust does not disappear because Bolas is worse

The official stories explicitly show that guilds are not instantly united.

Some are compromised.
Some retreat to defend their own territory.
Some need to be persuaded.
Some have leadership serving or influenced by Bolas.
Some simply do not trust the coalition being proposed.

That is the correct tone.

## Tactical unity, emotional distrust

A Boros commander may accept Rakdos fighters while assigning troops to watch them.

An Azorius survivor may work with Gruul while still calling them unlawful.

A Selesnyan healer may treat an Orzhov knight and refuse the knight's offer of a binding repayment.

A Dimir operative may provide life-saving intelligence while refusing to explain how it was obtained.

The invasion creates cooperation without erasing identity.

---

# 6. Orzhova becomes sanctuary, treasury, fortress, and political stage

Official War fiction moves through **Orzhova, the Cathedral Opulent** while efforts are being made to rally guild support.

During invasion, an Orzhov cathedral can serve many functions at once:

- shelter;
- command point;
- financial vault;
- negotiation site;
- fortified interior;
- source of magical resources;
- center of hierarchy.

## Ordinary people inside

Do not fill it only with leaders.

Use:

- borrowers trapped away from home;
- servants;
- clerks preserving ledgers;
- wounded enforcers;
- priests organizing food;
- families who fled nearby streets;
- wealthy patrons demanding private protection;
- spirits continuing old duties as though invasion were simply another breach of contract.

## Postwar leverage

Every emergency promise can become a future claim.

An Orzhov official who supplies food may later insist the transaction was recorded.

Another may deliberately waive payment and gain enormous social capital.

The guild is not required to make the same choice everywhere.

---

# 7. Rakdos becomes useful without becoming orderly

The War gives the Cult a situation where traits normally feared by the city can become military assets:

- willingness to confront terror;
- appetite for spectacle;
- physical daring;
- chaotic movement;
- contempt for authoritarian pretension;
- comfort with violence.

That does not turn Rakdos into Boros.

## A Rakdos defense scene

A Ringmaster does not say, "Form ranks."

They turn a street into a performance arena, lure Eternals into a kill zone, ignite scenery, and use an audience's panic as energy for coordinated movement.

Some civilians cheer.
Others are nearly killed by the solution.

Both responses are valid.

---

# 8. Selesnya's distributed community is wartime infrastructure

A vernadi can rapidly become:

- shelter;
- kitchen;
- field hospital;
- muster point;
- childcare site;
- information node;
- defensive position.

Selesnya's strength during civic collapse is that its communities already know how to feed, house, train, and organize people locally.

## The cost

Destroying a vernadi does more than remove military capacity.

It can remove:

- home;
- temple;
- family network;
- food;
- identity;
- spiritual connection.

A displaced Selesnyan survivor may experience the loss as several kinds of homelessness at once.

---

# 9. Golgari becomes responsible for what the surface cannot bear to look at

War produces:

- bodies;
- sewage failure;
- contaminated water;
- broken foundations;
- flooded lower levels;
- food disruption;
- abandoned animals;
- unstable ruins.

These are all problems the undercity understands.

Even when the Golgari leadership is politically complicated, local Swarm communities can become essential simply because they know how to process catastrophe.

## Postwar resentment

The surface may praise heroic guilds publicly while quietly expecting Golgari workers to remove the dead.

That hypocrisy should matter.

---

# 10. Boros fights the war it always imagined—and pays for it

The invasion validates Boros fears about external catastrophic violence.

It also pushes the Legion toward the most dangerous edge of its virtue: decisive force under impossible conditions.

## Show both

Heroism:
- holding evacuation routes;
- rescuing civilians;
- standing against Eternals;
- aerial deployment;
- exhausted soldiers refusing to abandon a block.

Cost:
- emergency requisition;
- demolished buildings to create defensive space;
- civilians ordered from homes;
- confused chains of command;
- friendly fire;
- triage;
- trauma;
- officers making decisions they will review for years afterward.

Boros becomes compelling when courage and coercion occupy the same uniform.

---

# 11. Azorius continuity becomes a form of survival

During invasion, procedure can look absurd.

It can also preserve civilization.

Azorius workers record:

- missing people;
- deaths;
- emergency authority;
- prisoners;
- damaged property;
- evacuation zones;
- supply transfers;
- orders;
- later testimony.

Those records become the foundation for rebuilding.

## Two Azorius reactions

**Emergency pragmatist:** suspends normal process because survival requires it.

**Continuity defender:** preserves process because if every emergency action becomes unrecorded precedent, postwar abuse will be impossible to untangle.

Neither needs to be a caricature.

---

# 12. Dimir information suddenly has obvious humanitarian value

In normal politics, secret information is leverage.

During invasion, route knowledge can save hundreds of lives.

Dimir assets may know:

- which tunnels remain open;
- where enemy movement is concentrated;
- which guild leader can still be reached;
- which officer is compromised;
- which building has a hidden exit;
- which warehouse actually contains food.

A spy can spend years building a secret and then burn it in one minute to open an evacuation route.

That does not make the House altruistic.

It demonstrates that information is power because power sometimes means **keeping people alive**.

---

# 13. Simic emergency medicine pushes ethics to the edge

The War places extreme demand on:

- trauma treatment;
- antidotes;
- biological adaptation;
- environmental survival;
- rapid field medicine;
- creation or deployment of defenders.

A procedure too risky for ordinary approval may be the only option during an invasion.

## Postwar question

If an emergency adaptation saved hundreds of people, should it remain available?

The Simic argument after the crisis can be fiercer than the argument during it.

Some say:
- the measure proved itself.

Others:
- wartime consent was compromised.

Others:
- the emergency revealed that old rules were too conservative.

Others:
- survival cannot justify permanent normalization.

That is excellent campaign politics.

---

# 14. Gruul participation does not equal acceptance of the city

A Gruul warrior can fight Bolas without deciding civilization is legitimate.

The logic can be simple:

"No dragon from another world gets to own the ruins I already hate."

That is enough.

## Wartime alliances

Gruul knowledge of rubble, unstable structures, hidden routes, and improvised movement can be extremely useful.

After victory, the alliance may collapse the moment Azorius planners propose rebuilding every destroyed block exactly as before.

The war can actually sharpen the question:

**what should be rebuilt, and who gets to decide?**

---

# 15. War creates new neighborhood reputations

Afterward, people remember specific acts.

"This was the street the Boros held."

"That café hid walkers."

"The Orzhov opened their cellar."

"The Azorius officer unlocked the gate without waiting for authorization."

"The Rakdos troupe led the Eternals away."

"The Selesnyans fed us."

"The Golgari came up at night and removed the bodies."

"The Izzet line exploded—but it also powered the evacuation lift."

These memories become political facts even when no official history records them.

---

# 16. Postwar rebuilding is the next campaign

The invasion ends before its consequences do.

Rebuilding creates conflicts over:

- property ownership when records are destroyed;
- who pays for repair;
- whether emergency shelters become permanent;
- whether ruined land returns to previous owners;
- whether Gruul claim newly open rubble;
- whether Simic biological defenses are removed;
- whether Boros keeps emergency powers;
- whether Azorius investigates wartime abuses;
- whether Orzhov emergency loans are collectible;
- whether Dimir secrets exposed during the war remain useful;
- whether Rakdos heroes are suddenly expected to behave respectably.

The end of the war is the beginning of a huge civic campaign.
