# Living in Ravnica — Player Reader

This is for players whose characters should feel as if they **grew up on Ravnica** rather than arrived five minutes before the campaign.

It contains ordinary knowledge, habits, assumptions, and social expectations. It avoids GM-only secrets.

# You have almost never seen an empty horizon

The first fact of your life is city.

You grew up with:

- walls;
- roofs;
- towers;
- domes;
- chimneys;
- pipes;
- bridges;
- balconies;
- statues;
- elevated roads;
- market awnings;
- windows looking into other windows;
- streets built above older streets.

Even open places are framed by construction.

A green courtyard has rooftops behind its trees.
A ruined block has bridges crossing overhead.
A plaza opens toward several different street levels.
A park feels special precisely because vegetation interrupts masonry.

When an off-worlder stares too long at the skyline, locals notice.

# You think vertically

"Next door" can mean a different level.

A destination might be directly below you but twenty minutes away because the nearest stair is three blocks over.

A broken lift is not a curiosity. It can ruin hundreds of commutes.

A bridge closure can separate people who live only a few hundred feet apart.

You routinely ask:

- which level?
- above or below the viaduct?
- street entrance or lower entrance?
- public stair or guild access?
- near the old arch or the new one?
- before or after the freight ramp?

Locals navigate by layered landmarks because a flat map only tells part of the truth.

# Guilds are not abstract political parties

Even if you are guildless, guilds touch your day.

You might:

- drink water through Izzet-maintained infrastructure;
- see Boros patrols;
- file a permit or complaint with Azorius;
- owe money to an Orzhov lender;
- buy cheap Golgari food;
- attend a Selesnyan healer or garden;
- use Simic medicine;
- work beside Rakdos laborers;
- hire a courier who may or may not be Dimir;
- pass the remains of a Gruul raid on the way to work.

You can hate a guild and still need its services.

That contradiction is normal.

# Guild membership can be your whole social world

Joining a guild can determine:

- your job;
- your housing;
- your training;
- your friends;
- your meals;
- your debts;
- your protection;
- your rank;
- who vouches for you;
- which doors open;
- who notices if you disappear.

Leaving can therefore resemble changing careers, religion, family, political identity, and social class at the same time.

Some people leave anyway.

Some spend years trying.

Some discover that technically being allowed to leave is not the same as being able to afford it.

# Guildless does not mean alone

Guildless citizens have:

- families;
- neighborhoods;
- professions;
- trade networks;
- clubs;
- local leaders;
- landlords;
- mutual-aid arrangements;
- criminal protection;
- teachers;
- temples or beliefs;
- social traditions.

A guildless neighborhood can have stronger local identity than a guild district.

Never write "guildless" on your sheet as though it means "no social context."

# You know the stereotypes

You have heard versions of these whether you believe them or not.

## Azorius

Slow.
Self-important.
Obsessed with forms.
Also the people you want when someone powerful is doing something clearly illegal.

## Boros

Brave.
Loud.
Protective.
Dangerous when certainty becomes permission.

## Dimir

Everybody jokes that the courier is probably a spy.

Then everybody uses couriers.

## Golgari

Dirty, according to people who never go below.
Indispensable, according to anyone who has been hungry.
Resentful, according to people who have never asked why.

## Gruul

Violent wreckers to one resident.
Dispossessed clans to another.
Both can be true in the same conversation.

## Izzet

Geniuses who keep the city running.
Also the reason everyone knows what ozone smells like.

## Orzhov

Priests.
Lenders.
Lawyers.
Patrons.
Gangsters.
Which word applies depends partly on whether you owe them money.

## Rakdos

Artists.
Laborers.
Night people.
Thrill-seekers.
Criminals.
Sadists.
Comedians.
People respectable citizens publicly condemn and privately pay to watch.

## Selesnya

Kind neighbors.
Communal idealists.
Healers.
Recruiters.
Militia.
The place you can belong completely—possibly more completely than you intended.

## Simic

Doctors.
Researchers.
Strange bodies.
Useful medicine.
The people who can solve a problem you did not know biology could solve and then ask whether they may keep the sample.

# You understand insignia

A guild insignia can communicate:

- employment;
- allegiance;
- rank;
- official authority;
- privilege;
- danger;
- access;
- responsibility.

You know that a Boros tabard changes how a crowd reacts.
You know that an Azorius seal can open a door or close it.
You know an Orzhov mark on a property means more than decoration.
You know a hidden insignia can matter more than a visible one.

# You know Boros and Azorius are not the same thing

An outsider may look at both and see "law."

You do not.

When somebody is being attacked **right now**, you think Boros.

When the question is warrants, hearings, legality, permits, injunctions, records, or whether the Boros were allowed to do what they just did, you think Azorius.

They cooperate.
They argue.
You have an opinion about which one is worse.

# You have waited in a line caused by someone else's guild problem

A bridge closed.
A search checkpoint.
A public hearing.
A military cordon.
A procession.
A Rakdos stage blocking a square.
An Izzet conduit failure.
A Selesnyan demonstration.
A loose krasis.
A Gruul attack.
An Orzhov seizure.
An argument about whether the line itself requires a street permit.

Ravnicans are good at queuing while complaining about why they should not have to.

# Magic can be boring

You have seen magic used as:

- heating;
- plumbing;
- lighting;
- security;
- transport;
- medicine;
- communication;
- industrial power;
- entertainment;
- evidence;
- infrastructure.

You can be annoyed by magic.

You can say, "the lift enchantment is acting up again," with the same emotion someone else uses for a broken hinge.

That is a very Ravnican relationship to magic.

# You know Izzet noises have categories

People who live near Izzet facilities learn to distinguish noises.

There is:

- normal venting;
- scheduled discharge;
- something a technician will fix;
- something a technician should already have fixed;
- "why is that light green?";
- "why did the humming stop?";
- the silence after an explosion when everyone waits to hear whether a second one is coming.

Children imitate Izzet warning whistles.

Adults pretend this is not funny.

# Food is part of neighborhood identity

Ravnica has:

- bakeries;
- coffee;
- tea;
- wine;
- street food;
- portable meals;
- restaurants;
- taverns;
- communal kitchens;
- expensive dining;
- cheap broth;
- festival food.

Your character should know what they eat when:

- they are broke;
- they are celebrating;
- they have five minutes before work;
- they are trying to impress someone;
- they are sick.

Golgari food exists as a subsistence floor for people who cannot afford better.

People make jokes about it.

People with empty cupboards make fewer jokes.

# Housing is vertical and social

A good address can mean:

- light;
- ventilation;
- reliable heat;
- private lift access;
- good security;
- quiet;
- distance from industrial smell.

A cheap room can still be solidly built and connected to utilities while suffering:

- crowding;
- damp;
- noise;
- poor repair;
- predatory rent;
- dangerous stairs;
- unreliable lifts.

A family may have lived in "the same building" for generations even though the street level outside rose through successive rebuilding.

# Old buildings are never just old

A sealed basement can open into:

- an older street;
- a crypt;
- an abandoned transit passage;
- Golgari territory;
- a Dimir route;
- an Izzet utility tunnel;
- a forgotten shrine;
- something older than anyone's records.

Children are warned not to crawl through certain cracks.

Children crawl through them.

# The undercity is not one place

You know people who speak about "the undercity" as if it is all the same.

Usually they do not live there.

It includes:

- sewers;
- old roads;
- buried neighborhoods;
- Golgari settlements;
- forgotten halls;
- utility tunnels;
- caverns;
- crypts;
- flooded spaces;
- ancient ruins.

Saying "I went below" is like saying "I went across town." It does not tell anyone enough.

# Night changes ownership of the street

Ravnica does not go to sleep all at once.

After dark:

- office workers go home;
- second and third shifts begin;
- couriers keep moving;
- Rakdos venues assemble;
- Golgari workers rise toward surface routes;
- taverns fill;
- private clubs open;
- patrol patterns change;
- some markets close;
- others become better.

A lifelong resident has a city map that changes by hour.

# You know which route you take when the main route is blocked

This matters.

A character who grew up here should have:

- a preferred shortcut;
- an alley they avoid;
- a stair that smells terrible but saves ten minutes;
- a bridge with a view;
- a vendor they use as a landmark;
- one route that only works when a certain gate is open.

That is what belonging to a city feels like.

# You have opinions about guild neighborhoods you rarely visit

You may have never entered Rix Maadi.

You still have an opinion.

You may have never seen Duskmantle.

You have certainly heard stories.

You may know Vitu-Ghazi mostly through festivals, sermons, visitors, or relatives.

Famous places become cultural objects long before people personally visit them.

# You are used to radically different bodies

Ravnica is cosmopolitan.

Seeing someone who is:

- loxodon;
- minotaur;
- goblin;
- centaur;
- vedalken;
- elf;
- viashino;
- Simic hybrid;
- flamekin;
- winged;
- huge;
- visibly magical

does not automatically make you stare.

You may still have prejudice.

Familiarity is not equality.

But architecture, furniture, transit, and crowds have adapted to many body types for a very long time.

# A centaur changes a room

This is the kind of detail locals stop noticing.

A narrow stair is inconvenient.
A lift has capacity limits.
A restaurant may have open floor seating.
A waiting room may be designed with several body types in mind.
A bridge may have fast and slow traffic patterns.
A tiny old shop may simply be inaccessible.

The city is fantastical through **practical adaptation**, not just spectacle.

# You know that paperwork can be dangerous

A document can:

- prove residence;
- transfer property;
- authorize search;
- freeze access;
- establish debt;
- invalidate a claim;
- open a secure area;
- create a paper trail.

Showing credentials solves one problem and tells institutions where you are.

People who live around bureaucracy understand that.

# Debt is social, not just financial

An Orzhov obligation can shape:

- where someone works;
- what risks they take;
- who they testify against;
- whether they can move;
- who inherits a problem;
- whether a spirit is truly done with service.

But non-Orzhov favors matter too.

You remember who got your child into a clinic.
Who helped after a fire.
Who lied to a patrol for you.
Who gave you work.

The difference is that Orzhov is extremely good at **recording obligation**.

# You know who fixes things

A child learns institutions through failure.

No heat?
Someone blames Izzet.

Blocked drain?
Someone asks whether it is surface infrastructure or Golgari territory below.

Broken bridge?
Azorius may close it, Boros may guard it, Izzet may repair it, Orzhov may finance it, Selesnya may object to what the repair removes.

You do not need formal political education to learn which guild touches which service.

You learn because adults complain.

# News arrives unevenly

Ravnica has:

- messengers;
- couriers;
- official notices;
- rumor;
- guild networks;
- street performers;
- private letters;
- public speeches;
- tavern gossip.

A neighborhood may know that something happened hours before it knows what actually happened.

Everyone has a cousin who "knows someone in Boros."

Everyone knows this source is unreliable.

Everyone listens anyway.

# Children know guilds before they understand politics

Children imitate uniforms.
They play at being skyknights.
They dare one another to touch Izzet fixtures.
They repeat Rakdos jokes they do not understand.
They know that an Orzhov collector at the door makes adults tense.
They know which Selesnyan courtyard gives out food.
They know not to follow strange people into lower tunnels.

Guild identity becomes childhood culture long before it becomes ideology.

# School depends on family and institution

A child may learn through:

- household teaching;
- guild instruction;
- local tutors;
- religious community;
- apprenticeship;
- university preparation;
- military training;
- neighborhood schooling.

A guild child may learn the values of the institution through chores before lessons.

An Izzet child learns not to touch marked apparatus.
A Selesnyan child learns communal duties.
A Boros family teaches discipline and service.
An Orzhov family teaches status and obligation.

# Ravnicans understand that public space is political

A plaza is never just paving.

It can be:

- market;
- protest site;
- parade ground;
- legal gathering area;
- performance venue;
- emergency muster point;
- place where a guild demonstrates presence.

Who is allowed to occupy a plaza tells you something about power.

# A mobile Rakdos stage can become a neighborhood event in an hour

Official Rakdos performances can appear on streets and plazas and disappear by morning.

That means people learn to react fast.

Shopkeepers decide whether to close.
Teenagers hear about it.
Boros hears about it.
Azorius checks permits.
Vendors arrive to sell food.
Pickpockets arrive.
People who swore they hated Rakdos show up anyway.

By morning there may be scorch marks, broken rigging, gossip, and somebody insisting the performance was better last year.

# Selesnyan hospitality can feel wonderful

A vernadi can offer:

- food;
- shade;
- healers;
- community;
- training;
- childcare;
- worship;
- safety.

People join because these things are genuinely attractive.

The pressure of conformity matters **because the warmth is real**.

# Golgari service is visible when the surface fails

If food supply breaks, waste backs up, or lower infrastructure is damaged, people discover quickly how much the upper city depends on work it prefers not to see.

A character who grew up poor may have a more practical opinion of Golgari than a wealthy character does.

# Gruul ruins have history before the fight

When you see a broken district edge, remember:

The Gruul often understand it as land that civilization took and may take again.

A surface resident understands the same place as unsafe abandoned property.

Both interpretations shape behavior before anyone rolls initiative.

# Simic medicine can be miraculous and unsettling

Your neighbor might owe their life to a Simic clinic.

You may also know someone whose treatment left a visible adaptation.

The question "would you let Simic change you to save you?" is not abstract in Ravnica.

People have family stories about it.

# Orzhov respectability is part of its power

An Orzhov basilica does not have to look criminal.

It can be:

- beautiful;
- organized;
- safe;
- wealthy;
- quiet;
- professionally staffed.

The danger is that all of those qualities can make obligation feel legitimate.

# Boros heroism is part of its power

People really are saved by Boros soldiers.

That is why criticizing Boros excess can be socially complicated.

A resident can believe:

"They saved us during the riot."

and

"They had no right to search every apartment."

at the same time.

# Azorius fairness can be real and still hurt

A rule can apply equally and harm people unequally.

A wealthy person can survive a six-week delay.

A worker who misses wages cannot.

A character who grew up around Azorius systems should understand that injustice can emerge without a corrupt judge.

# Dimir jokes are social camouflage

People can joke:

"Of course the messenger is Dimir."

Most of the time, they are joking.

The joke persists because the public knows information guilds and courier networks exist, while the deeper structure remains intentionally hard to understand.

# Famous people are not part of your daily life—until suddenly they are

You know names like:

- Jace Beleren;
- Aurelia;
- Niv-Mizzet;
- Trostani;
- Lazav;
- Rakdos;
- Jarad;
- Zegana;
- Isperia;
- Borborygmos;
- the Obzedat.

But knowing a guildmaster's name does not mean you know what their current plan is.

Most citizens experience leadership through:

- orders;
- rumors;
- speeches;
- changed patrols;
- policy;
- prices;
- recruitment;
- ceremonies.

When a guildmaster appears personally, that is an event.

# The Living Guildpact is both constitutional miracle and inconvenient human being

Jace Beleren embodies extraordinary authority.

He is also one person.

He can be:

- absent;
- tired;
- overwhelmed;
- late;
- unreachable.

Guilds can wait for rulings.

Lavinia and other officials can spend their lives managing the fact that the supreme constitutional mechanism sometimes leaves the plane.

You have an opinion about this, even if the opinion is only:

"I wish the guilds would stop blocking my street every time they have a constitutional dispute."

# Planeswalkers are not ordinary public knowledge in the same way guilds are

The city contains people who know more about the Multiverse than others.

Do not assume every baker understands what a Planeswalker is.

A strange arrival is more likely to be explained first through familiar categories:

Izzet accident.
Teleportation.
Dimir trick.
Guild magic.

Multiversal truth is unevenly distributed.

# Death is political

Who handles a body can matter.

- Azorius records.
- Boros investigates or honors service.
- Orzhov manages inheritance, debt, ritual, spirits.
- Golgari understands decay and reclamation.
- Selesnya places death in communal and spiritual context.
- Simic may investigate cause or disease.
- Dimir may care what information died.
- Rakdos may interpret mortality through spectacle.
- Gruul practices vary by clan.

A funeral can involve several guilds without anyone drawing a weapon.

# You know the calendar affects the street

The Ravnican year has twelve months and 365 days.

The civic year turns around the Guildpact celebration at the end of Zuun and beginning of Seleszeni.

Even if you do not care about politics, major civic dates change:

- traffic;
- work shifts;
- prices;
- patrols;
- visiting family;
- public performance;
- crowds.

Festivals are infrastructure events.

# Before play, answer these

You do not need a novel of backstory.

Answer twenty lived questions.

1. What neighborhood feels like home?
2. Which street do you know best?
3. What is your cheap meal?
4. What do you eat when celebrating?
5. Which guild service did your family depend on?
6. Which guild does your family distrust?
7. Which guild stereotype do you know is wrong?
8. Which stereotype do you believe anyway?
9. Which route do you use when the main road is closed?
10. Which famous place have you never actually visited?
11. Who taught you how to deal with officials?
12. What civic inconvenience makes you irrationally angry?
13. Who in your neighborhood would hide you from a patrol?
14. Who would report you?
15. What debt or favor does your household remember?
16. What sound tells you something is wrong?
17. What smell reminds you of home?
18. What guild insignia makes you relax?
19. What guild insignia makes you check the exits?
20. What part of Ravnica would you defend even against your own guild?

If you can answer those, your character already lives here.
