# One Hundred Things a Ravnican Knows Without Thinking

These are not one hundred universal laws. They are the kind of habits, observations, prejudices, and practical lessons that make a lifelong resident feel different from an outsider.

They are grounded in published Ravnica's city-world, guild civic functions, vertical geography, and official fiction; the exact formulations are table synthesis.

1. **The city continues past whatever horizon you can see.** A tall enough tower reveals more roofs, not countryside.

2. **Street level is a temporary concept.** Today's cellar can be yesterday's avenue.

3. **A map that does not mark elevation is only half a map.**

4. **If a bridge closes, the detour may be vertical rather than horizontal.**

5. **A public lift failure becomes neighborhood news within minutes.**

6. **The Transguild Promenade belongs to everybody precisely because nobody can afford to let one guild own it completely.**

7. **You can tell a freight lane by the paving before the carts arrive.**

8. **Centaur traffic changes how a crowd moves.**

9. **When roc shadows cross a plaza, people glance up before deciding whether to care.**

10. **A Boros sky patrol is reassuring until it circles twice.**

11. **Azorius notices are easier to ignore than Azorius seals.**

12. **A closed door with the right blue-white mark can be more absolute than a wall.**

13. **An arrester asking politely is still an arrester.**

14. **A Wojek and an Azorius officer can agree that you are in trouble for completely different reasons.**

15. **If Boros arrives first, the immediate problem gets smaller and the number of witnesses gets larger.**

16. **If Azorius arrives first, everybody starts asking who is authorized to be standing where.**

17. **If both arrive, someone will eventually argue about procedure.**

18. **An Orzhov clerk with no bodyguards can be more dangerous than an Orzhov enforcer with four.**

19. **Never sign something just because the person explaining it sounds bored.**

20. **"No interest" does not mean "no obligation."**

21. **A favor that the Orzhov did not write down may still have been written down somewhere else.**

22. **Rich Orzhov neighborhoods smell cleaner than most people expect. That is part of the point.**

23. **A Rakdos poster is an advertisement, a threat, a joke, or all three.**

24. **A Rakdos show that everyone claims to hate may still sell out.**

25. **The stage crew often knows more about structural safety than the audience thinks.**

26. **If the giant carrying the stage looks tired, give it room.**

27. **The second explosion is the one that tells you whether the Izzet are worried.**

28. **No visible smoke does not mean the experiment is safe.**

29. **Blue smoke is information. Green smoke is also information. You may not know what the information means.**

30. **An Izzet technician saying "interesting" is not always good news.**

31. **An Izzet technician saying nothing at all is worse.**

32. **The person carrying the clipboard in an Izzet lab may have more survival sense than the person casting the spell.**

33. **Maintenance workers know which warning labels are current.**

34. **Nobody laughs at Golgari food when the normal deliveries stop.**

35. **Surface residents often call undercity spaces abandoned that are visibly occupied to anyone who actually lives below.**

36. **Do not harvest mushrooms you cannot identify just because a surface herbalist says they are valuable.**

37. **A Golgari worker can tell useful rot from dangerous rot faster than a scholar can explain the distinction.**

38. **If a kraul tells you a tunnel is unstable, believe them.**

39. **If a Golgari says "the city above," they may mean it literally.**

40. **The Gruul did not discover ruins after the city abandoned them. Many clans understand those ruins as what remains after the city took everything else.**

41. **A new road can be somebody else's invasion.**

42. **Gruul graffiti on a construction notice may be a warning, not decoration.**

43. **A Slizt scout is probably looking down at you from somewhere you did not check.**

44. **A Zhur-Taa shaman may care more about old sacred geography than about whatever the current district map says.**

45. **Selesnyan hospitality is usually real.**

46. **So is Selesnyan recruitment.**

47. **A vernadi can feed you before anyone asks what you believe.**

48. **Once you have eaten there for a month, the question of belonging feels different.**

49. **A Selesnyan courtyard can become a militia assembly point much faster than an outsider expects.**

50. **When the communal song stops suddenly, pay attention.**

51. **Simic clinics can save people other healers cannot.**

52. **"Permanent side effect" means different things to a Simic researcher and to the patient.**

53. **A zonot has its own local culture. Saying "the Simic" is not enough.**

54. **Water sound can tell you that you are approaching a Simic space before you see one.**

55. **A sealed tank being moved through a street is everybody's business until the carriers say it is Simic business. Then people mostly move out of the way.**

56. **A Dimir courier is probably just a courier.**

57. **That sentence is exactly why the Dimir arrangement works.**

58. **If a librarian tells you the book does not exist, that may mean at least four different things.**

59. **Do not follow a stranger who says they can show you Duskmantle unless you are prepared for the possibility that you will not remember the route back.**

60. **The best information in a neighborhood often belongs to people with ordinary jobs.**

61. **Lift operators hear arguments all day.**

62. **Food vendors know who stopped coming.**

63. **Building superintendents know which apartments are officially empty.**

64. **Couriers know which offices use the back door.**

65. **Children know which ruins adults pretend not to use.**

66. **A public square has different owners at different hours even if the deed never changes.**

67. **Morning belongs to freight and workers.**

68. **Midday belongs to offices, markets, and queues.**

69. **Evening belongs to commuters, restaurants, worship, and gathering crowds.**

70. **Night belongs to everyone who works while respectable people claim the city is asleep.**

71. **Rain reveals which building maintenance was postponed.**

72. **Rain also reveals how much of the city is above you.**

73. **If water is falling from a clear sky, check whether there is another street overhead.**

74. **A warm grate in winter is valuable information.**

75. **A suddenly cold grate may mean the heating line failed.**

76. **A smell of ozone does not identify the problem, only the family of problems.**

77. **A smell of hot metal in Precinct Six may not indicate anything unusual at all.**

78. **A smell of hot metal in a Selesnyan courtyard probably does.**

79. **Every famous guildhall has a neighborhood of people whose living depends on servicing the people who enter it.**

80. **The person who sells breakfast outside New Prahv may know more about court scheduling than you do.**

81. **The tavern nearest a Boros garrison knows shift changes.**

82. **The café nearest a university knows which professors are feuding.**

83. **The cheap rooms near Nivix know which researchers pay late.**

84. **The shops near an Orzhov basilica know when funerals are frequent.**

85. **The market near a vernadi knows when Selesnya is preparing for a large communal gathering.**

86. **The city remembers emergencies through altered routes long after memorial speeches end.**

87. **People navigate by disasters: "the bridge they rebuilt after the fire," "the block where the weird escaped," "the stairs from the old riot."**

88. **Nobody trusts official casualty numbers until they hear the neighborhood list.**

89. **A guildmaster's order becomes real when a mid-level official explains what it means for tomorrow's shift.**

90. **The Living Guildpact can settle an impossible dispute. Getting the impossible dispute in front of him is a different problem.**

91. **Lavinia, or somebody doing Lavinia's kind of work, is the reason extraordinary authority can function at all.**

92. **If a guild dispute reaches Jace, it was probably already somebody's problem for days, weeks, or years.**

93. **A Planeswalker appearing from nowhere is less immediately believable to most citizens than an Izzet accident.**

94. **Outsiders reveal themselves by looking up too long, asking where the city ends, or assuming all undercity routes connect.**

95. **Another sign of an outsider is asking which guild "controls" an entire district.**

96. **All ten guilds can matter on the same street.**

97. **No guild is only its worst stereotype, because the city depends on too many of their ordinary members.**

98. **No guild is only its best civic function, because every institution can convert necessity into power.**

99. **The city existed before your problem and will continue after it. People around you are trying to finish their own day while you save the world.**

100. **If a scene could happen in any fantasy city after removing the guild names, you are not done describing Ravnica yet.**
