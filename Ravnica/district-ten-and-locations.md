# District Ten & Adventure Geography

The *Guildmasters' Guide to Ravnica* makes the **Tenth District** the principal D&D campaign area and divides it into six precincts connected by major trade ways and layered with undercity spaces.

## Running the district
Treat precinct boundaries as differences in density, architecture, institutional presence, wealth, traffic, and risk rather than hard biome walls. Guild influence overlaps.

### Precinct One
Governmental and monumental Ravnica. Use it for Senate business, major institutions, formal diplomacy, demonstrations, and crimes where jurisdiction matters as much as evidence.

### Precinct Two
A strong place for commerce, public life, guild interaction, and characters who need to cross social boundaries quickly.

### Precinct Three
Dense urban life and mixed interests make it useful for neighborhood-scale crises, civic services, and collateral damage from guild conflict.

### Precinct Four
A good transition space for trade, institutions, and plots that move from respectable surfaces toward stranger or more dangerous layers.

### Precinct Five
Use for strong contrasts between developed city and spaces where older structures, guild enclaves, or neglected infrastructure intrude.

### Precinct Six
Useful for edge conditions: rubblebelts, contested development, and adventures where the city's claim to total control becomes visibly false.

## Beneath the streets
The undercity is not a single sewer dungeon. It contains buried architecture, caverns, rot farms, Golgari communities, forgotten infrastructure, monsters, hidden routes, and secrets from older Ravnicas.

## Campaign-specific anchor
**The Glass Run** uses an Izzet workshop near Augustin Station, the Laboratory of Orientation's teleportation/spatial-recombination work, gravitational-inversion research, and the Smelting Quarter as grounded adventure infrastructure. These are useful templates: begin with a real civic or research function, then make the adventure emerge from competing claims on it.

## Map design principle
Every map should answer four questions: who built this layer, who uses it now, which guild claims it, and what older layer is underneath?
