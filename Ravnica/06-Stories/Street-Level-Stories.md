# Street Level Stories

ORIGINAL FICTION - non-canon examples.

## The Permit and the Fire
**Guilds/themes:** Azorius / Izzet / Boros

The laboratory roof was still burning when Arrester Vel arrived with a citation half-written. “Your suppression cistern is unlicensed.” Chemister Dovik stared through soot-blackened lenses. “It is also suppressing the fire.” A Boros medic pushed between them carrying a singed goblin. “Argue after everyone is out.” Vel stepped aside, watched the medic disappear, and began writing an emergency variance on the back of the citation. Dovik noticed. Neither mentioned it.

GM lesson: each participant should pursue a recognizable institutional or personal need; Ravnica emerges when those needs collide without erasing ordinary life.

## Bread from Below
**Guilds/themes:** Selesnya / Golgari / Orzhov

The mushroom cakes were gray, dense, and warm. “You could make the stall beautiful,” said the Selesnyan initiate. The kraul vendor clicked his mandibles. “I made it feed six blocks.” An Orzhov collector behind her marked a ledger. “Hunger is, fortunately, financeable.” Both turned on him. For one excellent second, the Conclave and the Swarm achieved complete harmony.

## Tuesday on Tin Street
**Guilds/themes:** Guildless / Izzet / Gruul

The guildless superintendent pointed at a collapsed foundation. “Run the new line through that. Leave the old trench open for drainage.” The engineer called it inefficient. The shaman called it insufficient. The tenants called it Tuesday.

## The Last Tram Witness
**Guilds/themes:** Dimir / Boros / Guildless

A fruit seller saw the knife. A Boros patrol knew she saw it. So did the man in gray who had been buying the same pear for ten minutes. The wojek asked for a statement. The man in gray asked whether she was sure there had been a knife. She looked at the blood on the rail. “Yes. But I am not saying whose hand was on it until my daughter is home.” Both institutions realized she understood the city perfectly.

## A Window in Whitestone
**Guilds/themes:** Azorius / Orzhov / Boros

An Azorius clerk and her husband unfolded an Orzhov foreclosure notice. “They cannot take the house.” “They can,” she said. Their son, home from Horizon Academy, put his Boros gloves on the table. “So we stop them.” His mother closed her eyes. “In this family, we are going to learn the difference between stopping something and making it illegal.”
