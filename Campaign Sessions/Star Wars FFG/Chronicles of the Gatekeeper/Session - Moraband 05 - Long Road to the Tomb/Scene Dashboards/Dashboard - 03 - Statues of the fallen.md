## GM fast scan
- The route passes between colossal statues of Sith rulers. Most have fallen in the same direction, as though bowing toward a distant structure. One remains upright. Its face resembles whichever party member is carrying the holocron—not perfectly, but enough to disturb everyone.
- Warde says, “It did not look like that yesterday.” This is the first unsolicited admission that his certainty fails.
- The path divides. The obvious route passes under the standing statue, where unstable stone threatens collapse. A longer route crosses exposed ground visible to pursuers. A third route can be made by toppling the statue deliberately, which risks awakening a dark relic chamber beneath it.
- Let the party solve the crossing. During the crisis, Warde’s cane snaps or is lost. He forgets to stoop afterward. If someone falls, he uses the Force openly to save them before remembering his disguise. He does not apologize. He asks, “Would you have preferred the consistency of my lie?”
- This is the cleanest point for an early identity confrontation. If the party names him, he can admit only: “The man you seek and the man guiding you share responsibility.” He still leads them onward because he believes the final scene must occur at the palace.
