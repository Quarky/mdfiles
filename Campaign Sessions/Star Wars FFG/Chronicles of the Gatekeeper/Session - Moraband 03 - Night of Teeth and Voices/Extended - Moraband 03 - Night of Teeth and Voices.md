# Session 3 — Night of Teeth and Voices: Full GM Walkthrough

**Campaign:** Chronicles of the Gatekeeper  
**System:** Star Wars FFG / Force and Destiny  
**Canonical status:** Repaired extended-session version, 2026-10-03  
**Repair type:** Preserves the strong v2.2.1 runtime walkthrough while adding unique world-motion, map-use, failure-forward, and carry-forward guidance. Shared boilerplate from the old scene toolkits is intentionally not duplicated here.

## Canonical runtime additions — world motion, consequences, and map handling

### What is happening without the PCs
Night changes the ecology. Predators move closer to warmth, wind makes voices difficult to locate, and the amphitheater begins producing stronger echoes as temperature falls. If the party makes a bright, noisy camp, threats find them sooner but they also gain visibility and morale. A hidden camp is safer from distant observers but gives local creatures more opportunities to approach unseen.

### Run-time map notes
Make camp placement a real choice. Give the players terrain, shelter, sightlines, and defensible options. The beasts of shadow should have an approach and objective rather than spawning beside tokens. The amphitheater should remain usable for exploration and roleplay even if the party avoided or quickly defeated the night threat.

### Failure forward
A poor camp or survival result changes the opening state of the encounter—fatigue, lost supplies, poor sightlines, or divided watches—but does not skip the night. Failed interpretation of the amphitheater still reveals a relevant echo while making it harder to distinguish history from manipulation.

### Carry-forward emphasis
Record which character trusted a vision, who protected whom during the night, and whether the party treats Moraband's manifestations as information, temptation, or both.

## Opening — Bonewind Canyon

Resume at the canyon entrance. The route is not merely difficult ground; it is a place that turns speech into accusation. Wind passes through thousands of round holes in the stone, producing tones like distant voices.

> The canyon walls rise until the red sky becomes a narrow wound overhead. Wind moves through the rock in long, breathy notes. At first the sound resembles mourning. Then one voice separates from the rest and speaks a name belonging to someone in the party.

Ask who answers. If nobody does, the canyon uses a phrase from the previous session. If someone answers, a second voice replies from farther ahead in that character’s own voice.

The party needs shelter before full darkness. Offer an upper ledge that is defensible but hard to reach, a ruin with intact walls but only one exit, and a low basin with water-stained stone but many approaches. Survival identifies tradeoffs; it does not reveal a correct answer.

During the passage, use one physical event and one personal event. Physically, a dust shelf collapses, an animal or companion panics, or a cable catches on carved stone. Personally, two characters hear incompatible versions of the same promise. If the party argues, the canyon repeats only the cruelest sentence.

## Making camp matter

Do not skip to an attack. Let players establish watches, light, perimeter devices, shelter, food, medical care, and conversation. Each preparation should matter later. Bright light keeps beasts at longer range but makes the camp visible. A tight perimeter prevents isolation but makes retreat difficult. Force meditation can steady the group, but Moraband’s automatic dark-side result makes the comfort feel dangerously easy.

Give one companion or rival a quiet scene. They ask a character whether the familiar face at the oasis was real enough to matter. Their goal is not exposition; they need permission to admit what they saw. If treated with patience, they warn the party before the ambush. If dismissed, they conceal fear and become the beasts’ first target.

Allow a brief period that almost feels safe. The fire burns normally. Food tastes ordinary. Then all ambient sound stops except breathing.

## Beasts of shadow

Begin with eyes beyond the light, not initiative.

> Something moves where the firelight ends. It has the gait of a hunting animal, but darkness clings to it like wet cloth. Teeth show first, then red eyes. A howl rises behind you. Another answers from the canyon wall. By the time you count the eyes, the camp is already surrounded.

Use the published phantasmal beasts, scaled to the party’s condition. If the party prepared well, they detect the approach and begin at medium range. Otherwise the first shapes are already at short range. The beasts seek fear and separation, not food. They Ensnare mobile characters, pressure the weakest position, and ignore intimidation.

Make the camp setup mechanically visible. Sensors remove Setback or expose a flanking group. Elevated positions protect one watch but require a dangerous descent to aid others. Tethered gear prevents loss when darkness surges. A protected fire can be used as a rally point.

At the end of the first round, blackness rises around everyone’s ankles. At the end of the second, the canyon voices begin issuing false tactical calls in the PCs’ voices. Ask players how their characters verify one another. A shared code phrase, physical signal, or trust developed earlier should work.

The beasts do not leave bodies. When the last is defeated or driven away, darkness withdraws and the camp contains only claw marks, scattered gear, and one object moved into a deliberate position. That object points toward an amphitheater cut into the canyon wall.

## After the fight

Give time for triage. A rival may demand immediate movement, arguing that the beasts will return. The gatekeeper recommends waiting long enough to distinguish panic from strategy. If characters used the dark side freely, they feel strong and unusually rested; tell them this plainly so the temptation has practical appeal.

Someone finds a line carved into the underside of a camp stone: THE FUTURE OBEYS THE STRONG. It is ancient, but dust inside the letters is fresh.

If the group refuses the amphitheater, the voices begin reciting private fears as they travel. If they investigate, move into the strongest spiritual trial before Warde.

## Echoes in the amphitheater

The amphitheater is open to the sky, its stone seats filled with eroded figures that resemble an audience. Extinguish or diminish ordinary light as the party reaches the center.

> The glow rods fade without going dark. Shadows detach from the seated statues and descend the steps. Robed figures rise from the soil around you, their faces empty beneath their cowls. Their voices overlap until the words become one declaration: “We are the Sith, and this is our world.”

Call for the published upgraded Hard fear check. Then pause combat long enough for the phantasms to speak. Address the character with the lowest Morality first. They know the group’s compromises because the vergence has been feeding on them.

Use specific enticements:

- To a protector: “You failed only because you lacked the authority to make obedience merciful.”
- To a seeker: “Warde’s tomb is beneath our feet. Kneel, and you will never be uncertain again.”
- To someone grieving: “The face at the oasis was an echo. We can teach you to make it flesh.”
- To someone who killed: “You have already learned that action is cleaner than doubt. Stop apologizing.”

Let every character answer. Accepting instruction or guidance carries the published Conflict but can provide a true direction or useful technique. The offer must be genuinely tempting, not a trick with no benefit.

If the party rejects them, the phantasms attack or turn the confrontation into a struggle of will if the party is badly injured. They pass through stone, reshape into personal fears, and use Willpower-based combat. Strong light-side declarations, protection of a fallen enemy, or refusing an easy killing blow should visibly weaken them.

## The obsidian stair

When the last phantasm dissipates, a sealed passage behind the amphitheater opens onto an obsidian stair climbing out of the canyon. The stairs are too regular to be natural and too exposed to be safe. At the top sits a small storm shelter built from fallen blocks.

As the party climbs, red lightning gathers on the horizon. Each flash shows an elderly man standing at the top. Between flashes, nobody is there.

If the group was overwhelmed, have the old man appear during the final exchange and disperse a phantasm with a quiet gesture. If they triumphed, he waits at the shelter with water warming over a small heater. Either way, do not reveal his identity.

His first line is simple:

> “You took longer than I expected. That is encouraging.”

End there, or allow only the characters’ immediate reactions. Session 4 is the full encounter with the Wanderer.

## Carry forward

Record camp preparations, injuries, promises made during the quiet scene, which voices deceived whom, who accepted Sith help, the exact words used to reject it, and whether the Wanderer intervened. Warde has observed enough through foresight to know outcomes and emotional shapes, but not private thoughts.
