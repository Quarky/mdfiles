# Session 1 — Arrival Under a Red Sky: Full GM Walkthrough

**Campaign:** Chronicles of the Gatekeeper  
**System:** Star Wars FFG / Force and Destiny  
**Canonical status:** Repaired extended-session version, 2026-10-03  
**Repair type:** Preserves the strong v2.2.1 runtime walkthrough while adding unique world-motion, map-use, failure-forward, and carry-forward guidance. Shared boilerplate from the old scene toolkits is intentionally not duplicated here.

## Canonical runtime additions — world motion, consequences, and map handling

### What is happening without the PCs
The basin is not empty between player actions. Wind steadily fills fresh tracks; a distant engine note can be heard before the pursuers are visible; scavenging creatures retreat from both the party and the incoming craft. If Onrein Hasar or another rival force is present, they are also choosing landing cover, reading the terrain, and deciding whether the party looks weak enough to pressure immediately. Moraband itself is not “sending” enemies, but the environment makes every group feel observed and exposed.

### Run-time map notes
Use the landing basin to establish vertical cover, broken walls, and several defensible approaches rather than a single battle line. The perimeter ruins should feel like a place where the party can negotiate, hide, flank, investigate, or retreat. Keep the ship visible only on the intended landing scene. The first rival contact should have an approach vector the players can notice and react to.

### Failure forward
A failed landing/search/social check should cost time, position, strain, cover, or information. It should not prevent the party from beginning the Moraband search. If rivals gain the advantage, they may take the better route, learn the party's goal, or force a bargain rather than simply producing an unavoidable fight.

### Carry-forward emphasis
Record who saw the party land, whether the ship is concealed, whether a rival group left alive with information, and whether the party openly used the Force. Those facts should affect later pursuit pressure.

## What this session is

This is the playable story, not a preparation outline. Begin aboard the ship after Delriss and the Shard Moon, carry every injury and strained relationship forward, and end only after the landing party has dealt with the people who followed it to Moraband. The characters should finish the session with a route into the wastes, a clear opinion about the pursuers, and the first evidence that Moraband reacts to fear and certainty.

Use the material under `Prep` only when you need a clock, map zone, adversary reference, or alternate complication. The story below is the default flow.

## Opening scene — forty-eight hours from certainty

Open in the ship’s common area before Moraband appears. Do not begin with a planet description. Begin with the cost of reaching it: repair tools left out after the Shard Moon, a companion sleeping in restraints or under observation, dried blood in a deck seam, the holocron resting where nobody quite trusts it.

Ask each player one concrete question: What has your character repaired, hidden, promised, or avoided since the last crisis? Let the answers become visible aboard ship. Then bring the gatekeeper online. It studies the old coordinates and says:

> “These coordinates are accurate to the hand that recorded them, but not to the years since. Moraband was forbidden because certainty there is dangerous. I can advise you. I cannot make this decision harmless.”

The final jump uses the old coordinates. Call for the published Hard Astrogation check. Before the roll, make the stakes explicit: success means an approximately forty-eight-hour passage and a controlled arrival; failure means another twelve hours of pressure on supplies, injuries, and pursuer distance. A severe negative result brings the ship near a gravity shadow, causing the published system strain and Critical Hit. Do not turn failure into “try again.” Play the additional hours.

During the passage, run two short conversations:

1. The gatekeeper asks why each character still wants Warde’s final crystal now that they know what his legacy has cost. It does not judge the answers, but it remembers exact phrases.
2. A companion, rival, or recovering crewmember asks what happens if Warde is alive. If nobody else is aboard, let the ship’s diagnostic system pose the practical version: extraction capacity, prisoner restraints, and medical reserves are insufficient for every possible survivor.

If the characters speak confidently about destiny, the holocron flickers on a sentence that nobody commanded it to produce: “Confidence is not evidence.” The gatekeeper denies saying it.

## Arrival in the Horuset system

When the ship leaves hyperspace, give the table a moment of ordinary procedure—alarms clear, engines settle, stars regain their fixed positions. Then reveal the system: an asteroid field, seven moons, and a single dark planet beneath the weak light of Horuset.

Let the party scan before descending. Technology can identify broad ruins, terrain, storms, and the faint possibility of a small metallic object on the surface, but it cannot label Warde’s tomb. Force powers can provide a direction, but every Force roll on Moraband gains the automatic dark-side result from the planetary vergence. Describe the temptation before asking whether the character uses it: power arrives quickly, warmly, and with the feeling that hesitation was always weakness.

Read or paraphrase:

> Horuset’s light does not vanish so much as lose the strength to follow you. Beneath the ship, red deserts and black ridges stretch through permanent dusk. Broken statues lean out of the sand like witnesses trying to rise. Ruins appear on the scopes faster than the computer can classify them, each large enough to have been a fortress, a temple, or a tomb. A crimson haze lies across the world like blood suspended in the air.

The gatekeeper becomes unusually quiet. If asked, it answers: “I have no memory of this place. I have only Warde’s reasons for fearing it.” That distinction matters later.

## Choosing the landing basin

Present three credible landing areas rather than one mandatory map:

- A sheltered basin among collapsed walls: safest for the ship, poor visibility beyond the ruins.
- A high stone shelf: excellent scanners and a fast departure, but exposed to storms and pursuers.
- A dry channel near a field of statues: easier travel inland, uncertain ground beneath the landing struts.

Let the players choose. Their choice changes description and later leverage, not whether the adventure continues. A successful Piloting or Survival check secures a strong landing position. Failure means the ship lands safely but gains a problem: one blocked departure vector, dust in an intake, a damaged strut, or no clear line of fire. Advantage can identify a defensible camp or a route marker. Threat means the landing is visible from farther away. Triumph detects the distant metallic echo of Warde’s Delta-7B. Despair allows the pursuing ship to triangulate the landing immediately.

Once the ramp opens, describe silence first:

> Red dust slides across the ramp without wind. The air is cold enough to tighten the lungs and dry enough to make every breath feel borrowed. Nothing moves among the ruins. Then, from somewhere beyond sight, stone scrapes against stone—as if something enormous has shifted to look at you.

Give everyone time to act. Masks, perimeter sensors, animal reactions, scouting, and securing the ship all matter. The first clue is free: tracks cross the basin, but some are ancient and some have edges too sharp to have survived years. Checks determine what else the group learns, not whether it can leave the landing site.

## The perimeter ruins

Run ten to twenty minutes of investigation before the pursuers arrive. Give the party three points of interest:

1. A toppled guardian statue whose face was deliberately removed. Beneath it is a narrow hollow containing a corroded travel token from a pre-Rule-of-Two Sith pilgrim. It is not powerful, but touching it produces the certainty that the palace lies east—even though the physical evidence points north.
2. A campsite that appears recent until examined. Ash lies in a fire pit, a blanket remains folded, and a cup contains a thin film of water. No footprints lead in or out. This is Moraband constructing the idea of safety from the party’s expectations.
3. A ridge overlooking the ship. Fresh scoring on the stone marks where a probe or rifle rest was placed minutes ago. The pursuers have already observed the landing if the Pursuit clock is high; otherwise this belongs to an automated scout.

Allow character conversation while they investigate. The gatekeeper recommends conserving energy and finding a tomb complex. It reasons that a mortally wounded Warde would seek a place prepared for death. A skeptical character can point out that this assumes Warde remained wounded and wanted to die. The gatekeeper pauses before admitting, “That is an inference. I should not have stated it as memory.” This is the first explicit lesson in revising certainty.

## Engines over the basin

Do not have the rival party teleport onto a battle map. Foreshadow them. Sensors chirp. Dust trembles in shallow bowls. A distant engine note circles once, disappears behind the ridge, and returns louder. Give the PCs one round of unstructured preparation: concealment, weapons, a message, defensive positions, or an attempt to lift off.

Then describe the arrival:

> A ship cuts across the red sky and circles low enough to throw grit against your hull. It lands beyond the broken statues, where its engines turn the basin into a moving wall of crimson dust. A pressure door opens. Figures emerge as silhouettes first. Then a familiar voice crosses the distance: “You have led me a very long way. Do not make me regret allowing you to arrive first.”

Use the campaign’s surviving opposition. Onrein Hasar is preferred if she learned of the quest; otherwise use Nex Pavros, surviving bounty hunters, or an established enemy. Do not invent a disconnected villain merely to fill the encounter.

### If Onrein Hasar leads them

Hasar wants proof that Warde is dead—or the right to end him herself. She is controlled, exhausted, and furious that the PCs may turn Warde’s teachings into reverence. Her opening offer is practical:

> “Guide me to him. I will keep my people from your backs, and when we find what remains, you may take the device. I take Warde. If he lives, I decide whether he continues.”

If asked why, she answers with a specific wound from prior campaign events rather than a speech about revenge. If the PCs accuse her of using them, she does not deny it: “You were going whether I followed or not. I chose not to waste the trail.”

She accepts an alliance only if it includes a concrete division of authority. She refuses to surrender weapons. Compassion toward one of her injured people surprises her and lowers her hostility. Threats confirm her belief that Warde’s power corrupts everyone who seeks it.

### If bounty hunters or another enemy leads them

Their goal is capture, payment, or revenge, not ritual suicide. Their leader demands the holocron and coordinates. A hunter who has survived a previous encounter may be personally angry, but the rest still care about getting off Moraband alive. Let that disagreement show:

> “The contract says breathing.”
>
> “The contract was written before they buried two of ours.”
>
> “Then collect your grief after we leave this planet.”

That fracture gives the PCs something to exploit socially.

## Negotiation, hunt, or battle

Let the players define the encounter. Negotiation can produce an armed truce, exchange of supplies, a prisoner arrangement, or a promise to settle Warde’s fate later. Deception can send the rivals toward a false signal, but the lie will return when the rival discovers it. Stealth can turn the ruins into a hunt, with each side unsure whether the shadows belong to an enemy or the planet. Combat should move among broken walls, dunes, and the two ships rather than remain a static firing line.

After the first strong emotional outburst—rage, terror, certainty, or cruelty—Moraband responds. Shadows point the wrong way. One combatant fires at a familiar figure that is not present. Someone hears their own voice give an order they never spoke. Do not make this random damage. Make it an invitation to mistrust perception.

If the party gains the upper hand, the opponents attempt withdrawal, surrender, or a bargain. They do not fight to the last person unless an established character truly would. If the PCs are losing, the ruins can split the forces with a dust collapse, giving both sides a reason to disengage. Failure changes who holds supplies, prisoners, or the best route; it does not end the campaign.

## The session’s closing movement

End after the conflict has produced a durable relationship. The pursuers are now allies of convenience, active enemies behind the party, prisoners who must be sustained, or wounded survivors heading into the wastes independently.

As the characters prepare to leave the landing region, the ship receives one final sensor echo: a small alloy object far beyond the basin, half buried and cold. The signal could be Warde’s fighter. At the same moment, someone looking east sees a line of distant red lightning illuminate a palace-shaped silhouette that the sensors insist is not there.

The gatekeeper says:

> “The metallic contact is evidence. The silhouette is not. We should remember the difference—even if both are true.”

Close by asking who remains with the ship, who carries the holocron, and what agreement governs the pursuers. Record those answers. Session 2 begins when the landing site drops below the horizon and turning back becomes a real decision rather than an assumption.

## If the party takes an unexpected direction

- **They leave Moraband immediately:** let them. The pursuers contest the departure or follow. The gatekeeper asks what evidence would make them return. The adventure remains available without declaring their choice invalid.
- **They destroy or abandon the holocron:** Warde can still foresee them, but the gatekeeper cannot assist. Recovering or replacing the device becomes a separate problem; do not secretly restore it.
- **They kill every pursuer:** show the moral and logistical consequences. Moraband uses familiar voices from the dead later, but do not invalidate the deaths.
- **They ally completely with Hasar:** give Hasar real competence and real agency. She is not a temporary hireling; she will challenge any attempt to redeem Warde.
- **They detect Warde’s fighter from the air and fly directly there:** move the Session 2 investigation to the crash site. The false oasis and causeway become complications on the ground route after landing.

## End-state to carry forward

Record the ship’s repair state; the chosen landing site; who stayed aboard; the status and location of every pursuer; the party’s declared reason for seeking Warde; whether anyone used the dark-side result; and the first route clue. These facts must appear in later conversations and visions rather than being reset between sessions.
