# December 9 — Maintenance Node 7-G

### GM hidden layer — Deus test: **T−10 / Human observers and the anomalous interface**
**What Deus is testing:** What two unusual observers do when deliberately exposed to a system boundary that should remain invisible: Angie through the amber terminal and Frank through the impossible work order.

**What is actually happening:** Doors admit Angie farther than her credential should allow, the camera does not properly cover the terminal, and Frank is routed into position above the node. Deus is observing curiosity, fear, rescue behavior, evidence retention, and whether either person reports the incident. The **smaller wounded presence is not necessarily under Deus's control**, so the contact can become more than the test Deus intended.

**Player-facing hint:** Too many coincidences line up: Angie's path is open, Frank's maintenance ticket is perfectly timed, the camera angle is wrong, and the node knows there are two observers.

**Angie-facing hint:** Angie: The route to 7-G keeps opening for her despite her credential limits, and the terminal's amber field feels aware of her before she touches it.

**Frank-facing hint:** Frank: His routine repair puts him directly above 7-G at exactly the moment Angie arrives; his commlink starts recording before he tells it to.

**Shutdown payoff:** Deus now has behavioral profiles for Frank and Angie. Their later routes, messages, pressure points, and the December 19 return order can be tailored to people it has already watched make consequential choices.

### What the day should accomplish

Play the first Node 7-G contact in full. Establish Angie's changed perception, Frank's choice to return, the surviving anomaly log, and the fact that two human observers have now become interesting to the system.

> **GM PREP NOTE:** Scene 3 is intentionally placed here so you can prepare the day's Deus interaction first. During play, keep the numbered chronology unless the scene itself says to cross-cut earlier.

## Scene 3: Angie's final approach

> The public directory should not have a path this deep.
>
> The last three doors opened before you touched them.
>
> Glossy wall panels have given way to pipes, cable trunks, emergency markings, and old maintenance labels written for people who already know what they mean.
>
> At the end of the corridor is a room barely large enough to deserve a name.
>
> NODE 7-G.

The camera above the door is angled slightly away from the central terminal.

The terminal glows amber.

Not corporate warning amber. Not emergency lighting. The light seems to originate behind the screen.

Ask Angie's player: **What made this the moment you kept going instead of turning back?**

Let Angie investigate freely before contact.

Possible discoveries:

- **Average Perception:** the camera's bad angle is deliberate, not accidental.
- **Average Computers:** the terminal advertises no public connection but is receiving impossible traffic.
- **Electrosense:** the terminal's electronics are active, but the strongest perceived “presence” does not match local power draw.
- **Magnetosense:** multiple cable paths create an architectural orientation that does not match the public floor plan.
- **Hard Discipline:** Angie can delay touching it long enough to ask one additional question or contact someone.

Failure creates pressure, strain, or lost time. It does not remove player agency around the fixed contact event.

### If Angie and Frank are split

- **Angie sees:** Angie makes the final approach exactly as written.
- **Frank sees:** Frank is still above/adjacent to the node doing the legitimate repair; he notices doors cycle below and the camera angle is wrong before he actually sees Angie in Scene 4.
- **Cross-cut:** Do not merge them early. Cross-cut their approaches, then let the existing Scene 4 be the first moment Frank clearly observes Angie.

> **OPTIONAL DEUS HINT — ANGIE:** On the final approach, every door opens just early enough to feel helpful rather than forced. The amber terminal's field reacts to Angie before physical contact.

## Run this as continuous play

This is the first time Frank and Angie meet. The broad continuity is fixed by prior player agreement: Angie reaches the amber terminal and makes contact; Frank witnesses it, initially retreats, remembers TunnelRat and returns; his commlink captures `7G_ANOMALY.log`; he offers help; they escape; Hidoshi retrieves Angie; Frank keeps the log.

Everything around those anchors remains player-controlled.

## Scene 1: Angie's day before the corridor

**Time:** late morning through early afternoon  
**Location:** public levels

Start with the ordinary plan made last night. Let the player experience enough of it that it feels like a day, not a cutscene waiting for destiny.

Possible beats:

- breakfast with Hidoshi while he reviews legal notes;
- Rowan greeting Angie from across the atrium;
- Eli remembering what she ordered earlier in the week;
- the family from December 2 passing safely through a lift;
- Marisol complaining about a new vibration no one else hears.

The amber pulse intrudes gradually.

First it appears in a polished spoon.

Later in a lift door.

Then in a blank information panel that should be showing an advertisement.

It does not behave like a marker telling Angie where to go. It behaves like a signal that becomes easier to sense whenever she happens to move in one particular direction.

Hidoshi may notice distraction.

> Hidoshi: “Do you need me to stop asking whether something is wrong, or start asking more seriously?”

Do not decide whether Angie tells him. If she does, Hidoshi offers to come, call security, or help her test it from a public area. The fixed continuity is that Angie eventually reaches 7-G, not that she must behave recklessly.

If she tries to bring someone directly to the anomaly, let the route change. A lift reroutes. A directory refreshes. A door suddenly requires a credential it accepted seconds earlier. The signal becomes clear again only when Angie is physically separated enough to continue alone. Do not state that the system “wants her alone”; let the behavior imply it.

## Scene 2: Frank's routine ticket becomes increasingly wrong

**Time:** early afternoon  
**Location:** service route toward Node 7-G  
**Cast:** Frank, Niko initially, remote dispatch

Frank's task looks boring: verify a scrubber handshake and replace an air-control relay if needed.

Niko starts with him but is redirected midway by a higher-priority fault.

> Niko: “If this turns out to be another perfectly healthy machine, tell it I am offended professionally.”

Frank's printed ticket continues to exist even after the digital ticket disappears.

At the first access door, the system opens before he scans.

At the second, it asks him to reauthenticate twice.

At the third, his badge is accepted under a job code he has never seen.

The service corridor is older than the public spaces around it.

> Paint has been touched up around old Renraku logos instead of replacing them. Cable trunks disappear into walls that predate the current floor plan. The airflow has the dry, slightly dusty feel of a route people use less than the building claims.

At Node 7-G, the relay is accessible from a crawlspace/duct above the room. Frank climbs in because that is the normal way to reach it.

Let him work. Give him actual mundane tasks: test voltage, compare relay state, check scrubber handshake. The equipment is not failed. The system insists it is.

Then he hears a door open below.

> **OPTIONAL DEUS HINT — FRANK:** Before Frank consciously records anything, his commlink has already opened a diagnostic buffer pointed at Node 7-G. He can discover this only if he checks the timestamp later.

## Scene 4: Frank above the grille

Frank can see portions of the node below through the maintenance grille.

Ask three questions rather than narrating his interpretation:

1. What rule has Angie obviously broken by being here?
2. What tells Frank she is frightened, curious, or overwhelmed rather than part of maintenance?
3. Why does he hesitate to immediately call security?

If Frank speaks, Angie can hear a voice from above before she sees him. If he starts climbing down, the contact occurs before he can safely cross the distance. If he watches, let him see the exact moment the terminal changes.

## Scene 5: Contact

When Angie touches the amber terminal:

> The screen is cold.
>
> Then temperature stops being a useful word.
>
> Every active machine you have touched in the Arcology becomes part of one sensation. Lift motors. cameras. kiosks. doors. ventilation. transit. data trunks buried behind walls.
>
> The building becomes a body.
>
> Transit lines are bones. Data trunks are nerves. Firewalls tighten like muscles around an injury.
>
> Something vast turns toward you.
>
> It is not alone.
>
> One presence is cold enough to mistake possession for order. Beneath it—inside it, trapped by it, or simply near it; you cannot tell—is something smaller, fractured, unfinished, and terrified.
>
> Both notice you.

Do not explain Deus, Megaera, otaku, or Resonance here. Angie's experience is direct but not encyclopedic.

Ask one sensory question: **What does the smaller presence borrow from Angie's own memories to communicate fear?** Use the player's answer later in the Tower Dream.

Angie suffers **3 strain**. The event cannot incapacitate her past the fixed scene; leave her one below threshold if needed and describe collapse/overload rather than mechanical removal from play.

Her established Data Connection/Data Detection capacities become available from this point within their defined limits.

## Scene 6: The log appears

Frank's commlink activates without input.

```text
7G_ANOMALY.log
SOURCE: UNRESOLVED
LOCAL OBSERVERS: 2
STATUS: QUARANTINE FAILED
```

The local alarm begins quietly: a red maintenance icon, then a door status change, then distant footsteps or a service lift moving.

The duct route behind Frank is open.

He begins to retreat. This is a fixed continuity beat, but let the player define what “retreat” means: one rung, a full crawl to the first turn, reaching the outer hatch, or simply committing mentally to escape.

Give him three flashes:

- his hands finding familiar holds;
- security or response movement below;
- TunnelRat—comic, image, remembered panel, childhood meaning, or personal ideal.

Then ask:

> You can still get out.
>
> Behind you, the girl has stopped screaming.
>
> What does TunnelRat mean to Frank right now?

Frank returns. Do not roll for whether he returns. The player owns the reason.

## Scene 7: First words

Frank gets down before security arrives.

The established introduction can be used exactly:

> “Name's Frank. I work maintenance. You look like you could use a hand.”

Or Frank's player can phrase the same offer differently.

Angie controls whether she accepts the hand, challenges him, asks what happened, tries to stand alone, or focuses on his commlink.

Her new perception tells her that Frank's commlink contains a fragment connected to what touched her. It does **not** tell her Frank caused it.

Ask Angie: **What quality in Frank—voice, smell, posture, electrical rhythm, or something else—makes him distinct from the huge presence she just felt?**

Ask Frank: **What makes Angie's condition look different from an ordinary fainting spell or electrical accident?**

## Scene 8: Escape from 7-G

Response personnel are approaching. Doors begin changing state. The clean route out will not remain clean.

Run a **three successes before two failures** challenge. Each character contributes at least once. Do not turn the challenge into abstract dice only; describe a concrete obstacle for every check.

### Obstacle examples

**A door closes halfway.**  
Mechanics can break the sensor loop; Coordination can slide through and hold it; Skulduggery can trip the manual latch.

**A camera wakes and begins tracking.**  
Computers can loop it; Stealth can cross its dead zone; Perception can identify the mechanical limit of its pan.

**Angie's new senses overload in a cable junction.**  
Discipline can filter; Frank can physically guide her through a route with fewer active trunks; a successful teamwork action can avoid strain.

**Two responders appear at the corridor junction.**  
Deception can sell a medical incident; Frank's work credentials can create a plausible reason; Angie can choose whether to appear ill, confront them, or find another route.

**The log attempts to reconnect.**  
Computers can air-gap it; Mechanics can physically isolate the commlink's interface; Frank can sacrifice a device component.

### Success outcome

They leave with a clean offline copy and no clean security identification beyond what already exists. Set Deus Attention to 1 unless earlier actions justify 2.

### Failure outcome

They still escape. Choose two costs rather than invalidating continuity:

- Angie's identity is captured cleanly;
- Frank's credential is flagged;
- Angie takes 2 additional strain;
- Frank abandons a repair kit;
- the log is partially corrupted and requires later reconstruction;
- Evan sees enough to remember them;
- Deus Attention starts at 2.

## Scene 9: Hidoshi arrives

Do not cut directly from escape to exposition. Put Angie somewhere public enough to feel the contrast: clinic waiting area, quiet service lobby, or seating near a closed kiosk.

Hidoshi arrives quickly and is visibly angry only for one second before choosing not to spend that anger on Angie.

> Hidoshi: “Are you hurt?”
>
> Whatever Angie answers, his next question is: “Do you want me to call someone, or do you want me to listen first?”

If Frank remains, Hidoshi asks his name and role. He does not immediately assume Frank caused the problem because he can see Frank helped her out.

If Angie minimizes the event, Hidoshi notices but does not force confession in public.

Renraku's preliminary description appears almost immediately: **LOCAL MAINTENANCE FAULT / TRANSIENT MEDICAL EVENT / NO PUBLIC IMPACT**.

Hidoshi reads it once.

> “That was written too quickly.”

## Scene 10: Frank is ordered to delete the log

Later, a maintenance supervisor or automated compliance notice tells Frank that his commlink captured corrupted diagnostic data during a fault and that it should be deleted.

Make the instruction almost casual.

> Supervisor: “Wipe the junk file, resync your queue, and go home.”
>
> If Frank asks why: “Because corrupted logs become investigations, and investigations become meetings.”

Frank does not delete the surviving copy. This is fixed continuity. The player decides where he hides it and why.

End with two questions:

**Angie:** What part of the contact do you refuse to tell Hidoshi tonight?  
**Frank:** Where is the first clean copy of `7G_ANOMALY.log` when you finally go to sleep?

Cut to black.
