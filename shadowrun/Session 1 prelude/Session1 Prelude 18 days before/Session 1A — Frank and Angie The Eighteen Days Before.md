# Session 1A — Frank and Angie: The Eighteen Days Before
## Full Living-World Scene Edition

### GM note — interpreting the pre-Shutdown tests
The boxes labeled **Deus test** are hidden GM truth for this campaign, not information the players automatically know. Most mundane infrastructure anomalies can be attributed to Deus probing SCIRE before the seizure. **Amber/Resonance phenomena are deliberately less certain:** the wounded secondary presence can interfere with, distort, or exploit a test Deus initiated. Do not make every strange event a clean message from Deus; the uncertainty is part of the mystery.

**Campaign:** Shadowrun Genesys — Renraku Arcology: Shutdown  
**Period:** December 1–19, 2059  
**Use:** GM-facing playable prelude immediately before the established Shutdown session  
**Continuity anchor:** Frank and Angie first meet in Maintenance Node 7-G on December 9. The Shutdown begins December 19 at 19:02:09 PST.

## How this version is different

This is not a day-by-day synopsis. Each date below is written as material that can actually be run at the table. The GM gets a place to put the characters, what the location looks and sounds like, what unrelated people are doing, what NPCs want, lines of dialogue that can be used verbatim or adapted, what the anomaly is doing in the background, and how the world reacts to common player choices.

Do **not** narrate decisions for Frank or Angie. The GM establishes the situation and plays the world. The players decide whether they investigate, ignore, argue, help, leave, lie, call someone, or take a different route. The only fixed actions are the already agreed continuity anchors on December 9 and the established return to Node 7-G on December 19.

### Recurring NPC voices

**Hidoshi Tarada — Angie's father.** Precise, affectionate, never cloying. He worries by asking practical questions rather than ordering Angie around.  
*Typical line:* “I am not asking you to explain the impossible. I am asking whether you want to face it alone.”

**Niko Adebayo — electrician and maintenance coworker.** Dry humor, competent, hates paperwork, notices when equipment behaves too neatly.  
*Typical line:* “Machines fail messily. Paperwork fails neatly. This is doing both.”

**Eli Mercer — food-kiosk worker.** Remembers faces and preferences, talks to lonely people without making a production of it.  
*Typical line:* “You do not have to buy anything. Standing somewhere warm is still free.”

**Marisol Vance — resident and habitual pattern-noticer.** Sharp, observant, frequently dismissed as a complainer.  
*Typical line:* “Normal is what people call a noise after they stop listening to it.”

**Mina Ortega — clinic nurse.** Calm, clinical, humane. Refuses to claim certainty she does not have.  
*Typical line:* “I can tell you what this is not. Anyone promising more than that is selling comfort.”

**Evan Cho — junior Renraku security officer.** Earnest and procedural. Still believes rules are supposed to protect people.  
*Typical line:* “A valid order is not automatically a good order. It is still an order I have to explain ignoring.”

**Rowan Vale — licensed busker.** Wry, socially perceptive, delighted by sincere performance inside a monetized environment.  
*Typical line:* “Corporate acoustics make everyone sound expensive. You sound alive.”

**Sana Mori — small retail shop manager.** Knows regulars, employees, delivery rhythms, and what has subtly changed in the public floors.  
*Typical line:* “I do not know what the system thinks is happening. I know what came through my door.”

### Living-world rule

In every public scene, keep three layers moving even if the players ignore the anomaly:

1. **Someone working:** a cleaner changing filters, a courier arguing with routing, a clerk closing a register, a technician kneeling beside a panel.
2. **Someone living:** children racing projected fish, a couple choosing dinner, residents carrying laundry, teenagers taking pictures, an elderly man feeding decorative birds that are actually AR.
3. **A system organizing both:** doors opening before people reach them, price signs changing, a lift re-routing, a drone waiting politely, announcements translating themselves to match nearby listeners.

The Arcology must first feel like a place people chose to live, shop, work, and spend time. That is what makes the Shutdown a loss instead of merely a dungeon door closing.
