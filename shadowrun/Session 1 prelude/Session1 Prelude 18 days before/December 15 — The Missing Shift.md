# December 15 — The Missing Shift

### GM hidden layer — Deus test: **T−4 / Make a person disappear administratively**
**What Deus is testing:** Whether workers can be removed from normal circulation before coworkers, payroll systems, security, or families produce a response that cannot be contained.

**What is actually happening:** Shifts are reassigned, names vanish from rosters, access records are rewritten, and replacements appear with plausible paperwork. The experiment is social as much as technical: how long before someone insists the missing person matters?

**Player-facing hint:** An absent worker's schedule looks as though they were never assigned; three names disappear from a roster; coworkers remember people whom the live system insists belong elsewhere.

**Angie-facing hint:** Angie: A familiar worker is gone; the substitute smells frightened and freshly clinical while insisting they have always held the job.

**Frank-facing hint:** Frank: Three names disappear from his roster while he is looking at it, but an older offline copy still contains them.

**Shutdown payoff:** Mass capture becomes easier when records can be made to follow the new reality. Prisoners, experimental subjects, and Banded personnel can disappear into the building before anyone knows where to search.

### What the day should accomplish

Move the threat from corrupted records to missing people. Make absence personal enough that the players begin preserving names and relationships.

> **GM PREP NOTE:** Scene 3 is intentionally placed here so you can prepare the day's Deus interaction first. During play, keep the numbered chronology unless the scene itself says to cross-cut earlier.

## Scene 3: Frank and Angie compare what “missing” means

If they communicate today, let them realize the pattern extends beyond data corruption.

Angie has seen a replaced public worker. Frank has names erased from maintenance. Sana has paper. Families have memories. Niko has old job habits. The system can alter the official story faster than it can erase every human relationship.

A useful Hidoshi line if brought in:

> “Then we stop treating this as a records problem. Records do not have families.”

Do not decide the fate of every missing person now. Preserve the names. Later one can appear among captives, Greens, Banded, or Resistance contacts depending on campaign need.

### If Angie and Frank are split

- **Angie sees:** Angie sees replacement and emotional/clinical residue.
- **Frank sees:** Frank sees names erased from the roster.
- **Cross-cut:** If they do not communicate, both independently conclude that 'missing' now means people, not just data.

## Scene 1: Someone is absent in a way that feels edited

**Time:** 08:45  
**Location:** breakfast concourse and nearby retail strip  
**Cast:** Angie, Sana, substitute clerk, commuters

Start with routine repetition. Angie has now been in the Arcology long enough to recognize who is usually where.

> The breakfast rush looks almost identical to yesterday. Same queue at Eli's. Same school group cutting diagonally across commuter traffic. Same woman at the flower stand misting leaves before opening.
>
> Except Sana's neighboring shop has someone else behind the counter.

The missing worker can be **Mara Kline**, a regular clerk Angie has seen several times, or another established face from previous scenes. The substitute, Devon, says he has always worked this rotation.

Sana hears that and looks over sharply.

> Sana: “No, you have not.”
>
> Devon: “I am on the roster.”
>
> Sana: “That was not the question.”

Devon is not necessarily malicious. Angie's scent can reveal one of two useful states depending on the tone you want:

- **afraid substitute:** elevated stress, antiseptic, recent clinic smell, trying not to say something;
- **emotionally flat substitute:** strong antiseptic/implant-clinic odors and unusually muted affect, suggesting conditioning, medication, or exhaustion without proving which.

Do not use scent as automatic lie detection.

If Angie asks where Mara is, the staff system claims **NO EMPLOYEE BY THAT NAME ASSIGNED TO THIS LOCATION**.

Sana quietly pulls an old printed shift board from a drawer.

Mara's name is on it.

> Sana: “Paper remains unfashionable and useful.”

If Angie pushes Devon too hard, he becomes defensive or frightened. If she treats him as another person caught in the system, he may whisper that he was told not to discuss “transfers.”

A social check may receive up to two Boost dice if Angie explicitly uses relevant scent cues.

> **OPTIONAL DEUS HINT — ANGIE:** The substitute worker smells frightened and freshly clinical while sincerely insisting they have always held the job. The clue should complicate rather than solve the situation.

## Scene 2: Frank's roster loses three people

**Time:** 10:30  
**Location:** maintenance dispatch  
**Cast:** Frank, Niko, dispatcher Len

Three names are missing from today's roster. Frank has worked with at least one of them.

Niko notices only because a job has been redistributed badly.

> Niko: “Rafi always takes coolant loops on fifteen.”
>
> Dispatcher Len: “Who?”
>
> Niko: “Rafi Mendez.”
>
> Len checks the roster.
>
> “No Mendez assigned to facilities.”

Archived local copies from older work orders still contain the names, but a live credential lookup returns **NEVER ASSIGNED**.

Let Frank choose how deep to dig.

**Average Computers or Streetwise:** preserve names, home floors, and prior shift data.

- Success: the names survive independently of the live system.
- Advantage: gain a **Friendly Face** in one missing worker's household.
- Threat: Corporate Attention +1.

If Frank visits a household, do not make it a conspiracy exposition scene. Make it personal.

A spouse might say:

> “He said he was working overtime.”
>
> “Then his messages stopped.”
>
> “Renraku says there is no employee record, but his boots are by the door.”

That image matters more than a technical clue.

> **OPTIONAL DEUS HINT — FRANK:** Have one roster name vanish while Frank is looking at it. An older paper note or isolated copy still contains the person.


## Optional scene — The spouse at the door

> **OPTIONAL:** A resident's credential no longer opens the apartment they shared with a missing worker. Their possessions are still inside, but the tenancy record now lists only one occupant.

Use this only if the day needs another playable beat, a stronger clue, or a moment for the quieter character. Omitting it changes no fixed continuity.
