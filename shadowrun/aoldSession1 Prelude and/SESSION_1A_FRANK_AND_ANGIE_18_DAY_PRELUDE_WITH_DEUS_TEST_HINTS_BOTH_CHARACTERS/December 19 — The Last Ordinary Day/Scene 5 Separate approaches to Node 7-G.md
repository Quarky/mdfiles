## Scene 5: Separate approaches to Node 7-G

### Angie's route

The amber pulse now has direction.

> Every nine seconds it touches the edge of Angie's perception and recedes.
>
> Cameras, frightened crowds, service carts, active doors, nearby cyberware, and ordinary human bioelectric fields all compete with it.
>
> The pulse remains steady beneath them.

Ask what Angie tells Hidoshi before she goes deeper.

If she travels openly, security congestion becomes the main obstacle. If secretly, the route is quieter but more isolated. If she asks an NPC for help, use the relationship earned over the prelude.

Examples:

- Evan can overlook one credential discrepancy but risks his job.
- Marisol can identify which service corridors changed ventilation patterns.
- Sana can provide a staff-only retail exit into a back corridor.
- Rowan can create a distraction in a public space, but this puts a civilian at risk.
- Mina can provide a medical pretext if Angie is willing to be treated as a patient in the records.

Do not make any option obviously correct.

### Frank's route

Frank's work order now includes a service path that avoids staffed checkpoints.

That is more suspicious than a locked door.

The hidden log may grow warm, wake its host device, or display a local indicator when Frank enters the correct route. Do not make it literally magical heat unless that fits established hardware; the point is autonomous activation.

Ask:

- What does Frank carry?
- Does he bring the original working copy or a decoy?
- Who knows where he is?
- Does he follow the provided route or an old Service Shortcut?

If he uses a Clean Credential, it works once and leaves a record. If he uses a Service Shortcut, it avoids logging but may be physically harder.
