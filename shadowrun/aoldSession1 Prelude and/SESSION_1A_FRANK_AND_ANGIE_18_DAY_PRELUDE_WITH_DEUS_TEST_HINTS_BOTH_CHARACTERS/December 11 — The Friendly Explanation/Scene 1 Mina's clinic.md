## Scene 1: Mina's clinic

**Time:** 10:15  
**Cast:** Angie, Hidoshi if invited, Mina Ortega, ordinary patients

> The clinic waiting area is deliberately boring. Neutral walls. Soft chairs. A cartoon fish swimming across a pediatric screen. A construction worker holding an ice pack to his hand. A child bargaining with a parent over whether a vaccination deserves two desserts.

This normality matters. Angie is not entering a secret corporate laboratory. She is visiting a nurse trying to do ordinary medicine inside a place where ordinary records are becoming unreliable.

Mina asks permission before every examination.

> Mina: “Tell me what you experienced in your own words first. I will supply medical words only when they are useful.”

She can confirm:

- no conventional toxin explains the event;
- the acute symptoms do not fit a typical seizure pattern;
- Angie's unusual biology is real and known, but this change is new;
- stress and sensory overload are physically measurable even when the cause is unclear.

If Angie describes perceiving electronics, Mina tests rather than mocks it. She powers three small devices on and off behind a screen in random order.

Do not make Angie automatically perfect at the test. She may distinguish active/inactive devices but confuse a nearby patient's cyberware with one of the test units.

> Mina, after a successful distinction: “All right. That is data.”
>
> “Data is not an explanation.”

If Angie asks for discretion:

> Mina: “I document what I can support. I do not chart theories because somebody in a suit might find them exciting.”

An **Average Charm or Cool** check can establish Mina as a discreet ally, but the scene should work without a roll if the conversation does.
