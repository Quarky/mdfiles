## Scene 3: Angie's final approach

> The public directory should not have a path this deep.
>
> The last three doors opened before you touched them.
>
> Glossy wall panels have given way to pipes, cable trunks, emergency markings, and old maintenance labels written for people who already know what they mean.
>
> At the end of the corridor is a room barely large enough to deserve a name.
>
> NODE 7-G.

The camera above the door is angled slightly away from the central terminal.

The terminal glows amber.

Not corporate warning amber. Not emergency lighting. The light seems to originate behind the screen.

Ask Angie's player: **What made this the moment you kept going instead of turning back?**

Let Angie investigate freely before contact.

Possible discoveries:

- **Average Perception:** the camera's bad angle is deliberate, not accidental.
- **Average Computers:** the terminal advertises no public connection but is receiving impossible traffic.
- **Electrosense:** the terminal's electronics are active, but the strongest perceived “presence” does not match local power draw.
- **Magnetosense:** multiple cable paths create an architectural orientation that does not match the public floor plan.
- **Hard Discipline:** Angie can delay touching it long enough to ask one additional question or contact someone.

Failure creates pressure, strain, or lost time. It does not remove player agency around the fixed contact event.
