## Scene 1: The retail gallery sells a version of you

**Time:** 14:20  
**Cast:** Angie, Rowan, Sana, shoppers

> The gallery does not advertise products. It advertises possible versions of the person walking past.
>
> Coats change color to match skin tone. Restaurants rotate dishes according to biometrics and recent purchases. A cosmetics display subtly alters the face in its mirror. Music follows a customer for three steps and fades if they do not turn toward the store.

A music kiosk plays a song linked to Angie's earliest online performances as **Missia**. It does not display her legal name. It uses the private performance identity.

The song is not simply similar. It is the correct track.

Sana, whose shop faces the kiosk, notices Angie's expression.

> Sana: “That machine once tried to sell my husband a retirement cruise the morning after we argued about money. It calls this ‘anticipatory hospitality.’”

Rowan is packing cables nearby.

> Rowan: “You know that look? That is the look people give a machine when it knows the wrong thing correctly.”

If Angie questions the kiosk, it claims “regional listening affinity.” If she asks for the data source, it provides a bland privacy page. If she walks away, the recommendation follows her to the next panel for four meters, then vanishes.

**Average Discipline:** remain composed long enough to inspect the anomaly.

On Advantage, the track contains **nine beats of absolute silence** not present in the public recording. On Triumph, Angie recognizes the silence as a **Residual Signature** even though she cannot name why.
