# Scene — Maintenance Level 7

**Foundry scene:** Session 1 — Maintenance Level 7  
**Built-in purpose:** Node 7-G, air scrubber galleries, cable trenches, and Service Room 7-Gamma.

Use this for Frank and Angie's first meeting on the morning of December 10, the local-alert escape, later investigation of `7G_ANOMALY.log`, or any encounter involving old service infrastructure. Keep the room acoustically intimate: fans, metal contraction, tools moving somewhere out of sight. The amber terminal should be visually distinct without illuminating the entire map.

Useful encounters: **The Spider's Garden**, **Echo of Tomorrow**, **Ghost Shift**, and **White-Band Message**.

# Scene — Arcology Clinic

**Foundry scene:** Session 1 — Arcology Clinic  
**Built-in purpose:** Treatment rooms, sealed wards, medicine storage, and failing life support.

Use this for Mina Ortega's rescue in Session 1 or Dr. Sora Kwan and Farah Al-Masri's power-allocation crisis in Session 2. Make the clinic feel actively inhabited: hand-written charts, patients supporting one another, curtains repurposed as blankets, and staff choosing care over clean procedure.

Useful encounters: **Oxygen Is Not Currency**, **Every Dose Has a Name**, **The Man Who Will Not Wake**, and **Heat in Cold Storage**.

# Scene — Freight and Parking

**Foundry scene:** Session 1 — Freight and Parking  
**Built-in purpose:** Cargo lanes, vehicle ramps, freight controls, and possible exterior access.

This is the recommended Session 2 finale. Its scale should contrast with the tight service scenes: cold moving air, distant loading lights, silent forklifts, and rain visible beyond armored glass. The pressure doors can represent hope, trap, or limited evacuation without changing maps.

Useful encounters: **Delivery Route Thirteen**, **Two Minutes of Daylight**, **Exit Interview**, and the Session 2 freight-relay challenge.

# Scene — Transit Concourse

**Foundry scene:** Session 1 — Transit Concourse  
**Built-in purpose:** False evacuation, crowd sorting, shutter separation, and disabled transit.

Use separate arrival doors to bring the PCs together without pretending they all began in the same place. Populate the map with luggage shelters, hand-written medical lists, automated advertisements, and departure boards that never change from DELAYED.

Useful encounters: **The Elevator Choir**, **The Badge That Opens the Wrong Doors**, **The Fire-Door Wedding**, and **The Survivor Trial**.

# Scene — Legal Offices and Service Wing

**Foundry scene:** Session 1 — Legal Offices and Service Wing  
**Built-in purpose:** Hidoshi's consultancy, corporate records, staff corridors, and the December 9 flashback.

Run this as a bright, functioning workplace first. After the Shutdown, repeat the same rooms with glass offices sealed, appointment software still calling absent clients, and confidential printers producing blank pages. Hidoshi's knowledge is contractual and social; Frank's is structural.

Useful encounters: **The Executive in Coveralls**, **The Badge That Opens the Wrong Doors**, **Ghost Shift**, and **The Silent Floor**.

# Scene — Arcology Service Junction

**Foundry scene:** General — Arcology Service Junction  
**Built-in purpose:** Modular industrial crossing, air control, maintenance access, and ambush geometry.

Use the junction whenever routes need to converge. Doors can lead to any current level, allowing the GM to insert an encounter without contradicting geography. In Session 1 it becomes the air-scrubber gallery. In Session 2 it hosts the first Greyline negotiation.

Useful encounters: **The Spider's Garden**, **The Yellow Line**, **Blue Band, Open Hands**, and **White-Band Message**.

# Scene — Residential Lockdown Floor

**Foundry scene:** General — Residential Lockdown Floor  
**Built-in purpose:** Apartments, domestic shelters, communal corridors, and classification searches.

Show how people have adapted: furniture barricades that still leave wheelchair clearance, shared cooking, watch schedules, improvised privacy, and messages for missing neighbors. The floor should contain reasons to stay as well as reasons to move.

Useful encounters: **The Fire-Door Wedding**, **The Honest Thief**, **The Silent Floor**, and **One Empty Seat**.

# Scene — Hydroponics and Aquaculture

**Foundry scene:** General — Hydroponics and Aquaculture  
**Built-in purpose:** Food production, clean-water conflict, dense cover, and environmental systems.

This can become a refuge, contested resource, or slowly failing ecosystem. Use humidity, reflected grow lights, pump vibration, insects, and the smell of wet soil to distinguish it from sterile corporate levels.

Useful encounters: **Rain in Hydroponics**, **The Honest Thief**, **Power for One Song**, and **Heat in Cold Storage**.

# Scene — Security Checkpoint

**Foundry scene:** General — Security Checkpoint  
**Built-in purpose:** Credential conflict, divided authority, controlled lanes, and standoffs.

The checkpoint works best when neither side begins ready to shoot. Give every security NPC a duty, a person behind them, and a threshold they will not cross. Cameras, shutters, scanners, and cover let a negotiation transform smoothly into infiltration or combat.

Useful encounters: **The Badge That Opens the Wrong Doors**, **The Samurai Who Received Two Orders**, **Blue Band, Open Hands**, and **The Executive in Coveralls**.

# Scene — Food Court and Atrium

**Foundry scene:** General — Food Court and Atrium  
**Built-in purpose:** December 9 slice of life, large survivor refuge, public conflict, and memory payoff.

Show this scene twice. Before the Shutdown it is busy, comfortable, commercial, and faintly intrusive. Afterward, reuse the projected koi, fountain, kiosk, performance terrace, and visitor system as hazards or survivor tools. Recognition should do the emotional work.

Useful encounters: **Delivery Route Thirteen**, **Parliament of the Food Court**, **Power for One Song**, and **The Survivor Trial**.

# Scene — Experimental Labyrinth

**Foundry scene:** General — Experimental Labyrinth  
**Built-in purpose:** Altered wayfinding, system tests, false environments, and psychological pressure.

Use this sparingly so spatial unreliability remains unsettling. The map does not need to physically change every round; signs, doors, sound, lighting, and personalized instructions can make stable geometry feel untrustworthy.

Useful encounters: **Echo of Tomorrow**, **The Vending Machine Oracle**, **Exit Interview**, and **The Man Who Will Not Wake**.

# Scene — Rooftop Infrastructure

**Foundry scene:** General — Rooftop Infrastructure  
**Built-in purpose:** Antennas, weather exposure, external signaling, and high-risk extraction.

Use wind, rain, maintenance gantries, signal dishes, and vast drops. The roof is not automatically freedom: external forces may treat anyone emerging as contaminated or hostile, and the Arcology still controls many access points.

Useful encounters: **Two Minutes of Daylight**, **The Yellow Line** adapted as chemical exhaust, **White-Band Message**, and **One Empty Seat** as a final radio contact.
