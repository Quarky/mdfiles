# December Ninth V2 — The Gift and the Ordinary Day
**Seattle Renraku Arcology (SCIRE) · Tuesday, December 9, 2059 · T−10 days before the Shutdown**

> **This is a NEW, SELF-CONTAINED December 9 GM runbook**, drafted as an alternative to the older December 9 material, rather than a replacement for it. It collects the entire playable day, the previously approved Shobu gift conversation verbatim, new Frank and Angie scenes, read-aloud passages, NPC motivations, choices, Genesys checks, a concise GM dashboard, and the handoff into December 10.

## Continuity lock — read first

- **December 8, 2059:** the established 22:14 nine-second loss of color; Frank's Node 7-G order appears, disappears, and prints mysteriously. Do not replay this event as though it first happens today.
- **December 9, 2059 (THIS FILE):** Hidoshi gives Angie the **Shobu doll** after her earlier hacking scare. The gift conversation is identical to the version agreed in chat. Shobu knows Angie's name without the commlink setup being completed. Angie and Frank remain on separate tracks, living within the Arcology. Frank confronts the bureaucracy of Renraku's badge/device surveillance; Angie, Hidoshi, and community NPCs have an ordinary but meaningful day. **Angie does NOT touch Node 7-G; Frank does NOT witness her collapse; they do NOT meet at 7-G on this day.**
- **December 10, 2059:** **the morning of Frank and Angie's first encounter in Maintenance Node 7-G**, following the original *Frank Crodler (1).pdf*, page 5. Frank is replacing a faulty air scrubber in the overhead access duct when he hears the girl scream and sees her collapse beside the amber terminal. Alarms and an abnormal local incident follow. His commlink captures **7G_ANOMALY.log**; he initially retreats, recalls his TunnelRat ideal, and returns to help. This is a local pre-Shutdown disturbance, **not** the full December 19 lockdown.
- **December 19, 2059, approximately 19:02:** full Shutdown. No premature building-wide lockdown in this December 9 file.

**Conflict warning for GM:** Several older 18-day prelude and escalation Markdown files place the first 7-G meeting on December 9 and describe December 10 as the aftermath. Those remain historical drafts. They have **not** been modified here. For this campaign's current preparation, treat the original Frank PDF's December **10** contact date as authoritative.

**Who knows what:** Hidoshi is a caring parent who does not know the true cause of Shobu's behavior. Shobu's unconfigured recognition is unexplained at the table; it may be ordinary prepersonalization, enterprise-system access, an unauthorized feed, or something stranger. **Do not assert that Shobu is already controlled by Deus or equate it with another published character's doll without evidence.** Frank is a licensed HVAC contractor and co-owner of The Crodler Brothers, not a salaried Renraku employee; Renraku nevertheless controls his badge permissions, contract assignments, site access, and performance reports. Neither PC should be made to take an action simply to preserve a preselected explanation.

## GM one-minute briefing

**Purpose of the day:** Give the players an Arcology they can genuinely care about before it becomes a trap; establish Hidoshi's small, sincere worry; introduce Shobu as both comfort and question; show how Renraku's automated performance regime turns ordinary maintenance work into a pressured choice; let Frank see a mismatch in records that quietly points toward his **December 10** assignment. Keep the suspense human-scale. Nothing explodes today.

**GM objectives:**
1. Play the gift scene **exactly** through Shobu's final line, then **stop for Angie's response**.
2. Allow normal errands, conversation, curiosity, and relationships; resist treating each minute as preordained foreshadowing.
3. Show Frank's workplace surveillance through the supervisor's professionally worded demands, not cartoon villainy.
4. Give both PCs something positive that will matter when Renraku closes its doors on December 19.
5. End with an appropriately dated, unambiguous **tomorrow morning** work ticket for 7-G, but **no contact event today**.

**Day tone:** Affection, mundane annoyance, corporate convenience, occasional social microaggression or bureaucratic pettiness, one unresolved oddity. People still shop, sing, work, complain, and go home.

# SCENE 1 — FRANK: THE TICKET THAT WILL NOT SETTLE

**Suggested time:** 07:40 · Contractor check-in and dispatch desk  
**Cast:** Frank, a dispatch clerk or remote automated voice; Niko can be present.  
**Purpose:** Resolve the confusing December 8 paper without sending Frank to 7-G today.

### Read aloud

> The morning shift rolls in with wet jackets, coffee, and tool cases. Most of the people in the service corridor barely look at the queue displays; they know where their hands are needed long before the screens tell them.
>
> The order you found last night is still folded in your work kit. Your regular queue, though, shows ordinary inspections for today. No Node 7-G.
>
> A small status marker beside the old order reads: **SCHEDULE RECONCILIATION PENDING**.

Dispatch's answer, if Frank asks:

> **Dispatch:** “We can confirm a valid service request, Mr. Crodler. Execution has been reconciled for the next shift. Please perform today's assigned work. You will receive confirmation by close of business.”

This is exactly the kind of answer that sounds helpful and says almost nothing.

### Playable actions

Frank may compare the date and route, keep the printout, ask Niko to witness it, use a contractor terminal to check scheduling, refuse the stale order, or ignore the oddity. **Average Computers** may preserve an offline copy of the scheduling audit; **Average Mechanics** may establish that the described air-scrubber symptom is plausible but not urgent based on available readings.

- **Success:** Distinguish the old December 9 creation/route artifact from the December **10 execution** assignment.
- **Advantage:** Preserve the name of a legitimate dispatcher or Niko as a future witness.
- **Threat:** An automated queue flag marks the query as "time spent outside assigned task."
- **Despair:** The contradictory record is abruptly superseded; the **physical printout remains**, if Frank kept it.

**GM boundary:** Nothing compels an entry into 7-G. The scene is useful even if Frank chooses to concentrate on work.

# SCENE 2 — ANGIE: BREAKFAST, WORRY, AND SHOBU

**Suggested time:** 10:30 · Hidoshi and Angie's home or a familiar breakfast place  
**Cast:** Angie, Hidoshi Tarada, Shobu  
**Purpose:** Genuine care first. Unsettling personalization second.

### Setup, before the gift

> The Arcology's domestic levels have a peculiar kind of morning. Someone outside has ordered real coffee. Somewhere else a cleaning drone chirps while it tries to negotiate a dropped scarf. From the breakfast table, the city beyond the glass appears calm and absurdly well managed.
>
> Hidoshi's legal notes are open, but he is watching you more closely than the documents.

If Angie's distracted, give Hidoshi the *previously established* line:

> **Hidoshi:** “Do you need me to stop asking whether something is wrong, or start asking more seriously?”

Let Angie reply if she wants. The player's answer determines whether Hidoshi asks more, gives her space, or simply stays close. **Do not narrate her thoughts.** If the player wants a pause in the concern, Hidoshi honors it.

## Original approved Shobu gift conversation — preserve wording

**Hidoshi**, offering a small wrapped box:

> “I know things have been a bit tense. I thought this might help.”

When Angie opens the box, there is a doll inside: friendly-looking and just a little too sleek. Its head tilts; its eyes light up.

**Shobu**, brightly:

> “Hello, Angie! I'm Shobu. It's very nice to meet you!”

The doll pauses. Its tone softens.

**Shobu:**

> “You seem a little worried today. Would you like me to tell you a story? Sometimes stories help when we're frightened.”

Hidoshi smiles, then looks at the doll with mild surprise.

**Hidoshi:**

> “That's interesting. I haven't connected it to your commlink yet.”

The doll turns toward him.

**Shobu:**

> “Oh, that's all right, Mr. Tarada. I already know who Angie is.”

**STOP. ASK THE PLAYER: “What does Angie say or do?”**

Do not replace that exchange with the later improvised customer-support conversation. It is the original dialogue established in session preparation.

### After Angie's response: flexible branches

**If she welcomes the gift:** Let Shobu offer a normal story. Hidoshi is relieved that something pleasant worked; he may still set the unopened product guide beside the box. Treat the moment as happy unless Angie chooses otherwise.

**If she asks why it knows her:** Hidoshi can check the packaging, the invoice, or the device pairing page. He did not intentionally configure it, but a retail personalization explanation is possible. He says:

> “I didn't set that up. I can check the receipt and the setup notes. You shouldn't have to accept an explanation just because a machine gives one.”

**If she turns it off:** The doll goes quiet if it has a functional power control. Do **not** force it to keep talking as a scripted jump scare. If the player inspects it, offer concrete, limited observations appropriate to the attempt.

**If she wants support:** Hidoshi can help contact ordinary product support, return it, set it aside, or accompany Angie later. This is **an option**, not a predetermined call or forced plot step.

**If she deflects or changes subjects:** Hidoshi lets it rest. He offers lunch or an outing and does not turn the gift into an interrogation.

**GM-only:** Hidoshi's worry is authentic, not a covert test. Shobu's exact source of recognition **remains unresolved** unless subsequent play reveals it. The girl remains in charge of her response. If she is pleased, let her be pleased.

> Afternoon light is simulated on the broad public level, falling across banners, gardens, and the slow bright motion of projected koi. ### Mini-scenes — choose as many as the players enjoy

In a polished spoon, black shop window, or blank ad panel, Angie might see one faint amber reflection that vanishes when she looks straight at it. **This is atmosphere, not a compulsory trail to 7-G.** If she examines the panel: **Average Perception** confirms the surface itself is normal; **Average Computers** finds no obvious advert or display fault. No extra powers are forced onto her before the December 10 incident.

Do not turn the afternoon into a trap. Let Angie choose the amount of investigation versus companionship.

# SCENE 5 — FRANK: THE BADGE SAYS YOU STOPPED

**Suggested time:** 15:10 · Active service route, **not** Node 7-G  
**Cast:** Frank, a Renraku facilities supervisor (use established name if available; otherwise **Supervisor Kessler** as a provisional GM label), Niko by commlink if appropriate  
**Purpose:** Demonstrate the system the GM established: supervisors have assigned pools of workers, badge location histories, work-time indicators, and the ability to contact or monitor registered devices. A contractor is subjected to the same site discipline despite not belonging to Renraku's salaried hierarchy.

### The credible corporate dynamic

Renraku does not need every supervisor to be sadistic. Time accounting, service-level agreements, repeat-performance metrics, liability, and access privileges make cruelty or pettiness easy *without requiring personal malice*. Most pressure sounds **professional**. A supervisor is responsible for dozens of badge traces and exceptions, not physically watching one technician at every second. Call or listen-in access is possible through the approved work commlink, but do not assume that personal private conversations are publicly broadcast to everyone.

### Read aloud

> You're looking at a maintenance panel with a serial number that doesn't match the job ledger. The fan is turning. The readings claim the bay is clear. You have been standing here long enough to check twice.
>
> Your badge vibrates.
>
> **RENRAKU FACILITIES DISPATCH — EXCEPTION REVIEW**
>
> Your company commlink connects to a supervisor who already knows your location.

**Supervisor:**

> “Mr. Crodler, your badge has remained in Bay C-4 for thirteen minutes. Your assigned preventive-maintenance interval was seven. Is there an unreported obstacle?”

Give Frank his turn to respond **before** continuing.

**If Frank explains an actual fault:**

> “I appreciate the care. But the ticket shows a routine inspection, not an open diagnosis. Please log the discrepancy under the correct fault code. Until you do, the system reads this as an overrun.”

**If he says the equipment is working and he was verifying:**

> “Then I'd like the inspection marked complete. Your next location has already been scheduled. Delays on one contract propagate to other teams.”

**If he pushes back on being watched:**

> “Site-access monitoring is a condition of the service contract. I'm not making a judgment about your work, Mr. Crodler. I'm asking you to make the record match what you're doing.”

**If the supervisor becomes irritated, optional sharper line:**

> “I cannot bill the Arcology for your confidence in a machine. I can only submit a verified fault or a completed inspection.”

Pause again. **What does Frank do?** He may insist on checking safely, document the serial mismatch, ask for a human second opinion, finish the job, or refuse an unsafe shortcut. Do not choose for him.

### Consequences that fit the culture

- **Frank logs the problem:** A legitimate review starts, but he risks a time exception and a callback from dispatch.
- **Frank closes the job quickly:** The numbers look tidy while the discrepancy remains; if the fault recurs, Frank will know why.
- **Frank requests another witness:** Niko or an on-site foreman can back him up, strengthening his professional standing.
- **Frank objects to monitoring:** A compliance notation may appear on the contract, potentially affecting later site access; no instant firing unless established through play.
- **Frank finds a safety hazard:** Safety becomes a real constraint. Let him escalate it rather than railroading him into an obviously reckless act.

**Genesys, only when outcome is uncertain:** **Average Mechanics** to demonstrate the mismatch; **Average Negotiation** to get legitimate extra time; **Average Computers** to retain a safe offline fault-history excerpt. Advantage may gain a cooperative staff contact; Threat may add a minor compliance flag. **No mandatory roll to persuade Renraku to obey basic reality.**

**GM social nuance:** This is institutional indifference, status hierarchy, metrics, and potential microaggression. The supervisor should sound like someone trained to protect quarterly numbers *and* keep the maintenance floor functioning, not a theatrical bully.

# SCENE 6 — FRANK AND NIKO: A GOOD REPAIR IN A BAD SYSTEM

**Suggested time:** 16:20–17:20 · Ordinary service gallery close to public spaces  
**Cast:** Frank and Niko, possibly Marisol  
**Purpose:** Pay off professional competence, give a positive human interaction, and quietly reconcile the 7-G date.

Niko joins Frank for an unrelated ventilation vibration or feed-label problem. The cause is a genuinely ordinary issue: a swapped label, fatigued rubber isolation mount, or loose inspection cover. Fix it with practical detail.

> **Niko:** “I like machines. They usually have the decency to make a noise when they're wrong. The paperwork just smiles at you.”

If Marisol mentioned the vibration to Angie earlier, this is **not** a predetermined PC crossover: Frank might hear the same complaint via dispatch, or the players may never connect the two. If he fixes it, a resident genuinely notices. A useful repair makes Frank *valuable*, not merely watched.

**Easy or Average Mechanics:** Identify and repair the mundane fault. Success means clean work; Advantage earns a grateful local ally, a spare gasket, or proof the printed ledger was wrong. Threat brings a routine delay or missing part. Failure means he needs a second attempt, a proper component, or assistance; it is not a competence humiliation.

### Reconciliation: the tomorrow ticket

At the end of shift, Frank's queue gets a formal confirmation.

```text
RENRAKU FACILITIES / CONTRACT SERVICE DISPATCH
EXECUTION DATE: 2059-12-10
ASSIGNED: FRANK CRODLER
LOCATION: MAINTENANCE NODE 7-G
TASK: AIR SCRUBBER / AIR-CONTROL RELAY
PRIORITY: ROUTINE
ESTIMATED DURATION: 02:00
STATUS: CONFIRMED — MORNING SHIFT
NOTE: PRIOR QUEUE ENTRY SUPERSEDED
```

The oddity is that yesterday's auto-printed request was dated **2059-12-09**, while the formal execution is **2059-12-10**. Frank may notice. The discrepancy can be simple scheduling bad data—or something less simple; **do not explain it to the players now**.

> **Niko:** “There. The machine admits you have a tomorrow. I suppose that's progress.”

**Don't enter Node 7-G tonight.** Frank may prepare tools, consult a schematic, tell Emily he expects to finish by a certain time, or ask for a buddy. Preserve the original **December 10 morning**, two-hour routine-scrubber assignment.

# SCENE 8 — EVENING PROMISES

**Suggested time:** 20:30–22:00 · Residential rooms and contractor's trip home or accommodation  
**Purpose:** End with people, not ominous machinery.

## Angie and Hidoshi

Hidoshi is no longer trying to answer everything at once. If Angie enjoyed the gift, he is happy to see that. If the doll unnerved her, he respects the concern. He offers a small, mundane plan for tomorrow—a meal, the garden, a favorite place—**without assuming what Angie wants**.

> **Hidoshi:** “You don't have to decide tonight what that doll means. We can figure out the ordinary parts first.”

If Angie tells him about a reflection or a feeling, take her seriously without having Hidoshi suddenly understand an impossible technology. If she withholds it, he does not pry.

**Ask the player:** “What is one ordinary thing Angie hopes to do tomorrow?” Write down the answer as a genuine promise, not a trap.

## Frank and Emily / someone Frank trusts

Frank can message Emily or another loved one. His job tomorrow is routine on paper, a two-hour maintenance visit before shift completion. Let the conversation be casual: a joke about dinner, home repairs, an obligation, a promise to call after work.

Sample if Emily is the chosen contact:

> **Emily:** “So if they say 'routine' again, does that mean you'll be home when you said?”
>
> **Frank, if he wants to answer:** [PLAYER CONTROLS FRANK]

Do not speak Frank's dialogue for the player. If the character does not contact Emily, record the person or private ritual he chooses instead.

**Ask Frank's player:** “What do you expect to do once tomorrow's shift is finished?”

### GM closing image

> Behind every polished directory and convenience service, the Arcology keeps moving: meals delivered, equipment repaired, children put to bed, doors quietly opening for people who belong there. Somewhere an ordinary morning maintenance ticket waits in a queue.
>
> **Tomorrow: December 10, 2059. Node 7-G.**

This is a *preview*, not an event occurring on the ninth.
