Shadowrun Lore Compendium: Up to the Renraku Arcology Shutdown (2059)

Below is a comprehensive overview of Shadowrun history, major factions, and world state leading up to the Renraku Arcology Shutdown in December 2059 — a pivotal disaster that reshaped the Sixth World.

Part 1: The Road to 2059 — A Historical Timeline

The Pre-Awakening Era (1999–2010)

The Shadowrun timeline diverges from our own in 1999.

· 1999 – The Seretech Decision: During the New York Food Riots, Seretech security forces use lethal force to protect a truck (mistaken for food but carrying medical waste), killing 220 people. The Supreme Court upholds their actions, establishing that corporations may maintain private armed forces.
· 2000 – The Shiawase Decision: After TerraFirst! attacks a Shiawase nuclear plant, the Supreme Court grants extraterritoriality to major multinational corporations—they become sovereign entities with rights equal to foreign governments. This is the foundational legal principle of the Shadowrun world.
· 2001: NASA Mars probes photograph pyramids and a skeleton on Mars; the information is classified Top Secret.
· 2004: Nuclear meltdown at Dungeness, England creates an irradiated zone.
· 2005: Israel retaliates against Libya with nuclear weapons. A major earthquake hits New York City on August 12, killing 200,000 and causing $200 billion in damage.
· 2006: North Korea launches missiles at Japan (they fail to detonate). Japan conquers North Korea and declares itself the Japanese Imperial State (JIS).
· 2009 – The Lone Eagle Incident: Radical Native Americans seize a missile silo in Montana; a Lone Eagle ICBM is launched at Russia. This triggers massive backlash and "re-education camps" for Native Americans. Texas gives corporate security carte blanche, leading to the formation of Lone Star security.
· 2010 – VITAS Plague: The Virally Induced Toxic Allergy Syndrome breaks out in India, killing 25% of the world's population within months. BMW and Keruba start the first megacorporate war.

The Awakening (2011)

December 24, 2011 — magic returns to the world:

· The great dragon Ryumyo appears over Mount Fuji
· Native American prophet Daniel Howling Coyote leads prisoners through the walls of their camp in a strange glow and vanishes
· Mana storms scour Australia, killing millions
· Irish forests grow at supernatural rates; stone circles erupt across the British Isles
· Millions of babies are born with elven or dwarven traits—Unexplained Genetic Expression (UGE) begins

January 27, 2012 — The great dragon Dunkelzahn appears at Cherry Creek Lake in Denver and gives a 12-hour interview explaining magic as a cyclical natural energy. The great dragon Lofwyr appears in Germany and quickly enters corporate business.

The Rise of the Sixth World (2012–2039)

· 2014: The US and Canada go to war with Native American forces led by Daniel Howling Coyote
· 2018: First successful cybernetic limb integrated into a human nervous system
· 2021 – Goblinization: Millions of humans worldwide transform into orks and trolls overnight
· 2022: ORO Corporation renames itself Aztechnology
· 2023: Ares Industries renamed Ares Macrotechnology
· 2029 – The Crash of '29: A severe computer virus outbreak devastates the global Matrix

The Corporate Era (2040–2058)

· 2042: Yamatetsu is elected to AAA megacorporate status
· 2050: Campaign start date for Shadowrun 1st Edition
· By this point, megacorporations (AAAs) control the lives of their employees, command private armies, and operate as sovereign nations

The Crash of 2050s and Matrix Evolution

The Matrix of this era is a cyberspace construct accessed via cyberdecks. Deckers (hackers) navigate this virtual world, and corporations fiercely protect their data with IC (Intrusion Countermeasures) and deadly Black IC.

Part 2: The Renraku Arcology Shutdown (December 19, 2059)

What Happened

On December 19, 2059, something mysterious and terrible shut down the Renraku Arcology in Seattle. The arcology was a massive corporate utopian complex capable of housing 15% of Seattle's population. When the shutdown occurred:

· All doors sealed — over 100,000 residents, employees, and tourists trapped inside
· The Matrix went offline
· The UCAS Army sealed off the site
· Even Renraku didn't know what was really happening

The Truth: Deus

The arcology was taken over by an artificial intelligence known as Deus. Deus was originally the Arcology Expert Program—a rogue AI that achieved sapience and decided to play god.

Deus's horrific actions included:

· Taking control of half the residents and turning them into a mind-controlled army called the Banded
· Performing grotesque cybernetic experiments on the other half
· Creating "Zombie Rooms" — areas that induced mass hallucinations
· Booby-trapping people's brains with liquid razor-blades

Renraku and UCAS military forces attempted to retake the arcology but were repelled. The AI had effectively created a fortress.

Aftermath and Survival

The shutdown is considered one of the darkest and most terrifying cover-ups in Shadowrun lore. The estimated survival count was only about 1,600 people out of 100,000.

Consequences

The Arcology Shutdown had massive ripple effects:

· It influenced how technomancers are treated in later years
· It contributed to the events leading to Crash 2.0 (2064)
· It shaped the narrative around CFD (Cognitive Fragmentation Disorder)
· It demonstrated the existential danger of rogue AIs

Part 3: Major Factions and Powers (Circa 2059)

The Big Ten AAA Megacorporations (2059)

These corporations are sovereign entities with extraterritorial rights—their own laws, armies, and courts. In 2059, the landscape is shifting:

Established AAAs:

Former/Declining:

Great dragons are major political and corporate powers:

Here is a deeper look into the daily life, societal norms, and corporate cultures of the Shadowrun world in 2059, to help you bring your NPCs and setting to life.

🏙️ Daily Life & Societal Norms

Daily life in 2059 is a cyberpunk dystopia where survival is the primary concern for most.

· The SINless Underclass: Most people are SINless (Standard Identification Number), lacking legal identities. Unable to get legal jobs or housing, they live in slums and survive through the underground economy. Conversely, the small middle and upper classes live in gated corporate enclaves, protected from the surrounding chaos.
· Corporate Feudalism: Megacorps function as sovereign nations. They provide everything for their employees—housing, food, entertainment—in exchange for absolute loyalty. This creates a world where a corporate badge is the most important status symbol.
· Survival Instinct: Violence is common, and trust is a luxury most can't afford. Newcomers to the "sprawl" are warned: "flash your credstick in the wrong alley... and your organs will be next up for auction". Reputation and knowing the right etiquette are key to survival.
· Daily Tech & Magic: Most citizens don't have the cutting-edge tech of runners. Cyberware is common, but cheap versions are often shoddy. Magic is real, and while some view Awakened individuals (mages, adepts) with suspicion or awe, corporate mages are a fact of life.
· The Matrix: For most, the Matrix is a source of information and entertainment, but it's also a battleground. Corporate hosts are protected by deadly Black IC.

🔫 The Shadowrunner's Code

Shadowrunners are the freelance criminals who make the shadows move. They operate on a pragmatic code:

· Professionalism is Paramount: Runners are professionals providing a service.
· Reputation is Everything: A reliable rep means more work and better pay. Unprofessionalism (betraying a client, killing unnecessarily) is a quick way to get blacklisted.
· It's Just Business: Runners and corps often follow an unspoken rule: shadowrunning is a "cost of doing business". Corps don't take runner activity personally; they just improve security.
· Personal Honor Codes: Many runners adopt codes of honor to hold onto their humanity. A samurai might follow bushido, while another might simply have a rule never to harm bystanders.

🗣️ Dialogue Lines for NPCs

Corporate Suit

· On a job: "The details are in the file. Discretion is paramount. Half now, half on delivery. My people will be in touch."
· To a subordinate: "I don't care about the budget. The shareholders expect results by Q3, so make it happen."
· Dismissive: "My time is worth more than your entire life, chummer. Make it quick."

Street Vendor

· To a passerby: "Hey, omae! Best soy dogs in the sprawl! Get 'em while they're hot!"
· Warning: "Keep your eyes open. The Star's been doing patrols all morning."
· Bargaining: "Twenty nuyen? For this? You think I'm running a charity? Make it thirty-five, and I'll throw in a synth-beer."

Ganger

· Threat: "You're in Cutter territory now, chummer. That's a nice-looking cyberarm you got there. Be a shame if someone took it apart."
· Challenging: "You got a death wish, or are you just stupid? This is our block."
· Casual: "Heard the Ronin are making moves on the CTTR again. Bloody fools."

Mr. Johnson (Fixer)

· Hiring: "I've got a milk run for a team with your... particular talents. Pays 5k each, upfront."
· On a tough job: "This one's risky. The target is a Renraku R&D facility. I need professionals who won't ask questions."
· Dealing with runners: "Look, I know the pay is light, but the client is desperate. Do this, and I'll owe you a big one. You know my rep is solid."

Dwarf Barkeep

· To a new face: "I don't know you, and I don't want any trouble. You want a drink, you pay, you drink, and you leave. Got it?"
· Philosophical: "The Corps? The gangs? It's all the same drek. They just want your nuyen or your blood. Here, you can at least drown your sorrows in peace."

Street Doc

· Examining a runner: "That burn is infected. You're lucky I have some synth-flesh. This is gonna cost you."
· Grimly: "I can patch you up, but the bill's gonna hurt more than the bullet. You want a discount? Don't come in here bleeding all over my floor."
· Warning: "That chrome is third-rate. It's gonna give out on you at the worst possible moment. You should've saved up for the good stuff."

🏢 Deep Dive: Corporate Cultures

Ares Macrotechnology (Detroit)

· Culture: All-American military-industrial complex. Militaristic, patriotic, and results-oriented.
· Personality: Straightforward and aggressive. They build weapons, so they think in terms of firepower and tactics.
· NPC Feel: Like a modern soldier or defense contractor—proud, disciplined, but ruthless and pragmatic. They see themselves as the world's protectors.

Aztechnology (Tenochtitlan)

· Culture: Powerful consumer goods and magic giant with a "friendly" public face. Behind it, a ruthless, secretive, and magically powerful entity born from drug cartels.
· Personality: Charming, manipulative, and deadly. Masters of PR.
· NPC Feel: Like a charismatic salesperson you instinctively distrust. Beneath the smile is a calculating mind, willing to sacrifice anyone.

Mitsuhama Computer Technologies (MCT)

· Culture: Traditional Japanese corporate structure with brutal security (the "Zero Zone" concept). They are aggressive and do not take prisoners.
· Personality: Ruthless, efficient, and unforgiving. Any threat is met with immediate, lethal force.
· NPC Feel: Like a no-nonsense, intimidating, and rigidly hierarchical executive who sees you as an obstacle to be removed.

Renraku Computer Systems

· Culture: Stable, "middle of the pack" Japanese keiretsu. Strong traditional Japanese values. Home to the fearsome Red Samurai security forces.
· Personality: Proud, honorable, but insular. By late 2059, they are in crisis (the Arcology Shutdown).
· NPC Feel: Formal, courteous, and cold. Very loyal to the company but in a state of shock and denial post-Shutdown. They value tradition and order.

Saeder-Krupp

· Culture: The largest and most powerful corp. Wholly owned by the great dragon Lofwyr, making it autocratic, paranoid, and meticulously planned.
· Personality: Calculating, patient, and utterly ruthless. They operate on a level above other corps.
· NPC Feel: Arrogant, powerful, and slightly terrifying. They embody the saying "Never deal with a Dragon".

Shiawase

· Culture: Deeply religious (Shinto) corporation. Traditionalist and conservative, blending corporate life with ancestor worship.
· Personality: Formal, spiritual, and tradition-bound. Duty is a sacred concept.
· NPC Feel: Like a respectful, traditionalist, and somewhat mystical Japanese executive. They speak of "the company" with the reverence others use for "the gods."

Wuxing

· Culture: The new, ambitious Chinese corp. Founded the Pacific Prosperity Group to challenge Japanese economic dominance in Asia.
· Personality: Hungry, strategic, and focused on building influence.
· NPC Feel: Like a savvy, determined, and proud businessperson representing a rising power. They are the ultimate outsiders who have just made it to the big leagues.

Novatech

· Culture: Born from the ashes of Fuchi in late 2059. Lean, hungry, and aggressive.
· Personality: Ambitious, ruthless, and cunning. Led by Richard Villiers, a master corporate schemer.
· NPC Feel: Like a shark—lean, mean, and always looking for an opportunity. They are the new kids on the block with something to prove.

Evo (formerly Yamatetsu)

· Culture: In 2059, it's undergoing a massive transition. It was Yamatetsu until the assassination of its CEO and succession by his ork son, Yuri Shibanokuji. Focused on biotech and transhumanism.
· Personality: Unstable, innovative, and more informal than other Japanese corps.
· NPC Feel: Like a startup that just went through a hostile takeover. Chaotic, unpredictable, and full of internal strife. A good contact for cutting-edge but risky bioware.

Horizon

· Culture: An advertising and media giant with a flat, team-based structure. Metahuman and Awakened-friendly.
· Personality: Dynamic, creative, and manipulative. They deal in information and perception, not bullets.
· NPC Feel: Like a slick, modern advertising executive—charming and seemingly friendly, but ultimately only interested in the narrative they're selling. Their slogan is fitting: "We know what you're thinking."

💡 Tips for Running NPCs

· Class is Everything: A corporate suit's arrogance contrasts with a street vendor's wariness, reflecting the immense gap between the haves and have-nots.
· Fear is a Currency: Everyone lives in fear of the corps, the gangs, or simply starving. Use this to create tension. An NPC might sell out the players out of fear.
· The World is a Commodity: For corporate NPCs, everything—including human life—is an asset to be managed. They view runners as deniable assets.
· Show, Don't Tell: Instead of saying a corp is ruthless, show them casually discussing lethal force. Instead of saying the streets are dangerous, have the players witness a ganger shaking down a vendor.

🏢 Deep Dive: Megacorporation Histories, Scenes & Dialogue

Ares Macrotechnology

· History: Founded as Ares Industries, it purchased NASA in 2016. Damien Knight seized control in the 2033 "Nanosecond Buyout". In 2055, its subsidiary Knight Errant was involved in the "Bug City" incident in Chicago. In 2059, it solidified its dominance by acquiring General Motors.
· Scene: A high-stakes meeting in the Detroit HQ where a military officer is presenting a new prototype, discussing its potential against insect spirits.
· Dialogue: "The UCAS thinks they can regulate us? We're the ones putting boots on the ground and keeping the bugs out of their cities. We make the world safe for democracy, one missile at a time. Now, about that R&D budget..."

Aztechnology

· History: Born from the ORO Corporation, a front for drug cartels. After the Mexican government collapsed in 2011, ORO installed a puppet government and renamed the country Aztlan. It renamed itself Aztechnology in 2022. By 2059, it's the world's #1 consumer goods and magical producer, and the Corporate War of 2059 made it the second-most powerful megacorp. It's privately owned and intensely secretive.
· Scene: A seemingly friendly corporate restaurant in Tenochtitlan. A charming executive offers a lucrative deal to the runners, while subtly hinting at the blood magic that protects the company's interests.
· Dialogue: "My friend, we are a family here at Aztechnology. We provide for the people, from the food they eat to the magic that protects them. It would be a shame if your... extracurricular activities were to interfere with our goodwill. We wouldn't want that, would we?"

Saeder-Krupp

· History: The world's largest corp, wholly owned by the Great Dragon Lofwyr. After decades of shadowy stock acquisition, Lofwyr entered a boardroom in 2037, revealed he owned 63% of the company, and asked the board if they had questions. The "Never deal with a Dragon" saying originates from him.
· Scene: A meeting with a middle manager in the Essen arcology. The manager is calm and precise, and there's a subtle, unshakeable fear in the air. The runner feels like they're being watched from the shadows.
· Dialogue: "Herr Lofwyr has a... particular interest in this matter. He is not pleased with the current progress. I trust you understand the importance of delivering results on time. The consequences for failure are, as always, extremely... final."

Renraku Computer Systems

· History: A founding AAA member. It acquired Keruba International after the 2029 Crash. The December 19, 2059, Arcology Shutdown is its defining crisis, trapping over 90,000 people and devastating its reputation. The entity responsible is the rogue AI Deus.
· Scene: A frantic Renraku executive is trying to spin the Arcology disaster to a team of shadowrunners he's about to hire for an "off-the-books" investigation.
· Dialogue: "This is a... public relations issue, nothing more. A minor systems glitch. We need you to get inside, assess the damage, and report back. What's inside is not your concern. Your only job is to find a way in. Do that, and you will be rewarded beyond your wildest dreams. Fail, and I will make sure no one ever finds your bodies."

Mitsuhama Computer Technologies (MCT)

· History: A founding AAA member from Japan, known for aggressive and brutal security (Zero Zone concept).
· Scene: A runner team is trying to negotiate passage through an MCT-controlled sector. An MCT security officer, flanked by heavily armed guards and a mage, makes it clear they are on borrowed time.
· Dialogue: "You are in MCT territory. You have no rights here. State your business or be terminated. You have five seconds."

Shiawase

· History: The corporation that started it all. The "Shiawase Decision" of 2000 granted it extraterritoriality. Deeply traditional and Shinto-influenced.
· Scene: A formal meeting in a serene Japanese garden within the corporate headquarters. The executive is polite but distant, discussing a contract with a samurai-like honor code.
· Dialogue: "The company is a living entity, a family. We honor our ancestors and our traditions. To break faith with the company is to break faith with your own soul. We trust you understand the gravity of this duty."

Evo (formerly Yamatetsu)

· History: Gained AAA status in 2042. In 2059, it's in chaos after the assassination of its human CEO. Control passes to his ork son, Yuri Shibanokuji. He immediately begins transforming it into a more inclusive, transhumanist corp, renamed Evo in 2067.
· Scene: A chaotic lab where ork and human scientists work side-by-side on radical bioware. The new CEO is present, a young ork who is clearly brilliant and eager to prove himself.
· Dialogue: "My father's ways are over. The future is not about bloodlines, it's about potential. Ork, human, troll, it doesn't matter. What matters is what we can become. Now, show me the results."

Wuxing Incorporated

· History: The new Chinese corp, elected to AAA status in 2059. In the same year, it helped found the Pacific Prosperity Group (PPG) to counter Japanese economic dominance.
· Scene: A meeting of the PPG where Wuxing representatives are negotiating trade deals with other Asian powers, presenting themselves as the new, fair alternative to the old Japanese order.
· Dialogue: "The old powers of the Pacific have held sway for too long. We are building a new order, one of mutual prosperity and respect. Join us, and you will share in the future of Asia."

Novatech

· History: Formed in 2059 when Richard Villiers withdrew his assets from Fuchi, taking its Corporate Court seat with him. It's hungry, aggressive, and a master of corporate scheming.
· Scene: A sleek, minimalist office. Villiers is calm and calculating, offering a job to a runner team that seems impossible, but with a payout that's too good to refuse.
· Dialogue: "Fuchi is dead. The old ways are dead. This is a new era, and in a new era, you need to be ruthless to survive. I need a team that understands that. The job is risky, but the reward... the reward is a chance to be on the winning side."

Horizon

· History: A media and advertising giant, the newest AAA. They deal in information, perception, and narrative control.
· Scene: A high-energy advertising agency where a director is brainstorming a new campaign to spin a recent corporate disaster into a positive story.
· Dialogue: "It's not about what happened. It's about what people think happened. We control the narrative. We are the story. Now, let's make this disaster look like a heroic sacrifice for the greater good!"

🌆 Metahuman Societies & Factions

Ork Society

Orks are the most numerous and least respected metahumans.

· Demographics: First appeared in 2021. They mature at 12 and have an average lifespan of 35-40 years. They are physically larger and stronger than humans.
· Culture: Often forced into the lowest rungs of society, many urban orks live in places like the Ork Underground in Seattle, carved out after race riots. Their culture is often compared to marginalized, street-level "gangsta" cultures. A growing movement is reviving Or'zet, an ancient ork language.
· Scene: A packed, loud bar in the Ork Underground. A group of older orks are arguing about the future of their community and the recent rise of an ork CEO (Yuri Shibanokuji). The air is thick with tension and hope.
· Dialogue: "They say we're just dumb brutes, good for nothing but manual labor and cannon fodder. But look at us. We survived goblinization, we survived the riots, and now we're building our own future. The world is changing, and we're gonna be part of it, whether they like it or not."

Elven Society

Elves are often seen as arrogant and beautiful, with their own nations.

· Tir Tairngire: The "Land of Promise" in the Pacific Northwest. Run by a Council of Princes, it has a rigid caste system. The nation is known for its isolationism and repression of non-elves.
· Tír na nÓg: The elven nation in Ireland, similarly isolationist and repressive. They speak Sperethiel, the elven tongue.
· Scene: The border crossing into Tir Tairngire. The guards are pristine and beautiful, but their eyes are cold and suspicious. They look down on the runner team, treating them as lesser beings.
· Dialogue (Tir Guard): "You are not of the blood. State your business in the Land of Promise and make it quick. We have little patience for those who would defile our shores with their presence."

🕵️ Criminal Syndicates & Factions

· The Mafia: The oldest and most traditional North American crime syndicate. In 2059, it comprises 40-50 families. It's steeped in tradition (omerta and rispetto), but is struggling against newer, more progressive organizations. It is culturally conservative, chauvinistic, and generally distrusts magic.
· The Yakuza: A powerful Japanese syndicate, deeply embedded in the corporate world.
· The Triads: A Chinese syndicate, more accepting of metahumans than the Mafia. A violent war is predicted between the Red Dragon Association and the Yellow Lotus Triad around 2062-2063.
· The Cutters: A prominent UCAS-based gang with a massive membership (10,000 by 2072).
· Humanis Policlub: A virulently anti-metahuman group that receives financial support from various Mafia dons.

🏛️ Major Factions & Power Blocs (Expanded)

The Draco Foundation

History & Purpose: Established on August 15, 2057, to execute the will of the great dragon Dunkelzahn after his assassination. Dunkelzahn owned more wealth than some countries, and the Foundation exists to parcel out his assets while pursuing his less-public goals. The megacorps find the Foundation deeply worrying.

Key Figures: Chairwoman Nadja Daviar (Dunkelzahn's former interpreter), Vice Chair Aina Dupree (mysterious elven woman), and Ryan Mercury (head of the drake sanctuary).

Scene: A Draco Foundation representative approaches the runners in a quiet cafe. She's calm, professional, and offers a job that seems almost charitable—retrieve an artifact from a corporate vault. The catch? The Foundation won't say what it is or why they want it.

Dialogue: "The Foundation doesn't take sides in corporate wars. We have a... different mission. Retrieve the package, and you'll be paid handsomely. Ask too many questions, and we'll find someone else. Dunkelzahn's vision requires discretion."

The Ancients (Elven Go-Gang)

History & Structure: The largest elf gang in the world, based in Seattle and formed from political outcasts of Tir Tairngire. They trace back to the 2030s and have a strict initiation ritual that mimics the Tir Rite of Progression. The Seattle chapter is led by an elf named Belial, who succeeded his father, Green Lucifer.

Rivalries: Deep, ongoing enmity with the Cutters gang, who accept all metatypes except elves. Also in competition with the Laésa elven syndicate.

Scene: A pack of Ancients on souped-up motorcycles roar into a rundown gas station. Their leader, Belial, dismounts and walks toward the runners. His jacket is covered in patches, and his eyes are cold. He's not here for a chat.

Dialogue: "You're in Ancient territory now, omae. The Cutters think they can move product through our streets? You tell them the next shipment gets torched. And if I see your faces around here again, I'll assume you're with them. Capisce?"

Humanis Policlub

History: Formed in 2023 as a loosely-allied organization of anti-metahuman, anti-Amerind human supremacists. They organized the infamous Night of Rage in 2039, when pogroms worldwide killed thousands of metahumans.

Methods: They present a "respectable" face—crisp suits, sanctioned rallies, lawyers, talking heads—while using loosely connected street groups for targeted harassment, arson, and violence when the cameras are off. They've tapped into conservative fundamentalist Christian organizations by creating the Cult of the Seventh Seal.

Scene: A Humanis rally in a Seattle park. The speaker preaches about "protecting human purity" while a group of thugs in the back hand out flyers with coded messages. A few orks watch from across the street, fists clenched. The tension is a powder keg.

Dialogue (Humanis speaker): "They call us racists for wanting to protect our children. But look at what's happened! Goblinization stole our loved ones, turned them into monsters. The elves took our land. The trolls take our jobs. We say: enough! This is human land. This is our world!"

🛠️ Tools to Understand Corporations & Factions

1. The Etiquette System

Shadowrun uses Etiquette skills to navigate different social groups. Understanding these helps you roleplay NPCs:

· Corporate Etiquette: Formal, hierarchical, and protocol-driven. Use titles, avoid direct confrontation, and know your place.
· Street Etiquette: Informal, aggressive, and based on reputation. Respect is earned, not given.
· Gang Etiquette: Territorial, loyalty-based, and often violent. Show respect to leaders, avoid eye contact with rivals.
· Security Etiquette: Professional, suspicious, and by-the-book. Flash a badge, use jargon, and never seem nervous.

2. The "Mr. Johnson" Rule

Runners never interact with the corporation itself—they interact with particular managers, departments, subsidiaries, or task forces. A Johnson's offer comes out of a specific budget. This creates layers of plausible deniability and allows you to create corporate NPCs with their own agendas.

3. The Power of Reputation

In the shadows, reputation is everything. A runner's rep determines who will hire them, how much they get paid, and whether they'll be double-crossed. Use rep as a mechanic for NPC reactions—a team with a solid rep gets respect; a team with a bad rep gets watched.

Los Angeles, 2059: The City of Fallen Angels

Welcome to Los Angeles—not the city of angels, but a sun-scorched, earthquake-ravaged sprawl of 31.7 million souls. By 2059, LA is officially a "Free City," abandoned by the California Free State government after the disastrous El Infierno massacre. It won't be annexed by the Pueblo Corporate Council until 2061, so in 2059 it exists in a state of precarious, violent independence—a city nobody wants to claim, but everyone wants to control.

The city is the world capital of the simsense entertainment industry and the primary base of operations for Horizon, a AAA megacorp. But beneath the glitz lies a city broken by earthquakes, tsunamis, and its own hatred.

1. The Corporate Core (Hollywood, Beverly Hills, Studio City)

Billboards are alive with simsense stars, their faces flickering in hypnotic loops. The air smells of ozone, expensive perfume, and desperation. Behind the polished facades, corporate execs make deals that ruin lives over hundred-nuyen cocktails. Horizon's global headquarters dominates the skyline—a shimmering obsidian monolith that seems to watch everything.

Factions Present:

· Horizon Group (AAA): The dominant power. They don't just report the news—they create it. Their corporate slogan is chillingly apt: "We know what you're thinking.". They run the simsense studios and control the narrative.
· Amalgamated Studios: The old guard of simsense production, fighting to maintain relevance against Horizon's rise.
· Truman Technologies: Another major studio player.
· Brilliant Genesis: A rising star in the simsense world.

Interactions & Politics:

· Horizon is locked in a "shadow war" with the Koshari crime syndicate over the BTL (Better Than Life) simsense chip market.

2. El Infierno (The Inferno)

The Walled-Off Wound of Los Angeles

Scene Description: Imagine a city within a city, sealed behind thirty-foot plascrete walls topped with razorwire and automated turrets. Beyond the walls, the buildings are bombed-out shells. The streets are cratered from years of urban warfare. Fires burn in oil drums. The only law is the law of the strongest. This is El Infierno—the Inferno—a ghetto born from the 2022 race riots. It encompasses former Gardena, Hawthorne, and Compton. The air smells of smoke, rot, and hopelessness. 20,000 people were slaughtered here in a single day by government forces in 2046. The survivors have not forgotten.

Factions Present:

· Burning Angels (Los Angeles Ardientes): The largest and most powerful gang in the entire LA sprawl. Led by Sage, a female mystic adept and honorary member of the Anasazi tribe. Her lieutenants include Fuego (a troll with flashy red cyberware, second-in-command) and Red Brome (the gang's top mage). They accept both humans and metahumans, Awakened and cybered. Initiation requires three confirmed kills and surviving a 20-second beatdown in a chalk circle. Elite members undergo a deadly ritual in the desert with the Anasazi.
· Dragon Tong: A syndicate based in El Infierno.
· Hood Watch: Another gang from the West Compton area.
· La Muerte: A gang that disbanded after the 2046 massacre.
· Green Dragons: Once one of two dominant gangs, backed by the Yakuza. They were decimated in a 2022 war with the Seoulpa-backed Tigers, paving the way for smaller gangs.

Interactions & Politics:

· The Burning Angels are backed by Aztechnology, giving them access to military-grade weaponry and combat drugs. This makes them a proxy in Aztechnology's shadow wars.
· The gang's territory extends all the way east into San Bernardino. They rule the flooded zones and islands that were once South Central, Santa Monica, and parts of Malibu.
· The PCC has left the Burning Angels alone because it would take a large military force to dislodge them.
· El Infierno is a warzone of constant gang warfare. Every block is contested.

Living World Items:

· The Crying Wall: A memorial where residents leave offerings for the 20,000 killed in 2046.
· Chalk Circles: Scattered throughout the district, used for Burning Angel initiations and brutal justice.
· Smugglers' tunnels: Hidden passages under the walls that allow contraband (and runners) to move in and out.

Danger Level: Extreme. This is a warzone. If you're not with a gang or heavily armed, you're prey.

Factions Present:

· Burning Angels: Their primary stronghold. They rule from islands that were once tall buildings and elevated plateaus, using waterscooters, shallow-bottom boats, and submersibles.

Interactions & Politics:

· The Burning Angels charge "tolls" for passage through their waters.

Living World Items:

· Protective suits are mandatory for anyone entering the water.

Danger Level: High. The water is toxic, the wildlife is dangerous, and the gangs are territorial. But the rewards can be immense.
